Yes. Because the F4 sibling category is a preference category, your sibling's spouse and unmarried children under 21 are derivative beneficiaries under section 203(d) of the Immigration and Nationality Act. They need no petition of their own, they share your sibling's priority date, and they can receive visas at the same time as your sibling or later. The catch is that a derivative child must still be a "child" when the visa is issued, which after a wait of many years is the hardest part of a sibling green card case. CLP's [sibling green card](/practice-area/key-services/sibling-green-card) practice plans for derivatives from the first filing; here is what families need to know.

## Who qualifies as a derivative on a sibling petition

Two groups: your sibling's spouse, and your sibling's unmarried children under 21. The children include stepchildren, if your sibling married their parent before they turned 18, and adopted children who meet the adoption rules. The marriage to the spouse does not have to predate the I-130; a spouse acquired after filing but before your sibling immigrates is added to the case. Derivatives of derivatives do not exist, so a grandchild cannot come through the spouse's or child's status. Your sibling's married children are not derivatives at any age.

Your sibling's parents are, of course, your parents too. They are not derivatives on the sibling petition; if you are a U.S. citizen you file separate immediate relative petitions for them, as our [green card for parents](/practice-area/key-services/green-card-for-parents) page explains. List every spouse and child in the family section of Form I-130 even though no separate petitions are needed, and report later marriages and births to USCIS or the National Visa Center so the file is complete before the interview.

## Accompanying versus following to join

A derivative who gets a visa together with your sibling, or within six months after your sibling is admitted, is "accompanying." A derivative who comes later is "following to join," and can do so at any point while your sibling remains a permanent resident, with no new petition and no new priority date, as long as the relationship existed when your sibling immigrated. If your sibling later naturalizes, the following-to-join route closes and your sibling must file I-130 petitions as a citizen instead, which for a spouse and minor children means immediate relative status without a wait. Our post on [how following to join works](/following-to-join-spouse-children) covers the Form I-824 process and the documents.

## The age-out problem for F4 derivative children

A child who is 12 when you file is likely to be past 21 when the priority date becomes current. The Child Status Protection Act helps, but less than families hope. The CSPA age is the child's age when a visa becomes available minus the days the I-130 was pending at USCIS before approval. In many sibling cases USCIS approves the petition within a year or two of filing, so the subtraction is one or two years, not ten. A child who was 24 when the date became current and whose petition was pending for 14 months has a CSPA age of just under 23 and has aged out.

When the formula works, the child must also "seek to acquire" residence within one year of visa availability, by submitting the DS-260, paying the immigrant visa fee, or filing Form I-485 if adjusting in the United States. A child who ages out of a sibling petition does not keep the priority date; the Supreme Court confirmed that reading in 2014. The remedy is a new F2B petition by your sibling once your sibling is a permanent resident, which starts a fresh wait. Our post on [how the CSPA age calculation works](/cspa-age-out-calculation-explained) walks through the formula and the one-year rule with examples.

## Marriage and divorce along the way

A derivative child who marries before the visa is issued loses derivative status, and a later divorce does not reliably restore it. This single rule has cost more sibling families a visa than any other, because the wait is long and the children grow up. Tell them early and tell them clearly.

A derivative spouse's status depends on the marriage continuing until the visa is issued. If your sibling and the spouse divorce during the wait, the spouse is removed; if your sibling remarries, the new spouse is added. If your sibling marries after becoming a permanent resident, the new spouse is not a derivative and needs an F2A petition filed by your sibling.

## Steps that protect the family

- Name every spouse and child on the I-130 and report changes to USCIS or the NVC as they occur.
- Calculate each child's CSPA age before the priority date is current, using the actual USCIS receipt and approval dates, so you know who is protected and who needs a different plan.
- Submit the DS-260 for every derivative within the first month of availability, and keep proof of the submission date.
- Prepare Form I-864 for a household size that includes every derivative, with a joint sponsor lined up if your income will not stretch.
- Plan a follow-on F2B petition for any child who will age out, so it can be filed the day your sibling becomes a resident.
