Most naturalization applicants must take the English and civics tests in English. But some applicants can skip the English test and take the civics test in their own language, including Spanish, with an interpreter. Eligibility depends on your age and how long you have been a permanent resident. Applicants with a qualifying medical disability can also be excused from one or both tests with Form N-648.

## Can I take the citizenship test in Spanish?

Yes, if you qualify for an age-and-residence exemption from the English requirement:

- **50/20 exemption**: You are 50 or older when you file the N-400 and have been a permanent resident for at least 20 years.
- **55/15 exemption**: You are 55 or older when you file and have been a permanent resident for at least 15 years.

If you qualify, you do not take the English reading, writing, or speaking tests. You still take the civics test, but in Spanish or another language, through an interpreter you bring to the interview. The interpreter must be fluent in English and your language and cannot be your attorney.

## The 65/20 special consideration

If you are 65 or older and have been a permanent resident for at least 20 years, you are exempt from English and receive special consideration on civics: you study a shorter list of designated questions. You can take that civics test in your language.

## The civics test itself

The civics test has changed in recent years, including a revised test for applications filed on or after a 2025 date. Check which version applies based on your filing date, and study from the official USCIS materials for that version. Questions can be asked in your language if you qualify for an exemption, but the content is the same.

## Questions for the citizenship test in Spanish

USCIS publishes the civics questions and answers in Spanish and other languages as study aids. Use the official versions. Unofficial lists may be outdated or match a different test version.

## The N-648 medical disability exception

If you have a physical or developmental disability or a mental impairment that prevents you from learning or demonstrating knowledge of English or civics, you can request an exception with **Form N-648, Medical Certification for Disability Exceptions**.

Key requirements:

- The form must be completed by a licensed medical doctor, doctor of osteopathy, or licensed clinical psychologist.
- The condition must have lasted, or be expected to last, at least 12 months, and cannot be caused by illegal drug use.
- The professional must explain how the condition prevents you from meeting the requirement, not just name the diagnosis.

The N-648 can be filed with the N-400 or brought to the interview. Officers review it carefully, and vague forms are often found insufficient.

## What the exemptions do not cover

- You still must meet every other requirement: continuous residence, physical presence, good moral character, and attachment to the Constitution.
- You still must take the Oath of Allegiance, unless a separate disability waiver of the oath applies.
- An exemption from English does not excuse you from understanding the interview, which is why the interpreter matters.

## How to prepare

- Confirm your age and green card date on the day you file.
- Choose a qualified interpreter early.
- If using an N-648, work with a provider who understands what USCIS needs to see.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to bring my own interpreter?
Yes. USCIS does not provide interpreters for naturalization interviews. Your interpreter must be fluent in both languages and complete the required declaration.

### What if I fail the civics test?
You get a second chance, usually within 60 to 90 days, to retake the part you failed.

### Can I use the N-648 for English only?
Yes. The form can request an exception from English, civics, or both.

### Does age alone exempt me?
Age plus years as a permanent resident does. Age alone does not.

### Can my family doctor fill out the N-648?
Only if the doctor is a licensed medical doctor or doctor of osteopathy, or a licensed clinical psychologist, and the explanation meets USCIS standards.

### Can I bring study materials in Spanish?
Yes. USCIS publishes civics study materials in Spanish and other languages.

### Does the interpreter need to be certified?
No formal certification is required, but the interpreter must be competent in both languages and impartial. An attorney representing you cannot serve as your interpreter.
