An Australian citizen with a specialty occupation job offer usually qualifies for both an E-3 and an H-1B, so the E-3 vs H-1B question is a real choice rather than a default. The short version: the E-3 wins on speed, cost, and the absence of a lottery, and its spouse work rights are better. The H-1B wins on dual intent and on the ability to start with a new employer the day a petition is filed. Which of those matters more depends on whether a green card is in the plan. Our [E-3 visa practice](/practice-area/key-services/e-3-visa) starts every Australian client with this comparison, and the sections below set out the points that decide it.

## Cap and lottery: the biggest practical difference

The H-1B regular cap is 65,000 per fiscal year, with another 20,000 reserved for holders of U.S. master's degrees or higher. Demand runs far past supply, so USCIS runs an electronic registration in March and selects randomly. A selected worker cannot start cap-subject H-1B employment before October 1. Only employers that are cap-exempt, which our article on [which employers are H-1B cap-exempt](/h-1b-cap-exempt-employers) describes, can file at any time of year.

The E-3 has its own limit of 10,500 per fiscal year, but it has never been a constraint in practice. There is no registration, no selection, and no fixed start date. If the offer comes in January, you can be at a consulate in February. For an Australian whose employer missed the H-1B registration window or lost the lottery, the E-3 is the obvious repair.

## Filing route and cost to the employer

Every H-1B requires the employer to file Form I-129 with USCIS, with the government fees that go with it, before the worker can apply for a visa. The E-3 consular route skips the petition entirely: the employer certifies a Labor Condition Application (Form ETA-9035), the worker applies at a consulate, and the first USCIS filing happens only if there is later a change of status or an in-country extension. Both categories require the same LCA with the same prevailing wage attestations, so the wage obligation is identical. The employer-side savings on the E-3 are real, and for a small company they can decide the question.

## Duration and renewals

An H-1B is granted in increments of up to three years with a six-year maximum, extendable beyond six only when a PERM labor certification or I-140 petition has been pending or approved long enough to trigger the AC21 extension rules. An E-3 is granted in increments of up to two years, and there is no six-year ceiling. You can renew for as long as the job, the LCA, and your intent to depart hold up. The tradeoff is more frequent paperwork: a two-year cycle instead of three.

## Dual intent and the green card plan

This is where the H-1B earns its reputation. H-1B status is dual intent by statute, so an approved I-140 or a pending adjustment of status does not undermine it, and consular officers do not refuse H-1B visas for immigrant intent. The E-3 is not dual intent. You must intend to depart when status ends, and while an immigrant petition is not an automatic bar, a consular interview after an I-140 approval needs careful handling.

In practice, Australians who want a green card take one of three paths: switch to H-1B before the immigrant process gets far, stay in E-3 and apply for adjustment of status inside the United States without consular travel at the sensitive stage, or run a self-petition such as EB-2 NIW with the same timing discipline. Our guide on [changing from H-1B to a green card](/can-you-change-h-1b-to-green-card) shows how the H-1B side of that plan works, and the [H-1B practice page](/practice-area/key-services/h-1b-visa) explains how we handle the switch.

## Spouses and children

The spouse of an E-3 worker holds E-3D status and is authorized to work for any employer incident to that status, with no separate petition and no wait for an employment authorization document. The spouse of an H-1B worker holds H-4 status and can work only after the H-1B principal has an approved I-140 (or qualifies for an AC21 extension) and the spouse obtains an EAD. For a two-career couple, that difference alone can settle the E-3 vs H-1B question. Children in either category may attend school but may not work.

## Changing employers

H-1B portability under INA 214(n) lets a worker start with a new employer as soon as the new employer's petition is received by USCIS. The E-3 has no portability rule. A new employer must either obtain approval of a change of employer request on Form I-129 or send the worker to a consulate with a new LCA for a new visa, and the worker cannot start until one of those is done. Both categories give a 60-day grace period after employment ends, once per authorized period, to find a new sponsor or leave.

## Which one to pick

Choose the E-3 if you need to start soon, the employer is cost-sensitive, your spouse wants to work, or the plan is a few years in the United States with an open mind about what follows. Choose the H-1B if a green card is the stated goal, the employer is cap-exempt or has already won a registration, or job mobility matters more than filing speed. Many Australians start on an E-3 and move to an H-1B when a green card plan firms up, which is a sound sequence as long as the H-1B cap is accounted for.
