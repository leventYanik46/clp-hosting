Moving from F-1 to H-1B is the most common path from studying in the U.S. to working here long term. Two rules make it work: change of status, which lets you switch to H-1B without leaving the country, and cap-gap, which keeps your F-1 status and work authorization alive while the H-1B is pending. Both changed in important ways in 2025, and getting the details right now has real financial stakes for your employer.

## How F-1 to H-1B works

1. Your employer registers you in the H-1B lottery in March.
2. If selected, the employer files Form I-129 with an approved LCA, requesting a change of status from F-1 to H-1B.
3. If approved, your status converts to H-1B on the start date, usually October 1, without a trip abroad.

You can also ask for consular notification instead, meaning you would leave and get an H-1B visa stamp at a consulate. Under current rules that choice can be very expensive, as explained below.

## What cap-gap protection covers

Without cap-gap, many students would see their OPT end in spring or summer and have no permission to work until October 1. Cap-gap fills that hole.

Under the H-1B modernization rule effective January 17, 2025, if your employer timely files a change of status petition for you, your F-1 status and OPT work authorization are automatically extended until April 1 of the fiscal year for which H-1B status is requested, or until the approved start date, whichever comes first. Before this rule, the cutoff was October 1, which left students stranded if USCIS had not decided the petition by then.

To qualify:

- The petition must request change of status, not consular notification
- It must be filed while you are in status, including during your 60-day grace period
- Your OPT must be valid when it is filed if you want to keep working

If the petition is filed during your grace period after OPT has ended, cap-gap extends your stay but does not give you permission to work.

Ask your school's DSO for an updated I-20 showing the cap-gap extension. Your employer will need your receipt notice and the I-20 for Form I-9 reverification.

## Why staying in the U.S. now matters more

Since September 21, 2025, a presidential proclamation requires a $100,000 payment for certain new H-1B petitions for workers outside the U.S. USCIS guidance says the payment does not apply to a petition requesting change of status for someone inside the U.S. when that change is granted. If the change of status is denied but the petition is approved for consular processing, the payment can become due.

In practice that means:

- Maintaining F-1 status through the process is critical
- Travel abroad while the petition is pending can abandon the change of status request
- Any gap in status, such as unauthorized work or a lapse between programs, deserves review before filing

## When cap-gap ends early

Cap-gap ends if the H-1B petition is denied, rejected, revoked, or withdrawn. If that happens, you generally have the regular 60-day F-1 grace period to leave, start a new program, or change to another status.

## STEM OPT and the lottery

If you have STEM OPT, you may get up to three lottery tries. Students without STEM extensions often get one or two. Plan the registration years early in your OPT period.

## Common mistakes

- Traveling internationally after the H-1B is filed, which can abandon the change of status
- Employers checking "consular notification" by default
- Letting OPT unemployment days run past the allowed limit before the filing
- Forgetting to update I-9 records with the cap-gap I-20


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I change employers during cap-gap?

Cap-gap is tied to the specific H-1B petition that was filed for you. If you leave that employer, the cap-gap extension may end. Talk to the DSO and an attorney before changing jobs.

### What if I was not selected in the lottery?

You keep your OPT for its remaining validity, plus any STEM extension you qualify for. You can register again the next year if you still have OPT time, or consider cap-exempt employers, the O-1, or other options.

### Does cap-gap cover my dependents?

F-2 dependents generally keep their status through the cap-gap period along with the F-1 student. They cannot work in F-2 status.
