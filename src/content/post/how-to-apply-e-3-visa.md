The E-3 visa application process has no USCIS petition stage when you apply at a consulate, which is why Australian professionals often go from signed offer letter to visa stamp faster than colleagues on other work visas. The sequence is short: the employer certifies a Labor Condition Application, you assemble your evidence, you file a DS-160 and attend an interview, and you enter the United States. If you are already in the country in another status, there is a second route through Form I-129. Our [E-3 visa team](/practice-area/key-services/e-3-visa) handles both; here is how each step works and where the timing can slip.

## Step 1: The employer files the Labor Condition Application

Everything starts with Form ETA-9035, filed by the employer in the Department of Labor's FLAG system with the E-3 Australia classification selected. The LCA names the job title, the worksite, the wage, and the validity period (up to two years). The employer attests that it will pay at least the prevailing wage for the occupation and location, that the hire will not harm the working conditions of similar U.S. employees, and that it has posted notice of the filing at the worksite.

Two practical points. First-time filers must have their Federal Employer Identification Number verified in FLAG before the LCA can be submitted, and that verification is a separate step that should be started early. Second, the start date on the LCA should match the realistic start date, because you cannot be admitted before the LCA validity begins.

## Step 2: Assemble your evidence

The consulate expects a tidy package that proves each element of eligibility. Bring:

- Your Australian passport, valid well beyond your intended start date
- The certified LCA, signed by the employer
- A job offer letter that states the title, duties, salary, start date, and the degree the role requires
- Your degree certificate and transcripts, with a credential evaluation if the degree is a three-year program or was earned outside Australia
- A current resume and, where the job is licensed, proof of the license or your eligibility for it
- Evidence of ties to Australia if your circumstances might raise an intent question

Our article on [the E-3 visa requirements](/e-3-visa-requirements-australian-citizens) explains what each of these documents must show.

## Step 3: Complete the DS-160 and book the interview

The DS-160 is the online nonimmigrant visa application. Fill it in from the LCA and the offer letter so that the employer name, address, job title, and salary match exactly. Inconsistencies between the DS-160 and the LCA are a common reason for a 221(g) hold. Pay the visa application fee, then schedule an interview.

Most Australians apply in Australia, but E-3 applications can be lodged at other posts as a third-country national, subject to each post's own rules on who it will see. Check appointment availability on the State Department's site before you resign from a current job, because wait times differ widely between posts and seasons.

## Step 4: The interview

E-3 interviews are usually short. The officer confirms your citizenship, looks at the LCA and offer letter, and asks about the job and your qualifications. Expect questions on what you will actually do day to day, why the role needs your degree, and what your plans are when the status ends. Answer in the same terms the LCA uses. If the officer needs more information, you will receive a 221(g) notice listing what is missing; respond with exactly what is asked and nothing extra.

If approved, the visa is issued for up to two years, matched to the LCA. The passport is returned by courier in most posts.

## Step 5: Entering the United States and checking your I-94

At the port of entry, Customs and Border Protection admits you in E-3 status for the validity period on the LCA, and may admit you up to ten days before the start date. Within a day or two of arrival, retrieve your I-94 record from the CBP website and check the class of admission and the expiration date. The I-94, not the visa stamp, controls how long you may stay. Spouses and children enter in E-3D status and can read more in our note on [E-3 spouse work authorization](/e-3-visa-spouse-work-authorization).

## The alternative: changing status from inside the United States

If you are already in the United States in a valid status (F-1 on OPT, H-1B, L-1, or even B-2 in narrow cases), the employer can file Form I-129 with the certified LCA and request a change of status to E-3. Premium processing is available. You cannot start work until USCIS approves the request, and you should not travel while it is pending, because departure abandons a change of status application.

Approval gives you E-3 status, not an E-3 visa. The next time you leave the country you will need to apply for the visa at a consulate before returning. For many clients that is a reason to go the consular route from the start. Our [change and extension of status](/practice-area/key-services/change-and-extension-of-status) page covers how we decide between the two.

## Timing choices that save trouble

Keep the offer letter identical to the LCA throughout, down to the job title. Do not let the LCA start date drift past the interview date without checking that the period still works. Give the employer a copy of your I-94 on arrival so its own records match. And if the plan includes a green card down the line, raise it before the interview rather than after, since the E-3 requires an intent to depart and the strategy around that question should be set before an officer asks it.
