Consular processing is the way people outside the U.S. complete a green card case. Instead of filing an I-485 with USCIS, the applicant goes through the State Department's National Visa Center and an interview at a U.S. embassy or consulate abroad, receives an immigrant visa, and becomes a permanent resident upon entering the U.S. It is used in both family and employment cases.

## When consular processing applies

- The beneficiary lives outside the U.S.
- The beneficiary is in the U.S. but is not eligible to adjust status, for example because of an entry without inspection
- The family chooses it because the local consulate is faster than USCIS for their case

## Step 1: The petition is approved

USCIS approves the I-130, I-140, or other immigrant petition and forwards it to the National Visa Center.

## Step 2: The National Visa Center stage

The NVC creates a case and sends a welcome notice with a case number and an invoice ID. In the online Consular Electronic Application Center, you will:

1. Pay the immigrant visa application fee and, for family cases, the affidavit of support fee
2. Complete Form DS-260, the online immigrant visa application
3. Submit the sponsor's Form I-864 Affidavit of Support with tax records, for family cases
4. Upload civil documents: birth certificate, marriage certificate, police certificates from countries where you lived, court records if any, and passport

Once the NVC confirms everything is complete, the case is "documentarily qualified" and waits for an interview slot.

## Step 3: The medical exam

Before the interview, the applicant completes a medical exam with a panel physician approved by the embassy. The panel physician sends results to the consulate. Vaccination requirements are similar to those in the U.S.

## Step 4: The interview

A consular officer reviews documents and asks about the relationship or job and the applicant's history. Bring originals of every document uploaded to the NVC. The officer may approve, refuse, or place the case in administrative processing under section 221(g).

## Step 5: Entering the U.S.

If approved, the applicant receives an immigrant visa in their passport and a sealed packet, if one is issued. Before traveling, pay the USCIS Immigrant Fee online so the green card can be produced. On arrival, CBP admits the person as a permanent resident, and the green card arrives by mail.

## Consular processing vs. adjustment of status

- **Consular processing:** Done abroad, often faster in some posts, but requires travel and leaves no option to stay in the U.S. if the case is delayed.
- **Adjustment of status:** Done in the U.S., allows a work permit and travel document while pending, but USCIS processing can be long.

People in the U.S. with unlawful presence should not leave for consular processing without advice. Departure can trigger the three- or ten-year bars, and a provisional unlawful presence waiver may be needed first.

## Common delays

- Missing or incorrect civil documents, especially police certificates
- Insufficient affidavit of support income without a joint sponsor
- Administrative processing after the interview
- Embassy appointment backlogs


## Frequently asked questions

### How long does consular processing take?

It depends on the category, the NVC's backlog, and the embassy. Some cases move from petition approval to interview in months. Others wait much longer for an appointment.

### Can my family attend the interview with me?

Derivative family members applying with you attend the interview. A U.S. petitioner usually does not attend.

### What if I am refused at the interview?

The officer will explain the refusal and whether more documents or a waiver could fix it. Some refusals can be overcome quickly. Others need legal review.

### Do I need a joint sponsor?

If the petitioner's income does not meet the affidavit of support requirement, a joint sponsor who is a U.S. citizen or permanent resident living in the U.S. can sign a separate I-864.
