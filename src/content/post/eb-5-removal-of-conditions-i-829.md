Form I-829 is the second half of every EB-5 case. Your first green card as an investor is conditional and valid for two years. To keep permanent residence, you must file Form I-829 during the 90 days before that card expires and prove three things: you invested the capital, you kept it at risk for the period the law requires, and the investment created, or will soon create, at least ten full-time jobs. Miss the window or fall short on the evidence, and conditional residence can end. This post explains the timing, the proof, and what happens if the project ran into trouble; our [EB-5 green card](/practice-area/key-services/eb-5-green-card) page covers how we handle both stages.

## When to file: the 90-day window

You file Form I-829 within the 90 days before the second anniversary of the date you became a conditional resident. That date is printed on your green card. Do not file early; USCIS will reject a petition filed before the window opens.

If you do not file on time, conditional residence terminates automatically at the two-year mark. USCIS can accept a late petition only if you show good cause and extenuating circumstances for the delay, and you should not count on that. Put the window on your calendar on the day the card arrives.

Your spouse and children who received conditional green cards through your case are usually included on your I-829. If a family member cannot be included, for example after a divorce or because a child is filing separately, that person may need to file a separate petition.

Once USCIS receives the petition, the receipt notice extends your conditional residence for a stated period. Keep it with your expired card; together they serve as proof of status for work and travel. Check the period on the notice, because the case may take longer to decide.

## What the I-829 must prove

The petition looks backward at everything you promised in the first stage. We build it around three questions.

Did you invest? The record must show the capital actually went into the new commercial enterprise in the amount required, not just that you subscribed.

Was the investment sustained? The capital must have stayed at risk for the required period. Distributions, early repayments, or a redemption that pulled your money out too soon can undermine the case.

Were the jobs created? For a direct investment, that means the enterprise's own payroll: at least ten full-time qualifying employees. For a regional center project, it means evidence that the project spent what the economic model assumed, on the activities the model assumed, so the modeled jobs can be credited. If not all jobs exist yet, USCIS can approve the petition when they will be created within a reasonable time, but that needs solid evidence about the project's progress.

## Evidence to gather

The documents differ by structure, but a typical I-829 file includes:

- proof of the capital transfer and the enterprise's records showing receipt
- the enterprise's financial statements and tax returns for the conditional period
- for direct investments, payroll records, quarterly employer tax filings, and Forms I-9 and W-2 for qualifying employees
- for regional center projects, construction and expenditure records, a project status report, and an updated economic analysis
- evidence that the business is still operating, or explanation if it has completed its purpose

In a regional center project, most of this comes from the developer. Ask early what the project will provide and when. Investors in the same project often file in waves, and developers that prepare the package once for everyone tend to deliver more complete files.

## If the project ran into trouble

Projects change. Construction slips, financing gets restructured, a hotel opens late, a business pivots. Whether a change hurts your I-829 depends on what changed, when, and which rules applied to your investment. Job creation that is merely delayed is often manageable with good evidence. A change that means the capital was never used the way the petition described is harder.

USCIS may issue a Request for Evidence or schedule an interview. Answer with documents, not assurances. If you invested through a regional center and the project's status is unclear, start collecting information a year before your window, not a month.

## After approval or denial

When USCIS approves the I-829, you receive a ten-year green card and become a permanent resident without conditions. The two years you spent as a conditional resident count toward the residence period for naturalization, so citizenship may be closer than you think.

If USCIS denies the petition, your conditional residence ends. There is no administrative appeal, but you can ask an immigration judge to review the denial in removal proceedings, where the government carries the burden of proof.
