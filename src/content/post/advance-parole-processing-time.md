Advance parole is a travel document, issued on Form I-512 or as part of a combination work and travel card, that lets certain people leave the U.S. and return without abandoning a pending application. It is most often used by green card applicants with a pending I-485, DACA recipients, and people with certain humanitarian cases. Advance parole processing time is often long, so plan ahead, and understand that the document permits you to seek reentry; it does not guarantee it.

## Who needs advance parole

- **Pending green card applicants.** If you leave the U.S. while your I-485 is pending without advance parole, USCIS will generally treat the application as abandoned. An exception exists for H-1B, L-1, and their dependents who maintain status and reenter on a valid H or L visa.
- **DACA recipients**, for humanitarian, educational, or employment travel.
- **TPS holders**, who request travel authorization through a similar process.
- **Asylum applicants**, though travel by asylum applicants carries serious risks discussed below.

## How to apply

File Form I-131 with USCIS, with the fee, photos, and evidence of why you need to travel. Green card applicants often file I-131 with the I-485 so the travel document arrives alongside the work permit as a combination card.

## Advance parole processing time

USCIS posts processing times by form and service center. Standalone I-131 applications can take many months. Check the USCIS processing times page before planning any trip, and do not book nonrefundable travel based on a hoped-for approval.

## Emergency advance parole

If you need to travel urgently because of a family member's serious illness or death, or another urgent humanitarian reason, you can request an emergency appointment at a local USCIS field office through the USCIS Contact Center. Bring:

- A completed Form I-131 and fee
- Passport photos
- Your ID and immigration documents
- Evidence of the emergency, such as a doctor's letter or death certificate, with translation
- Proof of planned travel dates

Approval is discretionary and not guaranteed, but in a true emergency it can sometimes be issued the same day or within days.

## Travel risks to weigh

**Unlawful presence.** For most applicants, travel on advance parole does not trigger the three- or ten-year bars. But anyone with complex history, such as prior removal orders or entries without inspection, needs individual advice.

**Reentry is discretionary.** CBP can deny parole at the port of entry.

**Asylum applicants.** Returning to the country you fear can seriously damage your claim.

**Prior removal orders.** Leaving the U.S. with an outstanding removal order may count as self-deportation and trigger reentry bars, even with advance parole.

**Pending interviews.** If USCIS schedules a biometrics or interview while you are away, missing it can delay or derail your case.

## Before you travel

- Carry the original advance parole document
- Carry copies of your pending application receipts
- Return before the document expires
- Keep proof of the reason for travel


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I travel while my advance parole is pending?

No. If you leave before the document is issued, USCIS will generally deny the pending advance parole request, and for green card applicants, the I-485 may be treated as abandoned.

### How long is advance parole valid?

The document shows its validity period, often one year or longer for pending adjustment cases. You must return before it expires.

### Can I use advance parole multiple times?

Many advance parole documents allow multiple entries during the validity period. Check the document itself.

### Can I request an expedite for advance parole?

Yes. USCIS considers expedite requests case by case for reasons such as urgent humanitarian need, severe financial loss, or a clear USCIS error. Submit evidence with the request.
