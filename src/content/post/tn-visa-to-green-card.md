You can move from TN status to a green card, but you have to manage one problem: the TN requires that you intend to leave the U.S. when your work ends. A green card application signals the opposite. Because the TN does not allow dual intent the way the H-1B and L-1 do, the order and timing of each step matter more than for most visa holders.

## Why TN and green cards conflict

TN status for Canadian and Mexican professionals is granted on the premise of temporary stay. When you renew TN status at the border or a consulate, or request an extension from USCIS, an officer can deny it if they believe you now intend to immigrate. That risk grows as your green card case advances.

Having an employer start a PERM labor certification is generally not treated as immigrant intent by itself. Filing an I-140 is usually manageable. Filing Form I-485 to adjust status is where the conflict becomes direct.

## Common strategies

### File the I-485 and rely on the pending case

Once your priority date is current, you can file the I-485 with applications for a work permit (EAD) and travel document (advance parole). After filing, you remain in the U.S. under a pending adjustment, but you should not travel on your TN or seek a TN extension. Plan for a period where your EAD is your work authorization and advance parole is your travel permission.

Because DHS ended automatic EAD extensions for renewals filed on or after October 30, 2025, keep careful track of EAD expiration dates if the case takes longer than expected.

### Switch to H-1B or L-1 first

The H-1B and L-1 allow dual intent. Moving to one of them before the green card stage removes the conflict. The H-1B requires a lottery selection unless the employer is cap-exempt. An L-1 requires a year of qualifying employment abroad with a related company.

### Consular processing

If you are outside the U.S. when your priority date becomes current, you can complete the green card through the National Visa Center and a consular interview, avoiding the adjustment problem entirely. This works best when the timing lines up with a natural break in TN employment.

### Marriage or family-based options

If you marry a U.S. citizen, adjustment may be possible, but the timing and your intent at each entry will be examined. Get advice before filing.

## Timing your TN renewals

The safest window to renew TN status, especially at a port of entry, is before the I-140 is filed or well before the I-485 stage. Many applicants renew with USCIS from inside the U.S. instead of at the border, though an extension request can still be denied for immigrant intent.

## Travel risks

- Traveling on TN after the I-485 is filed can be treated as abandonment of the adjustment
- At the border, CBP may ask directly whether a green card is in process; you must answer truthfully
- If you are refused TN entry, you may need to restart the work authorization plan from outside the U.S.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can my TN spouse work?

No. TD dependents cannot work. Once an I-485 is filed, the spouse can apply for an EAD based on the pending green card case.

### Will a PERM filing stop my TN renewal?

Usually not on its own, but officers have discretion. Be truthful if asked. Renewing before the employer files the I-140 lowers the risk.

### Can I keep TN status after the I-485 is approved?

Once you become a permanent resident, you no longer need TN status. You can work for any employer, although your sponsoring employer will expect you to take the job offered.

### Is TN to green card easier for Canadians or Mexicans?

The immigrant intent rules are the same. The practical difference is that Canadians usually get TN status at the border without a visa, while Mexican nationals need a TN visa from a consulate, which adds another point where intent can be questioned.
