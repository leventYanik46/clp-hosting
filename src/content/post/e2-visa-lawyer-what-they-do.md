An E-2 visa lawyer helps an investor from a treaty country prove three things to the U.S. government: that they are eligible by nationality, that the investment is real and substantial, and that the business is more than a way to earn a living. The strongest E-2 cases are built before the money is spent, which is why many investors speak to counsel before signing a lease or buying a business.

## What the E-2 visa is

The E-2 is a nonimmigrant visa for investors who are nationals of a country that has a qualifying treaty with the United States. The investor must have invested, or be in the process of investing, a substantial amount in a real and operating business, and must be coming to develop and direct it. The enterprise cannot be marginal, meaning it should be able to generate more than a minimal living for the investor and family, or have a real economic impact. Employees of the investor who share the same nationality may qualify as E-2 employees if they are executives, supervisors or have essential skills.

The E-2 can be renewed as long as the business keeps qualifying, but it is a temporary status with no direct path to a green card.

## What an E-2 lawyer handles

- **Treaty eligibility.** Confirming the investor's nationality and, for companies, the ownership structure that must be at least half owned by treaty nationals.
- **Investment structure.** Advising whether the amount is substantial in proportion to the business, and that the funds are committed and at risk, not simply sitting in an account.
- **Source of funds.** Documenting that the money was lawfully obtained, and tracing it from its origin to the business.
- **Business review.** Evaluating whether the enterprise is real and operating, and whether it can avoid being treated as marginal.
- **Business plan.** Reviewing or guiding the plan, projections and hiring forecast.
- **Application strategy.** Deciding whether to apply at a U.S. consulate or to request a change of status in the U.S., and preparing the evidence package.
- **Interview preparation.** Walking the investor through what a consular officer is likely to ask.
- **Follow-up.** Handling extensions, changes in the business and planning for what comes after.

## When an E-2 attorney is most helpful

- You are still choosing a business to buy or start, and want to know whether it will qualify.
- Your funds come from several sources or from outside the United States.
- You have been refused before.
- You want to bring employees of the same nationality.
- You are unsure whether to apply from abroad or inside the U.S.

## Choosing counsel

Ask how many E-2 cases the attorney has handled, whether they have experience with your treaty country and your type of business, and who prepares the documents. Also ask what is included in the fee, including interview preparation and responses to government questions.

## What you can do before the first meeting

Collect your passport and proof of nationality, evidence of the source of your funds, any business plan you have, and details of the business you want to buy or open. The sooner counsel sees real numbers, the more specific the advice can be.

An early consultation can save you from an investment that would not qualify.
