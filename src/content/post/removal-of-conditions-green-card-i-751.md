If you got your green card through a marriage that was less than two years old on the day the card was approved, your card is conditional and expires after two years. To keep your permanent resident status, you must file Form I-751, Petition to Remove Conditions on Residence, during the 90 days before that card expires. Miss the window without a good reason and your status ends automatically, which can lead to removal proceedings.

## Removal of conditions vs. green card renewal

This is the most common mix-up. A conditional green card cannot be renewed with Form I-90, the form used to renew or replace a 10-year green card. Conditional residents can use the I-90 to replace a lost or damaged card, but not to extend an expiring one. The I-751 is a new review of whether your marriage was genuine, not a card renewal. Once USCIS approves it, you receive a 10-year green card, and from then on the I-90 is the right form when that card needs renewing.

So if you are looking for an immigration lawyer for green card renewal and your card shows only two years between the issue and expiration dates, what you actually need is removal of conditions.

## When to file the I-751

For a joint petition, the filing window opens 90 days before the expiration date on your conditional card and closes on that date. Count backward from the date printed on the card, not the date it arrived in the mail. If you file too early, USCIS will send the petition back. If you file late, include a written explanation of why, and USCIS will decide whether to accept it.

If you file with a waiver of the joint filing requirement, you do not have to wait for the 90-day window. You can file once the circumstances that qualify you for the waiver exist, including after the card has expired.

## Joint petition or waiver

Most couples file jointly, with both spouses signing. You can file on your own and ask for a waiver of the joint filing requirement if:

- You entered the marriage in good faith, but it ended in divorce or annulment.
- Your U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse has died.
- You or your child were battered or subjected to extreme cruelty by your spouse.
- Losing your status and being removed would cause you extreme hardship.

Separation or a pending divorce makes strategy harder. If you file jointly while a divorce is in progress, USCIS may send a request for evidence asking you to convert the petition to a waiver once the divorce is final. If your marriage is in trouble, talk to a lawyer before you file.

## Evidence for removal of conditions on your green card

USCIS is asking the same question it asked at the original green card stage: was the marriage real when you entered it? The difference now is that you have up to two years of shared life to show. Focus on documents from the period since you became a conditional resident:

- A lease, deed, or mortgage with both names, and mail addressed to both of you at that address.
- Joint bank account statements that show real household use, not an account opened only for the case.
- Joint federal tax returns or IRS tax transcripts.
- Health, auto, or life insurance listing each other.
- Birth certificates of children born during the marriage.
- Photos together over time, travel records, and shared bills.
- Sworn statements from at least two people who know you both and can describe your relationship, with their contact details.

Quality matters more than volume. A few months of joint bank statements showing groceries, rent, and utilities say more than a hundred wedding photos.

## What happens after you file

USCIS sends a receipt notice that extends your conditional resident status past the card's expiration date. Keep the notice with your expired green card. Together they serve as proof of status for travel, work, and a new employer's I-9 check. In recent years USCIS has issued receipts extending status for 48 months, which tells you how long these cases can take.

You will then be scheduled for biometrics. USCIS may approve the petition without an interview or call you in for one. If you are married to a U.S. citizen, you may be able to apply for naturalization after three years as a permanent resident, counting from the start date on your conditional card, even while the I-751 is still pending.

## Common mistakes that cause delays or denials

- Filing before the 90-day window opens.
- Missing one spouse's signature on a joint petition.
- Using an outdated edition of the form or sending the wrong fee. Check the USCIS form page right before you mail it.
- Leaving out children who received conditional status at the same time as you or within 90 days after. They are usually included on your petition.
- Sending only evidence from before the green card, with nothing covering the last two years.
- Moving without updating your address with USCIS, then missing a biometrics notice or request for evidence.
- Not disclosing arrests or other issues that came up after you got your green card. These need to be dealt with directly.

## Do you need a lawyer for removal of conditions?

Many straightforward joint petitions are filed without a lawyer. Legal help matters most when you are separated or divorced, filing a waiver, short on joint evidence, have had an arrest, or have received a request for evidence or an interview notice.
