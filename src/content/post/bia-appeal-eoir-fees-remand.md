The Board of Immigration Appeals is the appeals body for immigration court decisions. If an immigration judge denies your case or orders you removed, you generally have 30 calendar days from the decision to file an appeal. That deadline is strict. Missing it usually ends your right to appeal, and the judge's order becomes final.

## How to file a BIA appeal

You file Form EOIR-26, Notice of Appeal from a Decision of an Immigration Judge. The BIA must receive it within 30 days. It can be filed electronically through EOIR's online system if you or your attorney are registered, or by mail or courier.

The notice must state the specific reasons for the appeal. A vague statement such as "the judge was wrong" can lead to summary dismissal. After filing, the BIA sets a briefing schedule, usually after the hearing transcript is prepared.

## EOIR fees

An EOIR-26 appeal requires a filing fee unless you request a fee waiver on Form EOIR-26A. EOIR fees were raised in 2025 for appeals and several motions, so confirm the current amount on EOIR's website or with your attorney before filing. DHS-initiated appeals do not require a fee from you.

## What the BIA reviews

The BIA reviews legal questions from scratch, but it gives deference to the judge's factual findings, including credibility, unless they are clearly erroneous. That means appeals usually succeed on legal errors, such as applying the wrong standard, or on a factual finding that the record cannot support.

New evidence is generally not considered on appeal. If you have new evidence, a motion to remand or to reopen may be the right tool.

## BIA appeal processing time

There is no fixed timeline. Appeals often take many months, and some take longer. Detained cases are prioritized. While the appeal is pending, a removal order is generally not enforced, because a timely appeal to the BIA automatically stays removal in most cases.

## What a BIA remand order means

A remand sends the case back to the immigration judge for further proceedings. A board of immigration appeals remand order typically says why, for example:

- To make additional factual findings
- To apply the correct legal standard
- To consider new evidence
- To allow background checks to be updated

A remand is not an approval. It reopens part of the case, and the judge may reach the same or a different result.

## Other BIA outcomes

- **Sustained:** The BIA agrees with you and may grant relief or remand
- **Dismissed:** The BIA affirms the judge's decision
- **Summary affirmance:** A short order affirming without a full opinion

If the BIA dismisses the appeal, the next step may be a petition for review in a federal court of appeals, which must generally be filed within 30 days of the BIA decision. That filing does not automatically stop removal.

## Contacting the BIA

The Board of Immigration Appeals phone number and case status information are available through EOIR's website and automated case information system. Have your A-number ready.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I be removed while my BIA appeal is pending?

A timely BIA appeal from most immigration judge decisions automatically stays removal. If the appeal is late or the BIA rules against you, the stay ends.

### Can I add new evidence on appeal?

Generally no. If you have new, material evidence, a motion to remand may be the right tool, filed with the BIA.

### Do I need a lawyer for a BIA appeal?

You are not required to have one, but appeals turn on legal arguments, and the brief is where cases are won or lost.

### Can I appeal a bond decision?

Yes. Bond decisions can be appealed to the BIA separately from the main case, and the appeal deadline is also 30 days.
