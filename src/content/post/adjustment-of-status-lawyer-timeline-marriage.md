Adjustment of status is the process of applying for a green card from inside the United States, using Form I-485, instead of leaving to apply at a consulate. It can save time and spare you a trip abroad, but only if you are eligible, and some eligibility problems are not obvious. An adjustment of status lawyer is most useful for spotting those problems before you file.

## What adjustment of status is

If a visa number is available to you in your category, and you are in the United States, you may be able to ask USCIS to change your status to permanent resident without consular processing. The application is Form I-485, filed with supporting forms. Most adjustment cases are based on family or employment.

## Who can adjust

In general, you must:

- Be physically present in the United States
- Have been inspected and admitted or paroled, with some exceptions
- Have an immigrant petition filed on your behalf, or be eligible to file one together with the I-485
- Have a visa number available, which is automatic for immediate relatives of U.S. citizens such as spouses, and subject to the Visa Bulletin in many other categories
- Not be inadmissible, or qualify for a waiver

Spouses of U.S. citizens benefit from a more forgiving rule on some issues. A person who entered legally and overstayed may still adjust as the spouse of a citizen, while someone who entered without inspection generally cannot, apart from specific exceptions. These rules are technical, and the details of how you entered matter.

## Marriage-based adjustment

For the spouse of a U.S. citizen living in the country, the I-130 and I-485 are usually filed together. Then comes biometrics, a possible work permit and travel document, an interview and a decision. If your marriage is under two years old when approved, you receive a conditional green card. Our other articles cover the timeline, interview and conditional residence in more detail.

## How long does adjustment of status take

Timing depends on the category and the USCIS field office. Immediate relative cases do not wait for a visa number, but they still wait for processing. USCIS posts current processing times by field office and form. Employment-based cases may wait for a priority date to become current, and that wait can be long for some countries. Do not rely on someone else's timeline.

## When a lawyer adds value

You can file an I-485 without a lawyer, and many straightforward cases are approved that way. A lawyer matters most if you have any of these:

- A past overstay, unauthorized work, or a status violation
- An entry without inspection or an unclear entry history
- A criminal record or arrest, even a minor one
- A prior immigration denial or removal order
- A previous marriage or a short courtship that may draw questions
- A public charge or financial sponsor issue
- A fraud or misrepresentation concern

The risk is that a mistake on an I-485 can lead to a denial, and in some cases to removal proceedings. A lawyer can review your history before you submit anything and tell you whether adjustment is safe, whether a waiver is needed, or whether consular processing is better.

## Questions to ask

- Am I eligible to adjust, given my entry and status history?
- Which supporting forms and evidence do I need?
- How will travel affect my case?
- What is the risk if USCIS denies it?
