Marriage-based adjustment of status is the process by which the spouse of a U.S. citizen or permanent resident gets a green card without leaving the United States. For the spouse of a citizen who was inspected and admitted on arrival, it usually means one combined filing: the petition and the green card application go to USCIS together, and the spouse waits here, with a work permit, until the interview. Here is the sequence, the forms at each stage and the points where cases stall.

## Who can use marriage-based adjustment of status

Two conditions have to be true. The foreign spouse must have been inspected and admitted or paroled into the United States, and a visa must be available. For the spouse of a U.S. citizen, a visa is always available, because immediate relatives have no annual cap. An overstay or a period of unauthorized work does not block an immediate relative from adjusting. Someone who crossed the border without inspection, however, generally cannot adjust no matter whom they married, and needs the consular route described on our [marriage-based green card](/practice-area/key-services/marriage-based-green-card) page.

The spouse of a permanent resident is in the F2A preference category. That spouse can file Form I-485 only when the Visa Bulletin shows the priority date current, and generally must have maintained lawful status the whole time. The difference between the two charts in the bulletin is explained in [priority date vs filing date](/priority-date-vs-filing-date).

## Step one: the filing package

For a citizen's spouse, everything goes in at once. The package includes:

- Form I-130, the relative petition, with Form I-130A, the beneficiary's supplemental biographic information.
- Form I-485, the application to register permanent residence, with the foreign spouse's passport, I-94 record, birth certificate and two photographs.
- Form I-864, the affidavit of support, with the petitioner's most recent tax return and proof of current income. A joint sponsor files a separate I-864 if the petitioner's income is below the guideline for the household size.
- Form I-765 for a work permit and Form I-131 for advance parole. There is no reason to skip these.
- Form I-693, the medical examination by a civil surgeon, in its sealed envelope. As of this writing, USCIS expects the medical to be filed with the I-485 rather than later; check the current I-485 instructions before filing.
- Evidence that the marriage is genuine. Our post on [bona fide marriage evidence](/bona-fide-marriage-evidence-uscis) covers what to include.

Pay the current USCIS filing fees listed on the form pages; they change, and a package with the wrong fee is rejected rather than held. Our walkthrough of [Form I-485 itself](/what-is-form-i-485-how-to-complete-step-by-step) covers the questions that trip up applicants.

## Step two: receipts, biometrics and the waiting period

USCIS mails a receipt notice for each form. The I-485 receipt is the document that shows the spouse is in a period of authorized stay while the case is pending. A biometrics appointment follows at an application support center, where USCIS takes fingerprints and a photograph for the background check.

The work permit and advance parole, usually issued as one combination card, arrive while the case is pending. Until advance parole is approved, the foreign spouse must not leave the United States; departure abandons the I-485. Certain spouses who hold valid H-1B or L-1 status can travel on that visa, but everyone else waits for the card. Processing times vary by field office; check the USCIS processing times page for the I-485 at your office.

## Step three: the interview

Most spousal cases end with an interview at the USCIS field office with jurisdiction over your home. Both spouses attend. The officer places each under oath, reviews the forms line by line, confirms that nothing has changed, and asks about the relationship: how you met, the wedding, who lives in the home, daily routines, recent events. Bring originals of every document you filed, updated evidence since filing and a government ID.

If the officer doubts the marriage, the spouses may be separated and asked the same questions, with the answers compared afterward. Preparation for that format is simple in principle: know the facts of your own life and answer honestly, including "I don't remember." Inconsistent guesses do more damage than admitted gaps.

## Step four: approval and what comes after

If the marriage is less than two years old on the day the I-485 is approved, the spouse becomes a conditional resident with a two-year card and must file Form I-751 in the 90 days before it expires. If the marriage is two years old or more at approval, the card is valid for ten years with no conditions. Either way, the spouse of a U.S. citizen can apply for naturalization after three years as a resident, provided the couple is still married and living together.

Three things delay or derail marriage-based adjustment more than anything else: an I-864 that does not meet the income test, a missing or expired medical exam, and travel without advance parole. All three are avoidable at the filing stage.
