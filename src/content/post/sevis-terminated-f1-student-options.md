If your SEVIS record has been terminated, you may be worried about your status, your studies, and your future in the U.S. A SEVIS termination is serious, but it isn't always the end of your F-1 journey. What you should do depends on why the record was terminated and how quickly you act.

## What SEVIS is

The Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS) is the government database that tracks F-1 and M-1 students and J-1 exchange visitors. Your school's Designated School Official (DSO) maintains your record. When the record is terminated, it usually means the school or the government has indicated you're no longer maintaining your student status.

## Common reasons for termination

- **Academic issues:** failing to enroll, dropping below full-time without authorization, or failing to make normal progress
- **Unauthorized employment:** working without proper authorization
- **Failure to report:** not reporting a change of address or not checking in at a new school
- **OPT issues:** exceeding allowed unemployment days or failing to report employment
- **Completion of studies:** a normal termination after you finish your program and any OPT
- **Government action:** in 2025, many students saw terminations initiated by immigration authorities, some later reversed

## Termination vs losing status

A SEVIS termination and losing F-1 status are related but not identical. SEVIS is a record. Status is your legal condition in the U.S. In some cases, courts and agencies have recognized that a SEVIS termination doesn't by itself end lawful status. In most cases, though, a termination for a status violation means you're no longer in valid F-1 status.

Unlawful presence for F-1 students admitted for "duration of status" generally doesn't begin accruing until an official finding, such as a denied benefit request or an immigration judge's order. That distinction matters for future visa bars, but it doesn't make it safe to stay without status.

## Your options

**1. Reinstatement.** You can apply to USCIS for reinstatement on Form I-539, generally within five months of the termination. You must show the violation resulted from circumstances beyond your control, or that failure to reinstate would cause extreme hardship. You must be pursuing a full course of study, not have a record of repeated violations, and not have worked without authorization. Your DSO must issue a new Form I-20 recommending reinstatement.

**2. Correction of the record.** If the termination was a clerical error, your DSO may be able to correct it in SEVIS. Talk to your school's international office right away.

**3. Departure and reentry.** You can leave the U.S. and apply for a new F-1 visa with a new I-20. This avoids the reinstatement process but requires a new visa interview, and the termination may come up.

**4. Change to another status.** Depending on your circumstances, you may qualify for another status, such as H-1B or a dependent status through a spouse.

## Effect on OPT

If your record is terminated while you're on OPT, your employment authorization ends. Stop working immediately, because continued work would be unauthorized employment.

## Visa revocation is separate

Sometimes a SEVIS termination is accompanied by a State Department visa revocation. A revoked visa means you can't use it to reenter, but it doesn't necessarily end your status while you're in the U.S. You'll need a new visa before your next trip abroad.

## What to do right now

1. Contact your DSO to learn the reason for the termination.
2. Stop any employment until you understand your situation.
3. Gather your academic records, I-20s, and employment documents.
4. Talk to an immigration attorney before deciding between reinstatement and departure.
5. Act quickly. The five-month reinstatement window matters.

## How a SEVIS termination affects a future visa interview

Even if you leave and start over with a new I-20, the consular officer will see the termination in your history. Expect questions about what happened and what has changed. A clear explanation, supported by your school's records, matters more than a perfect record. Students who were terminated because of a government-initiated action that was later reversed should keep the documentation of the reversal.

## Unauthorized employment is the hardest problem to fix

Reinstatement is not available if you worked without authorization. If the termination involved unauthorized work, including on-campus work beyond permitted hours or OPT employment outside your field, the options narrow quickly. An honest review of your work history with an attorney before you file anything is the best way to avoid making the situation worse.
