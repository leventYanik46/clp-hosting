U.S. citizens can sponsor their brothers and sisters for a green card in the F4 family preference category. The sibling green card petition is simple to file, but the wait is among the longest in the immigration system. Understanding the timeline is key to planning.

## Who can sponsor a sibling

- **U.S. citizens age 21 or older** can sponsor siblings.
- **Green card holders** can't sponsor siblings.
- **U.S. citizens under 21** must wait.

## Who counts as a sibling

You and your sibling must share at least one parent:

- **Full siblings:** same mother and father
- **Half siblings:** share one parent
- **Step siblings:** if the marriage creating the relationship happened before either of you turned 18
- **Adopted siblings:** if the adoption happened before age 16

## The process

1. **File Form I-130** with proof of the sibling relationship, usually both birth certificates showing a common parent.
2. **Wait for the priority date** to become current in the F4 category.
3. **National Visa Center processing:** Submit documents and fees.
4. **Consular interview** or adjustment of status if your sibling is in the U.S. in a qualifying situation.

## Derivatives

Your sibling's spouse and unmarried children under 21 can immigrate as derivatives. Children may age out during the long wait, though the Child Status Protection Act can sometimes help.

## Why the wait is so long

The F4 category has a limited number of visas each year, and demand far exceeds supply. Waits are:

- **Most countries:** more than a decade
- **Mexico, India, and the Philippines:** often much longer

Check the Visa Bulletin for the current F4 cutoff dates for your sibling's country.

## Planning for the wait

- **File early.** The priority date is set when you file the I-130.
- **Keep your address current** with USCIS and the National Visa Center.
- **Don't let your sibling overstay a visa** while waiting. Overstays can create bars.
- **Plan for children aging out.**

## Visiting while waiting

A pending I-130 can make it harder for your sibling to get a visitor visa, because consular officers may see immigrant intent. That's not always a bar, but it's a factor.

## If you die before the case is complete

If the petitioner dies, the petition may be revoked. But relief may be available under certain humanitarian provisions if the beneficiary lives in the U.S.

## Is it worth filing?

For many families, yes. Even a long wait can provide a path to permanent residence. Filing early secures a place in line.

## Practical example

A U.S. citizen files for his sister in the Philippines. She is married with two children, ages 8 and 12. Because F4 waits for the Philippines are long, by the time the priority date is current, her children may be adults. CSPA may help the younger child, depending on how long the I-130 was pending, but the older child may age out. If that happens, the family may need to file a separate petition once the sister becomes a permanent resident.

## Evidence of the sibling relationship

The core evidence is both siblings' birth certificates, showing at least one common parent. If a name changed through marriage, include marriage certificates. For half siblings through a father, also show the father's relationship to each child, such as legitimation records. For step siblings, include the marriage certificate of the parents and proof that the marriage happened before either of you turned 18.

## Checking the F4 wait for your sibling

Each month, the State Department publishes the Visa Bulletin, which lists cutoff dates for each family category and country. Find the F4 row and your sibling's country of birth. If your priority date is earlier than the listed date, a visa is available. Note that the dates can move forward slowly, stay still, or even move backward. A wait of 15 years or more is not unusual.

## Keeping the case alive

The National Visa Center may contact you years after filing. If it doesn't hear from the beneficiary within a set period after the visa becomes available, the case can be terminated. Keep your contact information and your sibling's contact information current, and respond to any NVC communication promptly.
