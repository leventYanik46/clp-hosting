USCIS case status updates can be confusing. Messages like "case is being actively reviewed by USCIS" or "notice explaining USCIS actions was mailed" don't always explain what's happening. Here's what common case status messages mean and when to take action.

## Case is being actively reviewed by USCIS

This message often appears when an officer is working on your case. It can mean:

- An officer has opened your file
- Background checks are being reviewed
- A decision may be coming soon

But this message can also appear without meaningful progress, sometimes for months. It doesn't guarantee a decision is imminent.

## Notice explaining USCIS actions was mailed

This means USCIS mailed you a notice. It could be:

- A request for evidence
- A notice of intent to deny
- A transfer notice
- An appointment notice
- A notice about a rejected fee or form

Watch your mail closely. Check your online account for a copy.

## Case was received

USCIS received your application and issued a receipt notice.

## Fingerprint fee was received

USCIS received your biometrics fee. A biometrics appointment may be scheduled.

## Request for evidence was sent

USCIS needs more information. You have a deadline to respond. Don't miss it.

## Response to USCIS' request for evidence was received

USCIS received your RFE response. The officer will review it.

## Interview was scheduled

USCIS scheduled an interview. Check the date, time, and location.

## Case was approved

Your application was approved. Look for an approval notice.

## New card is being produced

USCIS is producing your card, such as a green card or EAD.

## Card was mailed to me

Your card was mailed. Track the delivery.

## Case was denied

Your application was denied. Review the notice for appeal or motion options.

## Correspondence was received and USCIS is reviewing it

USCIS received a document or letter from you and is reviewing it.

## When to take action

- **RFEs and NOIDs:** Respond by the deadline
- **Interviews:** Prepare and attend
- **Denials:** Review options with an attorney
- **Long delays:** If your case is outside normal processing times, you can submit a case inquiry. If that doesn't work, options include contacting a congressional office, the CIS Ombudsman, or a mandamus lawsuit in some cases

## Tips

- Check your status regularly
- Keep your address updated
- Create a USCIS online account for paper-filed cases using the receipt number
- Keep copies of all notices

## How to read processing times

USCIS posts processing times by form type and office. If your case is outside the posted time, you can submit an online case inquiry. Note that USCIS may calculate the posted time based on the time it took to complete most cases, so many cases fall within the range even if they seem slow. Before escalating, check that your case is truly outside the posted range.

## When status messages and reality don't match

Sometimes the online status lags behind what's happening. You might receive a notice in the mail before the online status updates, or the status might change without a notice arriving. If a notice doesn't arrive within a few weeks of a status update, check your online account for a digital copy and contact USCIS if needed.

## Case status after an interview

After an interview, statuses can change quickly or stay the same for weeks. "Case is being actively reviewed by USCIS" often appears after an interview while the officer completes background checks or reviews additional evidence. If the officer said a decision would come soon and months pass with no change, follow up through a case inquiry.
