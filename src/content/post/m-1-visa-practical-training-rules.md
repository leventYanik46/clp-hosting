An M-1 student may not work, on campus or off, while enrolled in a vocational program. The only employment the M-1 category allows is practical training after the program ends, and it is rationed: one month of training for every four months of full-time study, with a ceiling of six months in total. That is the whole budget. There is no equivalent of the F-1 on-campus job, no Curricular Practical Training, and no hardship employment authorization for M-1 students.

## No employment during the program

The rule is absolute and it is where most M-1 problems begin. A flight student who tows aircraft for the school on weekends, a culinary student who takes shifts in a restaurant, a cosmetology student paid for client work beyond the supervised clinic that is part of the curriculum: each has engaged in unauthorized employment. The consequence is a status violation, and unauthorized employment is a ground that makes reinstatement unavailable. The student would have to leave, obtain a new visa, and explain the lapse to a consular officer.

Supervised clinical or shop work that is a graded part of the course is not employment, even if the school charges customers. The line is whether you are performing the training the Form I-20 describes or providing labor to an employer. If a school asks you to do something that feels like a job, stop and ask the Designated School Official (DSO) to confirm in writing. Our [M-1 vocational student visa](/practice-area/key-services/m-1-visa) page describes the status-maintenance review we do for students in technical and trade programs, because the training-versus-work question comes up in almost every one.

## How the one-for-four rule works

Practical training must be in your vocational field and must be unavailable in your home country, a point the DSO certifies when recommending it. The length is computed from months of full-time study completed:

- Four months of study earn one month of training.
- An eight-month program earns two months.
- A 24-month program earns six months, the maximum; longer programs do not earn more.

Partial months are not rounded up. A nine-month program still earns two months, not three. Compare this with F-1 students, who get 12 months of post-completion OPT regardless of program length, plus a STEM extension; the difference is described in our post on [how post-completion OPT works](/f-1-opt-rules-and-timing). That gap is one reason a student choosing between a certificate program at a community college (often F-1) and a vocational school (M-1) should ask which status the school issues before enrolling.

## Filing for practical training

The DSO recommends practical training in SEVIS and endorses your Form I-20. You then file Form I-765 with USCIS, under the M-1 practical training category, with the endorsed I-20, your I-94, passport, photos, and the current USCIS filing fee. The timing window is tighter than the F-1 window:

1. File no earlier than 90 days before your program end date.
2. File no later than 30 days after your program end date.
3. Do not begin work until the Employment Authorization Document is in hand and the start date has arrived.

Because the authorization period is short, an adjudication delay can consume a meaningful share of a two-month training period. File at the front of the window with a complete package. USCIS will not extend the training period to make up for processing time, and the employment must relate to the course of study shown on the I-20; a welding graduate cannot use the period to work in retail.

## Status after training ends

When the training period finishes, you have a 30-day grace period to depart. The grace period does not permit work. If you want to stay longer, your options are limited: M-1 students may not change to F-1 status inside the United States, and they may not change to an H category if the vocational training is what qualifies them for the H job. Those restrictions and the paths that remain are explained in our post on [changing from M-1 to F-1 or other statuses](/change-m-1-to-f-1-status). A student who wants a longer stay is usually better served by departing and applying for a new visa, which our [change and extension of status](/practice-area/key-services/change-and-extension-of-status) page contrasts with in-country filings.

## Dependents

M-2 spouses may not work under any circumstances and may not enroll in a full degree program, though they may take recreational or avocational classes. M-2 children may attend elementary and secondary school full time. Spouses who want to study or work need their own status, which generally means leaving and applying for an F-1 or a work visa in their own right.
