An LCA, or Labor Condition Application, is the form an employer files with the Department of Labor before it can file an H-1B petition. It is a set of promises about wages and working conditions. USCIS will not approve an H-1B without a certified LCA that matches the job, and many H-1B compliance problems trace back to an LCA that no longer reflects reality.

## What is an LCA for H-1B?

The LCA is Form ETA-9035 or ETA-9035E, filed online through DOL's FLAG system. By signing it, the employer attests that:

1. It will pay the H-1B worker at least the higher of the actual wage paid to similar employees or the prevailing wage for the job and location
2. Employing the worker will not adversely affect working conditions of similarly employed U.S. workers
3. There is no strike or lockout in the occupation at the worksite
4. It has given notice of the filing to workers at the place of employment

The LCA lists the job title, occupational code, wage level, worksite address, and dates of employment.

## LCA meaning for the worker

For the H-1B employee, the LCA sets the minimum salary the employer must pay, including during nonproductive time the employer controls, such as waiting for a project. It also fixes where you can work. Moving to a location outside the LCA's area usually requires a new LCA before you move.

## LCA approval time for H-1B

DOL generally certifies LCAs within seven working days of filing, as long as the employer's FEIN is verified in the system. New employers should verify their FEIN with DOL before they need the LCA, because that step can add time.

## Notice and the public access file

Employers must post notice of the LCA for 10 business days in two conspicuous locations at the worksite, or provide electronic notice. They must also keep a public access file with the certified LCA, wage documentation, and an explanation of how the wage was set. DOL investigators check these files during complaints and audits.

## When you need a new LCA

A new LCA is generally required when:

- The worker moves to a worksite outside the metropolitan area covered by the current LCA
- The job changes enough that the occupational classification changes
- The current LCA is about to expire
- A different employer will file the H-1B

When a move to a new area happens, the employer generally must file an amended H-1B petition with the new LCA. Moves within the same area usually need only a new posting at the new worksite.

There are short-term placement rules that allow limited travel to a new area for a set number of workdays without a new LCA, but these are narrow and should be confirmed before relying on them.

## Remote and hybrid work

If an H-1B worker works from home, the home is a worksite. If it is outside the LCA's area, the employer likely needs a new LCA, notice posted at the home, and possibly an amended petition. This is one of the most overlooked H-1B compliance issues.

## Common LCA mistakes

- Choosing a wage level that does not match the actual duties
- Forgetting to post notice at a new worksite
- Missing public access file documents
- Letting a worker relocate before filing a new LCA and amendment


## Frequently asked questions

### Does an LCA expire?

Yes. An LCA covers a set period of up to three years. The H-1B petition period cannot exceed the LCA period, so extensions need a new LCA.

### Can one LCA cover several workers?

Yes. One LCA can cover multiple positions in the same occupation and area, as long as each worker's wage and worksite fit the LCA.

### What happens if an employer pays less than the LCA wage?

DOL can order back wages, civil penalties, and in serious cases bar the employer from H-1B filings. Workers can file a complaint with DOL's Wage and Hour Division.
