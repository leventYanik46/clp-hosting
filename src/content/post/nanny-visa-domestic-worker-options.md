There is no "nanny visa" in U.S. immigration law. Families who want to bring a nanny or other household employee from abroad must fit one of several narrow categories, each with its own rules about who the employer must be. The right route depends on whether you are a U.S. citizen, a foreign national working in the U.S., or a diplomat, and on whether the arrangement is temporary or permanent.

## Option 1: B-1 domestic employee

A B-1 visa can cover personal or domestic employees in limited situations:

- **Employees of a nonimmigrant**, such as an H-1B, L-1, or E visa holder, who have worked for that employer abroad, usually for at least a year before the employer came to the U.S., or who have a longer-standing employment relationship.
- **Employees of a U.S. citizen** who resides abroad and is temporarily visiting the U.S., or who is temporarily assigned to the U.S., with an established employment relationship abroad.

The employment contract must meet State Department requirements, including pay at least the applicable minimum wage, free room and board if the employee lives in, and terms the employee can understand. The domestic worker then applies at a consulate and, once in the U.S., applies for an employment authorization document.

The B-1 domestic employee route does not work for a family that simply wants to hire someone new from abroad.

## Option 2: A-3 and G-5 for diplomats and international organizations

Diplomats and foreign government officials in A-1 or A-2 status can employ household workers in **A-3** status. Employees of international organizations in G-1 through G-4 status can employ household workers in **G-5** status. Both require written contracts with protections for the worker, and the State Department monitors these arrangements closely because of past abuse cases.

These categories matter in the Washington, DC area, where many diplomatic and international organization families live.

## Option 3: J-1 au pair

The au pair program, a J-1 exchange visitor program run by State Department-designated sponsors, places young people from abroad with U.S. host families for childcare. It is a cultural exchange, not standard employment, and it has age limits, education requirements, capped weekly hours, a set stipend, and limited duration. The family goes through a sponsor agency rather than sponsoring the au pair directly.

## Option 4: EB-3 green card for a live-in nanny

A U.S. family can sponsor a household worker for a green card in the EB-3 "other worker" category, for positions requiring less than two years of training or experience. The process requires:

1. A prevailing wage determination.
2. PERM labor certification, including recruitment to test the U.S. labor market.
3. An I-140 petition.
4. Adjustment of status or consular processing when a visa number is available.

The family must show it can pay the offered wage. EB-3 other worker waits can be long, depending on the worker's country. This option does not provide a temporary visa in the meantime.

## What does not work

- Bringing a nanny on a B-2 tourist visa to work. Tourist visas do not authorize employment, and both the worker and the family face consequences.
- "Sponsoring" a relative as a nanny through a family visa. Family green cards depend on the relationship, not the job.
- Paying under the table. Household employers have tax and labor obligations regardless of the worker's status.

## Protecting the worker and the family

Domestic workers are vulnerable to exploitation, and U.S. law includes protections for them. A clear written contract, fair pay, and accurate filings protect both sides.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I hire an undocumented nanny?
Employers must verify work authorization for employees. Household employers who knowingly hire unauthorized workers can face penalties, in addition to tax obligations.

### Can a nanny come on a J-1 if she is over the au pair age limit?
No. The au pair program has a set age range, and applicants outside it do not qualify.

### How long does an EB-3 green card for a nanny take?
It depends on PERM processing times and the EB-3 other worker line for the nanny's country. Expect years in many cases.

### Can my nanny work for another family too?
Most of these categories tie the worker to a specific employer. Working for others usually is not permitted.

### Do I need to pay overtime?
Federal and state wage laws can apply to domestic workers, including overtime for live-out workers. Check the rules in your state.

### Can my H-1B nanny come with us when we move to the U.S.?
If she has worked for you abroad long enough and meets the B-1 requirements, she may qualify as a personal employee of a nonimmigrant.

### Does a written contract matter?
Yes. The State Department requires specific contract terms for B-1, A-3, and G-5 domestic workers.
