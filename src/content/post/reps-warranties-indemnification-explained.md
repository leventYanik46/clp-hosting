Representations and warranties are the part of a purchase agreement where the seller describes the business in writing and stands behind the description. Indemnification is what happens when a description turns out to be wrong: the seller pays the buyer's loss, within limits the parties negotiate. For a buyer, these two sections are the main protection against problems that diligence did not catch. For a seller, they define how much of the purchase price is still at risk after closing, and for how long. In the [M&A deal work](/practice-area/key-services/mergers-acquisitions-business-transactions) Capitol Law Partners handles, more negotiating hours go into these sections than into the price.

## What representations and warranties actually say

A representation is a statement of fact as of a date, usually signing and again at closing. A warranty is a promise that the statement is true. In practice, purchase agreements use the terms together and treat a false statement as a breach either way.

Seller reps fall into two groups. Fundamental reps go to the heart of the deal: the seller owns the shares or assets, the company is validly organized, the seller has authority to sign, the capitalization table is accurate, and no broker is owed a fee. General or operational reps describe the business: the financial statements are accurate, there is no undisclosed litigation, taxes have been paid, material contracts are listed and in force, the company owns its intellectual property, employees are properly classified, and the company complies with law.

Each rep is qualified by a disclosure schedule. If the seller has one pending lawsuit, the rep says there is no litigation except as set forth on the schedule, and the schedule describes the lawsuit. A disclosed item is not a breach. That makes the schedules as important as the reps, and it is why sellers spend weeks preparing them. A diligence finding that the buyer discovers, like those described in our [due diligence guide](/due-diligence-checklist-buying-a-business), typically ends up either on a schedule or in a specific indemnity.

## How indemnification turns a breach into money

Indemnification is the seller's promise to compensate the buyer for losses arising from a breach of a rep, a breach of a covenant, or specific identified matters (an existing lawsuit, a known tax exposure, pre-closing environmental conditions). The negotiated limits are where the real economics sit:

- Survival. How long after closing can the buyer bring a claim? General reps commonly survive for a period measured in months or a few years; fundamental and tax reps often survive much longer, sometimes until the statute of limitations runs.
- Basket. A threshold below which the seller does not pay. In a deductible basket, the seller pays only losses above the threshold. In a tipping basket, once losses cross the threshold the seller pays from the first dollar.
- Cap. The maximum the seller will pay for breaches of general reps, usually a percentage of the purchase price. Fundamental reps are often capped at the full price, and fraud is normally uncapped.
- Escrow or holdback. A portion of the price held by a third party or withheld by the buyer for the survival period, so the buyer has a source of recovery without suing the seller.
- Exclusive remedy. A clause stating that indemnification is the buyer's only remedy for breaches, which prevents the buyer from bringing tort claims to escape the cap.

Each of these moves against the others. A seller who accepts a lower cap may insist on a deductible basket and a short survival period; a buyer who wants a long survival period may agree to a smaller escrow. The structure of the deal matters too, because a buyer in a stock purchase inherits every liability and will push harder on all of these than a buyer in an asset deal, a distinction covered in our [asset purchase vs stock purchase post](/asset-purchase-vs-stock-purchase).

## Materiality scrapes, sandbagging, and knowledge qualifiers

Three drafting points change the value of the whole package.

A materiality scrape reads the reps for indemnification purposes as if the words "material" and "material adverse effect" were deleted. Sellers negotiate materiality qualifiers into the reps to avoid breaches over small items; a scrape removes those qualifiers when deciding whether a breach occurred, when calculating losses, or both. Buyers want a double scrape; sellers resist.

A sandbagging clause addresses whether a buyer who knew about a breach before closing can still recover for it. A pro-sandbagging clause says yes: the buyer paid for the reps and can rely on them regardless of its knowledge. An anti-sandbagging clause says no. State law fills the gap when the agreement is silent, and the default rule differs between jurisdictions, so the agreement should say which rule applies.

Knowledge qualifiers limit a rep to what the seller knows, as in a statement that, to the seller's knowledge, no customer intends to terminate. The buyer will want knowledge defined to include what specific named people would have learned after reasonable inquiry, not just their actual awareness.

## Representation and warranty insurance

In larger deals, representation and warranty insurance (RWI) replaces much of the seller's indemnity. The buyer buys a policy, the insurer covers losses from breaches above a retention, and the seller's exposure drops to a small escrow or nothing. RWI has moved into smaller deals over the years, and it is worth asking about whenever the seller wants a clean exit and the buyer wants real protection. The policy excludes known issues and certain categories, so specific indemnities for identified problems still have to be negotiated with the seller directly.

## When a claim arises after closing

The agreement sets a procedure: written notice within the survival period, a description of the claim and the loss, an opportunity for the seller to dispute it, and, for third-party claims, rules about who controls the defense. Missing the notice deadline or the required contents can forfeit a valid claim. If the parties cannot agree, the dispute resolution clause decides the forum.
