A P-2 visa lasts as long as the exchange engagement it was approved for, up to one year on the initial petition. That is the short answer to how long a P-2 visa lasts, and it surprises artists who expected a multi-year work permit. The category is tied to a specific exchange, so the period of stay follows the dates in the agreement and the itinerary. When those dates move, the status has to move with them, which is where P-2 visa extension filings come in.

## The initial period of stay

USCIS approves a P-2 petition for the time needed to complete the event, activity, or performance, with a ceiling of one year. If the exchange runs eight weeks, the approval runs eight weeks, not a year. Customs and Border Protection then admits the artist for that validity period plus a short grace period on either side for travel and preparation. The grace period is not work time; performing during it is unauthorized.

The controlling document after entry is the I-94 arrival record, not the visa stamp in the passport. A visa can be valid for several years while the I-94 expires with the engagement. Check the I-94 online after every entry, and if the dates do not match the petition, raise it before you perform rather than after. ## How a P-2 visa extension works

Extensions are available in increments of up to one year, and only to continue or complete the same event, activity, or performance. The petitioner files a new Form I-129 requesting an extension, with a statement explaining why more time is needed and the updated exchange documentation. Because the exchange itself justifies the stay, the extension should show that the reciprocal engagement is also continuing, or that the original agreement always covered the longer period.

File before the current I-94 expires. A timely filed extension generally allows the artist to remain while USCIS decides, and work authorization for the same employer continues for a limited period under the regulations, but do not treat that as a reason to file late. If the engagement is ending and a new, unrelated engagement is starting, that is a new petition, not an extension, and it may need its own union consultation. The filing sequence is the same as the original, which we walk through in [the P-2 petition process](/p-2-visa-petition-process-steps).

## Changing or adding employers

A P-2 artist may only work for the petitioner and for engagements covered by the approved petition. A second employer who wants the artist to perform must file its own petition or be added through an agent petition with an amended itinerary. Material changes in the terms of employment, including new venues outside the itinerary or a change in the sponsoring organization, require an amended petition before the change takes effect. If the artist is in the United States and wants to move to a different category entirely, for example an O-1B after building a record of distinction, that is a change of status request; our [change and extension of status](/practice-area/key-services/change-and-extension-of-status) page explains how that filing differs from an extension.

## P-4 family members

The spouse and unmarried children under 21 of a P-2 artist may come in P-4 status, either with the artist or later. They apply at the consulate with proof of the relationship, or, if already in the United States in another status, file Form I-539 to change to P-4. P-4 dependents may attend school, including full-time study, but they may not work. A spouse who wants to work needs an independent basis, such as their own employment petition. P-4 status ends when the principal's P-2 status ends, so an extension filing for the artist should include or be paired with an extension for the family.

## Essential support staff

Support personnel approved in P-2S status are tied to the principal artists. Their period of stay matches the principal's, and their extensions move with the principal's extensions. If the principal's status ends early, so does theirs. A support worker who wants to stay on for a different artist needs a new petition from that artist's petitioner, with a fresh consultation and a fresh showing that the skills are essential and not readily available from a U.S. worker.

## When the exchange ends

Once the engagement finishes, the artist is expected to depart within the grace period or change to another status before the I-94 expires. Overstaying starts the accrual of unlawful presence after the I-94 date, which can affect future visas. If the group is invited back for a new tour without an exchange, that is usually a P-1B or O-1B question rather than a P-2 one; the comparison in [P-2 or P-1B for touring groups](/p-2-vs-p-1-visa-artists) will help you decide. And if the plan is to stay permanently, P-2 is not a direct path to a green card, so the immigrant options should be mapped separately and early.
