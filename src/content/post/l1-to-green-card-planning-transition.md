L-1 visa holders have a real advantage in the green card process: the L-1 allows dual intent, so you can pursue permanent residence while keeping your status. The harder question is which route fits your role and how much time is left on the clock. The L-1 has a maximum period of stay, and planning a green card before you reach it is the main task.

## Time limits that shape the plan

L-1A managers and executives may generally stay in the United States for up to seven years. L-1B specialized knowledge workers may generally stay up to five years. Time spent outside the United States can sometimes be recaptured, but you need records to prove it. A green card strategy that depends on running the clock down is risky.

## Route 1: EB-1C for L-1A holders

For many L-1A managers and executives, EB-1C is the natural next step. It requires that you:

- Worked abroad for a related company in a managerial or executive role for at least one year in the three years before coming to the U.S.
- Will work in a managerial or executive role for the U.S. company
- Are sponsored by an employer that has a qualifying relationship with the foreign company and has been doing business in the U.S. for at least a year

EB-1C does not require a PERM labor certification, so the process can be shorter. Visa availability varies by country of birth, and for some countries a backlog applies.

## Route 2: PERM-based EB-2 or EB-3

If EB-1C does not fit, the employer can sponsor you through PERM. This route works for L-1A and L-1B holders alike. It takes longer because of the labor certification step, but it can be the right answer when the manager or executive test is hard to prove. The category depends on the job requirements and whether the role requires an advanced degree or a bachelor's degree.

## Route 3: L-1B holders

EB-1C does not accept specialized knowledge. L-1B workers generally go through PERM, or through another category if they qualify, such as an EB-2 national interest waiver or EB-1A for people with extraordinary ability. Some L-1B workers also look at changing to an H-1B, which has its own cap and timing.

## What to prepare

- **Role documentation.** Organizational charts, detailed duties and time allocation for each position.
- **Corporate relationship evidence.** Share ownership records, tax returns and annual reports for both entities.
- **Work history.** Pay records, job offer letters and travel records for the L-1 period.
- **Family details.** Spouses and children can generally receive green cards together with you, and L-2 spouses may have work authorization.

## Common planning mistakes

- Waiting until the last year of L-1 status to start
- Assuming a title of manager is enough to meet the EB-1C standard
- Ignoring backlogs for your country of birth
- Changing employers in the middle of the process without checking the effect

## Making a plan

Start by asking your employer whether it will sponsor you, and for which category. If you are a manager, ask the attorney to test EB-1C against the actual duties of your job. If you are in specialized knowledge status, plan for a longer path. A consultation can map your options to your remaining time.
