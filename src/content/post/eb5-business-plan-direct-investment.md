For a direct EB-5 investment, the business plan is not a formality. It is the document USCIS uses to decide whether your company will actually create 10 full-time jobs. An EB-5 business plan that reads like a template, or that projects jobs without explaining how the business will pay for them, is one of the most common reasons direct EB-5 petitions run into trouble.

## Direct EB-5 vs regional center

In a regional center investment, the project developer prepares the business plan and economic report, and jobs can be counted indirectly through economic modeling. In a direct investment, you invest in and usually manage your own business, and only direct jobs count. That means real employees on your company's payroll, working at least 35 hours a week, filled by qualifying workers (U.S. citizens, green card holders, or other immigrants authorized to work, excluding you and your family).

Because the job count has to come from your own payroll, the business plan carries more weight.

## The Matter of Ho standard

USCIS evaluates EB-5 business plans using the standard set in *Matter of Ho*, a 1998 precedent decision. The plan must be comprehensive and credible enough to show the business will create the required jobs. Matter of Ho lists what a good plan covers:

- **The business description,** including products or services and the business's objectives
- **Market analysis,** with names of competitors, their strengths and weaknesses, and the target market
- **Marketing strategy,** including pricing and how the business will reach customers
- **Permits and licenses** required for the business
- **Organizational structure** and staffing timetable, with job descriptions for every position
- **Personnel experience** of the owners and managers
- **Sales, cost, and income projections,** with the assumptions behind them
- **Contracts and lease details,** where they exist

USCIS doesn't need certainty. It needs a plan that a reasonable reader would find realistic.

## Job creation: where most plans fall short

The job creation section is the core of the plan. It should show:

1. **When each position will be filled,** with a hiring timeline tied to revenue milestones.
2. **How each position will be paid for,** using the financial projections. A plan that projects 10 hires but shows revenue that couldn't cover payroll will draw a Request for Evidence.
3. **That jobs are full-time and permanent.** Seasonal or contract positions generally don't count, although some intermittent positions can qualify if they are full-time and long-term.
4. **The two-year window.** Jobs must be created within a reasonable time, generally measured by the end of the conditional residence period, so the plan should project hiring within that timeframe.

## Troubled business and existing businesses

If you buy an existing business, only new jobs count unless the business qualifies as a "troubled business," meaning it has existed at least two years and lost at least 20% of its net worth over the prior 12 or 24 months. In that case, preserving existing jobs can count. The business plan should document the losses and how the investment will stabilize the company.

## Choosing an EB-5 business plan service

Many investors hire an EB-5 business plan service. A good writer understands Matter of Ho, knows your industry, and works from your real numbers. Be cautious about:

- **Recycled plans** with boilerplate market sections that don't mention your city or competitors
- **Projections without sources,** such as revenue growth assumptions that aren't tied to industry data or existing contracts
- **Plans that contradict the petition,** such as staffing charts that don't match the job creation claim or investment amounts that don't match the capital contribution

Your immigration attorney should review the plan before it is filed, because USCIS will compare every number in the plan with the rest of the petition and again at the I-829 stage two years later.

## The I-829 connection

The business plan doesn't stop mattering after the I-526E approval. When you file Form I-829 to remove conditions, USCIS checks whether the jobs were actually created. A plan that projected 10 jobs and a business that hired four will need an explanation. Conservative, well-supported projections are safer than optimistic ones.

## Practical tips

- Use the same figures in the plan, the operating agreement, and your source-of-funds documents.
- Explain how your own experience prepares you to run this business.
- Include signed leases, letters of intent from suppliers or customers, and permits where possible.
- Update the plan if the business model changes before filing.
