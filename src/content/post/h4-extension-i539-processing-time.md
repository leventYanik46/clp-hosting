When an H-1B worker extends status, the spouse and children in H-4 status need their own extension, filed on Form I-539. The H-4 extension usually goes in the same package as the H-1B extension. Processing times can differ between the two, and a gap between them causes most of the problems H-4 families run into.

## Who files Form I-539

Each H-4 dependent needs to be covered. One I-539 can include the spouse and children listed as co-applicants on a supplement. The principal H-1B worker's employer does not file the I-539; it is the dependents' application, though employers' attorneys usually prepare it alongside the H-1B petition.

The H-4 extension can only last as long as the H-1B approval. If the H-1B is extended for three years, the H-4 can be extended for the same period, not longer.

## Filing with the H-1B or separately

Filing the I-539 together with the H-1B petition is the cleaner approach. It keeps the cases linked and helps USCIS process them together. When the H-1B is filed with premium processing, USCIS has in recent years generally decided a concurrently filed H-4 I-539 at the same time.

Filing the I-539 later, after the H-1B is approved, is possible but leaves the family more exposed. The dependents must still be in valid status when the I-539 is filed, so the timing has to work.

## H-4 extension processing time

Regular processing times for I-539 can run longer than for H-1B petitions. USCIS publishes current I-539 processing times on its website, and they vary by office and season.

Biometrics requirements for I-539 have changed several times. Depending on current procedures, USCIS may schedule an appointment at an Application Support Center or reuse biometrics already on file. Watch the mail and your online account for any appointment notice, because a missed appointment can lead to denial.

## Staying in the U.S. while the H-4 extension is pending

A timely filed I-539 generally allows dependents to remain in the U.S. while it is pending, even after their old I-94 expires. That protection depends on the filing being made before status ends and on the application not being frivolous.

Two cautions:

- **Travel.** Leaving the U.S. while an I-539 is pending usually abandons it. The dependent would then re-enter using a valid H-4 visa stamp and the principal's approved H-1B.
- **Work.** A pending I-539 does not give work authorization. An H-4 spouse with an H-4 EAD needs to track the EAD's own validity, which is tied to the H-4 status shown on the I-94.

## When the H-4 lags behind the H-1B

The most common problem is an approved H-1B with a pending H-4 extension. The H-1B worker is fine, but the spouse's EAD may expire, and the family cannot travel easily without risking the pending application.

Options include asking whether the case can be expedited, travelling only after weighing the abandonment risk, or having dependents obtain new H-4 visa stamps abroad and re-enter, which gives them a new I-94 based on the approved H-1B. Each option has tradeoffs, especially for spouses whose job depends on the EAD.

## Children approaching 21

H-4 status ends when a child turns 21. An I-539 cannot extend a child past that birthday. Families should plan a change to F-1 student status or another category well before then. If a green card case is pending, the Child Status Protection Act may preserve a child's eligibility as a derivative for the green card, but it does not extend H-4 status.

## A short checklist

- File the I-539 with the H-1B extension whenever possible.
- Include every dependent and confirm each one's I-94 date.
- Watch for biometrics notices.
- Plan travel and EAD renewals around the pending case.
- Track children's 21st birthdays.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file the H-4 extension myself?
Yes. The I-539 is the dependent's own application, and it can be filed without the employer, as long as the H-1B approval or pending petition supports it.

### Can premium processing be used for an H-4 extension?
Premium processing has been expanded to some I-539 categories, and USCIS has generally processed H-4 applications filed together with a premium H-1B petition at the same time. Check current USCIS guidance for your filing.

### Does an H-4 child need biometrics?
Biometrics requirements depend on the applicant's age and current USCIS procedures. Watch for any notice addressed to the child.

### What if the H-1B is denied?
The H-4 extension depends on the H-1B, so it will usually be denied too. The family should plan a change of status or departure.

### Can my spouse keep working on an H-4 EAD while the I-539 is pending?
Rules for automatic extension of H-4 EADs have changed. The pending I-539 alone does not guarantee continued work authorization, so file early and check current guidance.

### Does the H-4 extension need its own fee?
Yes. Form I-539 has its own filing fee, separate from the H-1B petition fees.

### Can an H-4 child turning 21 change to F-1 with an I-539?
Yes, Form I-539 is also used to change status to F-1 inside the U.S., with the school's Form I-20. File well before the 21st birthday.
