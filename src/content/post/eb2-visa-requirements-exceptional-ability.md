The EB-2 green card category is for professionals with an advanced degree or people with exceptional ability in the sciences, arts, or business. Most EB-2 cases also require an employer to sponsor you through the PERM labor certification. The national interest waiver, or NIW, is a separate door into the same category that removes both the job offer and PERM. Knowing which EB-2 door you are walking through is the first decision in any EB-2 case.

## What EB-2 is

EB-2 is the second employment-based preference category. It sits below EB-1, which is reserved for extraordinary ability, outstanding researchers, and multinational managers, and above EB-3, which covers skilled workers, professionals with a bachelor's degree, and other workers. Your category determines your place in the visa bulletin queue, so qualifying for EB-2 instead of EB-3 can shorten the wait for some nationalities.

## EB-2 requirement one: advanced degree

You qualify as an advanced degree professional if the job requires, and you hold, either:

- A U.S. master's degree or higher, or a foreign equivalent, or
- A U.S. bachelor's degree or foreign equivalent plus at least five years of progressive experience in the specialty.

Two details catch people. First, the job itself must require the advanced degree; it is not enough that you have one. Second, "progressive" experience means the responsibilities grew over time. Five years in the same role at the same level may not meet that standard. Experience letters should describe duties and advancement, not just dates.

## EB-2 requirement two: exceptional ability

If you do not hold an advanced degree, you may still qualify through exceptional ability, defined as expertise significantly above what is ordinarily encountered in your field. You must meet at least three of these six criteria:

1. An academic record showing a degree, diploma, or certificate related to your field.
2. Letters showing at least ten years of full-time experience in the occupation.
3. A license or certification to practice the profession.
4. A salary or other pay showing exceptional ability.
5. Membership in professional associations.
6. Recognition for achievements and significant contributions from peers, government entities, or professional organizations.

Meeting three criteria is not the end of the analysis. USCIS then looks at the evidence as a whole to decide whether you really are above the ordinary level in your field. Chefs, designers, financial specialists, and skilled tradespeople without graduate degrees often fit here.

## The standard EB-2 process

For most EB-2 cases, the path is:

1. **Prevailing wage determination** from the Department of Labor.
2. **PERM labor certification**, including recruitment to test the U.S. labor market.
3. **Form I-140** filed by the employer.
4. **Adjustment of status or consular processing** once your priority date is current.

The employer controls the process and pays the PERM costs. If you change jobs before the I-140 is approved, you typically start over.

## EB-2 vs EB-2 NIW

The NIW is still EB-2. You must first qualify as an advanced degree professional or a person of exceptional ability. The difference is that you ask USCIS to waive the job offer and PERM because your work benefits the United States.

| | Standard EB-2 | EB-2 NIW |
|---|---|---|
| Job offer | Required | Not required |
| PERM | Required | Waived |
| Who files | Employer | You can self-petition |
| Main test | Labor market and job requirements | Matter of Dhanasar three-prong test |
| Portability | Tied to employer until later stages | Not tied to an employer |

The NIW is attractive for researchers, founders, and specialists whose work has broader impact, but it carries a heavier evidence burden on the merits. Standard EB-2 is often the steadier choice for a professional with a supportive employer and a role that clearly requires an advanced degree.

## Common EB-2 problems

- A degree that does not match the field of the position.
- A three-year foreign bachelor's degree presented as a U.S. bachelor's equivalent without a proper evaluation.
- Experience letters missing titles, hours, and duties.
- A job description that requires less education than EB-2 demands, which can push the case into EB-3.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a three-year bachelor's degree from India count for EB-2?
On its own, usually not as a U.S. bachelor's equivalent. Some applicants combine it with a postgraduate diploma or use the exceptional ability route. A credential evaluation that addresses the specific degrees is essential.

### Can I qualify through exceptional ability and still need PERM?
Yes. Exceptional ability is a way into EB-2, but the standard EB-2 still requires a job offer and PERM unless you also qualify for the national interest waiver. One exception applies to Schedule A occupations, such as registered nurses and physical therapists, where the Department of Labor has already found a shortage.

### Is EB-2 faster than EB-3?
It depends on your country of birth and the month. For some countries the EB-3 date has at times been ahead of EB-2. Check both lines in the visa bulletin before deciding.

### Can my employer file in EB-2 if the job only needs a bachelor's degree?
No. The job itself must require an advanced degree or its equivalent. If the role truly needs only a bachelor's degree, EB-3 is the correct category.

## Getting the category right

The right category, standard EB-2, EB-2 NIW, or another one, depends on your degree, your experience, and your employer. A short review at the start avoids filing in the wrong category.
