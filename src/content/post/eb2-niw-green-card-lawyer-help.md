The EB-2 national interest waiver, usually called the NIW, is a green card category that lets you file without a job offer and without PERM labor certification, as long as you can show that your work is important enough to the United States. A NIW lawyer helps you define that work, assemble evidence for it, and present a case in the way USCIS expects.

## What the NIW is

The EB-2 category normally requires an employer to sponsor you and go through labor certification. The national interest waiver lets you skip both. You file Form I-140 yourself, or an employer can file it for you, and you ask USCIS to waive the job offer requirement because your work benefits the country.

## Who can apply

You must first qualify for EB-2 itself, which means one of two things:

- **An advanced degree** (a master's or higher, or a bachelor's degree plus five years of progressive experience), or
- **Exceptional ability** in the sciences, arts or business, shown through evidence such as degrees, licenses, salary, memberships and recognition.

Then you must meet the NIW test.

## The three-part test

USCIS uses the framework from a decision known as Matter of Dhanasar. You must show:

1. **Your proposed endeavor has substantial merit and national importance.** This is about what you plan to do in the United States, and the reach of its impact, not just your qualifications.
2. **You are well positioned to advance it.** Your education, skills, track record, and progress so far, along with plans, funding or interest from others, should all point in that direction.
3. **On balance, it would benefit the United States to waive the job offer and labor certification.** You explain why requiring a sponsor would not serve the national interest.

All three must be satisfied, and the strongest cases make the connections explicit rather than leaving the officer to infer them.

## What an NIW lawyer does

- **Defines the proposed endeavor.** This is the single most important step. A vague description such as "continue my research" tends to cause trouble. A specific one that explains the problem, the approach and the expected impact gives the rest of the petition something to hold on to.
- **Assesses fit honestly.** A lawyer can tell you early if your evidence looks thin and suggest what to build or whether another category works better.
- **Organizes evidence.** Publications, citations, patents, funding, media, letters of support and evidence of adoption of your work are matched to the three prongs.
- **Guides recommendation letters.** Strong letters are specific about what you did and why it matters. Independent experts carry more weight than close colleagues.
- **Drafts the petition letter** and builds the exhibit list.
- **Handles RFEs and later steps**, including the green card application stage once your priority date is current.

## Do you need a lawyer

Some people file on their own, and it is legal to do so. The tradeoff is that a thin or poorly framed petition can be denied or draw an RFE, which costs time. If your field is crowded or your record is borderline, professional help is often worth the fee.
