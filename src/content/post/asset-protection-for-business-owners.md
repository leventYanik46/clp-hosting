A business owner's personal assets are exposed from two directions: claims against the business that reach through to the owner, and claims against the owner personally that reach into the business. Asset protection for business owners means building layers so that no single lawsuit, default, or bad year can take everything. None of the layers works after the claim exists. The whole exercise depends on doing it early, which is why the [asset protection planning](/practice-area/key-services/estate-planning-asset-protection) we do for owners starts while there is no creditor to complain.

## Layer one: keep the business and yourself legally separate

Operating as an LLC or corporation puts a wall between business liabilities and your personal assets, but only if you respect the wall. Courts in Virginia, DC, and New York will disregard the entity, often called piercing the veil, when the owner treats company accounts as personal, skips the formalities, undercapitalizes the business, or signs contracts in a personal capacity. Keep separate bank accounts, sign as an officer or member, document major decisions, and maintain the operating agreement or bylaws. A single-member LLC gets the least deference, so a solo owner has to be the most disciplined.

Two limits apply. First, the entity does not protect a professional from liability for their own negligence; a physician, lawyer, or engineer is personally liable for their own malpractice no matter how the practice is organized. Second, personal guarantees on leases, loans, and vendor accounts hand the creditor a direct claim against you, bypassing the entity entirely. Read every guarantee before signing, negotiate caps and expirations, and track which ones are still live. Our [business and corporate law](/practice-area/business-corporate-law) team reviews these at formation and when the company takes on new obligations.

## Layer two: insurance for the claims you cannot structure away

Insurance is the first line against most lawsuits and the cheapest layer to add. General liability, professional liability or errors and omissions, employment practices, cyber, and commercial auto cover the business. A personal umbrella policy sits over your homeowner's and auto coverage and responds to the large personal claims that would otherwise reach savings. Review limits annually against what the business actually does, because policies bought at startup are often far too small five years later. Insurance also pays for the defense, which is frequently the larger cost.

## Layer three: hold assets in protected forms

Some assets are protected by law regardless of how they are titled. Employer retirement plans governed by ERISA, such as a 401(k), are shielded from most creditors by federal law. IRAs receive protection in bankruptcy and under state law, with the scope varying by jurisdiction. Life insurance cash value and annuities receive partial protection in many states. Contributing to these vehicles is both retirement planning and protection.

Married owners in Virginia, DC, and New York can hold certain property as tenants by the entirety, a form of joint ownership that a creditor of only one spouse generally cannot reach. The details differ: which assets qualify, and whether the protection survives a transfer into a trust, vary among the three jurisdictions, and the protection ends at divorce or the death of the non-debtor spouse. For a married couple it is often the most valuable protection available at no cost, provided the business debts have not been guaranteed by both spouses.

## Layer four: trusts and transfers to family

Assets you place in a properly drafted irrevocable trust for your spouse or children are no longer yours and are generally out of reach of your future creditors. The trade is that they are out of your reach too. Our post on [revocable versus irrevocable trusts](/revocable-vs-irrevocable-trust-asset-protection) explains what you give up and the tax consequences of the transfer. Outright gifts to family members accomplish the same removal with less control and more exposure to the recipient's own creditors and divorces, which is why a trust with a spendthrift clause is usually the better vehicle. Virginia also permits a self-settled asset protection trust under specific conditions; DC and New York do not.

Every transfer must be made while you are solvent and before any claim is pending or foreseeable. Transfers made with intent to hinder creditors, or made for less than fair value while insolvent, can be unwound under the voidable transfer statutes in each of the firm's jurisdictions, and the attempt can be used against you in the underlying case. The window for this layer is years before trouble, not weeks.

## Protecting the business from your personal creditors

The reverse problem is a personal judgment, a divorce, or a personal bankruptcy reaching your ownership interest in the company. A multi-member LLC with a well-drafted operating agreement can limit a creditor to a charging order against distributions, keeping the creditor out of management and unable to force a sale. Buy-sell provisions among owners can require a transfer at a formula price if an owner's interest is seized or an owner divorces, protecting the other owners and giving the debtor-owner a defined outcome. These are drafting choices in the governing documents, and they are much easier to include at formation than to negotiate after a problem exists.

## Fitting asset protection for business owners into the estate plan

Asset protection and estate planning use the same tools and should be designed together. A trust that removes assets from your creditors' reach also removes them from your taxable estate; a buy-sell agreement that handles a creditor seizure also handles your death; the retirement accounts you funded for protection pass by beneficiary designation and need to be coordinated with the will.
