The H-1B Employer Data Hub is a free USCIS tool that shows how many H-1B petitions a company had approved and denied in a given fiscal year. If you are weighing a job offer that depends on sponsorship, it is one of the few public ways to check whether an employer actually files H-1B petitions and how those petitions fare.

## What the H-1B Employer Data Hub shows

For each employer and fiscal year, the hub reports counts of:

- **Initial approvals and denials.** New employment, typically first H-1B petitions or new cap cases.
- **Continuing approvals and denials.** Extensions, amendments, and changes of employer.

You can filter by employer name, fiscal year, state, city, ZIP code, and industry code. Results can be downloaded for closer review.

## How to search it

1. Go to the H-1B Employer Data Hub page on uscis.gov.
2. Enter the employer's name. Try variations, because companies often file under a legal name that differs from their brand, such as "Example Technologies Inc." rather than "Example."
3. Narrow by fiscal year and state if the company is large or has a common name.
4. Compare several years to see a pattern, not a single snapshot.

## Reading the numbers

**Volume.** A company with hundreds of approvals each year has an established immigration process. A company with none may never have sponsored anyone, which is not disqualifying but means you should ask more questions.

**Denial ratio.** A consistently high denial rate relative to approvals can signal weak job descriptions, compliance problems, or roles that do not fit the specialty occupation standard. One bad year may just reflect a policy shift that hit the whole industry.

**Initial vs. continuing.** If you need a new cap petition, the initial approval numbers matter more. If you are transferring an existing H-1B, look at continuing approvals.

## What the data does not tell you

- **RFEs.** The hub does not show how many cases received requests for evidence before approval.
- **Withdrawals.** Petitions the employer withdrew do not appear as denials.
- **Appeals.** A denial later overturned may still appear as a denial.
- **Role-level detail.** You cannot see which job titles were approved or denied.
- **Timing.** The data is grouped by decision year, which may not match filing year.

Use the hub as one signal, not a verdict.

## Pair it with other sources

- **DOL LCA disclosure data** shows job titles, wages, and worksites the employer listed on Labor Condition Applications.
- **PERM disclosure data** shows whether the employer sponsors green cards, which matters for long-term planning.
- **Direct questions to the employer.** Ask who handles their immigration, whether they pay for premium processing, whether they sponsor green cards, and how they handled the $100,000 proclamation payment for workers outside the U.S.

## Red flags worth a closer look

- Offers requiring you to pay H-1B legal fees or the employer's filing fees, which is generally not allowed
- Employers with no history of filings who promise a fast H-1B
- Companies that cannot explain where you will actually work
- Pressure to accept a role with duties that sound unrelated to your degree


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I see data for my specific job title?

Not in the Data Hub. DOL's LCA disclosure files show job titles, wages, and worksites, which you can combine with Data Hub numbers.

### Does a high denial count mean I should reject an offer?

Not always. Look at the company's size, industry, and trend over several years. A large employer with thousands of filings will have more denials in raw numbers.

### Is the data updated?

USCIS updates the hub periodically, typically after the end of each fiscal year, so the newest year may be incomplete or missing.
