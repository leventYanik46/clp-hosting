An invitation letter for a U.S. visa is a letter from the person your parents will visit, explaining the trip and who will host them. It is not required, and it does not guarantee a visa. What decides most B-2 visitor cases is the interview, where the officer looks for strong ties to the home country and a believable travel plan. A good letter supports that story. It cannot replace it.

## What an invitation letter is for

The consular officer must presume every visitor visa applicant intends to immigrate until the applicant shows otherwise. The letter helps by confirming:

- Who your parents are visiting and why
- How long they plan to stay
- Where they will live during the visit
- Who will pay for the trip

## What to include in the letter

- **Your full name, address, phone number, and email**
- **Your status in the U.S.**: citizen, permanent resident, or visa holder, with a copy of your passport, green card, or visa and I-797 if applicable
- **Your relationship** to the applicants, with their full names and dates of birth
- **The purpose of the visit**: a graduation, the birth of a grandchild, a family vacation, or simply spending time together
- **Planned dates** of arrival and departure
- **Accommodation**: that they will stay at your home, with the address
- **Financial support**: whether you are paying for some or all expenses, with supporting documents such as recent pay stubs or a bank statement if you are covering costs
- **Your signature and the date**

Keep it to one page. Write it in plain language and sign it by hand.

## What to avoid

- Promising that your parents will "definitely return." Officers want evidence, not assurances.
- Mentioning plans for your parents to babysit long term, work, or stay indefinitely
- Inconsistent dates between the letter, the DS-160, and the plane tickets

## B1/B2 interview questions for parents

Parents should be ready to answer simple, direct questions in their own words. Common ones:

- Why are you going to the United States?
- Who will you visit, and what do they do?
- How long will you stay?
- Who is paying for the trip?
- What do you do for work, or are you retired?
- Who lives with you at home? Do you have other children nearby?
- Have you traveled abroad before?
- When will you return, and why?

The strongest answers point to real reasons to go home: a home they own, a pension, a business, other children and grandchildren, medical care, community responsibilities.

## Documents parents should bring

- Valid passport and any previous U.S. visas
- DS-160 confirmation and appointment letter
- Evidence of ties: property records, pension or income statements, business registration, family photos with other relatives at home
- Bank statements showing they can cover their share of the trip
- The invitation letter and copies of your status documents

Under current State Department rules, most first-time applicants interview in person in their country of nationality or residence. Some renewal applicants may still qualify for an interview waiver if their prior B visa expired recently.

## Visitor rules once they arrive

On B-2 status, parents can visit, travel, and spend time with family. They cannot work. Visitors are usually admitted for up to six months, but the CBP officer decides the period at entry. Check the I-94 record after arrival.

## If the visa is refused

A refusal under section 214(b) means the officer was not convinced of strong ties. There is no appeal, but parents can reapply when their situation changes or they can present stronger evidence.


## Frequently asked questions

### Does the invitation letter need to be notarized?

No. A signed letter is standard. Notarization does not usually add weight with consular officers.

### Can I go to the interview with my parents?

Usually no. Only the applicants attend the interview, although officers may allow a family member to assist elderly applicants in limited cases.

### Can my parents come more than once a year?

Yes, but frequent long visits draw questions about whether they really live abroad. Keep each trip to a clear purpose and length.
