The immigration medical exam is required for most green card applicants filing Form I-485 in the U.S. A USCIS-designated civil surgeon performs the exam and completes Form I-693. Small errors on this form are a frequent source of requests for evidence, and since December 2024, filing the I-485 without the I-693 can lead to rejection.

## Who needs the I-693

Most adjustment of status applicants must submit an I-693. Some refugees adjusting status have special rules, and certain applicants who had an overseas medical exam may only need a vaccination supplement. Applicants abroad do a separate exam with a panel physician overseas, not the I-693.

## Finding a civil surgeon

Only USCIS-designated civil surgeons can perform the exam. Use the USCIS "Find a Doctor" tool. A regular physician, even a specialist, cannot complete the form unless they are designated.

## Medical examination cost for immigration

USCIS does not set the fee. Civil surgeons charge their own prices, which typically include the exam and often the required blood tests. Vaccinations and chest X-rays, if needed, may be billed separately. Ask for a full quote before booking, and ask whether they accept insurance, since many do not for immigration exams.

## What the exam includes

- A review of your medical and vaccination history
- A physical exam
- Screening for tuberculosis, which may include a blood test and a chest X-ray if the result is positive
- A blood test for syphilis for applicants in certain age ranges
- A urine test for gonorrhea for applicants in certain age ranges
- A mental health and substance use screening

Bring your passport or ID, vaccination records, and a list of medications.

## I-693 vaccination requirements

Required vaccines follow CDC technical instructions and depend on age. They commonly include measles, mumps, rubella, tetanus and diphtheria, pertussis, polio, hepatitis B, varicella, and seasonal influenza during flu season, among others. The COVID-19 vaccination requirement was removed in January 2025.

If a vaccine is not medically appropriate for you, the civil surgeon can note that. Waivers are available for religious or moral objections in some cases, but they require a separate application.

## The sealed envelope rule

The civil surgeon gives you the completed I-693 in a sealed envelope. Do not open it. USCIS will reject or request a new exam if the envelope has been opened or tampered with. Ask the civil surgeon for a photocopy for your records before they seal it.

## Timing and validity

The civil surgeon's signature generally must be dated no more than 60 days before you file the I-485. Plan the exam close to filing. If the exam is done too early, you may need to repeat it.

## Common causes of an I-693 RFE

- Missing or incomplete vaccination records
- Unsigned sections or missing civil surgeon certification
- An opened envelope
- An exam signed outside the allowed window
- Old edition of the form


## Frequently asked questions

### What if the TB test is positive?

You may need a chest X-ray and possibly further evaluation. A positive test does not automatically disqualify you, but treatment may be required before the civil surgeon can complete the form.

### Can my children use the same civil surgeon?

Yes. Each applicant needs a separate I-693, but the whole family can go to the same civil surgeon.

### Do I need a new exam if my case takes a long time?

USCIS may ask for an updated exam in some cases. Keep your vaccination records handy in case.

### Can I bring my own vaccination records from abroad?

Yes. Bring them, translated if needed. The civil surgeon can count documented vaccines and give only the missing ones, which can save time and money.
