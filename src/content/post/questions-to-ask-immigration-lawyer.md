Choosing an immigration lawyer is an important decision. The right questions at your first consultation can help you understand your options and decide whether the lawyer is a good fit. Here are the questions to ask an immigration lawyer, along with what to expect from the consultation itself.

## Before the consultation

Prepare:

- Your passport, visas, I-94, and any prior immigration documents
- Notices from USCIS, the State Department, or immigration court
- A timeline of your immigration history, including entries and exits
- Any criminal records, even if minor or old
- A list of your questions and goals

## Questions about experience

1. **How many cases like mine have you handled?**
2. **Do you focus on immigration law,** or is it one of many practice areas?
3. **Have you handled cases with similar complications,** such as prior denials or criminal issues?
4. **Who will work on my case:** you, an associate, or a paralegal?

## Questions about your case

5. **What are my options?** A good lawyer will explain more than one path when possible.
6. **What are the risks of each option?**
7. **What is the realistic timeline?**
8. **What documents will I need?**
9. **What could cause a denial or delay?**
10. **Is there anything in my history that could be a problem?**

Be wary of any lawyer who guarantees results. No honest attorney can guarantee approval.

## Questions about fees

11. **Is the fee flat or hourly?**
12. **What does the fee include,** and what costs extra (such as responding to an RFE or attending an interview)?
13. **Are government filing fees included?**
14. **What is the payment schedule?**
15. **What happens to the fee if the case is denied or withdrawn?**

## Questions about communication

16. **How will you keep me updated?**
17. **How quickly do you respond to emails and calls?**
18. **Who is my main contact?**
19. **Will I get copies of everything filed?**

## Immigration lawyer consultation fee

Many immigration lawyers charge a consultation fee. It typically covers:

- Time to review your situation and documents
- An overview of your options
- An explanation of risks and next steps

A consultation fee usually doesn't cover preparing forms, filing applications, or ongoing representation. Some firms credit the consultation fee toward representation if you hire them.

## Red flags

- Promises of guaranteed approval
- Pressure to sign immediately
- Advice to lie or omit information
- No written fee agreement
- Notarios or consultants who aren't attorneys or accredited representatives

## After the consultation

Take notes and compare. Ask for a written fee agreement before you pay. A good attorney-client relationship depends on clear expectations from the start.

## Questions about the government process

Beyond the lawyer's practice, ask about the government side of your case:

- **Which agency will decide my case,** and where is it processed?
- **What are current processing times** for this type of case?
- **Will I need an interview,** and will you attend with me?
- **What happens if I receive a Request for Evidence?**
- **Is there any reason I should not travel while the case is pending?**

The answers tell you what to expect and also show how well the lawyer understands your specific category.

## Bring the hard facts up early

Clients sometimes hold back information they find embarrassing, such as an old arrest, a past overstay, or an earlier denied application. A lawyer can only plan around facts they know. Raising these issues at the consultation gives you a more accurate picture of your options and avoids surprises later in the process.

## How to compare two lawyers after consultations

After meeting more than one lawyer, compare their answers side by side. Did both describe the same options, or did one see a route the other missed? Did either flag a risk the other didn't mention? Were the fee structures for the same scope of work, or does one quote include interview preparation and RFE responses while the other doesn't? The lowest fee is not always the lowest cost if it excludes work your case is likely to need.

Also pay attention to how each lawyer explained things. You'll be working together for months or years. A lawyer who explains clearly at the first meeting is more likely to keep you informed later.
