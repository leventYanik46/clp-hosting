U.S. immigration documents carry several different numbers, and forms often ask for one specific number by name. The visa number on a U.S. visa, the card number on a green card, and your A-number are all different. Entering the wrong one is one of the easiest ways to slow down an application, a job onboarding, or a travel record.

## Where is the visa number on a U.S. visa?

The U.S. visa foil in your passport has a number printed in red, usually in the lower right corner. It is typically eight characters, often all digits, sometimes starting with a letter. This is the visa foil number, and it is what most forms mean by "visa number."

It is not the same as:

- **Your passport number**, which is printed on the passport's photo page
- **The control number**, a longer number printed elsewhere on the foil
- **Your annotation**, which may include a petition receipt number for H-1B or L-1 visas

## Visa number on an H-1B visa

An H-1B visa shows the same red foil number. The annotation section usually shows the petitioner's name and the petition receipt number. Forms such as the DS-160 for a future renewal or certain employment forms may ask for the foil number, not the receipt number.

## What is a visa number in the visa bulletin sense?

In green card discussions, "visa number" can also mean an immigrant visa available under the annual limits. When people say a "visa number is available," they mean their priority date is current. This is unrelated to the number printed on a visa foil.

## Where is the card number on a green card?

The green card has a 13-character card number, usually three letters followed by ten digits. The three letters show which USCIS office produced the card, for example EAC, WAC, LIN, SRC, NBC, MSC, or IOE.

Depending on the card design, the card number may be on the front or the back. On many cards it is on the back. If you cannot find it, check both sides, including the lines of text at the bottom of the back.

Forms that ask for it include Form I-9 for some employers, travel forms, and certain state ID applications.

## How these numbers differ from your A-number

- **A-number (USCIS number):** Your permanent immigration file number, usually 8 or 9 digits, starting with "A." On green cards it appears as "USCIS#." It stays the same for life.
- **Card number:** Unique to each physical card. A replacement card gets a new card number.
- **Receipt number:** The 13-character number on I-797 notices for a specific application. It looks similar to a card number, which causes confusion.
- **Visa foil number:** Unique to each visa stamp.

## Other numbers you may need

- **I-94 number:** An 11-character admission record number, found by downloading your I-94 from CBP's website.
- **SEVIS number:** For students and exchange visitors, printed on the I-20 or DS-2019, starting with "N."

## If a number is wrong

If your green card shows an incorrect A-number or other data because of a USCIS error, file Form I-90 to request a corrected card. Errors on a visa foil are handled by the issuing consulate. Errors on an I-94 go to CBP or USCIS, depending on who issued it.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I find my visa number online?

The consulate does not post visa foil numbers online. Keep a scan of your visa page. If you lose your passport, file a police report and contact the consulate.

### Does my green card card number change when I renew?

Yes. Every new card gets a new card number. Your A-number stays the same.

### Do I need my visa number for the I-9?

Most employees do not. Some workers presenting a foreign passport with an I-94 may be asked for document details. Your employer will tell you which numbers it needs from the documents you choose to present.
