Wrongful death damages are measured by what the family lost, not by what the deceased suffered, and the three jurisdictions where we practice draw that line in different places. Virginia lets a jury compensate a family's sorrow and lost companionship. The District of Columbia and New York, as of this writing, focus the wrongful death claim on financial loss. Those differences can change the value of an identical case by a wide margin, so the first question in any fatal injury matter is which state's law applies and what it allows.

## Economic losses: support, services, and expenses

Every wrongful death statute compensates the family's financial loss. The largest component is usually lost financial support: the income the deceased would have earned and contributed to the household over the rest of a working life, adjusted for the deceased's own living expenses and discounted to present value. This requires evidence of earnings history, career trajectory, benefits, and life expectancy, and in a serious case an economist to calculate it.

Lost services are the second component: childcare, household work, home maintenance, and the care of an aging parent. These are valued at what it would cost to replace them. Funeral and burial expenses are recoverable in all three jurisdictions. Medical expenses incurred between the injury and the death are recoverable too, though whether they belong to the wrongful death claim or to a separate survival claim depends on the state; our post on [wrongful death vs survival action](/wrongful-death-vs-survival-action) explains that split. Our [wrongful death practice](/practice-area/key-services/wrongful-death) assembles this proof from tax returns, pay records, benefit statements, and the testimony of people who saw what the deceased did for the family each day.

## Non-economic losses: where the states diverge

Virginia's statute is explicit. Beneficiaries may recover for sorrow, mental anguish, and solace, which includes the loss of the deceased's society, companionship, comfort, guidance, kindly offices, and advice. There is no fixed formula; the jury assigns a value based on the relationship, and these damages are frequently the largest part of a Virginia award.

The District of Columbia's wrongful death statute is directed at pecuniary loss, meaning financial loss to the beneficiaries. Grief and loss of companionship are generally not compensated under the wrongful death claim itself, though the separate survival claim can address the deceased's own pain and suffering before death.

New York limits wrongful death damages to pecuniary injuries. Courts read that phrase to include the loss of a parent's nurture, care, and guidance to a child, which has real value even though it is not a paycheck, but it excludes the family's grief. The legislature has passed bills to add grief damages more than once, and each has been vetoed as of this writing. Anyone with a New York claim should confirm the current rule, because the change, if it ever takes effect, would substantially alter case values.

## Punitive damages

All three jurisdictions permit punitive damages in some wrongful death cases, but only for conduct beyond ordinary negligence: drunk driving, a company that concealed a known defect, a reckless disregard for safety rules. Virginia's statute expressly allows them for willful or wanton conduct and caps them at a statutory amount. New York and DC require a similarly high showing. Punitive damages are the exception, not the expectation, and a claim should be valued on compensatory damages first.

## How the damages are proved

The defendant's insurer will challenge every component, so the evidence has to be specific.

- Earnings and support: tax returns, W-2s, pay stubs, employment contracts, and an expert's projection of future income.
- Services: testimony from family members about what the deceased did at home, and replacement cost evidence.
- Companionship and guidance: testimony from spouse, children, and friends; photographs; evidence of involvement in children's lives.
- Life expectancy and health: medical records establishing that the deceased was healthy before the event.
- Funeral and medical bills: itemized invoices.

Comparative or contributory fault applies here as in any injury case. In Virginia and DC, evidence that the deceased contributed to the accident can bar recovery entirely; in New York it reduces the award proportionally. The underlying negligence must be proved first, and the categories of loss available in an ordinary injury case, which we cover in our post on [what damages you can recover in a personal injury case](/damages-in-personal-injury-case), form the foundation.

## How the proceeds are divided

Wrongful death damages belong to the statutory beneficiaries, not to the estate, and must be divided among them. Virginia juries may specify the division, and the court approves it. New York's Surrogate's Court allocates based on each distributee's pecuniary loss. DC apportions among the spouse or domestic partner and next of kin according to their losses. When several family members have competing claims to the same fund, the division is often the most contested part of the case.
