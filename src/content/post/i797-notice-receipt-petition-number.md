Every H-1B petition generates at least two USCIS notices, both on Form I-797: a receipt notice when the case is accepted and an approval notice if it is approved. The receipt number on these notices is the petition number you will be asked for again and again, by consulates, by HR, and on later filings. Knowing how to read the notice, and checking it for errors the day it arrives, saves real trouble later.

## The types of I-797 you may receive

- **I-797C, Notice of Action.** Used for receipts, transfers, biometrics appointments, and rejections. An H-1B receipt notice is an I-797C.
- **I-797A, Notice of Action.** An approval notice for a change of status or extension inside the U.S. It includes a replacement I-94 at the bottom.
- **I-797B, Notice of Action.** An approval notice for a worker who will apply for a visa abroad. It has no I-94.
- **I-797E.** A request for evidence.

## Where to find the receipt number

The receipt number appears in the upper left of the notice under "Receipt Number." It has three letters followed by ten digits, for example "WAC" or "EAC" or "IOE." The letters indicate the service center or system that received the case.

When someone asks for your "H-1B petition number," they almost always mean this receipt number. Consulates use it on the DS-160, and employers use it to check case status online.

## Who is the petitioner?

The "Petitioner" field shows the company that filed the case. For H-1B petitions that is your employer, not you. You are the "Beneficiary." When a form asks for the "name of the person or company who filed the petition," enter the exact legal name of the employer as it appears on the notice, including suffixes such as "Inc." or "LLC."

## Reading an approval notice

An approval notice shows:

- **Classification**: H-1B, and the specific basis, such as a new employment, extension, or amendment.
- **Validity dates**: the period the petition is approved for.
- **Notice type**: approval.
- **Consulate or I-94 section**: an I-797B tells you to apply at a consulate; an I-797A includes a new I-94.

## The I-94 at the bottom of an I-797A

If you changed or extended status inside the U.S., the lower part of the I-797A is your new arrival/departure record. Cut along the line and keep it with your passport. It shows your status and the date your stay is authorized until.

Remember that your I-94 controls how long you may stay, not the visa stamp. If you travel abroad and return, CBP issues a new electronic I-94, which may differ from the paper one. Check the I-94 website after every entry and keep both records.

## Errors to check the day the notice arrives

Look for:

- Misspelled names or a wrong date of birth.
- A validity period that does not match what was requested.
- The wrong classification or basis.
- An I-797B when you expected a change of status inside the U.S.

If USCIS made the error, your employer or attorney can request a correction. If the error came from the filing itself, a corrected or amended petition may be needed. Do not travel on a notice that contains a material error without advice.

## Keeping notices organized

Store every I-797 for every petition, including expired ones. Future filings, green card applications, and naturalization all ask for your history, and the receipt numbers make it traceable. A simple folder with each notice, the matching I-94, and the visa stamp page saves hours later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is my receipt number the same as my case number?
Yes. USCIS uses the receipt number as the case number for checking status online.

### What if I lost my approval notice?
Your employer or attorney usually has the original. You can also request a duplicate approval notice from USCIS on Form I-824, which takes time.

### Does an I-797 approval let me travel?
It is one of the documents you need. To re-enter, you also need a valid visa stamp and passport, unless you are exempt from the visa requirement.

### Why does my I-94 say something different from my I-797?
The most recent I-94 controls. If you traveled after the approval, CBP's entry record may have replaced the I-94 on the notice. If CBP shortened your stay to match your passport expiration, you may need to fix it.

### Do I need my I-797 for the green card process?
Yes. Your immigration history, including every receipt and approval, is part of the green card application.

### Can HR use the I-797 for Form I-9?
An I-797A with the attached I-94, combined with your passport, is commonly used as evidence of H-1B work authorization with the sponsoring employer.

### What is the difference between a receipt notice and a transfer notice?
A transfer notice means USCIS moved your case to another office. Your receipt number stays the same.
