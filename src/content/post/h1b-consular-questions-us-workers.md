Since mid-2026, immigration attorneys have reported that consular officers are asking H-1B visa applicants some version of "Why can't an American do this job?" The question is not part of the H-1B legal standard for a visa interview, but it is being asked, and an unprepared answer can lead to delays or refusals. At the same time, the Department of Labor's Project Firewall has increased enforcement scrutiny on H-1B employers. Workers and employers both need to prepare.

## What consular officers are asking

Attorneys quoted in national reporting describe questions such as:

- Why couldn't your employer hire a U.S. worker for this position?
- What makes your skills specialized enough that an American could not do this job?
- How did your employer find you?

The questions appear most often in specialty-occupation cases where the officer wants to understand the employer's reasons for hiring from abroad. They come on top of expanded screening, including review of applicants' online presence.

## Why the question is awkward

The H-1B program does not require employers to test the U.S. labor market. That requirement belongs to PERM labor certification for green cards, and to H-1B "dependent" employers in limited situations. A standard H-1B employer attests that it will pay the required wage and that hiring the worker will not adversely affect U.S. workers' wages and conditions. It does not have to prove no American was available.

So the applicant is being asked a question the law does not ask the employer, and one the applicant usually cannot answer with certainty. Officers are not deciding labor economics at the window, but a confused or defensive response can make an officer doubt the job, the employer, or the applicant's credibility.

## How H-1B workers should prepare

**Do not compare yourself to U.S. workers.** You do not know who else applied, and claims that Americans are unqualified can sound false or offensive.

**Explain the role and your fit.** Describe the position's duties in concrete terms, the specialized knowledge it requires, and how your education and experience match. A clear, accurate description of what you do every day is the strongest answer.

**Know why your employer selected you.** If you were promoted internally, hired after a specific project, or recruited for a particular skill, say so plainly.

**Be consistent with the petition.** Your job title, duties, salary, and worksite should match the H-1B approval and the Labor Condition Application. Inconsistencies are more dangerous than any single answer.

**Bring support.** A current employment verification letter describing duties, recent pay statements, and the approval notice help the officer confirm what you say.

## What Project Firewall means for employers

In September 2025 the Department of Labor launched Project Firewall, an enforcement initiative focused on H-1B compliance. It allows investigations to begin on the Secretary of Labor's certification rather than only on a complaint. Investigators look at whether employers pay the required wage, maintain public access files, post notices, and treat H-1B and U.S. workers according to their attestations.

For employers, the practical steps are:

- Audit public access files for every active Labor Condition Application.
- Confirm actual pay matches or exceeds the required wage, including during nonproductive time.
- Track worksite changes and file amendments when needed.
- Prepare H-1B employees for consular interviews with accurate role descriptions.

Employer compliance and the worker's interview are connected. An officer who sees a well-documented role and an employer with clean records has less reason to look further.

## If the visa is refused or delayed

A refusal under section 214(b) or a hold under section 221(g) is not always final. Some cases are resolved with additional documents; others require a new application or a different strategy. Read the refusal letter closely and speak with counsel before reapplying.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the question a legal requirement for the visa?
No. Standard H-1B employers are not required to prove that no U.S. worker was available. The question reflects how some officers are probing applications, not a new legal test.

### Should I bring proof that my employer recruited U.S. workers?
Usually not, unless your employer is H-1B dependent and made recruitment attestations, or has a PERM case underway. Focus on documenting your role and qualifications.

### Will Project Firewall affect my visa interview?
Not directly, but an employer under investigation or with compliance problems can draw more scrutiny. Accurate filings and records protect both the employer and the worker.

### Can a refusal at the interview be appealed?
Consular refusals generally cannot be appealed in court. Some can be overcome with additional documents or a new application.

### Do these questions come up at H-1B extensions inside the U.S.?
Extensions approved by USCIS do not involve a consular interview. The questions arise when you need a new visa stamp abroad.

### Should my employer prepare a written statement on why it hired me?
A short, accurate description of how the employer found and selected you can help you answer consistently. It should match the petition and not overstate claims about the U.S. labor market.
