The wrongful death statute of limitations in Virginia, Washington, DC, and New York is two years, measured from the date of death rather than the date of the injury that caused it. That is shorter than the general injury deadline in DC and New York, and it comes with a practical complication: the lawsuit can only be filed by the estate's personal representative, so the estate has to be opened and the representative appointed with enough time left to file. Families who wait for the funeral, the criminal case, or the insurance company to finish often discover that two years is less time than it sounds.

## Two years from the date of death

In all three jurisdictions, the wrongful death claim accrues at death. If the person was injured in a crash and died six weeks later, the two years run from the death, not the crash. This matters in the other direction too: an injury claim that was already pending when the person died converts into a wrongful death claim with its own clock, and the family should not assume the original lawsuit's timing still controls.

The deadline is a filing deadline. A claim opened with an insurer, a letter from an attorney, or a demand package does not satisfy it. Only a complaint filed in court does. Our [wrongful death attorneys](/practice-area/key-services/wrongful-death) calendar the date of death as the anchor in every case and work backward from it.

## Why the estate has to be opened first

Unlike an injury claim, which the injured person files directly, a wrongful death claim must be filed by the personal representative of the estate. That person is appointed by the probate court: the circuit court clerk in Virginia, the Probate Division of the DC Superior Court, or the Surrogate's Court in New York. Appointment requires a petition, notice to interested parties, and in some cases a bond. New York allows limited letters that authorize only the wrongful death claim, which speeds the process when there is no estate to administer otherwise.

If the family has not agreed on who will serve, or if there is a dispute over the will, the appointment can take months. Those months come out of the two years. Our post on [who is allowed to file a wrongful death claim](/who-can-file-wrongful-death-claim) explains the appointment process and the order of priority when there is no will.

## Shorter deadlines that apply to particular defendants

The two-year period is the outer limit, and several categories of defendant impose earlier requirements that can end the claim long before it.

- Government defendants: a death caused by a city bus, a county vehicle, a public hospital, or a dangerous public road triggers notice requirements. New York requires a notice of claim within 90 days against municipal entities, and the period for wrongful death claims against them is measured in a particular way from the appointment of the representative, so the specifics must be checked immediately. The District of Columbia requires written notice to the Mayor within six months. Virginia requires notice within six months for claims against localities and within one year under the Virginia Tort Claims Act for claims against the Commonwealth.
- Medical negligence: a death caused by a medical error is still a wrongful death claim with a two-year period from death, but the underlying malpractice rules, including expert certification requirements in Virginia, shape how quickly the case must be prepared. Our [medical negligence](/practice-area/key-services/medical-negligence) page addresses those requirements.
- Survival claims: in DC and New York, the estate's separate claim for the deceased's pre-death suffering has its own limitations period, which may be different from the wrongful death deadline.

The general rules for injury deadlines, including government notice periods, are collected in our post on [how long you have to file a personal injury claim](/injury-claim-deadlines-dc-virginia-new-york).

## What does not pause the clock

Families often assume that a pending criminal prosecution of the at-fault driver, an ongoing insurance negotiation, or a police investigation pauses the civil deadline. None of them does. The civil claim and the criminal case run on separate tracks, and the civil lawsuit can be filed while the criminal case is pending. Tolling for minors generally does not apply to the wrongful death claim itself, because the claim belongs to the personal representative, not to the child beneficiaries. Incapacity of a beneficiary likewise does not extend the representative's deadline.

## Why filing early matters beyond the deadline

Evidence in fatal cases degrades quickly. Vehicle data is lost when a car is scrapped. Commercial truck logs are retained for limited periods. Surveillance footage is overwritten. Witnesses move and memories fade. The people who can describe what the deceased contributed to the family are easiest to find in the first year. A claim filed in the final month before the deadline is a claim built on whatever evidence happens to survive. A claim started in the first months is a claim built on the evidence that should exist.
