Form I-140 is the immigrant petition your employer files after PERM, or that a self-petitioner files in a category that skips it. An approved I-140 is a real milestone, but it is not a green card. It establishes your place in line, which is why the I-140 processing time, and the premium processing option, draw so much attention.

## What the I-140 does

The I-140 asks USCIS to classify you in an employment-based category, such as EB-1, EB-2 or EB-3. USCIS reviews whether the employer can pay the offered wage from the priority date forward, whether you meet the job requirements, and whether the petition fits the category. A decision on the I-140 does not give you permanent residence. That comes through a separate step, adjustment of status or consular processing, once your priority date is current.

## What affects I-140 processing time

USCIS posts its current processing times online, broken out by form, category and service center. Actual times depend on:

- **The category.** Different categories have different workloads and different handling.
- **Where the case was filed.** The service center affects timing.
- **Completeness of the filing.** Missing or inconsistent evidence leads to a Request for Evidence, which adds time.
- **Employer financial evidence.** Ability to pay issues are among the common triggers for requests.
- **Premium processing.** Whether the employer chooses it.

Check the USCIS processing times tool for the current range rather than relying on figures quoted in forums, which go out of date quickly.

## Premium processing

Premium processing is available for many I-140 categories for an additional fee. It guarantees that USCIS will take an initial action, such as approval, denial or a request for evidence, within a published window. If USCIS misses the window, it refunds the fee. Premium processing does not raise the odds of approval and does not shorten later steps, such as the wait for a priority date.

It often makes sense when speed matters for an H-1B extension, a travel plan or a spouse's work authorization, since these can depend on an approved I-140.

## Where to file

USCIS publishes direct filing addresses for the I-140 on its website, and they depend on the category, the location of the petitioner or beneficiary, and whether premium processing is requested. Addresses change, so check the USCIS Where to File page for the form on the day you file, or have your attorney confirm.

## What an approved I-140 gets you

- **A priority date.** In PERM-based cases, the date comes from the PERM filing, and it holds your place in the visa queue.
- **H-1B extensions beyond six years.** In many cases, an approved I-140 supports extensions past the usual limit.
- **H-4 EAD eligibility.** Your H-4 spouse may be able to apply for work authorization.
- **Portability after a pending I-485.** Once an adjustment application has been pending long enough, job changes within the same or similar field may be possible without restarting.

## What approval does not do

- It does not make you a permanent resident.
- It does not guarantee the employer will stay committed. The employer can withdraw the petition, though in some situations the priority date stays with you.
- It does not mean a visa number is available.

## Next steps after approval

Check the Visa Bulletin for your category and country of birth, gather documents for the adjustment application, and decide with your attorney when to file. If your priority date is not yet current, plan how you will keep valid status in the meantime.
