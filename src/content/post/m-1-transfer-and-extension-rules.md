An M-1 student may transfer to a different school only within the first six months after admission, and may extend stay only until the total time in M-1 status reaches three years from the original program start date. Unlike F-1 students, who transfer by having one Designated School Official release the SEVIS record to another, an M-1 transfer and an M-1 extension each require a Form I-539 filed with USCIS, supported by a new Form I-20. The filings are routine when done on time, and nearly impossible to repair when done late.

## How long you are admitted for in the first place

At the port of entry, an M-1 student is admitted for the period needed to complete the program shown on the Form I-20 plus 30 days, or for one year, whichever is shorter. The date is written on the I-94. A 15-month aviation program therefore produces a one-year admission, not a 15-month one, and the student must extend before that first year runs out. Students who assume the I-20 end date controls discover the problem only when a DSO notices the I-94 date has passed.

This is the first thing we check when a vocational student contacts us, and our [M-1 visa](/practice-area/key-services/m-1-visa) page describes the status review that follows: I-94 date, I-20 dates, SEVIS record, and whether the course load has been full time throughout.

## Extending M-1 status

Extensions are granted in increments of up to one year each, and the cumulative stay cannot exceed three years plus the 30-day grace period. To extend, you need to show that you are still a full-time student, that you have the funds for the remaining period, and that you need more time for a legitimate reason: a program longer than one year, a documented medical reduced course load, or similar.

The steps:

1. Ask your DSO to issue a new Form I-20 reflecting the extended end date and to update SEVIS.
2. File Form I-539 with USCIS, with the I-20, your I-94, passport, proof of funds, and the current USCIS filing fee. The regulation sets a window of at least 15 days and no more than 60 days before your current authorized stay ends.
3. Continue attending classes while the request is pending. A timely filed extension keeps you in a period of authorized stay until USCIS decides.

Three-year cap arithmetic matters for long programs and for students who switched programs. Time in M-1 status counts from the first program start date, including any earlier program at a different school. A student who spent two years in one certificate program and then started another has one year left, regardless of the new program's length.

## Transferring schools

M-1 transfers are allowed only during the first six months after you first enter in M-1 status or change to it. After six months, a transfer is permitted only if you cannot remain at your current school for reasons beyond your control, such as the school losing its SEVP certification or closing the program. Wanting a cheaper school or a better location is not enough.

To transfer, notify your current DSO, obtain a Form I-20 from the new school, and file Form I-539 with USCIS requesting permission to transfer. The DSO at the current school sets a release date in SEVIS and the new DSO issues the transfer I-20. Ask the new school's DSO in writing when you may begin attending; the regulation ties attendance to the release date and the pending I-539, and schools differ in how conservatively they read it. Do not stop attending your current school before the release date, and do not simply enroll elsewhere and hope SEVIS catches up. For a comparison of how F-1 transfers work without a USCIS filing, the [F-1 student visa](/practice-area/key-services/f-1-visa) page is the starting point.

## Reduced course load

M-1 status requires a full course of study for the entire period. The only exception is medical: a DSO may authorize a reduced course load or temporary absence for a documented illness or medical condition, supported by a letter from a licensed physician, doctor of osteopathy, or licensed clinical psychologist. The aggregate limit is five months. There is no academic difficulty exception of the kind F-1 students can use in their first term, and there is no exception for a job, a family emergency, or a final-term light schedule. A student who drops below full time without the DSO's prior authorization is out of status.

If that happens, reinstatement on Form I-539 is possible if you request it within five months of the violation, have not worked without authorization, and can show the lapse resulted from circumstances beyond your control. Reinstatement is a one-time, discretionary remedy, so the application needs documentation rather than assurances. Students who worked without authorization cannot be reinstated; the reasons are explained in our post on [M-1 employment and practical training limits](/m-1-visa-practical-training-rules).

## Dependents, travel, and the end of the program

M-2 dependents extend and transfer with the principal; include them on the I-539. Travel during a pending I-539 abandons the request, so time trips between filings. When the program finishes and any practical training ends, the 30-day grace period begins, and the remaining options are a different status or departure, as explained in [whether an M-1 student can change to F-1 or another status](/change-m-1-to-f-1-status).
