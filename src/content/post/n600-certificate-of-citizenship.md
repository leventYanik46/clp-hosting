Form N-600 is the application for a Certificate of Citizenship. It is for people who are already U.S. citizens through a parent, either at birth abroad or automatically as a child, and need official proof. If you qualify, you do not file an N-400 or take the naturalization test. You are not becoming a citizen. You are documenting that you already are.

## N-400 vs. N-600

- **N-400:** For permanent residents who meet residence and other requirements and want to become citizens through naturalization. Includes an interview, English and civics tests, and an oath.
- **N-600:** For people who acquired or derived citizenship through a U.S. citizen parent. USCIS reviews whether the legal conditions were met. There is usually no test, and many cases are decided on the documents, though an interview may be scheduled.

Filing an N-400 when you already qualify for an N-600 wastes time and money, and USCIS may deny it because you are already a citizen.

## Who qualifies

### Citizenship at birth abroad

A child born outside the U.S. can be a citizen at birth if a parent was a U.S. citizen and met physical presence requirements in the U.S. before the birth. The rules depend on the year of birth, whether one or both parents were citizens, and whether the parents were married. Older birth years have different requirements.

### Automatic citizenship after birth (Child Citizenship Act)

Under INA section 320, a child born abroad becomes a citizen automatically when all of these are true before age 18:

- At least one parent is a U.S. citizen, by birth or naturalization
- The child is a lawful permanent resident
- The child lives in the U.S. in the legal and physical custody of the citizen parent

Adopted children can qualify if adoption requirements are met.

Different rules applied to people who turned 18 before February 27, 2001. They may have derived citizenship under older laws.

## Do you need a Certificate of Citizenship?

Not always. A valid U.S. passport is also proof of citizenship. Some people get a passport directly using the same evidence. A Certificate of Citizenship never expires, though, and is useful when a passport is lost or when an agency asks for a primary document.

## Form N-600 fees

USCIS charges a filing fee for the N-600, with a different amount for online filing. Military-related applications may be exempt. Check the current fee schedule before filing.

## Evidence you need

- Your birth certificate
- Your parent's proof of citizenship: U.S. birth certificate, naturalization certificate, or passport
- Your parents' marriage certificate, and divorce or death records if relevant
- Evidence of the parent's physical presence in the U.S. for birth-abroad cases: school records, tax records, employment records
- Your green card, for section 320 cases
- Proof of legal and physical custody for section 320 cases

Gathering proof of a parent's U.S. physical presence from decades ago is often the hardest part.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming you must naturalize when you may already be a citizen
- Missing the custody requirement for children of divorced parents
- Not documenting the parent's physical presence clearly


## Frequently asked questions

### Can an adult file Form N-600?

Yes. There is no age limit. Adults who derived or acquired citizenship as children can file at any time.

### How long does N-600 processing take?

It varies by field office. Check the USCIS processing times page. Cases needing an interview or more evidence take longer.

### Can I get a passport instead?

Yes. If you have enough evidence, you can apply for a U.S. passport directly. Many people get both.

### What if my parent naturalized after I turned 18?

Under section 320, the conditions must be met before you turn 18. If they were not, you likely did not derive citizenship and would need to naturalize yourself.
