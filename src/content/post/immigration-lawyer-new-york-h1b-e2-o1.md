New York's employers, startups, investors, and creative professionals depend heavily on three visas: the H-1B for specialized employees, the E-2 for treaty investors opening businesses, and the O-1 for people with exceptional records in business, science, and the arts. Capitol Law Partners works with clients on all three from its office at 14 Wall Street. Here is what working with a New York immigration lawyer on these cases looks like.

## H-1B for New York employers

New York companies in finance, technology, healthcare, fashion, and media sponsor large numbers of H-1B workers. The issues that come up most often in the city include:

- **Worksite changes.** Moving between Manhattan offices is usually within the same metropolitan area, but moving a worker to New Jersey, Connecticut, or remote work in another state can require an amended petition.
- **Wage levels.** New York prevailing wages are high. The Labor Condition Application has to reflect the correct occupation and wage level.
- **Third-party placements.** Consulting firms placing workers at client sites must document the employer relationship and the actual work.

For employers, an H-1B lawyer in New York handles the registration, petition, compliance files, and the steps that keep employees in status through promotions and moves.

## E-2 for New York investors

New York attracts E-2 investors opening restaurants, retail stores, professional services firms, and tech startups. High rents and startup costs in the city affect how much investment is "substantial" for the business.

An E-2 visa lawyer in New York typically helps with:

- Reviewing leases and purchase agreements so they protect the investor if the visa is refused.
- Structuring ownership to keep at least 50% with treaty nationals.
- Coordinating the business plan with realistic New York costs and hiring.
- Preparing the consular package and interview.

## O-1 for New York professionals and artists

New York is home to artists, performers, designers, chefs, journalists, financiers, and founders who often fit the O-1. An O-1 visa lawyer in NYC helps with:

- Choosing between O-1A and O-1B based on the actual work.
- Building evidence from press, awards, reviews, and high compensation.
- Securing consultation letters from the right peer groups or unions.
- Using an agent and itinerary for artists working with several clients.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a lawyer in New York, or can I work with one elsewhere?
Immigration law is federal, so a licensed immigration attorney can represent you anywhere in the U.S. A local office helps when you want in-person meetings or have local filings.

### Which USCIS office handles New York cases?
Interviews for New York City residents are handled by USCIS field offices in the city, including the office at 26 Federal Plaza. Many petitions are decided at service centers without an interview.

### Can a New York startup sponsor its founder for an O-1?
Yes, if the company is a real employer that can control the founder's work, often through a board of directors.

### How do New York prevailing wages affect H-1B costs?
Prevailing wages in New York are high, which raises the minimum salary an employer must pay. Budget for that before making offers.

### Is a New York lease a problem for an E-2?
Leases are fine as investment, but sign them with contingencies tied to visa approval where you can, because New York commercial leases are long and expensive.

### Do artists need an agent for O-1B?
Not always, but artists working for several venues often use an agent with an itinerary so one petition covers multiple engagements.

### Can I meet with Capitol Law Partners remotely?
Yes. Clients can meet in person in New York or by video.

### How long do New York H-1B, E-2, and O-1 cases take?
With premium processing, USCIS acts on H-1B and O-1 petitions within 15 business days. E-2 timing depends mainly on consulate appointment availability.
