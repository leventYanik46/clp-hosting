A U.S. citizen can marry an undocumented immigrant, and the marriage can lead to a green card. But marriage alone does not fix status, and the path depends heavily on one fact: how the spouse entered the United States. A spouse who entered with a visa and overstayed can usually adjust status inside the U.S. A spouse who entered without inspection generally cannot, and must process abroad, often with a waiver.

## Why the manner of entry matters

To adjust status inside the U.S., an applicant generally must have been "inspected and admitted or paroled." A person who came in on a visa, even one that later expired, was admitted. Immediate relatives of U.S. citizens, including spouses, can usually adjust despite having overstayed.

A person who crossed the border without inspection was never admitted. With limited exceptions, that person cannot adjust status and must leave the U.S. to get an immigrant visa at a consulate.

## The unlawful presence problem

Leaving the U.S. after unlawful presence triggers bars to returning:

- **Three-year bar**: more than 180 days but less than one year of unlawful presence, followed by departure.
- **Ten-year bar**: one year or more of unlawful presence, followed by departure.

For most undocumented spouses, departing for the consular interview would trigger the ten-year bar. That is why the provisional waiver exists.

## The I-601A provisional waiver

The I-601A lets the spouse ask USCIS to waive the unlawful presence bar **before** leaving for the interview. The U.S. citizen spouse is a qualifying relative, so the applicant must show the citizen spouse would suffer extreme hardship if the waiver were denied.

The typical sequence:

1. U.S. citizen files Form I-130 for the spouse.
2. After approval, the case moves to the National Visa Center.
3. The spouse files Form I-601A and waits for approval while still in the U.S.
4. After approval, the spouse attends the immigrant visa interview abroad.
5. If the consulate finds no other ground of inadmissibility, the visa is issued and the spouse returns as a permanent resident.

The trip abroad is often short when everything is in order, but it carries risk if the consulate finds a problem the I-601A does not cover.

## When the I-601A does not work

- **Reentry after unlawful presence.** A person who was in the U.S. unlawfully for more than a year in total, left, and then reentered or tried to reenter without inspection is subject to the permanent bar. The I-601A cannot waive it.
- **Prior removal orders** may need additional forms, such as Form I-212.
- **Criminal history or fraud** require a different waiver, Form I-601, filed after the interview.

## Exceptions that allow adjustment

A few spouses who entered without inspection can still adjust inside the U.S.:

- **Section 245(i)**, for people who are beneficiaries of a petition or labor certification filed on or before April 30, 2001.
- **Military parole in place** for certain family members of service members and veterans.

A broader parole in place program for spouses of U.S. citizens was announced in 2024 but blocked by a federal court that November.

## Protecting the marriage case

A real marriage is required. Gather evidence of a shared life from the start: joint lease, bank accounts, photos over time, and affidavits from people who know you both. USCIS and consulates look for this in every case.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an undocumented immigrant legally marry a U.S. citizen?
Yes. Immigration status does not prevent a legal marriage in the U.S.

### Does marriage stop deportation?
Not automatically. Marriage can create a path to a green card, but it does not end removal proceedings by itself.

### How long does the I-601A process take?
It depends on I-130 processing, I-601A processing, and consular scheduling. Many cases take more than a year from start to finish.

### Can a permanent resident spouse use the I-601A?
Yes. A permanent resident spouse is also a qualifying relative for the unlawful presence waiver, though the I-130 wait is longer for spouses of permanent residents.

### What if I entered without inspection more than once?
Multiple entries can trigger the permanent bar, which the I-601A cannot waive. Your full entry history needs review.

### Can I travel to the interview and come back the same week?
Sometimes, but it depends on the consulate's schedule and medical exam timing. Plan for a longer stay.

### Do we need to live together?
Living together is strong evidence of a genuine marriage. If you live apart, be ready to explain why.

### Is there any new law for married couples in 2026?
Be cautious with claims of new programs. The 2024 parole in place program for spouses was blocked in court. Verify any announcement through official USCIS sources.
