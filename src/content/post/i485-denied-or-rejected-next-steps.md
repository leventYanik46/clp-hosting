A rejected I-485 and a denied I-485 sound alike but mean very different things. A rejection means USCIS refused to accept your filing, so no case was opened. A denial means USCIS reviewed your case and decided against it. The next steps depend entirely on which one you received, so start by reading the notice carefully.

## Rejection: your case was never accepted

USCIS rejects a filing when it has a basic defect. Common reasons include the wrong fee, a missing signature, an outdated form edition, a missing required page, or a filing sent to the wrong location. A rejected application returns to you with a notice, and the filing date does not count. No decision was made on the merits.

**What to do:** Fix the specific problem and file again. Check the current form edition and fee on the USCIS website, since both change. Pay attention to deadlines that matter in your situation, for example a status that is about to expire, because a rejection does not stop the clock.

## Denial: USCIS decided against you

A denial notice explains the reasons. Typical grounds include:

- **Ineligibility.** For example, no valid basis for the green card, or no visa number available.
- **Inadmissibility.** A ground such as unlawful presence, criminal history, fraud or misrepresentation, or health issues, with no waiver or no waiver approved.
- **Failure to respond.** Missing a request for evidence deadline or skipping a scheduled interview or biometrics appointment.
- **Insufficient evidence.** The officer was not satisfied by the proof of the relationship or the job.

## Your options after a denial

1. **Motion to reopen or reconsider.** You can file Form I-290B asking USCIS to take a second look. A motion to reopen relies on new facts. A motion to reconsider argues that the decision was legally wrong. The deadline is short, generally 30 days from the decision, or a few more days if the notice was mailed. USCIS usually does not allow an appeal to the Administrative Appeals Office for an I-485 denial.
2. **Refile.** If the problem can be fixed, such as a missing document, or a new qualifying basis, you may file a new I-485.
3. **Pursue another route.** A different category, a waiver, or consular processing may be a better path.
4. **Defend in court.** If USCIS issues a Notice to Appear, you may be able to renew the application before an immigration judge.

## The risk to your status

If your status expired while the I-485 was pending, a denial can leave you without lawful status. USCIS can issue an NTA, starting removal proceedings. Any work permit or travel document tied to the pending I-485 may stop being valid. This is the main reason to speak to an attorney immediately after a denial.

## What happens at an I-485 interview

Many I-485 cases include an interview. The officer reviews your application and asks you to confirm your answers under oath. Common topics are:

- Your identity, address and employment
- How you entered the United States and your immigration history
- The basis for your application, such as your marriage or job
- Arrests, criminal history and prior immigration violations
- Whether you have ever claimed to be a U.S. citizen, or worked without permission

Be honest and consistent. Bring identity documents, originals of civil records, the interview notice and updated evidence. If your case involves anything sensitive, bring your lawyer.

## Next steps

Read the notice, calendar the deadlines and speak to an attorney about whether to file a motion, refile or take another route.
