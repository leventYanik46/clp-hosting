NIW approval rates change from year to year, and between service centers, so any number you read about EB-2 national interest waivers should be treated as a snapshot. An NIW RFE is common enough that it should not alarm you on its own. What matters is understanding why USCIS asked and whether you can answer it with evidence.

## Reading NIW approval rate numbers

USCIS publishes data on I-140 filings, and websites and law firms often summarize them. When reading an NIW approval rate, keep a few things in mind:

- **Source and period.** Check which years the number covers. Policies and adjudication patterns have shifted over time.
- **Category mixing.** Some figures group NIW with other EB-2 cases, which can blur the picture.
- **Quality of the filings.** A rate reflects careful and sloppy petitions together. A well-prepared petition does not face the average.
- **Premium processing and RFEs.** Many approvals come after an RFE, and the rate alone does not show how many cases needed one.

## Service center comparisons

Discussions of the Texas and Nebraska Service Centers appear often in NIW forums. In some periods, decisions have differed between centers, and applicants have noticed. You usually do not choose where your case goes, since USCIS assigns the filing based on workload, so the difference is mostly useful as background. It does not justify changing a strategy.

## Why NIW RFEs happen

Most NIW RFEs track the three parts of the legal test. Common triggers include:

1. **A vague proposed endeavor.** The officer cannot tell what you will do in the United States, or the description reads like a job title.
2. **National importance not shown.** The evidence demonstrates benefit to a local employer or a small group, not wider impact.
3. **Not well positioned.** The record shows education but little track record, or plans without evidence of progress, funding or interest from others.
4. **Weak or generic letters.** Recommendation letters that praise without explaining specific contributions and their impact.
5. **No case for the waiver.** The petition does not explain why a labor certification process would be impractical or against the national interest.

## How to respond to an NIW RFE

- **Read the notice closely.** Identify which prong the officer found lacking and what evidence would address it.
- **Answer with new evidence, not repetition.** Resending the same exhibits seldom changes the result.
- **Tighten the endeavor description.** A clearer plan, with milestones and impact, often resolves a prong-one concern.
- **Add independent material.** Citations by researchers who do not know you, adoption by industry, funding awards, letters from experts at independent institutions.
- **Respect the deadline.** USCIS does not extend it, and you must respond in one package.

## When an RFE lawyer matters

If you receive an RFE, an NIW RFE lawyer can read it the way an officer would, tell you whether the concern is fixable, and structure the response around it. Policy guidance on NIW also gets updated, so an attorney who tracks it can help you meet current expectations.

If you are still preparing your filing, learn from the RFE patterns above and address them before you submit. The strongest NIW responses are the ones that were written into the original petition.
