VAWA, the Violence Against Women Act, lets certain abused family members of U.S. citizens and permanent residents apply for immigration status on their own, without the abuser's knowledge or cooperation. Despite the name, it protects people of any gender. For many survivors, a VAWA lawyer is the safest route to build the case while protecting confidentiality.

If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. Legal steps can wait until you are safe.

## Who can file a VAWA self-petition

The self-petition is filed on Form I-360. In general, you may qualify if you are:

- **The spouse** of an abusive U.S. citizen or permanent resident, including in some cases a former spouse if you divorced within a limited time and the divorce was connected to the abuse
- **The child** of an abusive U.S. citizen or permanent resident parent
- **The parent** of an abusive U.S. citizen son or daughter

You must also show that:

- **You were subjected to battery or extreme cruelty.** This can include physical violence, threats, coercion, psychological abuse and controlling behavior. It is not limited to physical injury.
- **You lived with the abuser** at some point.
- **You are a person of good moral character.**
- **For a spouse, the marriage was entered in good faith.**
- **The abuser is or was a U.S. citizen or permanent resident,** with limited exceptions where status was lost because of the abuse.

## Evidence USCIS expects

The case relies on your own sworn statement, and supporting evidence helps. Examples:

- A detailed personal declaration describing the relationship and the abuse
- Police reports, protective orders and court records
- Medical or counseling records
- Statements from friends, family, clergy or others who know of the abuse
- Photographs and messages
- Proof of the relationship and shared residence, such as a lease, bills, joint accounts, children's birth certificates
- Evidence of good moral character, such as a police clearance and statements from the community

USCIS says it will consider any credible evidence. You do not have to provide a specific document, although you must show enough.

## Common RFE issues

USCIS may send a Request for Evidence when:

- The proof of abuse is thin or inconsistent
- The good faith marriage is not well documented
- The shared residence is not shown
- The abuser's status is not proven
- Good moral character questions arise, for example from an arrest

A well-built response uses new evidence and a clear explanation. A lawyer familiar with VAWA can identify what the officer really needs.

## Confidentiality

The law places strong limits on how the government uses information in VAWA cases. Immigration officials generally may not use information from the abuser to deny your case, and may not disclose your information to the abuser. These protections are one reason to file through the proper channels.

## What VAWA can lead to

- **A prima facie determination,** an early finding that your case appears to meet requirements, which may open access to certain benefits
- **Deferred action and a work permit,** when the petition is approved
- **A green card,** once a visa number is available. Spouses and children of U.S. citizens can often proceed quickly. For others, a wait may apply.

## VAWA cancellation of removal

If you are already in removal proceedings, you may be able to seek VAWA cancellation of removal before an immigration judge. It requires showing, among other things, abuse by a qualifying relative, continuous physical presence for a set period, good moral character and extreme hardship. It is a separate process with different requirements from the I-360.
