Trademark registrations are rarely lost in a fight. They are lost when a renewal window closes and nobody noticed, because the USPTO's reminder went to an email address that stopped working two years earlier. IP docketing is the system that prevents that: a calendar of every deadline attached to every asset, each with an owner, a reminder schedule, and the grace period recorded separately from the deadline itself. CLP's [IP docketing and portfolio management](/practice-area/key-services/ip-portfolio-management) team treats the docket as the single most valuable document in a portfolio, because every other document depends on it staying accurate.

## What IP docketing actually is

A docket is more than a list of dates. For each asset it records the deadline, the statutory basis, the grace period and its surcharge, the consequence of missing it, the person responsible, and the reminders that fire before it. It is reviewed on a schedule, updated whenever an asset is added, assigned or abandoned, and kept somewhere that survives staff turnover.

The USPTO sends courtesy email reminders for maintenance filings, but only to the correspondence address on the registration. If that address belongs to a former law firm or an employee who left, the reminder is lost. A docket is the system you control; the reminder is one you do not.

## Trademark deadlines the docket must carry

For pending applications:

- Office action responses. As of this writing, the response period for most trademark office actions is three months, with one three-month extension available for a fee. Missing it abandons the application, and a petition to revive for unintentional delay has its own short deadline after the notice of abandonment.
- Statements of use for intent-to-use applications: due within six months of the notice of allowance, extendable in six-month increments to a maximum of three years from the notice of allowance, each extension with its own fee and deadline.
- Opposition windows on competing applications you are watching: 30 days from publication, extendable.

For registrations:

- Section 8 declaration of use between the fifth and sixth anniversaries, with a six-month grace period; see [when a Section 8 declaration is due](/section-8-declaration-of-use-deadline).
- Section 15 declaration of incontestability, typically with the first Section 8.
- Combined Section 8 and 9 renewal between the ninth and tenth anniversaries, and every ten years after that, each with a six-month grace period.
- Section 71 declarations for Madrid Protocol registrations on their own schedule.
- Post-registration audit responses, which arrive as office actions with their own deadline; our post on [USPTO post-registration audits](/uspto-post-registration-audit-response) covers what they ask for.

Once a maintenance deadline and its grace period pass, the registration is cancelled and cannot be revived. That single fact is why the docket exists. The filings themselves run through our [trademark renewal and maintenance](/practice-area/key-services/trademark-renewal-and-international-registration) practice.

## Foreign, domain and contract deadlines

Madrid Protocol registrations renew every ten years through WIPO, but the five-year dependency on the U.S. basic mark is its own date to watch, since a lapse in the U.S. registration during that period takes the foreign designations down with it. Direct national registrations renew on each country's schedule, and many countries allow cancellation of a mark unused for three or five years after registration, so the docket should carry local use deadlines, not just renewal dates. Paris Convention priority gives six months from a first filing to file abroad with the original date; that window belongs on the docket the day the U.S. application is filed.

Domain names expire on the registrar's schedule and can be snapped up within days of lapsing. License agreements have terms, renewal options, notice periods for non-renewal and royalty reporting dates. Coexistence agreements sometimes contain review dates or territorial milestones. All of it goes on the same calendar.

## Copyright and trade secret entries

Copyrights for works created today do not need renewal, but timing still matters. Registering within three months of first publication, or before an infringement begins, preserves the right to statutory damages and attorney's fees for that infringement, so the docket should flag publication dates for important works and prompt a registration decision. Trade secrets have no filing at all; the docket entry is a periodic review of the protective measures, the nondisclosure agreements and their expiration dates, and exit procedures when an employee with access leaves.

## How to set up a docket that survives turnover

Assign a single owner and a named backup inside the company, and have outside counsel maintain a parallel docket so that two independent calendars carry every date. Make the law firm the correspondence address on USPTO records, so that official mail and courtesy reminders reach someone whose job is to act on them. Record the grace period as a second entry with a different reminder, never as the deadline. Set reminders at ninety, sixty and thirty days, because gathering specimens and confirming use across a goods list takes longer than signing a form.

Review the entire docket once a year against the actual business: products discontinued, logos redesigned, entities merged, marks assigned. An annual review of this kind is the core of the audit we describe in [how to conduct an IP audit](/how-to-conduct-ip-audit). And teach whoever opens the mail to recognize the misleading private solicitations that mimic USPTO invoices; paying one of those does nothing for the registration and often means the real deadline is being ignored.

## What a missed deadline costs

An abandoned application can sometimes be revived if the delay was unintentional and the petition is filed promptly after the notice of abandonment. A cancelled registration cannot. The only path is a new application with a new filing date, and any third party that filed a similar mark in the meantime now has priority over you. The company's registration becomes a common-law claim limited to its area of actual use, and any incontestable status is gone. Compared with that, the cost of maintaining a docket is close to zero.
