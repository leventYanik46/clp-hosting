Refugees and asylum seekers must meet the same legal standard: a well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group. The difference is where you are when you ask for protection. Refugees apply from outside the U.S. Asylum seekers apply from inside the U.S. or at a port of entry.

## Refugee status: applying from abroad

The U.S. Refugee Admissions Program processes people who are outside their home country and outside the U.S. Most applicants are referred by the United Nations refugee agency, a U.S. embassy, or certain designated organizations. Individuals generally cannot simply apply on their own.

The process includes:

- Referral to the program
- Interviews with USCIS officers abroad
- Security and medical screening
- Approval and travel arranged to the U.S.

Refugee admissions are capped each year by presidential determination. Since 2025, the program has been sharply restricted, with admissions suspended for most nationalities and a very low ceiling. Anyone considering this path should check the current status of the program.

## Asylum: applying from inside the U.S.

Asylum is requested inside the U.S. or at the border. There are two main ways:

- **Affirmative asylum:** You file Form I-589 with USCIS if you are not in removal proceedings. An asylum officer interviews you.
- **Defensive asylum:** You request asylum before an immigration judge as a defense in removal proceedings.

Key rule: you generally must file within one year of your last arrival in the U.S., unless an exception applies for changed or extraordinary circumstances.

## Similarities

- The same persecution standard and protected grounds
- Similar bars, such as persecution of others, serious crimes, and firm resettlement in another country
- Both allow you to bring a spouse and unmarried children under 21, through derivative status or a follow-to-join petition
- Both lead to permanent residence

## Key differences

**Location.** Refugee: outside the U.S. Asylum: inside or at the border.

**Who decides.** Refugee: USCIS officers abroad. Asylum: USCIS asylum officers or immigration judges.

**Arrival.** Refugees arrive with status already granted and with resettlement support. Asylum seekers may wait years for a decision while living in the U.S.

**Work authorization.** Refugees can work right away. Asylum applicants must generally wait for a pending case to reach a set number of days before applying for a work permit.

**Deadlines.** Asylum has the one-year filing deadline. Refugee processing does not.

## Path to a green card

- **Refugees** must apply for a green card on Form I-485 one year after admission.
- **Asylees** can apply one year after asylum is granted.

Refugee green cards are generally backdated to the date of arrival, which can speed eligibility for citizenship. Asylee green cards are backdated by one year.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can a refugee or asylee travel abroad?

Yes, with a refugee travel document from USCIS. Returning to the country you fled can put your status at risk.

### Can I apply for asylum if I entered without a visa?

Yes. Entry without a visa does not bar asylum, though the one-year deadline and other rules still apply. Recent policies may affect eligibility for some recent border arrivals, so get legal advice.

### Can a refugee bring family later?

Yes. A refugee or asylee can file Form I-730 within two years of admission or grant to bring a spouse and unmarried children under 21 who were not included originally.
