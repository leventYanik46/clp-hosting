If you're married to a U.S. citizen, you may be able to apply for citizenship after three years as a green card holder instead of five. People often ask, "Can I apply for citizenship before 3 years of marriage?" The answer depends on how long you've been married, how long you've had your green card, and whether your spouse has been a citizen the whole time.

## The 3-year rule

You can apply for naturalization under the 3-year rule if you:

1. Have been a green card holder for at least three years
2. Have been married to and living with the same U.S. citizen spouse for those three years
3. Your spouse has been a U.S. citizen for those three years
4. Meet physical presence (18 months in three years) and continuous residence requirements
5. Have lived for three months in the state or USCIS district where you file
6. Meet the English, civics, and good moral character requirements

## Living in marital union

You must have been living in marital union with your citizen spouse. If you're legally separated, or living apart, you may not qualify under the 3-year rule. Temporary separations for work or school may not be a problem, but each case is evaluated individually.

## Can I apply for citizenship before 3 years of marriage?

Generally, no under the 3-year rule. If you got your green card through your marriage and haven't been married for three years, you'll need to wait or use the 5-year rule.

But if you've been a green card holder for five years (for example, through employment), you can apply under the 5-year rule, regardless of how long you've been married.

## The 90-day early filing window

You can file your N-400 up to 90 days before you reach the three-year mark. Use the USCIS early filing calculator to find the first date you can file. Filing too early can lead to a denial.

## Conditional green cards

If you got a two-year conditional green card through marriage, the time on the conditional card counts toward the three years. But you must have removed conditions (or have a pending I-751) before or while applying. USCIS may decide your I-751 and N-400 together.

## The cost to apply for citizenship

USCIS charges a filing fee for Form N-400. Under the fee schedule that took effect in April 2024, online filing is slightly cheaper than paper filing, and a reduced fee is available for applicants with household income at or below 400% of the federal poverty guidelines. Fee waivers are available for some applicants. Always check the current USCIS fee schedule before filing.

## Common problems

- **Spouse not a citizen for three years:** If your spouse naturalized less than three years ago, you can't use the 3-year rule until they've been a citizen for three years.
- **Divorce or separation:** You lose eligibility under the 3-year rule if the marriage ends before you naturalize.
- **Long trips:** Physical presence and continuous residence still apply.

## What to bring to the interview

- Your marriage certificate
- Proof your spouse has been a citizen for three years
- Evidence of your marital union, such as joint tax returns, a joint lease or mortgage, and bank statements

## Practical example

A woman got her conditional green card through marriage in March 2023, removed conditions in 2025, and has lived with her U.S. citizen husband the whole time. Her three-year anniversary as a permanent resident is March 2026, so she can file her N-400 up to 90 days earlier, in December 2025, as long as she also meets physical presence and residence requirements. If her husband had only naturalized in 2024, she couldn't use the three-year rule until his own citizenship reached three years, and she would compare that date with her five-year date to see which comes first.

## Spouses of U.S. citizens working abroad

A separate rule allows some spouses of U.S. citizens employed abroad by the U.S. government or certain U.S. companies and organizations to naturalize without meeting the usual residence requirements. This is narrower than the three-year rule and has its own conditions.
