The K-1 petition and the consular interview are both about one question: is this a genuine relationship that will lead to marriage? Strong K-1 visa evidence is specific, spread across time and shows that the two of you have met and know each other. The interview then tests whether the story on paper matches what each of you says.

## The two things the evidence must prove

1. **You have met in person** within the two years before filing, unless a waiver applies.
2. **You have a genuine, ongoing relationship** and both intend to marry within 90 days of arrival.

Evidence of the relationship should be easy to find in ordinary records. The aim is not to build a perfect album but to show a pattern.

## Proof that you met in person

- Airline tickets, boarding passes and itineraries
- Passport stamps showing entry and exit dates
- Hotel or rental receipts
- Photos from the visit with dates or context
- Messages that mention the trip

Gather these now, since travel records are easy to lose.

## Proof of a genuine relationship

- Photos taken on different occasions, with others where possible
- Chat logs, call records and emails over time, showing regular contact
- Letters and cards
- Evidence of introductions to family and friends
- Money transfers or gifts, where relevant
- Wedding plans, such as a venue booking, invitations or a dress purchase
- Statements from friends or relatives who know of the relationship

**A note on quantity:** Selecting representative examples works better than submitting every message. Officers see many cases, and clear, organized evidence persuades faster than a pile of screenshots.

## Preparing for the K-1 interview

The fiancé(e) attends an interview at the U.S. consulate. Plan for these basics:

- Passport and the appointment letter
- DS-160 confirmation page
- Medical exam results in the sealed envelope, if the consulate requires it
- Photographs meeting the requirements
- Civil documents and police certificates
- The approved petition and supporting evidence
- Evidence of the relationship, updated since filing

## Typical interview questions

Questions vary, but common themes are:

- How and when did you meet?
- How often did you visit, and where?
- What does your fiancé(e) do for work?
- What is their family like, and have you met them?
- What are your wedding plans, and who will attend?
- Where will you live in the United States?
- How do you communicate, and in what language?
- What do you know about each other's daily life?

Answers should be natural and consistent with the paperwork. Small differences in recall are normal. Contradictions on central facts are what raise doubts.

## Common issues

- Little or no proof of an in-person meeting
- Relationship that is hard to explain, such as a large age gap or a short courtship
- Language barriers between the couple
- A prior K-1 petition or prior marriage
- Evidence that looks assembled for the case only
