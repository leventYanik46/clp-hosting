When U.S. citizens adopt a child from another country, the child usually immigrates on an **IR-3** or **IR-4** immigrant visa, or the Hague equivalents, **IH-3** and **IH-4**. The difference is whether the adoption is full and final before the child comes to the U.S. That difference determines when your child becomes a U.S. citizen.

## IR-3 and IH-3: adoption completed abroad

An IR-3 or IH-3 visa is issued when:

- The adoption was finalized in the child's country, and
- At least one adoptive parent saw the child in person before or during the adoption proceedings.

Under the Child Citizenship Act of 2000, a child who enters the U.S. as a permanent resident on an IR-3 or IH-3 visa, lives in the legal and physical custody of a U.S. citizen parent, and is under 18 generally becomes a U.S. citizen automatically upon admission. USCIS mails a Certificate of Citizenship in many of these cases.

## IR-4 and IH-4: adoption to be finalized in the U.S.

An IR-4 or IH-4 visa is issued when:

- The child's country grants legal custody or guardianship for emigration and adoption in the U.S., or
- The adoption was completed abroad but neither parent saw the child beforehand.

The child enters as a permanent resident, not a citizen. The child becomes a U.S. citizen once the adoption is finalized, or re-adopted, in a U.S. state court while the child is under 18 and in the parents' custody. Parents should then apply for a Certificate of Citizenship with Form N-600 to document it.

If the adoption is never finalized in the U.S., the child remains a permanent resident and does not automatically become a citizen. That can create serious problems later, especially if the child reaches adulthood without anyone noticing.

## Hague vs. orphan process

**Hague Adoption Convention process.** Used when the child's country is party to the Hague Adoption Convention. Parents first file Form I-800A for suitability, then Form I-800 for the specific child. Hague cases lead to IH-3 or IH-4 visas.

**Orphan process.** Used for non-Hague countries. Parents file Form I-600A for advance processing and Form I-600 for the child, who must meet the immigration definition of an orphan. These cases lead to IR-3 or IR-4 visas.

**Family-based route.** A child adopted before 16 who has lived in the legal custody of and with the adoptive parent for at least two years may be sponsored through Form I-130. This is used for relative adoptions and some other situations.

## Re-adoption in the U.S.

Even when an adoption is final abroad, many families re-adopt or register the foreign adoption in their state. It gives the child a U.S. birth certificate in many states and simplifies proof of the adoption. For IR-4 and IH-4 children, U.S. finalization is what triggers citizenship.

## Common problems

- An IR-4 child whose adoption was never finalized in the U.S.
- Children who turned 18 before finalization, losing automatic citizenship.
- Mixing the Hague and orphan processes for the wrong country.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my IR-3 child need an N-600?
Not to become a citizen, but USCIS often issues a Certificate of Citizenship automatically. If it does not, filing Form N-600 documents citizenship.

### Can my child get a U.S. passport?
Yes, once citizenship is established. A Certificate of Citizenship or the required adoption and immigration documents support the passport application.

### What if my child is already over 18?
Automatic citizenship under the Child Citizenship Act does not apply after 18. The child would need to naturalize.

### Is re-adoption required in every state?
Not every state requires it, but for IR-4 and IH-4 children, U.S. finalization is what triggers citizenship.

### Can a single parent use these visas?
Yes. Single U.S. citizens can adopt and petition, subject to the same requirements.

### Does a relative adoption use the Hague process?
If the child lives in a Hague country, the Hague process may still apply even for relative adoptions. Get advice before starting.

### What is the difference between IR and IH visas?
IR-3 and IR-4 are used for adoptions through the orphan process; IH-3 and IH-4 are the equivalents for Hague Convention adoptions.

### Can the child come to the U.S. before the adoption is complete?
Only with the appropriate visa. Bringing a child on a visitor visa to complete an adoption can cause serious immigration problems.

### Do adopted children need a medical exam?
Yes. Immigrant visa applicants, including adopted children, complete a medical examination with a panel physician, with some vaccination exemptions for young adopted children.
