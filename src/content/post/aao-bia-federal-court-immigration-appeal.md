When an immigration case is denied, the first question is not whether to appeal but where. The answer depends on which agency made the decision and what kind of case it was. Some denials go to USCIS's Administrative Appeals Office, others to the Board of Immigration Appeals, others directly to federal court, and some cannot be appealed at all. Deadlines are short, often 30 days, and filing in the wrong place can waste them.

## Administrative Appeals Office (AAO)

The AAO reviews many USCIS decisions, including denials of:

- Most employment-based immigrant petitions on Form I-140.
- Many nonimmigrant worker petitions on Form I-129, such as H-1B, L-1, and O-1.
- Certain waivers, including many I-601 and I-212 denials.
- Some humanitarian cases, including T and U visa applications.

**How to file:** Form I-290B, Notice of Appeal or Motion, filed with USCIS. The deadline is generally 30 days from the decision, or 33 days if the decision was mailed. The same form is used for motions to reopen or reconsider, which go back to the office that denied the case.

## Board of Immigration Appeals (BIA)

The BIA, part of the Department of Justice, reviews:

- **Immigration judge decisions** in removal proceedings, including denials of asylum, cancellation of removal, and adjustment of status. File Form EOIR-26 within 30 days of the judge's decision.
- **Family-based petition denials** on Form I-130 by USCIS. File Form EOIR-29 with USCIS within the stated deadline.
- **Some bond decisions** by immigration judges.

## Federal courts

**Courts of appeals.** Final BIA removal orders are reviewed by the federal court of appeals for the region where the immigration judge sat, through a petition for review filed within 30 days of the BIA decision. Filing does not automatically stop removal; a separate stay motion is usually needed.

**District courts.** Some USCIS denials can be challenged in federal district court under the Administrative Procedure Act, especially when no administrative appeal is required. District courts also hear mandamus suits for unreasonable delay, habeas petitions for detention, and certain naturalization denials after an administrative hearing.

## Decisions that cannot be appealed

- **Most adjustment of status denials by USCIS** cannot be appealed. If the person is placed in removal proceedings, they can renew the application before an immigration judge.
- **I-751 waiver denials** are reviewed by an immigration judge in removal proceedings, not by appeal.
- **Consular visa refusals** generally cannot be appealed in court, though some can be addressed through reapplication or advisory review.

## Appeal, motion, or new filing?

Sometimes the better move is not an appeal:

- A **motion to reconsider** argues the decision was legally wrong based on the existing record.
- A **motion to reopen** presents new facts.
- A **new filing** with stronger evidence can be faster than an appeal.

## What an immigration appeals lawyer reviews

- Which body has jurisdiction.
- The exact deadline from the date on the decision.
- Whether the record supports a legal error or needs new evidence.
- Whether removal or loss of status needs to be stopped while the appeal is pending.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file an appeal and a motion at the same time?
Form I-290B lets you choose an appeal, a motion, or both in some cases. Strategy depends on the record.

### Does an appeal stop my removal?
Not always. A timely BIA appeal of an immigration judge's decision generally stays removal, but federal court petitions require a separate stay request.

### How long do AAO appeals take?
Processing times vary and can be long. In many cases, refiling with stronger evidence is faster.

### Can I submit new evidence on appeal?
The AAO can accept new evidence in some situations. The BIA generally does not consider new evidence on appeal; a motion to remand or reopen is used instead.

### What is a petition for review?
A request to a federal court of appeals to review a final BIA removal order, filed within 30 days.

### Can I appeal a denied naturalization application?
Yes. File Form N-336 for an administrative hearing with USCIS, then seek review in federal district court if denied again.

### What is the deadline to appeal to the BIA?
Generally 30 days from the immigration judge's decision for Form EOIR-26.

### Can I appeal a denied H-1B?
Yes, to the AAO on Form I-290B, or through a motion. Many employers instead refile with additional evidence.

### Do I need a lawyer to appeal?
Not legally, but appeals are technical and deadlines are strict.
