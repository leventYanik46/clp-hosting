Talent visa is not a legal term. It is shorthand for a group of U.S. immigration categories that reward achievement instead of a job offer or a labor market test: the EB-1A and EB-1B green cards, the EB-2 National Interest Waiver, and the O-1A and O-1B temporary visas. Which talent visa fits you depends less on your job title than on the shape of your record, who could petition for you, and whether you need a green card now or a work visa first. Our [overview of U.S. talent and extraordinary ability visas](/practice-area/key-services/talent-and-extraordinary-ability-visas) sets out each category; this post approaches the same question by profession.

## Researchers and academics

A postdoc or faculty member usually has publications, citations and peer review service, which satisfy two of the six EB-1B criteria and often three of the ten EB-1A criteria. The deciding factors are the employer and the strength of the citation record. If a university or qualifying research employer will offer a permanent position, the EB-1B is the lower bar and the employer files it. If no permanent offer exists, or you want a petition that is yours, the EB-1A (for a heavily cited, internationally recognized record) or the NIW (for a solid record tied to an endeavor of national importance) are self-petitions. Many researchers on J-1 or H-1B status hold an O-1A as a bridge while the record matures. See our post on [whether you qualify for the EB-1A](/eb-1a-visa-do-you-qualify) for the criteria.

## Startup founders and executives

Founders rarely have awards or scholarly articles, but they often have press, funding, a critical role in a company with a distinguished reputation, and high remuneration. For the temporary stage, an O-1A sponsored by the founder's own company is possible if the company is a separate legal entity that can act as employer; our post on [founders and the O-1 visa](/can-startup-founder-get-o-1-visa) covers that structure. For the green card, the NIW is the usual first choice because job creation, investment and work in a priority industry support national importance under Matter of Dhanasar, and the EB-1A becomes realistic once the company and the founder have measurable recognition. Executives of multinational companies who managed a function abroad for at least one year in the preceding three may instead fit the EB-1C, which tests corporate structure rather than acclaim.

## Physicians and clinicians

Clinicians divide into two groups. Physician-scientists with research output fit the researcher pattern above. Practicing physicians without a research record generally look to the NIW, where clinical work in an underserved area or a specialty shortage can establish national importance, or, for those bound by a J-1 home residence requirement, to a waiver first. The O-1A is available to clinicians with national recognition, but a medical license and the practical ability to practice in the state of employment are separate requirements that have to be addressed in parallel.

## Artists, performers and athletes

The arts have their own temporary category. The [O-1B visa](/practice-area/key-services/o-1b-visa) requires distinction in the arts, shown by a significant award or three of six arts-specific criteria such as lead or starring roles, critical reviews and commercial or critically acclaimed success, with a separate and higher standard of extraordinary achievement for motion picture and television work. An agent can petition when an artist works for multiple employers, which matters for touring musicians and freelance designers. For the green card, artists use the EB-1A, where the ten criteria include display of work at exhibitions and commercial success in the performing arts, and where comparable evidence is allowed when a criterion does not fit the discipline.

Athletes most often begin with the P-1A for internationally recognized athletes and teams, or with the O-1A where the record shows extraordinary ability, and move to the EB-1A once the record includes major awards, rankings and press. Coaches can qualify under the EB-1A on the strength of their athletic careers if they will continue in the same field, and comparable evidence often helps where criteria written for scientists do not fit sport.

## Engineers, data scientists and other professionals

Industry professionals without publications usually have patents, products in commercial use, critical roles and high salaries. Those can satisfy EB-1A criteria, but the final merits stage is demanding for anyone without a public profile. The [EB-2 National Interest Waiver](/practice-area/key-services/eb-2-niw-green-card) is often the better fit, especially for work in fields the government has identified as critical, and an O-1A is available where an employer will petition and the record shows distinction. For professionals already on an O-1, our post on [moving from an O-1 to a green card](/o-1-visa-to-green-card) describes the next step.

## What every talent visa profile has in common

Three questions decide the route in every case. Is there a petitioner, or must you self-petition? Does the record show acclaim today, or does it need time? And is your country of birth current in EB-1 and EB-2 under the Visa Bulletin? Answer those three and the category usually picks itself. The fourth question, which category to file first when more than one fits, is the one worth an hour with counsel.
