The I-601A provisional unlawful presence waiver lets certain people ask USCIS to forgive unlawful presence before they leave the United States for a consular interview. Without it, a person who must leave to get an immigrant visa may trigger a three-year or ten-year bar, and would have to wait abroad for a decision. The provisional waiver reduces that risk, but only for people who fit its narrow rules.

## The problem it solves

Many people who are in the United States without status cannot adjust their status here and must apply for an immigrant visa at a U.S. consulate. The moment they leave, the unlawful presence bars can start. A person with more than 180 days but less than a year of unlawful presence who departs generally faces a three-year bar. A person with a year or more faces a ten-year bar. The I-601A allows an applicant to receive a decision on the waiver before departing, so they know whether the bar will be forgiven.

## Who may apply

In general, you must:

- Be physically present in the United States
- Be the beneficiary of an approved immigrant visa petition, or a selected diversity visa applicant, or be an eligible applicant in another category
- Be inadmissible only because of unlawful presence, and not because of another ground
- Have a U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse or parent who would suffer extreme hardship if you were refused
- Be at least 17 years old
- Not be in removal proceedings, unless proceedings have been administratively closed and not recalendared

The hardship must be to the qualifying relative, not to you or to your children. That is a common surprise.

## What counts as extreme hardship

Extreme hardship is more than the usual consequences of separation. USCIS considers all factors together, and common ones include:

- Medical conditions of the qualifying relative and the availability of care
- Financial effects, such as loss of income or inability to pay a mortgage
- Family ties in the United States and in the other country
- Conditions in the country of relocation
- Length of residence and community ties
- Mental health effects of separation or relocation

Evidence is critical. Medical records, evaluations by qualified professionals, financial documents, tax records, and sworn statements all play a role. Claims without proof are weak.

## What the waiver does and does not do

The approved I-601A does not give you a green card or a visa. It indicates that, if you are otherwise eligible, the unlawful presence bar will be waived after you attend your consular interview. If the consular officer finds another ground of inadmissibility, the provisional waiver may be revoked, and you may need a different waiver. It also does not cover other grounds such as fraud, criminal convictions or certain immigration violations.

## Fees and processing

USCIS publishes the current filing fee for Form I-601A and the current processing times, and both change from time to time. Check the official pages before you file. Also expect separate fees for the immigrant visa process.

## Common pitfalls

- Claiming hardship to the wrong person
- Not including documents to show the hardship
- Other inadmissibility grounds that make the waiver unusable
- Leaving the country before the waiver decision
- Filing before the underlying petition is approved
