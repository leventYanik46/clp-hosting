Form I-130 and Form I-485 are both part of most family-based green card cases, but they do different jobs. The I-130 is the petition that proves a qualifying family relationship. The I-485 is the application to adjust status to permanent resident from inside the U.S. Understanding how they fit together helps you pick the right filing strategy and set realistic expectations about timing.

## What the I-130 does

The I-130, Petition for Alien Relative, is filed by a U.S. citizen or green card holder for a relative. It asks USCIS to recognize the relationship. An approved I-130 does not give the relative any status by itself. It establishes eligibility and, for preference categories, a place in line through the priority date.

## What the I-130A is

When a U.S. citizen or permanent resident petitions for a spouse, the spouse beneficiary fills out Form I-130A, Supplemental Information for Spouse Beneficiary. It collects the beneficiary's address and employment history and parents' information. It is filed with the I-130, not separately.

## What the I-485 does

The I-485, Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status, is filed by the relative who is in the U.S. It asks for the green card itself. USCIS reviews admissibility, background checks, the medical exam, and the sponsor's financial support.

## Filing them together (concurrent filing)

If the relative is an immediate relative of a U.S. citizen, meaning a spouse, parent of an adult citizen, or unmarried child under 21, a visa is always available. If the relative is in the U.S. and was inspected and admitted or paroled, the I-130 and I-485 can usually be filed together. This is the most common path for marriage green cards inside the U.S.

For preference categories, such as spouses of green card holders or siblings of citizens, the I-485 can only be filed once the priority date is current in the visa bulletin.

## I-130 fees

USCIS charges a filing fee for the I-130, with a slightly lower fee for online filing. The I-485 has its own fee. The Form I-693 medical exam and the I-864 affidavit of support carry their own costs. Check the current USCIS fee schedule before filing.

## I-130 approved: what next?

It depends on where the relative is and the category:

**The relative is in the U.S. and an I-485 is already pending.** The case continues toward biometrics, possible interview, and a decision on the green card. "I-130 approved but I-485 pending" is common and usually just means the second form takes longer.

**The relative is in the U.S. and no I-485 was filed.** If a visa is available and the relative is eligible to adjust, file the I-485 now.

**The relative is abroad.** USCIS sends the approved I-130 to the National Visa Center. The NVC collects fees, the DS-260, the affidavit of support, and civil documents, then schedules a consular interview.

**Preference category with a wait.** The approved petition holds the priority date. Monitor the visa bulletin until the date is current, then move to adjustment or consular processing.

## What is the next step after I-130 approval for a spouse?

For a spouse of a U.S. citizen abroad, the next step is the NVC stage, then a consular interview, then entry on an immigrant visa, after which the green card is mailed. For a spouse in the U.S. with a pending I-485, the next step is usually the interview.

## Common mistakes

- Filing an I-485 for a preference relative before the priority date is current
- Assuming I-130 approval allows the relative to work or travel
- Leaving the U.S. while an I-485 is pending without advance parole
- Missing the I-130A for a spouse


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I work while the I-485 is pending?

Yes, if you file Form I-765 and USCIS approves it. Until then, you cannot work based on the pending green card case.

### Does the I-130 alone protect me from removal?

No. A pending or approved I-130 does not give status. If you are in removal proceedings, raise it with the judge and your attorney.
