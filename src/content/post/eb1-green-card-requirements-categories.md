EB-1 is the first-preference employment green card, and its requirements depend entirely on which of the three subcategories you use. EB-1A is for people with extraordinary ability, EB-1B is for outstanding professors and researchers, and EB-1C is for multinational managers and executives. They share a name and a visa category, but little else.

## Why EB-1 attracts so much interest

EB-1 does not require the labor certification (PERM) that most EB-2 and EB-3 cases need, in any of its three subcategories. Skipping that step can save significant time. Visa availability for EB-1 is also often better than for EB-2 and EB-3, though backlogs still exist for some countries.

## EB-1A: extraordinary ability

You may file for yourself, with no employer or job offer. You must show sustained national or international acclaim, either through a one-time major award or by meeting at least three of ten criteria, followed by an overall merits review. You also have to show that you intend to continue working in your area of expertise in the United States.

Common examples include scientists, researchers, athletes, artists, and business leaders who have a strong external record. The standard is demanding.

## EB-1B: outstanding professors and researchers

This category requires a job offer from a university, an institution of higher education, or a private employer with a research department that meets requirements. The employer files the petition. You need international recognition as outstanding in your academic field, supported by at least two of a list of criteria, and at least three years of teaching or research experience in the field.

It suits academics and some corporate researchers whose employer is willing to sponsor them.

## EB-1C: multinational managers and executives

EB-1C is for people who have been employed outside the United States by a company related to the U.S. employer (a parent, subsidiary, branch or affiliate). You must have worked for the company abroad for at least one year in the three years before you came to the U.S. as a nonimmigrant, in a managerial or executive role, and you must be coming to work in a managerial or executive position in the U.S. The U.S. entity must have been doing business for at least a year.

This is employer-sponsored. It is often a natural next step for someone in L-1A status.

## How to choose among them

1. **Do you have an employer ready to sponsor?** If not, only EB-1A is available as a self-petition.
2. **What is your role?** Academics fit EB-1B. People who have managed or run a company in a related entity abroad fit EB-1C. Everyone else with a record of acclaim looks at EB-1A.
3. **How strong is your documented record?** A thin record in EB-1A is more likely to draw an RFE than a clean EB-1B or EB-1C filing.
4. **What is the backup?** If EB-1 does not fit, EB-2 with a national interest waiver or standard PERM routes may be available.

## When to talk to a lawyer

Because the three categories use different evidence, many people choose the wrong one at first. An EB-1 visa lawyer can match your history to the category, point out gaps early, and prepare a backup plan if needed. A consultation before you start collecting documents usually saves effort.
