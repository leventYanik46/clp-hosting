An RFE, short for Request for Evidence, is a notice from USCIS saying it cannot approve your case yet because something is missing or unclear. It is not a denial. It is also not a formality, and a weak response is one of the most common ways a good case ends in a denial.

## What does RFE mean in immigration?

When an officer reviews a petition or application and finds a gap, they can either deny the case or ask for more. The notice, usually titled Request for Evidence, lists what the officer found lacking and tells you what kind of documents might fix it. Sometimes the problem is plain missing paperwork. Sometimes it is that the officer is not persuaded by what you already sent.

An H-1B RFE, for example, often questions whether the job is a specialty occupation, whether the employer has real work for the beneficiary, or whether a worksite at a client is documented. Other forms generate their own recurring RFEs: a marriage-based filing may be questioned on the evidence of a real relationship, and an I-485 may be missing an item the officer expected to see.

## Is getting an RFE a good sign?

Neither good nor bad on its own. An RFE tells you the officer is not ready to approve based on the file as it stands, and it gives you a chance to fix that. Many RFEs are answered successfully. But an RFE often points to a real weakness in the case, so treat it seriously.

## The response deadline

The notice states a response deadline, and USCIS does not extend it. You can send in everything at once or tell USCIS in writing that you are submitting nothing, but you cannot respond in installments. If you miss the deadline, USCIS can deny the case for abandonment.

Read the date as soon as the notice arrives. Because the notice is mailed, build in time for collecting documents and for delivery.

## How to build a response that works

1. **Read the notice line by line.** Identify the exact issue the officer raised, not the general topic.
2. **Answer the question that was asked.** If the officer doubts that the job requires a degree, a thick stack of unrelated evidence does not help. A focused argument with targeted exhibits does.
3. **Use the officer's categories.** If the notice lists examples of acceptable evidence, address each one or explain why it does not apply.
4. **Put the strongest points first** and include a cover letter that maps each exhibit to the issue it answers.
5. **Include the original notice** on top of the package, as the notice instructs.

## What to avoid

- Sending the same documents again with no new explanation.
- Letting the employer or applicant write a response with no legal analysis.
- Waiting until the last few days to start collecting evidence.
- Ignoring a point in the notice because it seems minor.

## When to get a lawyer involved

Some RFEs are straightforward and one missing form can be sent in. Many are not. If the notice challenges eligibility itself, an attorney who has seen similar RFEs can recognize what the officer is really worried about and structure the response to meet it. If you have received an RFE and the deadline is close, speak to an attorney before you send anything.
