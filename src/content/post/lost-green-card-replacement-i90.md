Losing your green card does not end your permanent resident status. Your status and the card are two different things. But you still need to replace the card, because you need it to prove your status for work, travel, and identification. Replacement is done with Form I-90, and while it is pending you can get temporary proof of status.

## First steps after losing your green card

- **If it was stolen, file a police report.** A copy helps document the loss and protects you if someone misuses the card.
- **Check your records.** Find a copy or photo of the card, your A-number, and your USCIS online account login.
- **If you are outside the U.S.**, do not file an I-90. See the travel section below.

## Replacing a lost green card with Form I-90

Form I-90, Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card, covers lost, stolen, damaged, and expiring cards. You can file it online through a USCIS account or by mail.

### The filing fee

USCIS charges a filing fee for Form I-90, which is lower for online filing than for paper filing under the current fee schedule. Check the USCIS fee page before filing. No fee is charged when the card was never received or contained an error caused by USCIS.

### Biometrics

USCIS usually schedules a biometrics appointment to collect your photo, signature, and fingerprints. Some applicants have prior biometrics reused.

## How long it takes to replace a green card

I-90 processing times vary and can run for many months. USCIS posts current times online. Do not plan international travel around a quick replacement.

## Proving status while the I-90 is pending

**I-90 receipt notice.** For renewals, USCIS issues receipt notices that extend the validity of the existing card for a set period. When the card is lost, you have no card to pair it with, so the receipt alone may not satisfy an employer or other agency.

**Temporary I-551 stamp.** You can request an in-person appointment at a USCIS field office through the USCIS Contact Center to obtain an Alien Documentation, Identification and Telecommunication (ADIT) stamp, also called an I-551 stamp, in your passport. It serves as temporary proof of permanent residence for work and travel.

**Employers.** For Form I-9, a receipt for a replacement of a lost, stolen, or damaged green card is an acceptable receipt for a limited period. Ask HR to check the current Form I-9 rules.

## If you lose your green card abroad

If your card is lost or stolen while you are outside the U.S., you generally need **Form I-131A**, Application for Travel Document (Carrier Documentation), filed with the State Department or USCIS abroad. It allows you to board a flight and return. Once back in the U.S., file the I-90 to replace the card.

If you have been abroad for a long time, the consulate may also look at whether you abandoned residence.

## Avoiding problems in the future

- Keep a copy of both sides of your card in a secure place.
- Carry your card when traveling internationally and when required by law.
- Consider applying for naturalization if you are eligible. A U.S. passport and naturalization certificate end the cycle of card replacements.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I work without my green card while the I-90 is pending?
Yes. You remain authorized to work as a permanent resident. You may need an acceptable receipt or I-551 stamp to show your employer.

### Do I need my old card number to file?
It helps, but USCIS can find your record with your A-number and other information.

### Can I get a driver's license without my card?
State requirements vary. An I-551 stamp in your passport is often accepted.

### Should I apply for citizenship instead?
If eligible, naturalization ends the need for future cards. You can apply for citizenship even with a lost card, but you may still need proof of status in the meantime.

### What if someone used my stolen card?
Report the theft to police and keep the report. If you discover misuse, report it to the relevant agency and consider identity theft protections.

### Can I replace a damaged card the same way?
Yes. Form I-90 also covers damaged cards. Submit the damaged card if you still have it.

### Can I file Form I-90 if I am about to naturalize?
If your N-400 interview is close, you may not need a replacement card, but you still need proof of status for work and travel in the meantime. Ask before filing both.
