The strongest I-751 filings are built around evidence of what happened in the years since you got your conditional card. USCIS is asking whether the marriage was real when you married and whether it has continued as a real marriage. Here is a checklist of what tends to help, how the timeline works, and what changes if you file a waiver.

## What the I-751 evidence should show

You are not starting again from zero. USCIS already has your first green card file. The new evidence should fill in the period since, and show an ongoing marriage. Aim for documents from every year of the conditional period and from several areas of life.

## Evidence checklist

**Shared finances**
- Joint federal tax returns for each year since your conditional card was issued, or transcripts
- Joint bank and credit card statements, spread across the period
- Loan or mortgage documents with both names

**Shared home**
- A lease, mortgage or deed with both names
- Utility bills, internet, phone and other mail at the same address
- Renter's or homeowner's insurance covering both

**Insurance and benefits**
- Health, life or auto insurance listing each other
- Beneficiary designations

**Family and life together**
- Birth certificates of children, with school or medical records
- Photos spanning the period, with family, friends and events
- Travel records, tickets and bookings
- Communications that show a continuing relationship

**Affidavits**
- Sworn statements from people who know you as a couple, with specific details and ID copies

**Standard forms and documents**
- A copy of your conditional green card, front and back
- Form I-751 signed by both spouses for a joint filing
- A copy of your marriage certificate
- The filing fee, as listed on the current USCIS page
- Evidence of name or address changes

Do not send originals unless asked. Make a copy of the full package for yourself.

## I-751 processing time

USCIS posts processing times for the I-751 on its website, and they change as workload changes. A few things to know:

- The case may take months to decide. Plan accordingly rather than counting on a quick decision.
- After you file, USCIS issues a receipt notice. This notice can extend your status for a period stated on it while the case is pending, and it can serve as evidence of your continued status when paired with your expired card. Keep it safe, and check the specific rules for travel and employment verification.
- USCIS may schedule biometrics, request additional evidence, or call you for an interview.

Check your processing time against the current information, not a forum post.

## If you need a waiver

Waivers have different evidence requirements.

- **Divorce or annulment.** Show that the marriage was real from the start, with the same kinds of evidence, and include the divorce decree.
- **Abuse or extreme cruelty.** Evidence can include police reports, protective orders, medical records and statements from professionals or others who know of the abuse.
- **Extreme hardship.** Evidence should show hardship that goes beyond the normal consequences of having to leave, such as medical conditions, country conditions and ties.
- **Death of a spouse.** A death certificate and evidence of the marriage.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Filing evidence from only one year
- Sending photos with no dates or context
- Missing signatures
- Using an address that does not match USCIS records
