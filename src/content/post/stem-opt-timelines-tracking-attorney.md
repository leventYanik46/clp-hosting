STEM OPT is a 24-month extension of work authorization for F-1 students who finish a degree in a designated science, technology, engineering or math field. It is a valuable bridge to an H-1B, but the program runs on deadlines and reporting duties, and missing one can cost you your work authorization. A simple tracker, started early, prevents most problems.

## Who qualifies

You must be on F-1 status, be on a period of post-completion OPT, hold a degree in a field on the DHS STEM designated degree list from an accredited, SEVP-certified school, and work for an employer enrolled in E-Verify. The job must be related to your STEM degree, and the employer must provide a structured training experience.

## The key timeline

1. **Before your OPT ends,** talk to your school's Designated School Official (DSO). The DSO recommends the extension in SEVIS and issues an updated Form I-20.
2. **Complete Form I-983,** the training plan, with your employer. It describes your duties, goals and how you will be supervised.
3. **File Form I-765** with USCIS. You can file up to 90 days before your current OPT expires, and no later than 60 days after the DSO enters the recommendation in SEVIS. The filing also has to happen before the current OPT ends.
4. **Wait for a decision.** If you filed on time, you may be able to keep working for a limited period while the application is pending. Confirm the rules for your situation before relying on that.
5. **Report changes.** Employer changes, name or address changes, and similar events must be reported to your DSO within the window the rules set, which is short.
6. **Submit evaluations.** The I-983 requires evaluations at the 12-month point and at the end.

## Tracking unemployment days

OPT has a cap on days of unemployment. The initial post-completion OPT allows a limited number, and the STEM extension adds a smaller number. Days without qualifying employment count toward the limit, so a gap between jobs matters. Keep a calendar from your OPT start date and note every job start and end date. An OPT timeline tracker, even a spreadsheet, works well.

## Common problems

- Missing the filing deadline for the I-765
- An employer that is not enrolled in E-Verify or that does not complete the I-983 properly
- Unemployment days adding up faster than expected
- Failing to report an employer change
- A job that does not relate to the STEM degree
- Delays at USCIS that leave a gap before the next status

## Planning the next step

Most STEM OPT students aim to move to an H-1B, which has its own lottery and timeline. If your H-1B petition is filed and meets the requirements, the cap-gap rule may extend your status and work authorization after the OPT end date. Plan ahead for what happens if you are not selected. Options may include another degree program, a different visa category such as an O-1, or work abroad.

## When to call an immigration attorney

Your school's DSO handles the SEVIS side, and many cases go smoothly without outside help. A STEM OPT immigration lawyer is worth calling when:

- You have received an RFE or denial on your I-765
- You are close to the unemployment limit
- You are thinking about changing employers or fields
- You want to plan an H-1B, O-1 or other next step in advance
- Your employer is a small company or startup that needs help with E-Verify or the I-983

A short consultation early in the process is usually cheaper than fixing an error later.
