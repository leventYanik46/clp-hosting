A parent who is already in the United States after a lawful entry can usually become a permanent resident without leaving, through adjustment of status. For the parent of an adult U.S. citizen, adjustment of status for parents means filing Form I-130 and Form I-485 together, waiting here with a work permit, and attending one appointment at a USCIS field office, if USCIS asks for an interview at all. The rule that decides whether this route is open is the manner of entry, not how long the parent has stayed. Here is the process in order, and the facts that take a parent out of it.

## Who qualifies for adjustment of status for parents

Three conditions must be met. You, the petitioner, are a U.S. citizen at least 21 years old. Your parent was inspected and admitted or paroled into the United States, which a visa entry, a Visa Waiver Program entry, or a parole document establishes. And your parent is admissible, or any ground of inadmissibility can be waived.

Because parents of adult citizens are immediate relatives, a visa number is always available and the two usual bars to adjustment do not apply: a parent who overstayed a visitor visa, even by years, or who worked without authorization, can still adjust. Our [green card for parents](/practice-area/key-services/green-card-for-parents) practice sees this case constantly, and the overstay rarely causes a problem on its own.

A parent who crossed the border without inspection cannot use this process. The exceptions and the consular alternative are covered in [green cards for parents who entered without inspection](/parent-entered-without-inspection-green-card).

## The entry intent question

The most common complication is a parent who entered on a visitor visa and, soon after, decided to stay. A visitor visa requires an intent to return home, and a parent who already planned to stay and file when they boarded the plane may be found to have misrepresented that intent. The Department of State presumes misrepresentation when a visitor files for status within 90 days of entry, and USCIS looks at the same facts without a formal rule.

The distinction that matters is when the plan to stay formed. A parent who came for a visit and whose circumstances changed, because of a new grandchild, a health decline or a change at home, has a straightforward case. A parent who sold a house and shipped belongings before a "visit" does not. We ask these questions at intake, and when the facts point to a preformed intent, consular processing is often the safer route.

## The filing package

File everything at once. For a parent in the United States, the package includes:

- Form I-130, with proof of your citizenship and of the parent-child relationship. The evidence rules, including fathers not named on a birth certificate and stepparents, are in [I-130 for parents: documents that prove the relationship](/i-130-petition-for-parents-documents).
- Form I-485, with your parent's passport, visa, I-94 record, birth certificate and photographs.
- Form I-864, your affidavit of support, with your most recent tax return and proof of current income. The household size counts you, your dependents, anyone you have sponsored before, and each parent you are sponsoring now; a joint sponsor is allowed if your income falls short.
- Form I-765 for a work permit and Form I-131 for advance parole.
- Form I-693, the medical examination, completed by a USCIS-designated civil surgeon with the required vaccinations, in its sealed envelope.

Pay the current USCIS fees shown on each form's page. Line-by-line help with the green card application itself is in [what Form I-485 is and how to complete it](/what-is-form-i-485-how-to-complete-step-by-step).

## After filing: receipts, biometrics, permits and travel

USCIS issues receipt notices, then a biometrics appointment. The I-485 receipt places your parent in a period of authorized stay, so unlawful presence stops accruing. The work permit and advance parole follow while the case is pending; check the USCIS processing times page for current estimates.

Travel is the main hazard in this period. A parent who leaves the United States without advance parole abandons the I-485. Even with advance parole, a parent who had accrued more than 180 days of unlawful presence before filing should get advice first: under current Board of Immigration Appeals precedent, a trip on advance parole is not the kind of departure that triggers the unlawful presence bar, but the question deserves a review of the parent's dates before anyone books a ticket.

## The interview and the decision

USCIS may interview you and your parent at the field office or may decide the case on the record; parent cases are among those where the interview is sometimes waived. If scheduled, the officer confirms the relationship, reviews the I-485 answers and the medical, and checks the affidavit of support. Bring originals of everything filed, your parent's passport and any updated financial documents.

On approval, your parent receives a ten-year green card; conditional residence applies only to marriage cases. Your parent becomes eligible to apply for naturalization after five years as a resident, subject to the physical presence and English and civics requirements, with exemptions for age and length of residence that often apply to older parents.

Two further points families ask about. Your parent's green card does nothing for your siblings; they need their own petition in the F4 category, which our [family-based immigration](/practice-area/key-services/family-based-immigration) page describes. And a parent who is still abroad cannot be pre-approved before travel; if the plan is to immigrate, the right process is consular processing, not a visit followed by adjustment.
