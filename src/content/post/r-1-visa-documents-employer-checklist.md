Most R-1 requests for evidence are about the employer, not the worker. The worker's ordination certificate and membership letters are usually easy; the hard R-1 visa documents are the ones that prove the organization is a bona fide nonprofit religious entity, that the position is religious, and that the compensation is real. Below is the checklist in the order USCIS tends to read it, with notes on what each item has to show.

## Proof of nonprofit religious status

The foundation of the package is a currently valid determination letter from the IRS establishing that the organization is exempt from taxation under section 501(c)(3). If the petitioner is a church covered by a group exemption through its denomination, include the denomination's group ruling letter and evidence that the petitioner is listed under it, such as a letter from the parent body or a directory entry.

If the petitioner is not itself a religious organization but is affiliated with a denomination (a religious school, hospital, or charity), it must submit its own determination letter plus documentation establishing its religious nature and purpose, organizational literature describing its purpose and activities, and a religious denomination certification signed by an authorized official of the denomination confirming the affiliation. Organizations that are tax-exempt under a different subsection of the Code, or that have simply never applied, cannot petition until this is resolved; our [R-1 visa](/practice-area/key-services/r-1-visa) practice checks the IRS status before drafting anything else.

## The employer attestation

The R-1 Classification Supplement to Form I-129 contains an attestation that an authorized official of the petitioner must sign under penalty of perjury. It covers the number of members of the organization, the number of employees working at the location where the worker will be employed, the number of R-1 and special immigrant religious worker petitions the organization has filed in the past five years, the title of the position, a detailed description of the duties, the hours per week, the compensation and its form, the location of the work, and the worker's membership in the denomination and qualifications. Every figure in the attestation should be reconcilable with the supporting documents; an attestation that lists 30 employees next to a budget that shows two is an RFE waiting to happen. The attestation is also what the site inspector will test, as described in [how the R-1 visa process works](/r-1-visa-process-i-129-site-visit).

## Evidence of the position and compensation

Include a job description that explains the religious duties in the denomination's own terms, with the proportion of time spent on each duty if the role includes any administrative work. For ministers, add the denomination's requirements for ordination or its equivalent and proof that the worker met them. For religious occupations, add evidence that the denomination recognizes the position as a religious one, such as an organizational chart, a job classification, or a letter from the denominational authority.

Compensation must be documented, not just promised. Useful exhibits are the organization's budget showing funds set aside for the position, past W-2 forms or tax returns for prior holders of the position, bank statements, and, for non-salaried support, evidence of the housing, board, or stipend to be provided and its value. If the worker will be self-supporting under an established missionary program, the petition must show that the program has traditionally sent uncompensated missionaries, that it is part of a broader international missionary program of the denomination, and that the worker has the means to support themselves, including a statement of those means and an explanation of how support is handled for similar workers. That exception is narrow and is documented more often than it is approved.

## R-1 visa documents about the worker

- Proof of at least two years of membership in the denomination immediately before filing, typically letters from the worker's prior congregations with dates, membership records, or baptismal and confirmation certificates together with attendance evidence
- For ministers, the certificate of ordination or equivalent, and documents showing the denomination's training requirements and that the worker completed them (diplomas, transcripts, or a letter from the seminary or governing body)
- For religious vocations, evidence of the vows or investiture and the denomination's rules for that class of persons
- For religious occupations, evidence of the qualifications the denomination requires, such as training or certification
- A copy of the worker's passport and, if in the United States, evidence of current status; an in-country request follows the rules on our [change and extension of status](/practice-area/key-services/change-and-extension-of-status) page

Where a worker has previously held R-1 status, include the prior approval notices and I-94 records, because the five-year limit is calculated from them; see [R-1 visa maximum stay and extensions](/r-1-visa-maximum-stay-extensions).

## Translations and organization

Any document not in English needs a full certified translation. Assemble the package with a cover letter that walks the officer through each requirement and points to the exhibit that satisfies it. Officers adjudicate many petitions a day, and a package that makes the eligibility argument in two pages, with exhibits tabbed in the same order, gets a cleaner review than one that asks the officer to find the evidence.

## What an RFE on these documents looks like

The recurring requests are for the IRS letter when a church relied on an undocumented group exemption, for proof of compensation when the budget was not included, for membership evidence covering the full two years rather than a single letter, and for a clearer job description when the duties looked administrative. Each is avoidable at the drafting stage. If an RFE arrives, respond to every item, even those you think were already answered, and keep copies of everything submitted. Employers who later sponsor the same worker for a green card will reuse much of this file, as our post on [sponsoring an employee for a green card](/how-to-sponsor-employee-green-card) notes, so building it carefully once pays off twice.
