The EB-1B category gives outstanding professors and researchers a first-preference green card without the labor certification (PERM) process. The EB-1B requirements are lighter than EB-1A on the evidence side, but they come with a condition EB-1A does not have: a qualifying permanent job offer from a U.S. employer that files the petition for you.

## The core EB-1B requirements

To qualify, you need all three of the following:

1. **International recognition as outstanding** in a specific academic field.
2. **At least three years of experience** in teaching or research in that field. Research done while earning an advanced degree can count if you have the degree, or if your work was recognized as outstanding.
3. **A qualifying offer of employment** in a tenured or tenure-track teaching position, or a permanent research position. "Permanent" means employment of indefinite or unlimited duration where you have a reasonable expectation of continued employment.

The petitioner can be a university or other institution of higher education. It can also be a private employer, but only if the company employs at least three full-time researchers and has documented accomplishments in an academic field.

## The six EB-1B criteria

To show international recognition, you must meet at least two of these:

- Major prizes or awards for outstanding achievement in the field
- Membership in associations that require outstanding achievement of their members
- Published material written by others about your work, in professional publications
- Participation as a judge of the work of others, such as peer review for journals or serving on grant panels
- Original scientific or scholarly research contributions to the field
- Authorship of scholarly books or articles in journals with international circulation

Meeting two criteria is only step one. USCIS then looks at the record as a whole and asks whether you are recognized internationally as outstanding. A researcher with ten published papers and a handful of peer reviews may technically meet two criteria and still fall short if nothing shows that others in the field rely on the work. Citation counts, independent expert letters, invited talks, and evidence that your methods or findings were adopted by other groups carry real weight at this stage.

## EB-1B vs EB-1A

Researchers often ask whether to file EB-1B or EB-1A. The practical differences:

| | EB-1B | EB-1A |
|---|---|---|
| Who files | Your employer | You can self-petition |
| Job offer needed | Yes, permanent or tenure-track | No |
| Evidence standard | Outstanding, internationally recognized | Extraordinary ability, top of the field |
| Criteria to meet | 2 of 6 | 3 of 10 (or a one-time major award) |
| Portability | Tied to the employer's offer until adjustment | Not tied to an employer |

EB-1B is generally easier to win because the standard is "outstanding" rather than "extraordinary." It is the better choice when you have a stable employer willing to sponsor you and a solid but not exceptional record. EB-1A makes sense when you want independence from your employer, when your position is not permanent (many postdoctoral roles are not), or when your record is strong enough to clear the higher bar.

Some researchers file both. There is no rule against it, and a self-petitioned EB-1A can provide a backup if your job situation changes.

## Timeline and premium processing

Because EB-1B skips PERM, the timeline can be much shorter than EB-2 or EB-3. Premium processing is available for EB-1B I-140 petitions, with a 15 business day decision window. After approval, you file Form I-485 if a visa number is available, or go through consular processing abroad. For nationals of India and China, EB-1 can still have a wait, so check the Visa Bulletin for your country.

## Common EB-1B problems

- **The job offer isn't permanent.** A two-year postdoc appointment, a grant-funded position with a fixed end date, or a visiting scholar role usually won't qualify. The offer letter needs to show indefinite employment.
- **Generic recommendation letters.** Letters that praise you without explaining what you discovered and who used it add little. The strongest letters come from independent experts who know your work from publications, not from your supervisor.
- **Counting routine work as judging or contributions.** Reviewing two manuscripts can satisfy the judging criterion, but it doesn't establish international recognition by itself.
- **Private employer evidence gaps.** Companies must document the three full-time researchers and their research accomplishments.

## Is EB-1B a fit for you?

If you hold a permanent research or tenure-track role, have several years of publications, and can show others have built on your work, EB-1B is often the fastest employment-based route available to you. If your position is temporary, look closely at EB-1A or EB-2 NIW instead.
