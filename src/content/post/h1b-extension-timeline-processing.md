An H-1B extension should usually be filed several months before your current approval expires. USCIS accepts extension petitions up to six months in advance. If your employer files on time, you can keep working for that employer for up to 240 days past your expiration date while the extension is pending. This guide covers routine extensions within the six-year limit; extensions past six years have their own rules.

## When to file an H-1B extension

The earliest an employer can file is six months before the end date on your current I-797 approval notice and I-94. Filing early has real advantages:

- It leaves room to answer a request for evidence before your status runs out.
- It reduces the chance you will need to rely on the 240-day rule.
- It avoids travel problems close to the expiration date.

Employers often begin gathering documents three to four months before the earliest filing date. You will usually need recent pay statements, your current I-797 and I-94, and confirmation that your job duties and worksite have not changed.

## H-1B extension processing time

There are two speeds:

**Regular processing.** Times vary by service center and season and can run for months. USCIS publishes current processing times by form and center on its website.

**Premium processing.** For an additional fee, USCIS guarantees action on the petition within 15 business days: an approval, a denial, a request for evidence, or a notice of intent to deny. If it issues a request for evidence, the 15-day clock restarts when USCIS receives the response.

Premium processing does not make an approval more likely. It buys certainty about timing, which matters if you need to travel, renew a driver's license, or get a new visa stamp soon.

## The 240-day rule: working while the extension is pending

If your employer files the extension before your current status expires, you may continue working for the same employer under the same conditions for up to 240 days after the expiration date on your I-94. The receipt notice, your expired I-94, and the expired approval notice together serve as evidence of work authorization for Form I-9 purposes.

The rule has limits:

- It applies only to the **same employer**. A new employer's petition is a portability filing, not an extension.
- The filing must be **timely**, made before your status ends.
- If USCIS **denies** the extension, work authorization ends at that point.
- It does not cover dependents' work permits automatically. H-4 EAD holders have separate rules.

## Traveling during a pending extension

Leaving the U.S. while an extension is pending does not by itself abandon the petition, but your return depends on having a valid visa stamp and an approved petition, or being within the validity of the old approval. If you leave after your old approval expires and before the extension is approved, you may not be able to return until it is. Premium processing helps when travel is planned.

## When an extension becomes an amendment

If something material has changed, such as a new worksite outside the original metropolitan area, significantly different duties, or a change from full-time to part-time, the employer needs an amended petition rather than a simple extension. Many employers file both together. Filing a simple extension when an amendment was needed is a common reason for denials and later problems.

## Reaching the six-year limit

Each extension counts toward the six-year H-1B maximum. Time spent outside the U.S. can be added back. If your green card process has reached the right stage, the AC21 rules allow extensions beyond six years, so check where your PERM or I-140 stands well before year five ends.

## A short checklist

- Mark the date six months before your current expiration.
- Confirm whether your role, location, or hours have changed.
- Decide whether premium processing is worth the fee given your travel plans.
- Keep copies of your receipt notice, old I-797, and I-94 together.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file an H-1B extension if my old I-94 has already expired?
A late filing can be accepted only in limited circumstances outside your control. In most cases, an expired I-94 means the employer must file for consular notification, and you must leave and return with a new visa. File early to avoid this.

### Does an approved extension give me a new visa stamp?
No. USCIS approves the petition and issues a new I-94. You need a new visa stamp only if you leave the country and your current stamp has expired.

### Can I travel while an extension is pending?
Yes, but your return depends on a valid visa and either the old approval still being valid or the new one approved. Talk to your attorney before booking.

### Do H-4 family members need their own extension?
Yes. They file Form I-539, usually together with the H-1B extension.
