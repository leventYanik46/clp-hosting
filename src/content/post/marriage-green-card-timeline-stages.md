The marriage-based green card timeline depends on where you live, who your spouse is, and which USCIS office handles your case, so no single number fits everyone. What you can plan around is the order of events. The wait is easier when you know what each stage is for.

## First, which path are you on

- **Spouse of a U.S. citizen, living in the United States.** You can usually file the immigrant petition (Form I-130) and the green card application (Form I-485) together. This is called concurrent filing, and it is the most common path.
- **Spouse of a U.S. citizen, living abroad.** The case goes through the I-130 and then the National Visa Center and a U.S. consulate, a process called consular processing.
- **Spouse of a green card holder.** The category may be subject to visa availability, so the priority date and the Visa Bulletin affect the timeline.

This article focuses on the most common path, a spouse of a U.S. citizen applying inside the United States.

## Stage by stage

1. **Preparing the filing.** Gather identity and civil documents, evidence of your relationship, the sponsor's financial documents, and a medical exam by an approved civil surgeon. Preparing carefully tends to save time later.
2. **Filing.** The package typically includes the I-130, I-130A, I-485, an affidavit of support (I-864), the medical exam form (I-693) and often applications for a work permit (I-765) and travel permission (I-131).
3. **Receipt notices.** USCIS sends notices confirming that it has received each form. Keep them, since they prove the case is pending.
4. **Biometrics appointment.** USCIS schedules you to give fingerprints and a photo, after which background checks run.
5. **Work permit and travel document.** If you applied for them, these may arrive while the main case is pending. The travel document lets you leave and return without abandoning your application, but check with an attorney before travelling.
6. **Possible request for evidence.** If something is missing or unclear, USCIS sends a notice with a deadline.
7. **Interview.** USCIS typically schedules an interview at the field office closest to you. Some cases are decided without one, but you should expect to be called.
8. **Decision.** An approval is followed by the arrival of the card. A denial, or a notice of intent to deny, gives you chances to respond or appeal in certain situations.

## What affects the pace

- **The field office.** Processing times differ, and USCIS posts current estimates on its website.
- **Completeness of the filing.** Missing documents lead to requests for evidence.
- **Background checks.** Name checks and prior immigration history can add time.
- **Prior immigration issues.** Overstays, past denials, or criminal history may need extra work.
- **Interview scheduling.** The wait for an interview slot varies.

## Conditional residence

If your marriage is less than two years old when the green card is approved, you receive a conditional card valid for two years. You then file Form I-751 jointly to remove the conditions. Plan for this stage, since it has its own timeline.

## How to keep the timeline from slipping

- Respond to any USCIS notice quickly
- Keep your address updated with USCIS
- Keep copies of every document you send
- Save new evidence of your marriage as time passes, such as joint bills, photos and travel records
- Check the USCIS case status online and the field office processing time
