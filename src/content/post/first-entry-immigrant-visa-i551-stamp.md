After a consular interview, many new immigrants travel to the U.S. with an immigrant visa in their passport. On arrival, they become permanent residents. The physical green card comes later in the mail. Here's what to expect when traveling with a green card for the first time.

## Before you travel

**Pay the USCIS immigrant fee.** After receiving your immigrant visa, pay the USCIS Immigrant Fee online. USCIS won't produce your green card until the fee is paid. Paying before you travel helps avoid delays.

**Keep your sealed packet, if you have one.** Some consulates give you a sealed envelope to hand to CBP. Many now process electronically. Follow the consulate's instructions.

**Check the visa's validity.** Your immigrant visa is usually valid for six months. You must enter before it expires.

## At the port of entry

CBP inspects your documents and admits you as a permanent resident. The officer may stamp your passport with an I-551 stamp, also called an ADIT stamp, or rely on your immigrant visa annotation.

## The I-551 stamp

The I-551 stamp, or the machine-readable immigrant visa with the "upon endorsement serves as temporary I-551" annotation, serves as temporary proof of permanent residence for one year from the date of admission. You can use it to:

- Work
- Travel internationally and reenter
- Complete Form I-9 for employment

## When does the green card come in the mail?

The card is usually mailed to the address listed during consular processing. Many new immigrants receive it within a few weeks to a few months after entry. Delays can happen if:

- The USCIS Immigrant Fee wasn't paid
- The address was wrong or changed
- There was a processing issue

## If the card doesn't arrive

1. **Check that the fee was paid** through your USCIS account.
2. **Check the case status** online.
3. **Update your address** if needed.
4. **Contact USCIS** if months pass without the card.
5. **Request an I-551 stamp** at a USCIS field office if your temporary proof is expiring.

## Traveling before the card arrives

You can travel with your passport containing the immigrant visa and I-551 annotation, as long as it's within one year of admission. Some airlines may question the document, so carry proof of admission.

## Conditional residents

If you came on a marriage-based immigrant visa for a marriage of less than two years, you'll receive a conditional green card. Plan for filing Form I-751 in the 90 days before it expires.

## Common mistakes

- Not paying the USCIS Immigrant Fee
- Providing an address you won't be at
- Waiting too long to follow up on a missing card

## Checking your entry record

After you arrive, check your I-94 record or admission details online to confirm your class of admission was recorded correctly. Errors are rare but can affect later filings. For a conditional resident, the class should reflect conditional status, which matters when you calculate the window to file Form I-751.

## Starting work and life in the U.S.

You can work right away. Show your employer your passport with the immigrant visa and I-551 annotation for Form I-9 purposes. Apply for a Social Security number if you didn't request one during consular processing. Keep your address current with USCIS, and keep a copy of your passport's visa page and entry stamp in a safe place.

## Children and family members arriving together

Each family member who enters on an immigrant visa needs their own USCIS Immigrant Fee payment and will receive their own card. Children who enter as permanent residents may automatically acquire U.S. citizenship if they are under 18, residing in the U.S. in the legal and physical custody of a U.S. citizen parent. In that case, apply for a U.S. passport or Certificate of Citizenship (Form N-600) to document it.
