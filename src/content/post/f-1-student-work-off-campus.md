An F-1 student can work off campus during a degree program, but only through a specific authorization: Curricular Practical Training approved by the school, an Employment Authorization Document based on severe economic hardship, or a narrow category such as work for a recognized international organization. There is no general permission to take a part-time job, drive for a delivery app, or freelance online. A single week of unauthorized work is a status violation, and it is one of the few violations that cannot be cured through reinstatement.

## What on-campus work allows first

Before looking off campus, know what you already have. F-1 status permits on-campus employment without a separate USCIS approval: up to 20 hours per week while school is in session and full time during official breaks and the summer vacation, provided you intend to enroll the next term. On-campus means work performed on the school's premises for the school or for a vendor that serves students (a campus bookstore or cafeteria), or work at an off-site location that is educationally affiliated with your program. A job at a chain coffee shop across the street does not qualify just because students go there.

Students who are unsure whether a role is on campus should ask the Designated School Official (DSO) in writing before accepting. Our [F-1 visa practice](/practice-area/key-services/f-1-visa) page describes how we review these borderline questions, because a mistaken classification by a well-meaning department is still unauthorized work in the eyes of USCIS.

## Curricular Practical Training

CPT is the main path to off-campus work during a program. It is authorized by your DSO, not by USCIS, and it must be an integral part of an established curriculum: a required internship, a practicum, or a course that awards credit for the work. The authorization is written on a new Form I-20 and is employer-specific and date-specific. You cannot start before the authorization date or stay past the end date.

Three points trip students up:

- You generally need one full academic year of enrollment before CPT, unless your graduate program requires immediate practical training.
- CPT can be part time (20 hours or less per week) or full time. Twelve months or more of full-time CPT at the same degree level eliminates your eligibility for Optional Practical Training at that level. Part-time CPT does not have that effect.
- The work must fit the stated curriculum. Schools that authorize CPT for "any job in your field" with no course link have been a USCIS target for years, and the student bears the consequences.

If you are weighing CPT now against OPT after graduation, read [how post-completion OPT works](/f-1-opt-rules-and-timing) before you use up a large block of full-time CPT.

## Severe economic hardship

If circumstances beyond your control change after you arrive, you can apply to USCIS for off-campus employment authorization based on severe economic hardship. Qualifying events include a currency devaluation at home, loss of financial aid through no fault of your own, a sponsor's death or serious illness, or unexpected medical bills. You must have completed one academic year, be in good standing, and show that on-campus employment is unavailable or insufficient.

The DSO recommends the request in SEVIS and you file Form I-765 (category (c)(3)(iii)) with evidence of the hardship and the current USCIS filing fee. If approved, the EAD allows any job, in any field, up to 20 hours per week during the term and full time during breaks. It is valid in one-year increments and ends if you transfer schools. Because the standard is "severe" and "unforeseen," a general wish to earn spending money will not succeed; the file needs documents that show what changed and when.

## Other narrow categories

Two further routes exist but apply to few students. Employment with a recognized international organization (within the meaning of the International Organizations Immunities Act) can be authorized through an I-765 with a letter from the organization. Pre-completion OPT also allows off-campus work related to your major before graduation, though every month used is subtracted from the 12 months available after you finish, so it is rarely the best choice.

What does not exist: authorization for remote work for a foreign employer while you sit in the United States. Many students assume that because the employer and the payment are abroad, the rule does not apply. USCIS looks at where the work is performed, and the safer reading is that it counts as employment. Discuss this with counsel rather than assume.

## Why unauthorized work is different from other violations

Most F-1 violations, such as dropping below a full course load, can be addressed through a reinstatement request on Form I-539 if the student acts within five months and the lapse was beyond their control. Unauthorized employment is excluded from reinstatement by regulation. The realistic remedy is to depart, obtain a new visa, and re-enter in fresh status, which means explaining the violation at the consular interview.
