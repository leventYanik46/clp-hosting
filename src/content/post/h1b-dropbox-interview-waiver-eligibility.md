For years, many H-1B workers renewed their visa stamps without an interview through the "dropbox," the interview waiver program. In 2025 the State Department ended most of those waivers. Today, nearly every H-1B worker applying for a new visa stamp should expect an in-person interview at a U.S. consulate, usually in their country of nationality or residence. That changes how you plan any trip that requires a new stamp.

## What the H-1B dropbox was

The dropbox let certain applicants submit their passport and documents without appearing for an interview. H-1B workers renewing a visa in the same category within a set period after the prior visa expired often qualified. It made routine renewals quick and predictable.

## What changed in 2025

In 2025 the State Department narrowed interview waivers sharply. Under the updated policy, most nonimmigrant visa applicants, including H-1B renewals, must appear in person. A short list of exceptions remains, mostly for diplomatic and official visa categories and limited renewal situations in a few visa classes such as B visas and H-2A. Consular officers can still require an interview from anyone, even if an exception appears to apply.

Around the same time, the State Department also directed that applicants generally apply in their country of nationality or residence, which limits the practice of booking faster appointments in a third country.

Because these rules have changed more than once, check the current guidance on the State Department and specific consulate websites before booking.

## Who still qualifies for an interview waiver

Treat the H-1B dropbox as unavailable unless current State Department guidance clearly places you in an exception. In practice, H-1B principal applicants and H-4 dependents should plan on an interview. If you believe an exception fits, confirm it with the consulate's own instructions and have a fallback date booked.

## How to plan an H-1B stamping trip now

**Check whether you need a new stamp at all.** If your visa stamp is still valid and you are returning to the same employer, you may not need a new one. An extension approved by USCIS does not put a new visa in your passport, but you need a new stamp only to re-enter after travel.

**Look at appointment wait times first.** Interview wait times vary widely by consulate and season. Book before you buy airline tickets.

**Prepare for questions.** Consular officers have been asking more detailed questions about job duties, the employer, and why the position needs you. Bring your approval notice, a recent employment verification letter, recent pay statements, and a clear understanding of what your role involves.

**Allow time for administrative processing.** Some applications are refused under section 221(g) while the consulate completes additional review. That can take weeks or longer, and you cannot return to work in the U.S. until it is resolved.

**Coordinate with your employer.** Your manager should know you may be outside the U.S. longer than planned, and whether remote work from abroad is acceptable for that period.

## Should you travel at all?

For some workers, the safest choice is to postpone international travel until it is necessary. That is especially true if your employer has a pending petition, your job details recently changed, your name or record raises questions, or your nationality has faced recent restrictions. Travel is still possible, but it is no longer routine paperwork.

## Common mistakes

- Booking non-refundable flights before securing an interview date.
- Traveling on a recently changed job without a current approval for the new role.
- Assuming a third-country appointment will be accepted.
- Bringing documents that do not match the petition, such as a different job title.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do H-4 dependents still qualify for the dropbox?
Generally no. Under the 2025 changes, most H-4 applicants also need an interview unless they fit one of the narrow exceptions in current State Department guidance.

### Can I apply in Mexico or Canada to get a faster appointment?
Third-country applications are now heavily restricted. The general rule is to apply in your country of nationality or residence. Check the consulate's current instructions before booking.

### Does an interview mean my case is being treated as suspicious?
No. Interviews are now standard for almost all H-1B applicants. Prepare for one as a normal step.

### Can I re-enter the U.S. with an expired visa stamp if my H-1B is approved?
Usually not. You need a valid visa stamp to return, except in limited situations such as automatic visa revalidation after short trips to Canada or Mexico, which has its own conditions.

### How long does administrative processing take?
It varies from a few days to several months. There is no reliable way to speed it up, which is why trips that require a new stamp need flexible return dates.

### Should my employer send a letter with me?
Yes. A recent employment verification letter that matches the petition helps the officer confirm your role quickly.
