To renew a green card, you file Form I-90 with USCIS. You can usually file online through a USCIS account, or by mail. The process is simpler than most immigration filings, but timing, fees and what you carry while you wait are worth understanding before you start.

## Who needs to renew

A permanent resident card is generally valid for ten years. Your status as a permanent resident does not expire when the card does, but you are required to carry valid proof of status, and an expired card can cause trouble with employment verification, travel and ID. Renewal replaces the card, not the status.

You also use Form I-90 to replace a card that is lost, stolen or damaged, to update information such as a legal name change, and in some cases when a child reaches the age at which a new card is required.

Conditional residents with a two-year card do not file Form I-90. They file Form I-751 to remove conditions, which is a different process.

## When to file

USCIS generally advises filing within six months before the card expires. If your card has already expired, file as soon as you can. Waiting longer causes problems when you travel, apply for a job or seek benefits.

## How to file

1. **Create or log in to your USCIS online account,** if you are using the online option.
2. **Complete Form I-90,** with your biographical details and the reason for filing.
3. **Upload or include supporting documents,** such as a copy of your current card, and evidence if you are changing information.
4. **Pay the fee.** Check the current amount on the USCIS site, as fees change.
5. **Attend a biometrics appointment** when USCIS schedules one.
6. **Receive your new card** by mail after approval.

If you file by paper, check the current form edition and filing address on the USCIS website, because USCIS rejects outdated forms.

## Cost

USCIS sets the I-90 filing fee and may update it, and the biometrics fee has been handled differently over time. Look at the Form I-90 page for the exact amounts, and check whether any fee waiver or exemption applies to your situation. Costs of legal help, if you hire it, are separate. If you search for fees from earlier years, you may see outdated numbers.

## How long it takes

USCIS posts processing times for the I-90 on its website. They change with workload, and renewal can take several months. Do not rely on a figure you read in a forum. After you file, you receive a receipt notice. USCIS may issue a notice that extends the validity of your expiring card for a period stated on it, which you can use with your card as temporary evidence of status. Check the language of your notice for the period it covers.

## What to carry while you wait

- Your expired or expiring card
- The receipt notice, and any extension notice
- Your passport

If you plan to travel, check the rules first. Reentry with a pending renewal is often possible, but you should confirm for your situation, especially if you have a long absence, any immigration history or criminal records.

## Common mistakes

- Filing the wrong form because you hold a conditional card
- Paying an outdated fee
- Using an old form edition
- Waiting until after the card expires to file
- Not updating your address with USCIS
