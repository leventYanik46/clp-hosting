Most L-1B workers get a green card through employer sponsorship based on PERM labor certification, in the EB-2 or EB-3 category. Some qualify for an EB-2 national interest waiver or an EB-1 extraordinary ability petition, which skip PERM. What L-1B workers usually cannot use is the EB-1C multinational manager green card that their L-1A colleagues rely on. Because the L-1B stops at five years, the route you choose and the year you start matter as much as eligibility.

## Why the EB-1C usually does not fit

The EB-1C requires that the employee worked abroad for the company group in a managerial or executive capacity for at least one year in the three years before coming to the United States, and that the U.S. job is managerial or executive. An L-1B employee qualified for the transfer because of specialized knowledge, not management, so the foreign year usually does not meet the EB-1C definition. A promotion to manager in the U.S. fixes only half the problem.

There are narrow exceptions, such as an employee who held a qualifying managerial role abroad before an L-1B assignment, but those depend on dates and job histories that need careful review. Our article on [EB-1C requirements for multinational managers](/eb-1c-multinational-manager-requirements) explains the test. For most specialists, the realistic options are the ones below. Our [L-1B intracompany transfer visa](/practice-area/key-services/l-1b-visa) page covers how we coordinate the transfer and the green card plan.

## Route one: PERM, then an EB-2 or EB-3 petition

The standard path has three stages. First, the employer obtains a prevailing wage determination from the Department of Labor and tests the U.S. labor market through recruitment. Second, the employer files the PERM application on Form ETA-9089. Third, after certification, the employer files Form I-140 with USCIS, and once the employee's priority date is current under the Visa Bulletin, the employee files Form I-485 to adjust status, or completes consular processing abroad.

The job sponsored must be a real future position, and the employee must meet its requirements with experience gained before joining the sponsoring employer, or in a substantially different role. Specialized knowledge employees sometimes struggle here, because the experience that made them valuable was gained inside the company group. How experience with the foreign affiliate counts depends on the facts, so the job requirements must be drafted with care before recruitment starts. Our guide to the [PERM labor certification process](/perm-labor-certification-process) walks through each step.

EB-2 requires an advanced degree, or a bachelor's degree plus five years of progressive experience. EB-3 covers professionals with a bachelor's degree and skilled workers with two years of experience. The category affects the wait: for employees born in countries with heavy demand, the Visa Bulletin can add years after the I-140 is approved.

## Route two: the EB-2 national interest waiver

An employee whose work has substantial merit and national importance may qualify for an EB-2 national interest waiver, which removes the PERM requirement and the need for a job offer. USCIS applies the three prongs from Matter of Dhanasar: the proposed endeavor has substantial merit and national importance, the person is well positioned to advance it, and on balance it would benefit the United States to waive the job offer and labor certification. Engineers, researchers and technical leaders in areas like semiconductors, energy, health technology and advanced manufacturing are common NIW candidates. CLP's [EB-2 NIW green card](/practice-area/key-services/eb-2-niw-green-card) team can assess whether the employee's record fits.

The NIW has one practical advantage for L-1B workers: the employee files the I-140 personally, so the case does not depend on the employer's PERM timing.

## Route three: promotion to L-1A

If the employee's role grows into management, the employer can file an L-1A petition. That buys time, since an employee who holds L-1A status for at least six months can stay up to seven years total, but it does not by itself create EB-1C eligibility, for the reason above. Treat the promotion as a way to extend the runway for a PERM or NIW case, not as a green card route on its own.

## Timing the L-1B green card against the five-year limit

L-1 is a dual intent category, so filing a labor certification or an I-140 does not hurt the L-1B status or travel on the L-1B visa. The constraint is time. PERM takes many months before the I-140 is filed, and the priority date may not be current when the I-140 is approved. If the employee's L-1B years run out before the I-485 can be filed, the employee needs another status. Unlike H-1B workers, L-1B workers cannot extend past their limit based on a pending PERM or an approved I-140.

The practical rule: start the green card case early in the L-1B period, ideally in the first year or two. When the backlog is long, consider moving the employee to H-1B status, where AC21 extensions beyond six years are available. Our post on the [L-1B five-year limit](/l-1b-five-year-limit-extensions) covers how the clock is counted.
