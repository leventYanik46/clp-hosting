An Employment Authorization Document, or EAD, is the work permit card USCIS issues to people who need separate proof that they can work in the U.S. It is common for green card applicants, asylum applicants, DACA recipients, TPS holders, and certain spouses of visa holders. The biggest change to know right now: for most categories, filing a renewal on time no longer extends your card automatically.

## Reading your work permit card

The front of the card shows:

- **USCIS#:** Your A-number. This is the "A number on EAD card" many forms ask for. It is usually 8 or 9 digits.
- **Category:** A code such as C08 (asylum applicant), C09 (pending adjustment of status), C33 (DACA), A12 or C19 (TPS), A17 or A18 (certain E and L spouses), or C26 (H-4 spouse).
- **Card Number:** A 13-character number unique to this card.
- **Valid From and Card Expires:** The work authorization period.
- **Terms and Conditions:** Notes such as "Valid for reentry to U.S." on combination cards that also serve as advance parole.

## EAD filing fee

Fees for Form I-765 depend on the category and whether you file online or by paper. Some categories, such as initial asylum-based EADs, have specific fee rules set by law. Applications filed with a pending I-485 have their own fee structure. Check the current USCIS fee schedule and the form instructions before you file, because fees changed in 2024 and 2025 for several categories.

## Renewing your EAD

You can generally file a renewal up to 180 days before your current card expires. Under a DHS interim final rule effective October 30, 2025, later adopted as final, renewals filed on or after that date no longer receive the automatic extension of up to 540 days that many categories used to get. When the old card expires, your authorization to work stops until the new card is approved, unless your category has a separate extension by law or Federal Register notice, such as some TPS designations.

What this means in practice:

- File as close to the 180-day mark as possible
- Make sure the application is complete so it is not rejected
- Respond to any RFE quickly
- Tell your employer about the expiration date in advance

## If your card expires before the renewal is approved

You must stop working on the expiration date unless an exception applies. Your employer must reverify on Form I-9 and cannot accept a receipt notice alone for most categories. Losing work authorization does not always mean losing your underlying status, but it can affect income, benefits, and in some cases eligibility for other benefits.

## Compelling circumstances EAD

Certain workers in E-3, H-1B, H-1B1, L-1, or O-1 status with an approved I-140 whose priority date is not current may qualify for a one-year EAD if they face compelling circumstances, such as serious illness, employer retaliation, or substantial harm. It is a narrow, discretionary option meant to bridge a crisis, not a routine work permit.

## Lost, stolen, or incorrect cards

- Lost or stolen: file a new I-765 for a replacement
- Incorrect due to USCIS error: file I-765 for a corrected card without a new fee in many cases, with evidence of the error


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I work with just the receipt notice?

For most renewals filed on or after October 30, 2025, a receipt notice does not extend work authorization. Some categories have separate rules, so check before relying on it.

### Can my EAD and advance parole be on the same card?

Yes. Many I-485 applicants receive a combination card that serves as both. The card will say "Serves as I-512 Advance Parole."

### Does my EAD let me work for any employer?

Most EAD categories allow work for any employer. Some, such as certain student-based categories, carry restrictions.

### Is there a grace period after my EAD expires?

No general grace period exists for EAD holders. Once the card and any applicable extension expire, work must stop until a new card is issued.
