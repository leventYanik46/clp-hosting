Yes, it is possible to be detained at a green card interview. Reports of ICE arrests at USCIS offices have increased since 2025, including at marriage-based interviews. For most applicants with a clean history and lawful entry, the risk is low. For some, it is real, and it should be assessed before the interview, not in the waiting room.

## Why detentions happen at interviews

USCIS is not a law enforcement agency, but it shares information with ICE. When an applicant has an outstanding removal order, certain criminal history, or another enforcement priority, ICE can be notified and make an arrest at the field office. The interview gives enforcement officers a known date, time, and place.

## Risk factors that matter

Applicants face higher risk if they have:

- **A prior removal or deportation order**, including an in absentia order they may not know about
- **A prior removal and reentry** without permission
- **Criminal arrests or convictions**, especially recent or serious ones
- **Pending criminal charges**
- **Fraud or misrepresentation findings** in a past application
- **An entry without inspection** combined with no clear basis to adjust

Applicants who entered lawfully, are immediate relatives of U.S. citizens, and have no criminal or removal history generally face much lower risk.

## How to prepare

### Know your record

Request your immigration file through FOIA, and check EOIR's automated case information system using your A-number to see whether you have ever had an immigration court case. Get certified dispositions for every arrest.

### Get a legal assessment

An attorney can tell you whether you are eligible to adjust at all, and whether something in your history creates enforcement risk. If you have an old removal order, it may need to be addressed through a motion to reopen before the interview.

### Bring an attorney

Your attorney can attend the interview, respond to questions, and act quickly if something goes wrong.

### Plan for the worst case

- Give a family member your A-number, full name, date of birth, and country of birth
- Keep your attorney's contact information with someone you trust
- Arrange childcare and access to important documents
- Know where your passport and identification are

## What to do if a family member is detained

1. Stay calm and get the officer's name and agency if possible.
2. Call the attorney immediately.
3. Use ICE's online detainee locator, with the person's A-number or name, country of birth, and date of birth.
4. Do not sign anything on the detained person's behalf without legal advice.
5. Gather evidence for a possible bond hearing: proof of family ties, employment, community support, and a fixed address.

An immigration detention lawyer can request a bond hearing, challenge detention, and seek release where the law allows. In some prolonged detention cases, federal court options may be available.

## Should you skip the interview?

Missing the interview usually leads to denial, and avoiding it does not make an old removal order go away. The better approach is to understand the risk early and resolve problems before the date arrives.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can a U.S. citizen spouse be detained?

No. U.S. citizens cannot be detained for immigration purposes. Bring proof of citizenship to the interview.

### Will ICE be at every interview?

No. Most interviews proceed with no enforcement involvement. The risk is concentrated among applicants with removal orders or criminal history.

### Can my attorney stop an arrest at the interview?

An attorney cannot physically prevent an arrest, but can confirm whether a removal order exists, request release, and start bond or other proceedings quickly.
