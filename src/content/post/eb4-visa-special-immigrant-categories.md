EB-4 is the fourth employment-based green card category, reserved for "special immigrants." It is a collection of distinct groups with little in common except the category label: religious workers, certain Iraqi and Afghan nationals who worked with the U.S. government, retired international organization employees, special immigrant juveniles, and several smaller groups. Most EB-4 cases start with Form I-360.

## Who qualifies for an EB-4 visa

### Religious workers

Ministers and people working in religious vocations or occupations for a bona fide nonprofit religious organization can qualify. Requirements include:

- At least two years of membership in the religious denomination immediately before filing.
- At least two years of continuous qualifying religious work before filing.
- A job offer from a qualifying U.S. religious organization.

Ministers have a permanent EB-4 category. The non-minister religious worker program has repeatedly been extended by Congress for set periods, so check whether it is currently authorized before relying on it.

Many religious workers come first on an R-1 visa and later transition to EB-4. USCIS may conduct site inspections of the sponsoring organization.

### Special immigrant visas for Iraqi and Afghan nationals

Certain Iraqi and Afghan nationals who worked for or on behalf of the U.S. government, including as translators and interpreters, can qualify for special immigrant visas. These have their own numerical limits and processes through the State Department and Chief of Mission approval.

### Retired G-4 international organization employees

Long-serving employees of international organizations, their surviving spouses, and certain children may qualify after meeting residence and timing requirements tied to G-4 status.

### Special immigrant juveniles

Children who cannot reunify with one or both parents because of abuse, neglect, or abandonment may qualify through SIJS. This category is large and drives much of EB-4 demand.

### Smaller groups

Other EB-4 groups include certain broadcasters working for the U.S. Agency for Global Media, certain members of the U.S. armed forces with long service, Panama Canal employees, and certain physicians from earlier programs.

## The EB-4 visa bulletin

EB-4 has an annual limit, and demand from some countries has exceeded supply. As a result, the EB-4 line in the visa bulletin has shown cutoff dates for many countries, not just high-demand ones. Approval of an I-360 does not mean a green card is immediately available. You need a current priority date to file for adjustment of status or complete consular processing.

Because special immigrant juveniles, religious workers, and other groups share the same EB-4 numbers, a backlog in one group affects all of them.

## The basic EB-4 process

1. **File Form I-360**, or for some SIV groups, follow the State Department's process.
2. **Wait for a current priority date** in the EB-4 visa bulletin.
3. **Apply for the green card** by adjustment of status or consular processing.

Religious workers may face a gap between R-1 status expiring and the green card being available. Planning for that gap is important.

## Common EB-4 problems

- Religious work interrupted by unauthorized employment or gaps in status.
- Sponsoring organizations unable to show nonprofit religious status or ability to pay.
- Missing the age or timing windows in the G-4 or juvenile categories.
- Assuming I-360 approval provides work authorization. It does not by itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a religious worker adjust status in the U.S.?
Yes, if eligible and a visa number is available. R-1 holders often adjust after the I-360 is approved, though gaps in status must be avoided.

### Does an approved I-360 give work authorization?
No. Work authorization comes from your current status, such as R-1, or from an employment authorization document tied to a pending green card application.

### Is EB-4 backlogged for all countries?
EB-4 cutoff dates have applied to many countries in recent years. Check the current visa bulletin for your chargeability country.

### Can my spouse and children come with me?
Yes. Spouses and unmarried children under 21 can usually immigrate as derivatives in most EB-4 groups.

### Do Afghan SIV applicants file the same way?
No. Afghan and Iraqi special immigrant visa cases have their own steps, including Chief of Mission approval, and usually proceed through the State Department.

### How long must a religious worker be a member of the denomination?
At least two years immediately before filing the petition.

### Can a church file for a part-time worker?
Religious worker positions generally must be compensated and meet minimum hour requirements. Part-time work can create eligibility problems.

### Does an R-1 visa holder automatically qualify for EB-4?
No. The EB-4 petition has its own requirements and evidence, including continuous qualifying work, which USCIS reviews closely.
