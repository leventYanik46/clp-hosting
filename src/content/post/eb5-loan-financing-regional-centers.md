You can fund an EB-5 investment with a loan, but USCIS looks closely at how the loan is secured and where the money came from. The investment amounts are significant: $1,050,000 in most areas, or $800,000 in a targeted employment area or for certain infrastructure projects. Many investors combine savings, gifts, and borrowing to reach that figure, which makes the financing structure part of the immigration case.

## Can you use an EB-5 loan?

Yes. Loan proceeds can count as investment capital, but two conditions matter:

1. **You must be personally liable.** The loan has to be your obligation, not the new commercial enterprise's.
2. **The security must be your own assets.** A loan secured by property you own, such as a home or a business share, can work. A loan secured by the assets of the EB-5 business itself does not count.

You also have to document the lawful source of the collateral you pledged and the path of the loan proceeds into the investment. USCIS wants to see that the assets behind the loan were legally obtained.

## Common EB-5 financing sources

- Mortgages or home equity loans on property you own
- Loans against shares in a company you own
- Loans from family members, documented with a written agreement and the lender's own source of funds
- Gifts, with proof of the donor's lawful source

## EB-5 loan interest rate and terms

USCIS does not set interest rates or require particular terms. What matters is that the loan is genuine, enforceable, and documented. Keep the loan agreement, collateral appraisal, title documents, and bank records showing every transfer. Unusual terms, such as no repayment obligation, invite scrutiny into whether the loan is really a gift or a sham.

## Direct investment vs. regional center

**Direct EB-5** means investing in your own business and creating at least 10 full-time jobs for qualifying U.S. workers. You control the business, but the job creation must be direct.

**Regional center EB-5** means investing through a USCIS-designated regional center project. Indirect and induced jobs can be counted using economic models, which makes the job requirement easier to meet. The trade-off is that you are a passive investor relying on the developer.

## Comparing regional centers

Since the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022, regional centers face stricter oversight, but you still need to do your own due diligence:

- **Track record.** How many projects has the center completed, and how many investors received permanent green cards?
- **Capital structure.** Where does EB-5 money sit relative to senior loans? What happens if the project fails?
- **Job cushion.** Does the project's economic report show job creation well above the requirement?
- **Exit strategy.** When and how is capital expected to be returned?
- **Fees.** What administrative fees apply on top of the investment?
- **Compliance.** Is the center in good standing with USCIS and up to date on required reporting?

Independent review by a securities attorney or financial advisor is worth considering alongside the immigration analysis.

## Timeline considerations

After filing Form I-526E (regional center) or I-526 (direct), you wait for approval and then for your priority date to be current. Applicants born in high-demand countries may face visa backlogs, although the 2022 Act set aside visas for rural, high unemployment, and infrastructure projects that may move faster.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can a family member lend me the money?

Yes, but you need the lender's own source of funds, a written loan agreement, and proof that the loan is your personal obligation. Family loans are scrutinized like any other.

### Is EB-5 investment guaranteed?

No. The capital must be at risk. Projects that promise a guaranteed return or a fixed redemption are not allowed. Your money can be lost.

### How long must the investment stay in place?

Under current rules, the investment must remain at risk for a required period tied to the conditional residence process. Plan for the money to be committed for years, not months.
