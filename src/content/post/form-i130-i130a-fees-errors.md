Form I-130, Petition for Alien Relative, is the first step in most family-based green cards. A U.S. citizen or permanent resident files it to prove a qualifying relationship to a relative who wants to immigrate. When the relative is a spouse, Form I-130A, Supplemental Information for Spouse Beneficiary, goes with it. Getting these forms right at the start avoids months of delay.

## What is Form I-130?

The I-130 establishes the relationship. It does not give the relative a green card or any status. After approval, the relative applies for the green card through adjustment of status in the U.S. or consular processing abroad.

**Who can file:**
- U.S. citizens for spouses, children, parents (if the citizen is 21 or older), and siblings (if 21 or older).
- Permanent residents for spouses and unmarried children.

**Who files it:** The petitioner, meaning the U.S. citizen or permanent resident. Each relative needs a separate I-130, though derivative family members can sometimes be included in certain categories.

## What is Form I-130A?

The I-130A collects biographic information about the **spouse beneficiary**: addresses and employment over the last five years, and parents' information. It must be filed with every spouse I-130.

The spouse beneficiary signs the I-130A only if they are in the United States. If the spouse is abroad, the form is still submitted but does not need the beneficiary's signature.

## I-130 fees

Under the USCIS fee rule that took effect in 2024, the I-130 filing fee is $675 for paper filing and $625 for online filing. Fees can change by later rule, so confirm on the USCIS fee page before filing. There is no separate fee for the I-130A.

## Evidence to include

- Proof of the petitioner's U.S. citizenship or permanent residence.
- Proof of the relationship: marriage certificate, birth certificates, or adoption records.
- For spouses, proof that prior marriages ended: divorce decrees or death certificates.
- For spouses, evidence of a bona fide marriage.
- Passport-style photos where required.
- Translations of any foreign-language documents with a translator's certification.

## Errors that slow approval

- **Missing proof that prior marriages ended.** One of the most common reasons for requests for evidence.
- **Wrong relationship evidence.** A father petitioning for a child born out of wedlock needs proof of legitimation or a bona fide relationship.
- **Outdated form edition.** USCIS rejects filings on old editions.
- **Inconsistent addresses or dates** between the I-130, I-130A, and later forms.
- **Missing signatures** or the wrong person signing.
- **Filing for a sibling or parent before the petitioner turns 21.**

## What happens after approval

For immediate relatives of U.S. citizens, there is no wait for a visa number, so the green card step can proceed immediately or even be filed concurrently if the relative is in the U.S. and eligible to adjust. Preference relatives wait for their priority date in the visa bulletin.

For relatives abroad, the approved case goes to the National Visa Center for consular processing.

## When to involve an immigration lawyer for an I-130

Many I-130s are straightforward. Get advice if the relative has prior immigration violations, entered without inspection, has a criminal record, or if the relationship involves adoption, stepchildren, or prior marriages.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file the I-130 online?
Yes, for many relationships. Online filing usually costs less than paper filing.

### How long does I-130 processing take?
Processing times vary by category and service center. USCIS posts current times.

### Does a permanent resident file I-130A for a spouse?
Yes. Any spouse I-130 requires an I-130A, whether the petitioner is a citizen or a permanent resident.

### Can I file I-130 and I-485 together?
If the beneficiary is an immediate relative of a U.S. citizen and is in the U.S. and eligible to adjust, the forms can often be filed together.

### What if my spouse is abroad?
File the I-130 and I-130A. After approval, the case goes to the National Visa Center for consular processing.

### Do I need certified translations?
Foreign-language documents need full English translations with a translator's certification of competence and accuracy.

### What happens if the I-130 is denied?
Denials of family petitions can generally be appealed to the Board of Immigration Appeals, or the petitioner can refile with better evidence.

### Can I petition for more than one relative at a time?
Yes, but each relative needs a separate I-130 and fee.

### Does the I-130 give my relative permission to live or work in the U.S.?
No. An I-130, whether pending or approved, does not create status or work authorization. Those come from a later application such as adjustment of status.
