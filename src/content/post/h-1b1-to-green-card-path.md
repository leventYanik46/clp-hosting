Yes, you can move from H-1B1 to a green card, but the H-1B1 will not carry you there the way an H-1B does. The H-1B1 requires nonimmigrant intent, so every renewal and every visa interview asks whether you plan to leave, while a green card process states plainly that you plan to stay. The way through is sequencing: choose the immigrant category, decide whether to switch to H-1B along the way, and time renewals and travel around the filings that reveal immigrant intent. Our [H-1B1 visa practice](/practice-area/key-services/h-1b1-visa) builds that sequence with Chilean and Singaporean clients before the first immigrant filing, which is when it is cheapest to get right.

## Why H-1B1 to green card takes planning

The rule that makes the H-1B friendly to green card applicants is INA 214(h), which says that seeking permanent residence is not evidence of an intent to abandon a foreign residence for H-1B, L, and a few other visas. The H-1B1 is not on that list. Worse, INA 214(b) presumes every visa applicant is an intending immigrant unless they prove otherwise, and Congress specifically left the H-1B1 inside that presumption when it created the category.

In practice, this means a PERM filing, an I-140 petition, or an I-485 adjustment application is not an automatic bar to an H-1B1 renewal, but each one has to be explained. Neither USCIS nor the State Department is required to ignore it. Consular interviews are where the tension shows most, because the officer applies 214(b) directly and has wide discretion.

## Route 1: Employer sponsorship through PERM

The most common path is an employer-sponsored green card in the EB-2 or EB-3 category. The employer obtains a prevailing wage determination, runs a recruitment campaign to test the U.S. labor market, files the labor certification on Form ETA-9089, and then files an I-140 petition with USCIS. Our walkthrough of the [PERM labor certification process](/perm-labor-certification-process) covers each stage.

Chile and Singapore are charged to the worldwide category in the Visa Bulletin, so your wait depends on demand from all countries other than the most oversubscribed ones, not on the long backlogs that affect India and China. Your priority date is the date the PERM was filed, and you can file for adjustment of status once that date is current. Our explainer on [priority dates and filing dates](/priority-date-vs-filing-date) explains how to read the chart.

## Route 2: Self-petitions without an employer

If your work has national importance or you have a strong record of recognition, you may not need an employer at all. An EB-2 national interest waiver lets you petition for yourself if you can meet the three prongs of Matter of Dhanasar; we explain them in [the three prongs USCIS tests for an NIW](/eb-2-niw-eligibility-three-prongs). The EB-1A extraordinary ability category is another self-petition option for people at the top of their field. Self-petitions are often a good fit for H-1B1 holders because they let you control the timing.

## Route 3: Family-based immigration

Marriage to a U.S. citizen, or a petition by a U.S. citizen parent or adult child, opens a separate path. The intent problem is the same: the family petition is evidence of immigrant intent, and travel and renewals need the same care.

## Should you switch to H-1B first?

For many clients, the cleanest answer is to move into H-1B status before the immigrant process gets far along. H-1B status is dual intent, H-1B renewals are not refused for immigrant intent, H-1B workers can extend past six years based on a pending or approved PERM or I-140, and the H-4 spouse of an H-1B worker can apply for work authorization once the I-140 is approved. None of that is available to H-1B1 families.

The catch is the H-1B cap. Unless the employer is cap-exempt, it must register you in the March lottery, and a selected worker cannot start H-1B employment before October 1. In the meantime, keep the H-1B1 renewed so you never lose status. Our [H-1B specialty occupation visa](/practice-area/key-services/h-1b-visa) page explains how the switch is filed.

## Staying in H-1B1 until adjustment of status

You can also remain in H-1B1 status throughout and file Form I-485 inside the United States once your priority date is current. While the I-485 is pending, you are in a period of authorized stay and can apply for an employment authorization document and advance parole.

Two cautions. First, once immigrant intent is on file, rely on in-country H-1B1 extensions through USCIS rather than consular renewals where you can, because an in-country extension avoids a 214(b) interview. Second, do not travel on your H-1B1 visa while an I-485 is pending without advice. The travel rules that protect H-1B workers with pending adjustment applications do not map neatly onto the H-1B1.

## A sequence that works for most clients

Decide the immigrant category before filing anything. If your spouse needs to work or the process will take years, plan an H-1B registration for the next March. If you stay in H-1B1, schedule renewals so that the consular interviews happen before PERM or the I-140 is filed, and use in-country extensions afterward. Keep every renewal on time, because a gap in status can make you ineligible to adjust inside the United States in the employment-based categories.
