A biometrics appointment is a short visit to a USCIS Application Support Center where you give fingerprints, a photograph, and a signature. USCIS uses them to run background checks with the FBI and other agencies and to produce cards such as a green card or work permit. The appointment itself usually takes less than an hour. What comes after depends on the type of case.

## What is a biometrics appointment?

After you file certain applications, such as Form I-485 for a green card, Form N-400 for citizenship, Form I-765 for a work permit, or Form I-751 to remove conditions, USCIS mails an appointment notice on Form I-797C. The notice lists the date, time, and location of your Application Support Center.

## What to bring

- The appointment notice (I-797C)
- A valid government photo ID, such as a passport, green card, driver's license, or EAD
- For a pending green card or citizenship case, it can help to bring your current green card or other status document

Arrive at your scheduled time. Some centers allow walk-ins on other dates, but this is not guaranteed.

## What happens at the appointment

1. Check-in and an ID review
2. Digital fingerprinting of all ten fingers
3. A photograph
4. A digital signature

How long does a biometrics appointment take? Usually 15 to 45 minutes once you are called, though waiting time varies.

## Rescheduling

You can request to reschedule online through your USCIS account or by calling the USCIS Contact Center, generally before the appointment date and with good cause. Missing the appointment without rescheduling can lead USCIS to treat your application as abandoned.

## What happens after biometrics?

Your fingerprints go to the FBI for a criminal history check, and your information is checked against other government databases. After that, the case moves to the next step for that form:

- **Green card (I-485):** The case may be approved without an interview, scheduled for an interview, or receive a request for evidence. Approval times vary widely by field office.
- **Citizenship (N-400):** You will be scheduled for a naturalization interview, including the English and civics tests.
- **Work permit (I-765):** The card is produced using your biometrics once the application is approved.
- **I-751:** USCIS may approve on the record, send an RFE, or schedule an interview.

## How long after biometrics to get a green card?

There is no fixed answer. Some applicants receive approval within a few months of biometrics. Others wait much longer, particularly if an interview is required or the case receives an RFE. Check the USCIS processing times page for your form and field office, and track your case online.

## Biometrics for U.S. citizenship

N-400 applicants usually attend biometrics a few weeks to a few months after filing. USCIS sometimes reuses biometrics from a prior case instead of scheduling a new appointment. If that happens, your case may move directly toward the interview.

## When biometrics raise a problem

If the background check returns an arrest you did not disclose, or a record that does not belong to you, the case may stall or receive an RFE. Always disclose arrests on the application, even dismissed ones, and bring certified court records to any interview.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I bring someone with me?

Generally only the applicant enters the service area, except for people who need assistance, such as a parent accompanying a child or someone helping an applicant with a disability.

### Do I need biometrics for every case?

Not always. USCIS sometimes reuses previously collected biometrics, and some forms do not require a new appointment. Watch for a notice either way.

### What if I am outside the U.S. on the appointment date?

Request a reschedule before the date. For some forms, such as a reentry permit, you must attend biometrics in the U.S. before leaving.
