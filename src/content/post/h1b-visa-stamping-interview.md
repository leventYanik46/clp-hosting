H-1B visa stamping is the process of getting the H-1B visa in your passport at a U.S. embassy or consulate so you can travel and reenter the U.S. An approved H-1B petition gives you status. The visa stamp is the travel document. Since September 2025, the rules for getting that stamp have tightened, so plan more time and more preparation than you might have in past years.

## The current rules

Two State Department changes now shape almost every H-1B stamping trip:

- **In-person interviews.** As of September 2, 2025, the interview waiver, often called dropbox, was eliminated for nearly all H-1B applicants, including renewals.
- **Home country only.** As of September 6, 2025, nonimmigrant visa applicants are expected to schedule interviews in their country of nationality or residence. Third-country stamping, such as an Indian national stamping in Canada or Mexico, is no longer a reliable option.

For applicants from India, stamping of the H-1B visa from India means booking at one of the U.S. posts there and planning around appointment wait times.

## What to bring

- Passport valid for at least six months beyond your stay
- DS-160 confirmation page and appointment confirmation
- Form I-797 approval notice
- Copy of the I-129 petition and LCA, if available
- Employer letter confirming your job, title, salary, and that you are returning to work
- Recent pay stubs and, for renewals, tax returns or W-2s
- Degree certificates and transcripts

## Common H-1B interview questions

Officers generally confirm that the job is real and matches the petition:

- What does your company do, and what is your role?
- Who is your supervisor?
- What is your salary?
- Where will you work, and is it at a client site?
- What is your educational background, and how does it relate to the job?

Consular officers have also been reported to ask about the employer's efforts to recruit U.S. workers. Answer factually and consistently with the petition.

## 221(g) and administrative processing

If the officer needs more information or security screening, you may receive a 221(g) refusal. This is not a final denial. It means the case is on hold. Some cases clear in days. Others take weeks or months, especially those that require additional security review.

During that time you may be stuck abroad. Talk to your employer about remote work arrangements before you travel.

## Does the $100,000 payment apply?

USCIS and State Department guidance indicate that the September 2025 proclamation does not apply to workers with currently valid H-1B visas or with approved petitions filed before the effective date. It targets certain new petitions filed for people outside the U.S. If your situation does not fit neatly into those categories, get advice before you leave.

## Should you travel at all?

Ask yourself:

- Is a visa stamp needed now, or can travel wait?
- Do you have any arrests, prior refusals, or status issues that could complicate an interview?
- Is your role at a client site or a third-party worksite, which tends to draw more questions?
- Is your green card case at a stage where travel matters, such as a pending I-485 that needs advance parole or a valid H-1B visa to return?


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I renew my H-1B visa inside the U.S.?

The State Department has run limited domestic renewal programs for certain applicants. Availability changes, so check current State Department announcements before planning travel.

### What if my passport expires soon?

Renew it first. The visa is placed in a valid passport, and many consulates require at least six months of validity.

### Can my family stamp separately?

Yes, but dependents usually interview with the principal or must show the principal's approved petition and status documents.

### What is the difference between a visa refusal and a 221(g)?

A 221(g) is technically a refusal, but it is temporary and usually means the case is on hold for documents or security checks. A refusal under other sections, such as 214(b) or a ground of inadmissibility, is final for that application.
