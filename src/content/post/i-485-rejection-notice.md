An I-485 rejection notice means USCIS did not accept your green card application for processing. Nobody reviewed your eligibility, and your case was not denied. USCIS sent the package back because of a problem it caught at intake, such as a missing signature, a payment issue, or a missing required document. The good news is that you can usually fix the problem and file again. The bad news is that until you do, your application was never filed.

## I-485 rejection vs. denial

These two words get used interchangeably, but they mean very different things.

- **Rejection:** USCIS returns the application at intake. No receipt notice is issued, the fee is returned or never processed, and there is no filing date. You correct the defect and file again.
- **Denial:** USCIS accepted the application, reviewed it, and decided you do not qualify. The fee is not refunded. Your options may include a motion to reopen or reconsider on Form I-290B, a new application if you are still eligible, or, if you have no other status, raising your case again in immigration court.

A rejection is almost always easier to fix. Read the notice carefully to confirm which one you received.

## Why it matters that nothing was filed

A pending I-485 does more than start the green card process. For many applicants, a properly filed adjustment of status application gives them a period of authorized stay, lets them apply for a work permit and advance parole, and locks in a filing date. When the package is rejected, none of that happened. That can be serious if:

- Your visa status expired while you were waiting, which means you may be accruing unlawful presence.
- Your case depends on the Visa Bulletin and your priority date has since retrogressed, so you may not be able to refile until it becomes current again.
- A child in the case is close to turning 21, where the timing of the filing can affect age-out protection.

In any of these situations, refile quickly and get advice if the timing is tight.

## Common reasons for an I-485 rejection notice

- **Payment problems:** the wrong amount, a declined card, a check returned for insufficient funds, or one payment covering several forms that each require their own fee. Fees change, so confirm them on the USCIS fee schedule before you file.
- **Missing signatures:** an unsigned form, or a parent not signing for a child under 14.
- **Outdated form edition:** USCIS only accepts the editions listed on its website.
- **Missing medical exam:** USCIS now generally requires the sealed Form I-693 from a designated civil surgeon to be filed with the I-485. Applications sent without it may be rejected.
- **Filing when a visa is not available:** for family preference and employment-based cases, the priority date must be current under the Visa Bulletin chart USCIS designates for that month.
- **Problems with the underlying petition:** if you file an I-130 and I-485 together and the I-130 is rejected, the I-485 usually goes back with it.
- **Wrong filing address:** sending the package to a lockbox that does not handle your category.
- **Missing required initial evidence:** depending on the item, this can lead to a rejection or a request for evidence later.

## How to refile after an I-485 rejection

1. **Read the rejection notice.** It usually identifies the reason, often with a checked box and a short explanation.
2. **Fix that problem, then review everything else.** A second rejection for a different defect costs you more time.
3. **Get current forms, fees, and filing addresses** from the USCIS website on the day you prepare the new package.
4. **Check every signature and date** on every form.
5. **Confirm your medical exam is still usable.** The civil surgeon's signature has to fall within a limited window before filing, so a delayed refiling may need a new exam.
6. **Keep a full copy** of everything you send, and use a trackable delivery method.

If you believe USCIS rejected a properly prepared application by mistake, you can contact USCIS and ask for a review. In practice, refiling a corrected package is often faster and safer.

## When to talk to an adjustment of status lawyer

A single rejection for a missing signature is easy to fix on your own. A rejection is also a good moment to step back and look at the whole case. Talk to an adjustment of status lawyer if your status has expired or is about to, your priority date is close to the cutoff, your history includes a past immigration violation or arrest, or your package has been rejected more than once.
