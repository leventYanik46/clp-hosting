Specialized knowledge for an L-1B visa means knowledge of your employer's products, services, research, equipment, techniques or management that is special, or an advanced level of knowledge of the company's own processes and procedures. It does not mean being a skilled engineer or an experienced analyst. Plenty of employees are good at their jobs; the L-1B is for the ones whose knowledge is distinct from what others in the same field have, because it was built inside this company. Most petitions handled by our [L-1B specialized knowledge visa](/practice-area/key-services/l-1b-visa) team succeed or fail on whether the record shows that difference in concrete terms.

## The two ways to have specialized knowledge

The statute and the regulation give two paths, and a petition can rely on either or both.

### Special knowledge

Special knowledge is knowledge of the company's product, service, research, equipment, techniques or management and its application in international markets that is distinct or uncommon compared with the knowledge of others in the industry. A field service engineer who has spent four years configuring the company's own control systems, which no competitor sells, can have special knowledge even though the engineering principles are standard.

### Advanced knowledge

Advanced knowledge is a deeper or more complex understanding of the company's specific processes and procedures than most employees in the company have. The comparison here is internal. An analyst who built and maintains the company's proprietary pricing model, and trains others on it, may have advanced knowledge even if the model's general method is known in the industry.

The knowledge does not have to be proprietary or unique, and it does not have to be held by only a handful of people. Those older, stricter readings were dropped. What USCIS does expect is a comparison: the petition must explain how this employee's knowledge differs from that of similarly employed workers inside the company or in the industry. A strong support letter builds that comparison in rather than leaving it implied.

## How USCIS weighs the evidence

The standard of proof is preponderance of the evidence: the petitioner must show it is more likely than not that the employee has specialized knowledge. In practice, USCIS looks at factors like these:

- Whether the knowledge is specific to the company and not readily available in the U.S. labor market.
- How long it takes to acquire, and whether it came from internal training, project work or years on a product line.
- Whether the employee would be hard to replace without significant cost, delay or disruption to the business.
- Whether the employee's role in the U.S. will actually use that knowledge.
- How the employee's pay, title and responsibilities compare to coworkers in similar positions.

None of these factors is required on its own, and none is decisive. A strong petition addresses most of them with documents rather than adjectives.

## Evidence that proves the claim

Good L-1B evidence is specific and verifiable. Training records with course names and hours are better than "extensive training." Project lists that name the internal systems, client deployments or product versions the employee worked on are better than "worked on key projects." A letter that names how many employees across the group perform the same function, and why this one is different, carries more weight than a general statement that the employee is "one of our best." Patents, internal technical documentation the employee authored, and performance reviews that describe the specific expertise help when they exist.

Avoid industry buzzwords that describe the whole field. If the knowledge can be described in terms any competitor would recognize, an officer will read it as general skill. Name the proprietary tool, method or product, and explain what it does in plain words.

## The third-party worksite rule

An L-1B employee who will work primarily at the site of an unaffiliated client faces two extra conditions. The client cannot control and supervise the employee's work, and the placement cannot be essentially an arrangement to provide labor for hire. The work must be connected to the petitioner's own product or service, using the specialized knowledge specific to the petitioner. IT consulting and outsourcing companies see the most scrutiny here, and the contract terms, reporting lines and statement of work need to back up the claim.

## When the L-1B is the wrong fit

If the employee will manage people or a function in the United States, compare the L-1A, which allows up to seven years and leads to the EB-1C green card. Our [L-1A versus L-1B comparison](/l-1a-vs-l-1b-comparison) walks through that choice. If the knowledge is professional rather than company-specific, an [H-1B specialty occupation petition](/practice-area/key-services/h-1b-visa) may be available, though subject to the annual cap. And if the company already holds a [blanket L approval](/blanket-l-petition-explained), only specialized knowledge professionals can use it; others still need an individual petition.
