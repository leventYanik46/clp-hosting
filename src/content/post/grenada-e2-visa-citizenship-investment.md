Investors from countries without an E-2 treaty, including India and China, cannot apply for the E-2 visa on their own nationality. For years, many acquired Grenadian citizenship through investment and then applied for an E-2 as Grenadian nationals. That route still exists, but since late 2022 U.S. law requires investors who gained treaty nationality through investment to show three continuous years of domicile in that country before applying. The route has become slower and more demanding.

## Why Grenada?

Grenada is an E-2 treaty country and runs a citizenship by investment program. A citizen of Grenada can apply for the E-2 like any other treaty national. For investors whose home countries lack an E-2 treaty, a second citizenship from a treaty country is the only way into the E-2.

## The two investments

The Grenada E-2 route involves two separate investments:

1. **Grenadian citizenship.** Through a contribution to the government fund or an approved real estate investment under Grenada's program.
2. **The U.S. business.** A substantial investment in an operating U.S. enterprise, just like any E-2.

The Grenada contribution does not count toward the E-2 investment. Budget for both from the start.

## The three-year domicile rule

The National Defense Authorization Act for fiscal year 2023, signed in December 2022, added a requirement for E-1 and E-2 applicants who acquired treaty country nationality through a financial investment. They must have been domiciled in that country for a continuous period of at least three years at some point before applying.

Key points:

- **It applies to citizenship gained through investment.** Grenadians by birth or descent are not affected.
- **It does not apply to people who already held E status** before the change, under the generally accepted reading of the law.
- **"Domicile" is not defined in detail** in the statute. Consular officers are likely to look at where you actually lived: a home, local bank accounts, tax filings, and presence in Grenada.

Some advisers argue that citizenship obtained through a donation rather than an investment may fall outside the rule. That argument is untested, and planning around it carries real risk.

## E-2 visa for Indian investors: what this means in practice

For an Indian investor, the Grenada route now often means planning a multi-year move: obtaining citizenship, relocating to Grenada for at least three years, and then applying for the E-2. That makes it far less attractive for someone who needs to be in the U.S. soon.

Alternatives worth comparing:

- **EB-5**, which leads directly to a green card and has no nationality restriction, though Indian applicants should check current visa availability.
- **L-1A** through an existing Indian company opening a U.S. office.
- **O-1A** for founders with a strong record.
- **EB-1C** later, for a manager or executive of a multinational company.

## Other risks to weigh

- **Grenadian law changes.** Citizenship by investment programs change their requirements and pricing.
- **Ownership structure.** The U.S. business must be at least 50% owned by Grenadian nationals. Mixing owners of different nationalities can break eligibility.
- **Family members.** A spouse who is a Grenadian national by investment may face the same domicile rule. Plans based on the spouse applying as principal need careful review.
- **Consular scrutiny.** Officers look closely at the nationality history of Grenada-based applicants.

## Is the Grenada E-2 route still worth it?

For some families with time and a willingness to live in Grenada, yes. For most investors who want to be in the U.S. within a year or two, another route usually fits better.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to live in Grenada after the three years?
The statute requires three continuous years of domicile at some point before applying. It does not clearly require that you still live there when you apply, but consular practice should be checked.

### Does the rule apply to my children?
The law does not clearly address derivative family members. Treat their position as uncertain and plan for scrutiny.

### Can I hold an E-2 under my Indian nationality?
No. India does not have an E-2 treaty with the U.S. You must apply as a national of a treaty country.

### Is Turkey an option for citizenship by investment and E-2?
Turkey is an E-2 treaty country with a citizenship by investment program. The same three-year domicile rule applies to treaty nationality acquired by investment.

### Will the U.S. business need Grenadian ownership?
Yes. At least 50% of the business must be owned by Grenadian nationals for a Grenada-based E-2.

### How long is a Grenada E-2 visa valid?
Visa validity depends on reciprocity with Grenada and is listed in the State Department's reciprocity schedule. E-2 status at entry is granted in increments of up to two years.

### Should I talk to a U.S. lawyer before applying for Grenadian citizenship?
Yes. The value of the citizenship for U.S. purposes depends on U.S. law, so check it before you invest.
