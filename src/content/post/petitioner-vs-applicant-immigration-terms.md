Immigration forms use precise words for the people involved and the documents they file. A **petitioner** files a petition for someone else. A **beneficiary** is the person the petition is for. An **applicant** files an application for a benefit for themselves. Mixing these up leads to forms signed by the wrong person and confusion about who is responsible for what.

## Petition vs application

**A petition** asks the government to recognize a relationship or qualification that allows someone else to immigrate or work. Examples:

- Form I-130, Petition for Alien Relative, filed by a U.S. citizen or permanent resident for a family member.
- Form I-129, Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker, filed by an employer for an H-1B, L-1, or O-1 worker.
- Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Worker, filed by an employer, or by the worker in self-petition categories.

**An application** asks for a benefit for yourself. Examples:

- Form I-485, Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status.
- Form N-400, Application for Naturalization.
- Form I-765, Application for Employment Authorization.

A petition usually comes first; the application follows once the petition is approved or alongside it.

## Applicant vs petitioner

- **Petitioner**: the person or company that files a petition. Your employer is the petitioner on an H-1B. Your U.S. citizen spouse is the petitioner on an I-130.
- **Applicant**: the person seeking a benefit for themselves through an application.

The same person can be both in different steps. In a self-petition such as EB-1A or the national interest waiver, you are the petitioner on the I-140 and later the applicant on the I-485.

## Beneficiary and derivative beneficiary

- **Beneficiary**: the person who benefits from a petition. On an I-130, the relative being sponsored is the beneficiary.
- **Derivative beneficiary**: a spouse or child who gets status through the principal beneficiary's case, such as the spouse of an H-1B worker or the children of an EB-2 green card applicant.

## What is an immigrant petition?

An immigrant petition is a petition for permanent residence, as opposed to a temporary visa. The I-130 and I-140 are immigrant petitions. The I-129 is a nonimmigrant petition.

## Sponsor

"Sponsor" is used loosely. It can mean:

- The petitioner on a family or employment petition.
- The person who signs Form I-864, Affidavit of Support, and takes on financial responsibility. That person is the sponsor in a legal sense, and joint sponsors and household members can sign related forms.

## Other terms worth knowing

- **Priority date**: your place in line for a green card, usually the date the petition or labor certification was filed.
- **Adjustment of status**: getting a green card from inside the U.S.
- **Consular processing**: getting an immigrant visa at a U.S. embassy or consulate abroad.
- **Receipt number**: the case number on USCIS notices.
- **A-number**: your alien registration number, used across agencies.

## Why it matters

Forms must be signed by the correct person, fees are paid by specific parties, and obligations such as the Affidavit of Support fall on specific people. Knowing your role tells you what you must sign and what you are agreeing to.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the beneficiary sign the petition?
No. The petitioner signs the petition. The beneficiary signs their own applications and, for spouses in the U.S., the I-130A.

### Is a joint sponsor the same as a petitioner?
No. A joint sponsor signs a separate Affidavit of Support to add income. The petitioner remains the person who filed the I-130.

### Who is the "principal applicant"?
The person whose qualifications create eligibility, as opposed to derivative family members who benefit through them.

### Can a company be an applicant?
Companies are petitioners, not applicants. Applications are filed by individuals for themselves.

### What is a self-petition?
A petition you file for yourself, such as EB-1A, EB-2 NIW, VAWA, or certain special immigrant categories.

### Is a receipt number the same as an A-number?
No. A receipt number identifies a single filing. An A-number identifies you across all filings and agencies.

### What is a derivative beneficiary?
A spouse or child who receives status through the principal's case without filing a separate petition, such as the family of an employment-based green card applicant.

### What does "immediate relative" mean?
Spouses, unmarried children under 21, and parents of adult U.S. citizens. They have no annual visa limit.

### What is the difference between a visa and status?
A visa is a travel document for entering the U.S. Status is the legal category you hold once inside, shown on your I-94.

### Who is the "sponsor" in an employment case?
The employer, which acts as the petitioner. In family cases, "sponsor" also refers to whoever signs the Affidavit of Support.
