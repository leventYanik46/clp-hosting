Advance parole is permission, granted before you leave, to be paroled back into the United States without a visa. For most people with a pending Form I-485 it is the only thing that makes a trip abroad survivable, because a departure without it is treated as abandonment of the adjustment application. The document is requested on Form I-131, it is usually granted to adjustment applicants without any showing of emergency, and it carries one well-known legal benefit and several less-known limits. This post explains who needs advance parole, how to apply, what the Arrabally decision protects, and who should not travel even with the document in hand.

## Who needs advance parole and who is exempt

Anyone with a pending adjustment application needs advance parole to travel, with a narrow exception. Applicants who hold valid H-1B or L-1 status, or H-4 or L-2 dependent status, and who are returning to resume that employment may travel on the H or L visa instead, and the I-485 survives. K-3, K-4 and V nonimmigrants have a similar exemption. Everyone else, including F-1 students, B-2 visitors who became immediate relatives, and applicants whose status has ended, must wait for the document. Our [advance parole and travel document](/practice-area/key-services/travel-and-reentry-permits) practice advises H-1B holders to think carefully before using parole instead of the visa, because entering on parole changes what status you hold on arrival.

## Applying on Form I-131

Most applicants file the I-131 together with the I-485 and Form I-765, and USCIS often issues the travel permission and the employment authorization on a single combined card. Check the current USCIS fee rule for an I-131 filed with an I-485, because the fee structure for travel documents has changed in recent years and the form page states what applies. You do not have to prove an emergency; a short statement of the purpose of travel, such as visiting family or attending a conference, with supporting documents, is enough. Two timing rules matter. Do not leave while the initial request is pending, because departure abandons it and, with it, the I-485. And watch the expiration date, because advance parole is valid for a set period and you must be back inside the United States before it ends. Renewals should be filed well before the current document expires. Our guide to [Form I-485](/what-is-form-i-485-how-to-complete-step-by-step) explains where the I-131 sits in the full filing.

## What advance parole protects: the Arrabally rule

The three-year and ten-year bars of INA 212(a)(9)(B) are triggered by a departure after accruing unlawful presence. In Matter of Arrabally and Yerrabelly, the Board of Immigration Appeals held that an adjustment applicant who leaves under a grant of advance parole has not made the kind of departure that triggers those bars. That ruling is what allows someone who overstayed before marrying a U.S. citizen to visit a sick parent abroad without becoming inadmissible for a decade. The protection has edges. It covers advance parole granted to adjustment applicants, and its application to other travel authorizations is decided case by case. It does nothing for other grounds of inadmissibility: criminal convictions, fraud, a prior removal order, or the permanent bar for someone who accrued a year of unlawful presence and later entered without inspection. Our post on [unlawful presence versus being out of status](/unlawful-presence-vs-out-of-status) explains how those grounds are counted.

## Who should not travel even with the document

The card does not guarantee admission. On return you are inspected by CBP, which can deny parole at the port. Applicants in these groups should have the whole history reviewed before booking:

- Anyone with a prior removal, deportation or expedited removal order, including an old in absentia order they may not know about.
- Anyone with an arrest or conviction, since a departure gives CBP a fresh chance to find inadmissibility that an I-485 interview might have handled with a waiver.
- Anyone who entered without inspection more than once, or after a prior order.
- TPS beneficiaries, who now receive travel authorization on Form I-512T rather than advance parole, and DACA recipients, whose travel is subject to ongoing litigation.
- Applicants in removal proceedings, for whom departure can execute an order or end the case.

## Emergencies and expedites

If a parent is gravely ill or has died abroad and you have no document, USCIS field offices can issue emergency advance parole in person, usually on the same day, with proof of the emergency and of the pending I-485. For urgent but less immediate situations, file the I-131 with an expedite request that documents severe financial loss, an urgent humanitarian reason, or a similar ground recognized in USCIS policy. Expedites are discretionary and the documentation does the persuading.

## Returning: parole is not admission

When you come back on advance parole, you are paroled into the United States rather than admitted. For adjustment purposes that is fine, because INA 245(a) requires that you were inspected and admitted or paroled, and a parolee qualifies. For nonimmigrant status it is different. An H-1B worker who enters on parole is no longer in H-1B status on arrival, even though the underlying petition remains valid, and USCIS policy allows continued employment for the petitioning employer in that situation; whether to use the visa or the parole document is a choice to make with counsel before each trip.
