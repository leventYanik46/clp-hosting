What is the Visa Waiver Program? It lets citizens of certain countries visit the U.S. for tourism or business for up to 90 days without a visa. Instead, travelers get an approved ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorization) online. It's convenient, but it comes with strict limits.

## Who is eligible

You must:

- Be a citizen of a Visa Waiver Program country
- Have an e-passport from that country
- Travel for tourism, business, or transit
- Stay 90 days or less
- Have an approved ESTA

Participating countries include most of Western Europe, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Australia, New Zealand, and others. The list changes, so check the current list.

## Who isn't eligible

Some citizens of VWP countries can't use the program, including people who:

- Have traveled to certain countries, such as Iran, Iraq, Syria, Sudan, or Cuba, since certain dates
- Are dual nationals of certain countries
- Have been denied a U.S. visa or entry before
- Have overstayed under the VWP before
- Have certain criminal records

## Applying for ESTA

Apply online through the official CBP ESTA website. Approval usually comes quickly, but apply at least 72 hours before travel. ESTA is generally valid for two years or until your passport expires.

Use only the official government website. Third-party sites charge extra fees.

## The 90-day limit

You can stay up to 90 days. Time in Canada, Mexico, and nearby islands may count toward the 90 days if you return to the U.S. during that trip.

## Restrictions on VWP travelers

- **No extensions:** You can't extend your stay beyond 90 days, except in limited emergencies.
- **No change of status:** You generally can't change to another status, like F-1 or H-1B, inside the U.S.
- **Limited adjustment:** VWP entrants can adjust status in limited cases, mainly as immediate relatives of U.S. citizens.
- **Waiver of rights:** You waive the right to contest removal, except for asylum.

## When a B-2 visa is safer

Consider getting a B-2 visa if:

- You want to stay longer than 90 days
- You may need to extend your stay
- You've had ESTA problems
- You've traveled to excluded countries
- You plan activities that may not fit VWP limits

## Common mistakes

- Using third-party ESTA websites
- Overstaying, even by a day
- Working on VWP
- Assuming VWP allows a change of status

## Recent changes to watch

The ESTA fee has increased in recent years, and the government has expanded screening for ESTA applicants, including questions about social media and travel history. Check the current ESTA requirements before each trip, since your existing approval doesn't change when the rules change, but you may need to reapply if your passport or circumstances change.

## When something goes wrong at the border

CBP can refuse admission to a VWP traveler without a hearing. If you're refused, you'll generally need a visa for future trips. Keep records of your travel and prior compliance, and consider a B-2 visa if you travel often or for longer periods.

## Visa Waiver Program and adjustment of status

VWP entrants generally can't adjust status, with a key exception for immediate relatives of U.S. citizens, such as spouses. Even then, USCIS may consider whether the traveler entered with preconceived intent to stay. Marrying shortly after a VWP entry and filing for adjustment can raise questions about misrepresentation at entry. Recent USCIS policy also encourages applicants in temporary status to apply from abroad, so get advice before filing.
