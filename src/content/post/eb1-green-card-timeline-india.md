The EB-1 category is the fastest employment-based green card for many people, because it skips the PERM labor certification. But the EB-1 green card timeline isn't the same for everyone. Your country of birth can add years to the wait, especially for Indian nationals.

## The three EB-1 categories

- **EB-1A:** extraordinary ability, self-petition allowed
- **EB-1B:** outstanding professors and researchers, employer-sponsored
- **EB-1C:** multinational managers and executives, employer-sponsored

All three skip PERM.

## Stage 1: Preparing the petition

The first stage is gathering evidence. For EB-1A and EB-1B, that means documenting awards, publications, citations, judging, and recommendation letters. For EB-1C, it means documenting the managerial or executive role abroad and in the U.S.

**Typical time:** 1 to 3 months, depending on how organized your evidence is.

## Stage 2: Form I-140

The I-140 is the immigrant petition. Regular processing times vary by service center and can take several months to over a year.

**Premium processing:**
- EB-1A and EB-1B: 15 business days
- EB-1C: 45 business days

Premium processing costs $2,965 as of March 1, 2026.

## Stage 3: Visa availability

Once the I-140 is approved, you need a visa number. Check the Visa Bulletin for EB-1 and your country of birth:

- **Most countries:** EB-1 is often current or close to current, meaning you can move to the next stage right away.
- **India and China:** EB-1 has a backlog. Your priority date (the date your I-140 was filed) must be current before you can file for adjustment or complete consular processing.

## Stage 4: Adjustment of status or consular processing

**Adjustment of status (in the U.S.):** File Form I-485, along with work permit and travel document applications. Processing typically takes several months to over a year.

**Consular processing (abroad):** After the National Visa Center processes your case, you attend an interview at a U.S. consulate.

USCIS guidance issued in May 2026 tells officers to approve adjustment of status for people in temporary status only in limited circumstances, while carving out some categories, such as dual intent visa holders. Check how that guidance affects you before choosing adjustment.

## EB-1 timeline for Indian applicants

For Indian nationals, the EB-1 wait can be several years. Some practical points:

- **File early.** Your priority date is set when the I-140 is filed.
- **Watch the Dates for Filing chart.** USCIS sometimes allows I-485 filing based on that chart, giving you a work permit and travel document earlier.
- **Maintain your nonimmigrant status.** H-1B holders can extend beyond six years with an approved I-140.
- **Consider porting.** If you later qualify for a different category, you can keep your earliest priority date in some cases.

## Typical EB-1 timeline estimates

For applicants from countries without a backlog, a realistic EB-1 timeline is often under two years from petition preparation to green card, and sometimes faster with premium processing. For Indian and Chinese nationals, the wait can stretch much longer.

## How to speed things up

1. Use premium processing for the I-140
2. Prepare strong evidence up front to avoid RFEs
3. File the I-485 as soon as you're eligible
4. Respond quickly to any RFE

## What porting and interfiling mean in practice

Applicants already in an EB-2 or EB-3 queue sometimes file an EB-1 petition to move to a faster line. If the EB-1 petition is approved, they may be able to keep their earlier priority date from an approved I-140 in another category, which can significantly reduce the wait. If an I-485 is already pending, USCIS can transfer the underlying basis to the new petition in some situations, which avoids starting over. These moves need careful timing, and each one depends on the facts of your existing filings.

## A realistic timeline example

An EB-1A applicant born in Brazil files an I-140 with premium processing in January, receives approval in February, and files an I-485 in March because EB-1 is current for her country. She receives a work permit and travel document while the I-485 is pending, attends an interview, and gets her green card roughly a year after filing. An applicant born in India with the same petition and approval date may receive the I-140 approval just as quickly, but then wait years for her priority date before she can file the I-485, all while maintaining H-1B or another status.

## Keeping options open during the wait

Indian and Chinese EB-1 applicants often keep building their record while they wait. A stronger record can support an EB-1A filing if the original petition was EB-1B or EB-1C, or a backup EB-2 NIW petition. The priority date from the first approved petition can usually be retained, so there's little downside to strengthening the case over time.
