Moving from an E-2 visa to a green card takes a separate immigrant case. The E-2 itself never converts into permanent residence, however many times you renew it or however well the business does. What the E-2 gives you is time and a U.S. business, and both can become the foundation for an immigrant petition: the EB-1C if your company has a related company abroad, the EB-2 National Interest Waiver if your work has national importance, the EB-5 if you can invest more and create ten jobs, or sponsorship through labor certification in some cases. The right choice depends on how the company is structured, which is why it pays to plan before you form the U.S. entity.

## Why an E-2 visa does not convert to a green card

The E-2 is a nonimmigrant visa. You must intend to depart when your E-2 status ends, though that is a lighter standard than for a visitor and you do not need a foreign residence you plan to return to. Filing a green card case does not automatically disqualify you from E-2 renewals, but a consular officer can weigh it, so the sequence of filings and trips matters. Our [investor and treaty visa](/practice-area/key-services/investor-and-treaty-visas) overview shows how the temporary and permanent routes fit together.

## Route 1: EB-1C, if your company has a related company abroad

The EB-1C is often the cleanest route for E-2 investors who also own a business at home. If the U.S. company is a subsidiary, affiliate, or branch of a foreign company that employed you as an executive or manager for at least one year in the three years before you came to the United States, and the U.S. company has done business for at least a year, the U.S. company can petition for you. No labor certification is required.

The catch is structure. The foreign company must still exist and the relationship must still qualify when the petition is filed. USCIS also looks closely at whether your U.S. role is genuinely managerial, with real staff below you. Our post on [EB-1C multinational manager requirements](/eb-1c-multinational-manager-requirements) explains the test, and our [EB-1C green card](/practice-area/key-services/eb-1c-green-card) page covers how we prepare those cases.

## Route 2: EB-2 National Interest Waiver

The NIW lets you petition for yourself, without an employer and without labor certification, if you hold an advanced degree or show exceptional ability and your proposed work meets the three-part test from Matter of Dhanasar: substantial merit and national importance, being well positioned to advance the work, and a balance of factors that favors waiving the job offer requirement. A founder whose company addresses something of national significance, such as health technology, energy, or critical manufacturing, may fit. A typical restaurant or retail franchise usually does not. Our post on the [three NIW prongs](/eb-2-niw-eligibility-three-prongs) walks through what USCIS tests, and our [EB-2 National Interest Waiver](/practice-area/key-services/eb-2-niw-green-card) page covers the filing.

## Route 3: EB-5, by investing more and creating jobs

If your E-2 business has grown, or you can invest more, the EB-5 may fit. It requires the statutory minimum investment, which is far higher than most E-2 investments, and the creation of at least ten full-time jobs for qualifying U.S. workers. A direct EB-5 in your own expanding business is possible, but the capital and job counts from your E-2 years need careful analysis, since not everything may count. Many E-2 investors instead invest separately through a regional center project.

## Route 4: Employer sponsorship through labor certification

E-2 employees who do not own the company can be sponsored like any other worker: PERM labor certification with the Department of Labor, then an EB-2 or EB-3 petition. For the owner-investor, this route is harder. The labor certification process asks whether the foreign worker has an ownership interest or influence over hiring, and when the beneficiary owns the company, the employer must show the job opportunity is genuinely open to U.S. workers. It can be done in some structures, but it rarely is the first choice for a controlling owner.

## Route 5: Other categories

Some E-2 investors qualify on their own record for the EB-1A extraordinary ability category, and some gain a family-based path through marriage to a U.S. citizen or through a relative. Those routes do not depend on the business at all and can run alongside it.

## Timing and travel while the green card case is pending

Two practical points catch E-2 investors off guard. First, if you file Form I-485 to adjust status in the United States, traveling abroad without advance parole is generally treated as abandoning the application. H-1B and L-1 holders have a special exception that lets them travel on their visas while an I-485 is pending; E-2 holders are not covered by it. Get the advance parole document before you travel.

Second, keep the E-2 business qualifying the whole time. Your E-2 status is your fallback if the immigrant case is delayed or denied, and the business must keep meeting the E-2 requirements at every renewal.
