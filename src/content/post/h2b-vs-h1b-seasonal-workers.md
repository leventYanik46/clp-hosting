The H-1B, H-2A, and H-2B visas all let U.S. employers hire foreign workers, but they solve different problems. The H-1B is for professional jobs that require a degree. The H-2A is for temporary or seasonal agricultural work. The H-2B is for temporary nonagricultural work such as landscaping, hospitality, seafood processing, and construction. Picking the wrong one wastes a filing season, so start with the job, not the visa.

## The core difference: the job

**H-1B** jobs are specialty occupations that require at least a bachelor's degree in a related field. Think engineers, analysts, accountants, and physicians. The worker can stay for years and the job can be permanent.

**H-2A** jobs are agricultural labor or services that are temporary or seasonal: planting, harvesting, livestock work tied to a season, and similar roles.

**H-2B** jobs are nonagricultural and temporary. The employer must show a one-time occurrence, a seasonal need, a peak-load need, or an intermittent need. A year-round housekeeping role at a hotel does not qualify. Extra summer staff at a beach resort may.

The difference between H-1B and H-2B is not about skill alone. It is about whether the need is temporary. A temporary need for a professional still points to H-1B. A permanent need for seasonal-type labor does not fit H-2B.

## Caps and timing

- **H-1B:** 85,000 new cap-subject visas per year, allocated by lottery in March for an October 1 start. Cap-exempt employers can file any time.
- **H-2B:** 66,000 per fiscal year, split between the first and second halves. Demand regularly exceeds supply, and DHS has sometimes released supplemental visas. Filing windows are tight and competitive.
- **H-2A:** No annual cap.

## Labor certification and wages

Both H-2 programs require a temporary labor certification from the Department of Labor before the I-129 is filed. The employer must recruit U.S. workers first and offer at least the required wage.

H-2A carries extra employer obligations, including free housing for workers who cannot reasonably return home each day, transportation, meals or kitchen facilities, and the Adverse Effect Wage Rate.

The H-1B uses a Labor Condition Application instead, which is faster but requires paying at least the prevailing or actual wage and keeping a public access file.

## Length of stay

- **H-1B:** Up to three years initially, extendable to six, with longer extensions possible for green card applicants.
- **H-2A and H-2B:** Tied to the season or need, generally up to one year at a time, with a maximum of three years before the worker must leave for a period.

## Cost considerations

H-1B petitions carry several government fees and, for certain workers outside the U.S., a $100,000 payment under the September 2025 proclamation. H-2 programs carry DOL and USCIS fees plus recruitment costs, and employers generally must cover visa and travel costs for workers. For large seasonal workforces, H-2 costs scale with headcount, so budget early.

## Which visa fits?

- Degree-level role, year-round or multi-year: **H-1B**
- Farm work tied to a season: **H-2A**
- Seasonal or peak-load work outside agriculture: **H-2B**


## Frequently asked questions

### Can an H-2B worker switch to H-1B?

Yes, if the worker has the required degree and the employer has a specialty occupation role and lottery selection or cap exemption. The H-2B and H-1B have different purposes, so the new petition must stand on its own.

### Can H-2 workers bring their families?

Spouses and children can come in H-4 status, but they cannot work.

### How early should an H-2B employer start?

The DOL process must start months before the date of need. Because of the cap, many employers plan for filing windows around the start of each half of the fiscal year and prepare recruitment and wage paperwork well in advance.

### Can the same worker return each season?

Yes. Returning H-2 workers are common. Each season needs a new labor certification and petition, and time in H-2 status counts toward the three-year maximum unless the worker spends enough time outside the U.S. to reset it.
