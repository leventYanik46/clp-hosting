The choice between EB-2 and EB-3 is largely decided by the job the employer is offering, not by how qualified you are. A person with a master's degree can still end up in EB-3 if the position only requires a bachelor's degree. Understanding that rule is the starting point for comparing the two categories and their wait times.

## What EB-2 requires

EB-2 is for professionals whose job requires an advanced degree, or for people of exceptional ability. In practice that means:

- A master's degree or higher, or a bachelor's degree plus at least five years of progressive experience in the field, and
- A job offer where the position itself requires that level of education or experience.

The employer normally files a PERM labor certification and then an I-140 petition. The national interest waiver is an exception, since it skips the job offer and PERM.

## What EB-3 covers

EB-3 has three groups:

1. **Professionals.** The job requires at least a bachelor's degree, and the worker has it.
2. **Skilled workers.** The job requires at least two years of training or experience, not counting seasonal work.
3. **Other workers.** Unskilled positions requiring less than two years of training or experience.

EB-3 also uses PERM and an I-140. The legal requirements are lower than EB-2, so EB-3 can reach a larger group of workers, including many who cannot qualify for EB-2.

## Why the job controls the category

The labor certification describes the job's minimum requirements. If the employer says the position needs a bachelor's degree plus two years of experience, that cannot become an EB-2 case, even if you hold a doctorate. You qualify under the position's requirements, not under your personal credentials alone.

This is why employers and attorneys discuss the job description carefully at the start. The wording affects category, wait time and the strength of the PERM case.

## Wait times and per-country limits

Both categories are subject to annual limits and per-country caps, which creates a backlog for some countries of birth. The wait for an EB-2 priority date can be different from the wait for EB-3, and the order sometimes flips. For some countries, EB-3 has moved faster than EB-2 for periods of time. The Visa Bulletin shows the current dates for each category.

So when people ask how long an EB-3 visa takes, the answer has three parts: the PERM timeline, the I-140 timeline, and the wait for your priority date to become current. The last part depends on your country of birth and the category.

## Can you use both

Some people file under EB-3 while an EB-2 case is also pending, or move between them when dates change. If your I-140 has already been approved in one category, it is sometimes possible to use that priority date in a different one. Rules are technical, so ask a lawyer before you try it.

## What to ask your employer and attorney

- What are the minimum requirements in the job description, and do they match the category you want?
- Is the employer willing to file in more than one category?
- How is the Visa Bulletin affecting my country of birth right now?
- If the I-140 is approved, what happens to my priority date if I change jobs?

Choosing well is mostly a matter of matching the position to the person, then checking timing against the latest bulletin.
