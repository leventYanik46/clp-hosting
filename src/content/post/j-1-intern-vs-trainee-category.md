The J-1 intern and J-1 trainee categories look alike from the outside: both involve a structured, supervised placement with a U.S. host company, both run through a State Department designated sponsor, and both require a Form DS-7002 training plan. The dividing line is where you are in your career. An intern is a current student or a graduate within the last 12 months. A trainee has either a degree plus one year of related work experience outside the United States or five years of related experience without a degree. If you fit both, the choice turns on duration and on what you plan to do afterward.

## Eligibility for each category

For the intern category, you must be enrolled full time at a post-secondary institution outside the United States, or have graduated from one no more than 12 months before your program start date. The internship must relate to your field of study. There is no minimum work experience.

For the trainee category, you must hold a degree or professional certificate from a foreign post-secondary institution and have at least one year of prior related work experience acquired outside the United States, or have five years of related work experience acquired outside the United States. The training must be in your occupational field. Both categories require sufficient English to function in the placement, which sponsors test by interview or a recognized exam.

Host companies sometimes try to place a recent graduate as a trainee to get the longer period. Sponsors check the dates on diplomas and employment letters against the start date, and a mismatch stops the case. ## Duration limits

Interns receive up to 12 months. Trainees receive up to 18 months, with one exception: trainees in hospitality and tourism are limited to 12 months unless the program includes at least six months of rotation through different departments. Neither category can be extended beyond its maximum, and the program start and end dates on the DS-2019 control everything, including your 30-day grace period after completion.

Repeat participation has its own waiting rules. A former intern can do a second internship if they remain a student or recent graduate, and can later return as a trainee once they meet the experience threshold. A former trainee generally must spend two years outside the United States before a new trainee program; the regulations allow an additional program sooner only in narrow circumstances tied to more advanced skills or a different field, and sponsors read those circumstances differently, so confirm eligibility with the sponsor before planning a second program. These counting rules are where many applicants make assumptions that the sponsor will not share.

## The DS-7002 training plan

The Training/Internship Placement Plan is the center of the application. It must describe, phase by phase, what you will learn, how you will be supervised, what methods will be used, and how progress will be evaluated. For trainee programs it must show that the training builds on your prior experience rather than teaching entry-level work. Sponsors review the host company's capacity: staffing, insurance, physical space, and whether the host has the resources to deliver each phase.

Two content rules apply to both categories:

- Clerical work cannot exceed 20 percent of the program. Filing, data entry, answering phones, and similar duties are allowed only in that proportion.
- Unskilled or casual labor is prohibited, as is any placement that involves patient care or contact, child care, or elder care. Positions that displace U.S. workers or fill ordinary staffing gaps are also barred.

Host companies that treat the placement as a cheap hire run into site visits, which sponsors must conduct for hosts with fewer than 25 employees or less than three million dollars in annual revenue. ## Costs, insurance, and the two-year rule

Sponsors charge program fees, and the participant pays the SEVIS fee and the visa application fee; check the current amounts with the sponsor and the consulate rather than relying on older figures. Participants must carry health insurance meeting the State Department minimums for the full program. Many host companies pay a stipend; some pay a full salary. Payment is permitted and does not convert the program into employment, as long as the activity matches the DS-7002.

Section 212(e), the two-year home residency requirement, can attach to intern and trainee programs when the field appears on the Skills List for your country or when government funding is involved. Our explanation of [who is subject to the two-year home residency rule](/j-1-two-year-home-residency-requirement) walks through the triggers. Check this before you accept a placement, because it governs whether you can move to H-1B or a green card afterward.

## Which one to choose if you qualify for both

A graduate who finished within the past year and has a year of related foreign experience can be either. The trainee category gives six extra months and signals a higher skill level, which can help if the host later wants to sponsor an H-1B. The intern category is cheaper for some sponsors and keeps the door open to a later trainee program. Also consider the [Summer Work and Travel program](/work-and-travel-2026-expenses-things-students-want-to-know), which is a separate J-1 category for current students who want seasonal work rather than career training.
