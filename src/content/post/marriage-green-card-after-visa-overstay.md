Yes, in the most common case. A foreign national who entered the United States on a visa, stayed past the date on the I-94, and later married a U.S. citizen can usually get a marriage green card after overstaying, without leaving the country and without a waiver. The law excuses immediate relatives of citizens from the bars that an overstay creates for almost everyone else. The answer changes, sometimes completely, when the spouse is a permanent resident rather than a citizen, when the entry was without inspection, or when the overstay comes with a misrepresentation or a removal order.

## Why an overstay does not block the spouse of a citizen

Adjustment of status under section 245(a) of the Immigration and Nationality Act requires that the applicant was inspected and admitted or paroled. A visa entry satisfies that. Section 245(c) then bars people who are out of status, worked without authorization or failed to maintain status, but it carves out immediate relatives: spouses, parents and unmarried children under 21 of U.S. citizens. That is the whole reason a marriage green card after overstaying works. The overstay is not forgiven as a matter of grace; it simply is not a ground of ineligibility for this group.

Two practical consequences follow. First, the spouse should not leave the United States before the green card is approved. Departure after more than 180 days of unlawful presence triggers a three-year bar, and after a year a ten-year bar, which is a problem adjustment avoids entirely by keeping the spouse here. Second, the file should be complete and accurate about the overstay. Our [marriage-based green card](/practice-area/key-services/marriage-based-green-card) team lists the dates plainly on the I-485; hiding them creates the misrepresentation problem that the overstay itself did not.

## The cases where the answer is different

Spouse of a permanent resident. The F2A category is not an immediate relative category, so section 245(c) applies in full. A spouse who overstayed generally cannot adjust unless protected by section 245(i), and leaving for a consular interview triggers the unlawful presence bar. The usual solutions are to wait for the petitioner to naturalize, which converts the case to an immediate relative petition, or to pursue a consular case with a provisional waiver. The wait for an F2A visa number is explained in [priority date vs filing date](/priority-date-vs-filing-date).

Entry without inspection. An overstay presupposes an admission. A spouse who crossed without inspection was never admitted, so section 245(a) is not satisfied, and marriage to a citizen does not cure it. That spouse finishes the case at a consulate, usually after a Form I-601A waiver; see [how the I-601A provisional waiver works](/i-601a-provisional-waiver-explained).

A prior removal order, a false claim to citizenship or fraud at entry. None of these is an overstay problem, but they often travel with one. A removal order requires its own motion or waiver. A false claim to U.S. citizenship made after September 30, 1996 is generally not waivable. Fraud or misrepresentation needs a Form I-601 waiver based on extreme hardship to a citizen or resident spouse or parent. Each must be identified before filing, which is why we review every entry, exit and encounter with immigration authorities first.

## Visa Waiver Program entrants

Someone who entered under the Visa Waiver Program signed away the right to contest removal except through asylum. Immediate relatives who overstay a visa waiver entry can still generally adjust, and USCIS policy permits it, but the position is weaker than a visa overstay: the person has no right to a hearing if the case is denied. These files should be strong and filed promptly, and the spouse should not travel.

## The timing of the marriage and the 90-day rule

An overstay and a quick marriage together draw scrutiny of intent at entry. The Department of State presumes misrepresentation when someone who entered as a visitor marries or files for status within 90 days of entry, and while USCIS does not apply that presumption as a rule, its officers ask the same question. If the relationship began before the visitor entry, be ready to show that the plan to stay developed afterward. If the couple met after entry, document the timeline. Our post on [bona fide marriage evidence](/bona-fide-marriage-evidence-uscis) covers the proof that answers both the fraud question and the intent question.

## How the case proceeds

Once the overstay analysis is clear, the case is a standard concurrent filing: Form I-130 with Form I-130A, Form I-485 with the medical exam, Form I-864 for support, and Forms I-765 and I-131 for the work permit and advance parole. The sequence is laid out in [marriage-based adjustment of status, step by step](/marriage-based-adjustment-of-status-steps). The pending I-485 places the spouse in a period of authorized stay, so unlawful presence stops accruing on the filing date, and the work permit allows lawful employment while the case waits.

If an overstayed spouse has already received a Notice to Appear, the marriage petition can still move forward, but adjustment is decided by the immigration judge, and a marriage entered after proceedings began must be proven genuine by clear and convincing evidence.
