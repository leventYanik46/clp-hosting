For many professionals, the choice between the O-1 vs H-1B comes down to a tradeoff: the H-1B has a lower evidence bar but a lottery, while the O-1 has no lottery but requires proof of extraordinary ability. The right answer depends on your record, your timeline, and how much flexibility you need.

## The short version

| | H-1B | O-1 |
|---|---|---|
| Lottery or cap | Yes, for most new employers | No |
| Who qualifies | Bachelor's degree or equivalent in a specialty occupation | Extraordinary ability, sustained acclaim |
| Evidence needed | Degree and job requirements | Awards, publications, press, high salary, expert letters |
| Initial stay | Up to 3 years | Up to 3 years |
| Maximum stay | 6 years, with limited extensions | No fixed limit, renewable in 1-year increments |
| Self-employment | Limited | Possible through an agent or own company |
| Dual intent | Yes | Effectively yes in practice |

## How the H-1B works

The H-1B is for jobs that require at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field. Most employers must register in the annual lottery each March. If selected, they can file a petition starting April 1, with employment beginning October 1. Cap-exempt employers, such as universities and affiliated nonprofit research organizations, can file at any time.

The evidence requirement is modest. You need a qualifying degree, a job that requires it, and an employer that pays the prevailing wage. The challenge is the lottery itself. Selection rates have varied widely in recent years, and changes to the selection process and new fees on some petitions have shifted the math for employers. A September 2025 presidential proclamation added a large payment requirement for certain new H-1B petitions, mainly for workers outside the U.S., so cost is now part of the decision for some employers.

## How the O-1 works

The O-1A covers extraordinary ability in sciences, business, education, or athletics. The O-1B covers the arts, film, and television. To qualify for an O-1A, you generally must meet at least three of eight criteria, including:

- Nationally or internationally recognized awards
- Membership in associations requiring outstanding achievement
- Published material about you
- Judging the work of others
- Original contributions of major significance
- Scholarly articles
- Employment in a critical role for distinguished organizations
- High salary compared with others in the field

There is no lottery and no cap. An O-1 petition can be filed any time of year, and premium processing gives a decision within 15 business days.

## When the O-1 is the better fit

- **You weren't selected in the H-1B lottery** and your record is strong enough to clear the O-1 bar.
- **You're a founder.** An O-1 can be sponsored by your own company or by an agent, which makes it more workable for startup founders than the H-1B.
- **You've used up most of your H-1B time.** The O-1 doesn't count against the six-year H-1B limit.
- **You don't have a traditional degree** but have an exceptional track record.

## When the H-1B is the better fit

- **Your record is solid but not exceptional.** A strong engineer with a good career may qualify easily for H-1B but struggle to meet three O-1 criteria.
- **You're early in your career.** Many recent graduates simply haven't had time to build the evidence for an O-1.
- **You want a predictable path to a green card through PERM.** Both visas work with green card filings, but H-1B extensions beyond six years are available once certain green card steps are underway, which gives long-term stability during backlogs.

## Pursuing both

Many professionals pursue both at once. An employer might register you in the H-1B lottery while preparing an O-1 petition as a backup. If the lottery comes through, you may still choose the O-1 if it offers a better fit, or keep the H-1B and save the O-1 evidence for an EB-1A green card later. The two categories rely on similar evidence, so building an O-1 record often strengthens a future EB-1A or EB-2 NIW petition too.

## Costs and timelines

The base filing fee for an O-1 petition is higher than for an H-1B petition, and attorney fees for O-1 cases are usually higher because building the evidence takes more work. On the other hand, an O-1 can be approved and in use within weeks, while an H-1B starting from the lottery takes six months or more.

## Choosing between them

Start by honestly assessing your evidence against the O-1 criteria. If you can meet three criteria with documents rather than adjectives, the O-1 deserves a serious look. If you can't, focus on the H-1B lottery and cap-exempt opportunities, and start building the record that would support an O-1 or EB-1A later.
