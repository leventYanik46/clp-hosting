The one-year asylum deadline defeats more claims than any question about persecution. INA 208(a)(2)(B) requires you to show, by clear and convincing evidence, that Form I-589 was filed within one year of your last arrival in the United States. Miss it and the asylum officer or immigration judge must deny asylum regardless of how strong the fear is, unless you fit one of two statutory exceptions: changed circumstances or extraordinary circumstances. Both are proven, not presumed, and both require you to file within a reasonable period after the circumstance arises. Our [asylum and humanitarian protection practice](/practice-area/key-services/asylum-and-humanitarian-protection) starts every late case with the deadline analysis, because it decides whether the claim is for asylum or for the narrower protection that remains.

## How the one-year asylum deadline is counted

The year runs from your last arrival, not your first. If you entered in 2019, left, and returned in 2023, the 2023 entry starts the clock. The date is the day you physically arrived, whether or not you were inspected. For an affirmative filing, the application is filed when USCIS receives it, not when you mail it, so a package that arrives on day 366 is late. In immigration court, it is filed when the court accepts it.

You carry the burden of proving the arrival date by clear and convincing evidence. A passport stamp or an I-94 record does that easily. If you entered without inspection, the proof is harder: bus tickets, money transfer receipts, phone records, a dated photo, a sworn statement from the person who received you. Build that file early; a judge who cannot pin down the arrival date will not reach the exceptions.

Unaccompanied children are exempt from the deadline entirely under INA 208(a)(2)(E).

## Changed circumstances

The regulation at 8 CFR 208.4(a)(4) describes changed circumstances as changes that materially affect your eligibility for asylum. They fall into three groups.

- Changes in your country: a coup, a new law targeting your religion or ethnic group, the election of the party that persecuted you, the outbreak of conflict in your region, a family member arrested or killed after you left.
- Changes in your own circumstances, including activities you took up outside your country: public political activism, religious conversion, coming out, publishing criticism of the government, a diagnosis that makes you a target.
- Loss of the relationship that made you a derivative: a divorce from the principal applicant, or a child turning 21 or marrying, so that you now need your own claim.

The change must be material, which means it must matter to the outcome. Evidence that conditions got somewhat worse in a country that was already dangerous when you arrived does not reset the clock. Dated news reports, country-condition reports, the threatening messages themselves, a baptism certificate, a membership card, or a letter from an organization you joined are the kind of proof that works.

## Extraordinary circumstances

The regulation at 8 CFR 208.4(a)(5) lists events that excuse a late filing when they directly caused the delay and you did not create them intentionally. The list is not exhaustive, but it is where most successful arguments sit: serious illness or a mental or physical disability during the first year, including the effects of the persecution itself; a legal disability such as being a minor or having a mental impairment; ineffective assistance of a prior lawyer or representative; maintaining lawful immigrant or nonimmigrant status, Temporary Protected Status, or parole until a reasonable period before filing; a timely application that USCIS rejected as improperly filed and that you refiled within a reasonable period; and the death or serious illness of your legal representative or an immediate family member.

### The lawful status exception

The most common extraordinary circumstance in our practice is a student or worker who feared return from the day they arrived but stayed in F-1 or H-1B status for years. The exception covers you if you maintained that status until a reasonable period before filing. The safest reading is to file while you are still in status. Filing months after the status ends invites a finding that the period was not reasonable.

### The ineffective assistance exception

Blaming a prior representative requires more than saying so. You must submit an affidavit describing the agreement with the representative and what went wrong, show that you notified the representative and gave them a chance to respond, and state whether you filed a complaint with the relevant bar or disciplinary authority, or explain why not. Courts take these requirements seriously, and an incomplete showing is treated as no showing.

## What counts as a reasonable period

Neither the statute nor the regulation sets a number of days. The Board of Immigration Appeals has refused to adopt an automatic grace period, and it has held delays of several months unreasonable when the applicant offered no explanation for them. The practical rule is that every week between the triggering event and the filing must be accounted for. If a change in country conditions happened in March, a filing in April is easy to defend and a filing in November needs a story. When a client comes to us past the year, we often file a lean but complete I-589 immediately and supplement the evidence afterward, because a filing date you can defend is worth more than a polished package you cannot.

## When no exception fits

A late filing with no exception ends the asylum claim, not the case. Withholding of removal and protection under the Convention Against Torture have no filing deadline. They require a higher showing of harm and are decided only by an immigration judge, and they do not lead to a green card, but they stop a removal to the country where you face persecution. Our comparison of [asylum, withholding of removal, and CAT protection](/asylum-vs-withholding-of-removal-cat) explains the trade-offs. Depending on your history, a U visa, a VAWA self-petition, or cancellation of removal may also be in play, and our [removal defense team](/practice-area/key-services/removal-defense) presents them together with the protection claims.

One more point on strategy. The deadline question is decided alongside the merits, not before them. An officer or judge who finds your account credible and your fear real has more room to accept a reasonable-period argument than one who doubts the claim. Prepare the [I-589 and the supporting evidence](/removal-asylum-u-s-asylum-process-legal-advice) as carefully as you would for a timely case, and treat the deadline argument as one part of the file rather than the whole of it.
