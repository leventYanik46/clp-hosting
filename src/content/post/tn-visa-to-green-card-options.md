Yes, you can get a green card while on a TN visa, and many Canadian and Mexican professionals do. The complication is that TN status requires temporary intent. Unlike the H-1B or L-1, the TN is not a dual intent status, so each time you apply for admission, renew your TN or request an extension, the officer can ask whether you really plan to leave. Moving from a TN visa to a green card is mostly a timing exercise: choosing the right route and filing in an order that keeps your status safe until the green card case can carry you.

## Why temporary intent matters

To receive TN status, you must show that your stay in the United States is temporary. There is no requirement to keep a home abroad, but an officer who believes you intend to stay permanently can refuse admission or an extension. A green card case is, by definition, evidence of an intent to stay.

There is no rule that a PERM labor certification or an I-140 petition automatically ends TN eligibility. In practice, the risk rises with each stage: low when the employer is only preparing the PERM, higher once an I-140 is approved, and highest after the I-485 adjustment application is filed. The risk is also concentrated at border crossings and TN renewals, where a CBP or consular officer decides the question in a few minutes. ## Route one: employer sponsorship through PERM

The most common route for TN holders is employer sponsorship. The employer gets a prevailing wage determination, recruits to test the labor market, files Form ETA-9089 with the Department of Labor, then files Form I-140 with USCIS. When your priority date is current under the Visa Bulletin, you file Form I-485. Our [PERM labor certification process guide](/perm-labor-certification-process) covers each step, and our [employer-sponsored green card](/practice-area/key-services/business-employment-immigration-solutions) practice runs these cases for TN employers.

The PERM stage is usually the longest. During that time, renew your TN before the PERM is filed if a renewal is due soon, and keep international travel to what you need. Some TN holders choose to extend through a USCIS filing rather than at the border during this period.

## Route two: switch to H-1B first

Many TN holders move to H-1B status before the green card case reaches the sensitive stages. Because the H-1B is dual intent, travel and extensions are no longer tied to temporary intent, and H-1B workers can extend beyond the usual six years while the green card case is pending. The obstacle is the annual H-1B lottery, unless the employer is cap-exempt. Our comparison of [TN and H-1B for Canadian and Mexican professionals](/tn-visa-vs-h-1b-comparison) explains when the switch is worth it.

## Route three: self-petitions and other categories

Some TN holders qualify for a green card without employer sponsorship. An EB-2 national interest waiver or an EB-1A extraordinary ability petition is filed by you, not the employer, and skips PERM. Managers who worked for a foreign affiliate of their U.S. employer may fit the EB-1C category. These routes carry the same intent question, because the I-140 and I-485 still show an intent to immigrate, but they often move faster, which shortens the window of risk.

Family-based routes, such as marriage to a U.S. citizen, follow different rules. They raise separate questions about intent at the time of entry and should be reviewed with counsel before you travel or file.

## Filing the I-485 and protecting your status

When your priority date is current, file the I-485 with Form I-765 for a work permit and Form I-131 for advance parole. Once the I-485 is on file, most TN holders stop relying on TN status for travel, because a pending adjustment application is clear evidence of immigrant intent. You can travel on advance parole and work on the adjustment-based work permit instead. If you travel on your TN while an I-485 is pending, you risk being refused admission, and leaving without advance parole can cause USCIS to treat the I-485 as abandoned.

If you will complete the green card through consular processing abroad, plan your last entries on TN with care. The officer at a renewal may see the immigrant visa case.

## A practical TN to green card sequence

A common plan looks like this: renew your TN for the full three years before the PERM is filed; let the employer file the PERM and I-140 while you limit travel; register for the H-1B lottery if the wait for your priority date looks long; and file the I-485 as soon as the date is current. The right order depends on your country of birth, your employer and your travel needs, so it is worth mapping before the first filing.
