When an investor or employee holds E-2 status, the spouse and unmarried children under 21 can come too. Families often search for the "E21 visa," which is the consular code for the E-2 spouse. Since 2022, E-2 spouse work authorization has become much simpler, but children face a hard limit at age 21 that catches many families off guard.

## E-2 spouse work authorization

E-2 spouses are authorized to work incident to their status. Before 2022, spouses had to apply for an Employment Authorization Document (EAD) and wait months. USCIS changed its policy so that the spouse's I-94 record serves as proof of work authorization.

What to check:

- **The I-94 annotation.** Spouses admitted after the policy change should have an I-94 showing the E-2S class of admission. That I-94 is acceptable for Form I-9 as evidence of work authorization.
- **Older I-94 records.** Spouses admitted before the change may have a plain E-2 I-94. In that case, they may need to reenter the U.S. or request a corrected I-94 to get the E-2S annotation.
- **Any employer.** E-2 spouses can work for any employer, start their own business, or work part-time. They don't need to work for the E-2 company.

An EAD remains optional. Some spouses still apply for one because it doubles as a photo ID and can make employer onboarding easier.

## E-2 children: school, but no work

Unmarried children under 21 hold E-2 dependent status. They can attend public or private school at any level, including college, without changing to F-1 student status. This is a real advantage, since in-state tuition rules vary by state and E-2 children can sometimes qualify depending on residency rules.

E-2 children cannot work. Part-time jobs, paid internships, and summer work are not allowed. Unpaid volunteer activity is generally fine, but be careful with "volunteer" roles that look like unpaid employment.

## The age-21 problem

E-2 dependent status ends when a child turns 21. There is no extension. Unlike some green card categories, the Child Status Protection Act doesn't help in nonimmigrant status. A child who turns 21 while in college needs a new status to remain in the U.S. lawfully.

Common options include:

- **F-1 student status,** filed as a change of status before the 21st birthday, or obtained through a visa abroad.
- **Their own E-2 status,** if they are a treaty national and can qualify as an investor or as an employee of a treaty company of the same nationality.
- **H-1B or another work visa** after graduation, if they have a qualifying job offer.

Plan this well before the birthday. Processing times for a change of status can be long, and the child must maintain status while the application is pending.

## Why the green card question matters for families

The E-2 visa has no direct path to a green card. For families with children approaching 21, this is often the deciding factor in whether to start a green card process. If the investor files for an EB-5 or employer-based green card, children can be included as derivatives if they are under 21 when the case is filed (with Child Status Protection Act calculations applying in some cases). Once a child ages out, they would need their own petition.

## Travel and reentry

E-2 dependents usually need their own E-2 visa stamps to travel internationally and reenter. Each dependent's visa is tied to the principal's status, so if the principal's E-2 ends, the family's status ends too. After a divorce, the former spouse loses E-2 dependent status and needs a new basis to remain.

## A quick planning checklist

1. Confirm each spouse's I-94 shows the E-2S annotation if they plan to work.
2. Note each child's 21st birthday and set a reminder at least a year in advance.
3. Decide whether a green card process is worth starting to protect children's eligibility.
4. Keep the principal's E-2 status current, since every dependent's status depends on it.

## What happens to E-2 dependents if the business is sold or closes

Dependent status rises and falls with the principal. If the investor sells the business, closes it, or stops directing it, the investor's E-2 basis ends and so does every dependent's. A spouse who has built a career on E-2S work authorization can lose the right to work overnight. Families in that position usually need a new plan before the sale closes, such as a new E-2 investment, a change to another status for the spouse in their own right, or a planned departure.

The same is true for E-2 employees' families. If the employee changes jobs to a company that does not share the treaty nationality, the new employer cannot use E-2, and the family's status needs a different foundation.

## A note on spouses who want to study

E-2 spouses can attend school without changing status, including degree programs. That makes E-2S a flexible status for a spouse who wants to retrain or earn a U.S. credential while working part-time. Just keep in mind that the education doesn't create a separate status, so the spouse still depends on the principal's E-2.
