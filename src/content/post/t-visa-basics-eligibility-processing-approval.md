The T visa is immigration protection for survivors of severe forms of human trafficking who are in the United States because of that trafficking. It offers temporary status, work authorization and a path to a green card, and it is meant to support victims who help in the investigation of the crime. If you think you may qualify, a T visa attorney can help you assess your options safely.

If you or someone you know needs help right now, the National Human Trafficking Hotline can be reached at 1-888-373-7888.

## Who qualifies

To be eligible for a T visa, you generally must show that:

- **You are or were a victim of a severe form of trafficking in persons.** This includes sex trafficking in which a commercial sex act is induced by force, fraud or coercion, or in which the person is under 18, and labor trafficking, which involves recruiting, harboring or transporting a person for labor or services through force, fraud or coercion for involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage or slavery.
- **You are physically present in the United States,** American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands or at a port of entry because of the trafficking.
- **You have complied with reasonable requests from law enforcement** for help in investigating or prosecuting the trafficking. There are exceptions for people under 18 and for those who cannot cooperate because of trauma.
- **You would suffer extreme hardship involving unusual and severe harm** if you were removed from the United States.
- **You are admissible,** or can qualify for a waiver.

Trafficking does not require that you were moved across a border. It is about the use of force, fraud or coercion to exploit someone.

## How to apply

The application is Form I-914. You submit it with evidence, which usually includes:

- A personal statement describing what happened
- Evidence of trafficking, such as records, messages, photographs or witness statements
- Proof of cooperation with law enforcement, or an explanation of why you could not cooperate
- Evidence of the hardship you would face on removal
- Identity documents

A declaration from a law enforcement agency, known as Supplement B, can help but is not required. Some family members may qualify as derivatives and can apply with Form I-914 Supplement A.

## Approval rates

People often ask about the T visa approval rate. USCIS publishes data on how many T visa applications it receives, approves and denies. These numbers are a general guide and not a prediction. They include well-prepared and weak applications, and they change from year to year. What matters more is how well your own facts fit the legal requirements, and how well you can document them. A lawyer familiar with trafficking cases can give you a candid view.

## Processing time

USCIS posts processing times for the I-914 on its website, and they change with workload. Expect months or longer. You can also learn about interim steps, such as a bona fide determination, which can allow work authorization for some applicants while the case is pending. Check the current USCIS guidance for details, because the process changes.

## After approval

T status generally lasts for four years. You can apply for a work permit. You may apply for a green card after holding status for a period, or sooner if the investigation is complete, and if you meet the other requirements. Derivative family members may also be eligible.

## Confidentiality and safety

Federal law protects the confidentiality of information about victims in these cases. Be careful about who you share information with, and avoid helpers who are not licensed attorneys or accredited representatives.
