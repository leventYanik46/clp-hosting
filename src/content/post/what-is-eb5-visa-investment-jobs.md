The EB-5 visa is the only U.S. green card you can obtain by investing capital, regardless of your nationality or profession. The current investment amounts are $1,050,000, or $800,000 if the project is in a targeted employment area, and the investment has to create at least ten full-time jobs for U.S. workers. Everything else in an EB-5 case is about proving those three things: the money, where it went, and the jobs.

## Who the EB-5 visa is for

EB-5 suits investors who want permanent residence for themselves, their spouse, and their unmarried children under 21, without depending on an employer or a family petition. You do not need a degree, a job offer, or English skills. You do need lawfully sourced funds and the willingness to keep them at risk for the required period.

## The two investment amounts

The EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022 set the current figures:

- **$1,050,000** for a standard investment.
- **$800,000** for an investment in a targeted employment area (TEA) or an infrastructure project.

A TEA is either a rural area or an area of high unemployment, generally at least 150% of the national average. Under the 2022 law, USCIS rather than state governments determines whether a high-unemployment area qualifies. Because the TEA status of a project affects the amount you invest, confirm it before you commit, not after.

The law also set aside visa numbers for rural projects, high-unemployment projects, and infrastructure projects. For investors from countries with long EB-5 backlogs, these reserved categories can mean a much shorter wait than the unreserved line.

## Direct investment or regional center

You can invest in two ways.

**Direct investment.** You put money into a business you are involved in, often one you control. The jobs must be direct W-2 jobs at that business, counted in actual employees. This route gives you control but puts the hiring burden on you.

**Regional center investment.** You invest in a project sponsored by a USCIS-designated regional center. Jobs can be counted indirectly through economic modeling, which makes the ten-job test easier to meet for large projects. You give up control and depend on the developer's execution and on the regional center's compliance with the 2022 integrity rules.

Neither route is better across the board. Direct investment favors entrepreneurs who want to run the business. Regional center investment favors people who want the green card with less day-to-day involvement.

## How the job creation rule works

Each EB-5 investor must create at least ten full-time positions, meaning at least 35 hours a week, for qualifying employees. Qualifying employees are U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and others authorized to work, but not the investor or the investor's spouse and children.

Timing matters. You need a credible plan to create the jobs when you file the initial petition, and proof the jobs exist, or will exist within a reasonable time, when you file to remove conditions.

## The EB-5 process in brief

1. **Source the funds and document them.** Every dollar has to be traced to a lawful source: salary, sale of property, a business, a gift, or a loan secured by your own assets.
2. **File the petition.** Regional center investors file Form I-526E; direct investors file Form I-526.
3. **Apply for the green card.** Through adjustment of status if you are in the U.S. and eligible, or through consular processing abroad.
4. **Live as a conditional resident for two years.**
5. **File Form I-829** to remove conditions, showing the investment was sustained and the jobs were created.

## Where EB-5 cases go wrong

- **Weak source-of-funds evidence.** Cash savings without records, gifts from relatives who cannot document their own income, and currency transfers through informal channels are the most common problems.
- **Assuming a project's TEA status is permanent.** It is measured at a specific point in time.
- **Returning capital too early.** Funds generally must remain at risk through the required period.
- **Choosing a project for the advertised return rather than the immigration structure.** The green card depends on job creation, not profit.

## When to bring in an EB-5 attorney

An EB-5 attorney should be involved before you transfer funds, not after. Counsel reviews where your money comes from, how it will move, whether the project documents meet the 2022 requirements, and which visa category gives you the shortest realistic wait. An immigration lawyer represents you, not the project, which matters when the sponsor's interests and yours diverge.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I borrow money for an EB-5 investment?
Yes, if the loan is secured by your own assets, such as property you own, and you are personally liable for repayment. A loan secured by the project's own assets does not count as your capital.

### Does my spouse need to invest separately?
No. One investment covers the investor, the spouse, and unmarried children under 21. Each family member files their own green card application as a derivative.
