An H-2A employer must provide housing at no cost to every H-2A worker, and every worker in corresponding employment, who cannot reasonably return to their permanent home at the end of the workday. The housing must meet the applicable federal, state, or local standards, and it has to be inspected and approved before DOL will certify the labor application. That makes the H-2A housing requirements a critical path item, not a detail to sort out later: no approved housing means no certification, and no certification means no workers. Our [H-2A visa team](/practice-area/key-services/h-2a-visa) treats housing as the first logistics question in every new season.

## Who is entitled to free housing

The duty covers every H-2A worker and every U.S. worker doing the same work who was recruited from outside normal commuting distance. A local U.S. worker who goes home each night does not need employer housing, but a U.S. worker who came from another state for the season does, on the same terms as the H-2A crew.

The housing must be free. No rent, no deposits, and no utility charges that turn free housing into paid housing. Where it is the prevailing practice in the area and occupation to offer family housing, the employer must provide it to workers with families who request it.

## Which H-2A housing standards apply

The applicable standard depends on who owns or controls the housing.

### Housing the employer owns or controls

Employer-provided housing must meet one of two federal standards, depending mainly on when it was built: OSHA's temporary labor camp standards, or an older set of DOL Employment and Training Administration standards that still apply to some older housing. Both cover the same core issues. The site must drain well and be free of hazards. There must be a safe water supply, adequate toilets, showers, and laundry facilities, proper sewage and garbage disposal, enough sleeping space for the number of occupants, a separate bed for each worker, heat where the weather calls for it, screens, lighting, and fire safety measures.

### Rental and public accommodations

If the employer houses workers in rental property or public accommodations such as motels, the housing must meet local standards for that type of housing. Where there are no local standards, state standards apply; where there are neither, federal standards apply. The employer is still responsible for the housing, pays for it directly, and must make sure that occupancy limits, sanitation, and cooking facilities meet the rules. Handing workers a list of local motels does not satisfy the obligation.

## The pre-certification inspection

The state workforce agency, or another authorized agency, inspects employer-provided housing before DOL issues the labor certification. In most states, the inspection is tied to each season's application, so last year's approval does not automatically carry forward. For rental or public accommodations, the employer may need to show documentation that the property meets local or state requirements.

Book the inspection as soon as the job order is filed. Our guide to [the H-2A process and timeline](/h-2a-visa-process-timeline) shows how housing approval fits into the 60-to-75-day job order window and the 45-day application deadline. An inspection that is scheduled late, or that finds a problem with only a few weeks to go, is one of the most common reasons workers arrive after the date of need.

## Meals and cooking facilities

Housing and meals are linked. If the employer does not provide three meals a day, the housing must include free and convenient cooking and kitchen facilities. A motel room with no kitchen works only if the employer provides meals, and meal charges are capped at a daily amount DOL publishes. Our article on [what an H-2A employer has to pay](/h-2a-wage-requirements-employers) covers meals alongside the other costs of the season.

## Mistakes that hold up certification

Most housing problems are planning failures rather than legal disputes:

- Booking housing late, after the job order has gone in
- More workers than the beds and square footage allow under the applicable standard
- No cooking facilities in housing where the employer is not providing meals
- Missing paperwork for rental housing that the inspector or DOL requests
- Assuming last season's inspection still covers this season
- Charging deposits, utilities, or cleaning fees to workers

## Housing after the workers arrive

The obligation continues through the season. DOL's Wage and Hour Division and OSHA can inspect housing during the contract period, and a crowded or broken-down camp in July is a violation even if it passed inspection in April. H-2A workers themselves are excluded from the Migrant and Seasonal Agricultural Worker Protection Act, but U.S. migrant workers living in the same housing may be covered by it, which adds a second set of housing safety rules. Keep maintenance logs and fix problems in writing.
