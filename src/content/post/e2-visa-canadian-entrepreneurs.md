Canadian citizens are eligible for the E-2 treaty investor visa. For a Canadian who wants to own and run a business in the U.S., the E-2 is often a better fit than the TN or L-1, which are built for employees rather than owners. The right choice depends on whether you are investing in a new U.S. business, transferring within an existing company, or taking a job.

## Is Canada an E-2 treaty country?

Yes. Canadian citizens qualify, provided the U.S. business is at least 50% owned by Canadian nationals and the investor will develop and direct it. Permanent residents of Canada who are not Canadian citizens do not qualify on that basis; they must be nationals of a treaty country in their own right.

## Why the TN rarely fits a business owner

The TN visa under the USMCA covers professionals in listed occupations, such as engineers, accountants, and scientists, working for a U.S. employer. Two problems arise for owners:

- **Self-employment is not allowed.** A TN professional cannot work for a business they essentially are. Owning a share of the U.S. company is not automatically fatal, but a TN cannot be used to run your own business.
- **The job must be on the list.** "Entrepreneur," "CEO," and "general manager" are not TN occupations.

A TN works for a Canadian taking a qualifying job with an independent U.S. company. It does not work for building your own.

## When the L-1 makes sense

The L-1 lets an established foreign company transfer an executive, manager, or specialized-knowledge employee to a U.S. affiliate. For a Canadian who has run a Canadian company for at least a year and is opening a U.S. branch, the L-1A is a serious option, especially because it leads to the EB-1C green card for multinational managers.

The L-1 requirements that rule some people out:

- The Canadian company must continue doing business during the L-1.
- You must have worked for it for one continuous year within the last three.
- A new U.S. office is approved for one year initially, and must grow to support an executive role.

## When the E-2 is the better route

The E-2 fits Canadians who:

- Are buying or starting a U.S. business that is not connected to an existing Canadian company.
- Want to sell or close their Canadian business.
- Do not want the pressure of a one-year new-office L-1.
- Want renewable status with no fixed maximum period.

The tradeoff: the E-2 does not lead to a green card on its own, and children lose dependent status at 21.

## How Canadians apply for the E-2

Canadians are generally visa-exempt for visits, but E-2 status requires an E visa. Most Canadian investors apply at a U.S. consulate, often in Canada, with a full investment and business package. Someone already in the U.S. in another status may be able to change to E-2 through USCIS, though that approval does not allow re-entry without an E visa.

## Comparison for Canadian entrepreneurs

| | E-2 | TN | L-1A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Built for | Owners and investors | Professionals in listed jobs | Transferring executives and managers |
| Needs existing foreign company | No | No | Yes |
| Self-employment allowed | Yes, it is the point | No | Owner can be transferred |
| Green card path | Not directly | Not directly | EB-1C |
| Initial approval | Up to two years per entry, renewable | Up to three years | One year for new office, three otherwise |

## A Canadian-specific tip

If you own a Canadian company with operations and staff, consider whether the L-1A to EB-1C path gives you a green card the E-2 cannot. If you want to keep things simple and you are investing in a new U.S. venture, the E-2 is usually cleaner.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Canadian permanent resident get an E-2?
Only if they are a citizen of another E-2 treaty country. Canadian permanent residence alone does not qualify.

### Can I apply for E-2 status at the border like a TN?
Generally no. Canadians need an E visa issued by a consulate, or a change of status through USCIS if already in the U.S.

### Can my spouse work in the U.S. on E-2?
Yes. E-2 spouses are authorized to work incident to status, and their I-94 should reflect that.

### How much do Canadians need to invest?
The same standard applies as for any E-2: substantial in relation to the cost of the business, at risk, and committed.

### Can I move from TN to E-2 later?
Yes. Many Canadians start on a TN with an employer and later move to E-2 when they invest in their own business.

### Does the E-2 help me get a green card?
Not directly. E-2 owners who want permanence often look at EB-5 or, for those with a related foreign company, an L-1A to EB-1C plan.

### Can I own a franchise on an E-2?
Yes. Franchises are common E-2 businesses, as long as the investment and role requirements are met.
