A Stokes interview is a second, more detailed marriage interview that USCIS uses when an officer has doubts about whether a marriage is genuine. Spouses are interviewed separately, and their answers are compared. It can feel intimidating, but couples with real marriages can prepare for it.

## What a Stokes interview is

The name comes from a 1970s court case that set procedures for these interviews. In a Stokes interview:

- Each spouse is interviewed separately by an officer
- Questions cover detailed aspects of your daily life
- The officer compares answers for consistency
- The interview may be recorded

## What triggers a Stokes interview

- **Inconsistent answers** at the first interview
- **Weak evidence** of a shared life, such as no joint accounts or lease
- **Large age or cultural differences**
- **Short relationship** before marriage
- **Different addresses** on documents
- **Prior marriages** that ended shortly before this one
- **Prior petitions** by the petitioner for other spouses
- **Tips or reports** that question the marriage

## Sample topics

Stokes interview questions are detailed and personal, such as:

- Your daily routines: who wakes up first, who cooks
- Your home: the layout, furniture, the color of the bedroom walls
- Your relationship history: how you met, the proposal, the wedding
- Your families: names of in-laws, siblings, and their jobs
- Recent events: what you did last weekend, last holiday
- Finances: who pays the bills, how you share expenses

## How to prepare

1. **Talk with your spouse** about your shared life and history. Don't memorize answers.
2. **Gather evidence** of your marriage: joint bank statements, lease or mortgage, insurance, tax returns, photos, and travel records.
3. **Review your forms** so you know what's been filed.
4. **Bring an attorney.** You have the right to have an attorney with you.
5. **Be honest.** If you don't know something, say so. Guessing can create inconsistencies.

## Small differences are normal

Couples in genuine marriages often remember details differently. An officer expects some differences. Major discrepancies, such as not knowing where your spouse works, are what raise concerns.

## Possible outcomes

- **Approval:** The officer is satisfied the marriage is genuine.
- **Request for evidence or a notice of intent to deny:** The officer wants more documentation.
- **Denial:** The officer concludes the marriage isn't bona fide.

A denial based on marriage fraud findings can have serious consequences, including future bars. An attorney can help you respond.

## What to bring to a Stokes interview

Bring originals and copies of documents that show a shared life:

- A joint lease, mortgage, or property deed
- Joint bank statements and credit card statements
- Joint tax returns
- Insurance policies listing each other as beneficiaries
- Utility bills in both names
- Photos across different dates and events
- Travel records, such as boarding passes and hotel bookings
- Birth certificates of children born to the marriage

Organize the documents by category and date, so you can find them quickly if the officer asks.

## After a difficult interview

If you think the interview went badly, write down what you were asked and how you answered right away, while you still remember. That record helps your attorney respond to any later notice of intent to deny with specific evidence that addresses the officer's concerns.

## How a Stokes interview differs from a regular marriage interview

At a standard marriage green card interview, the couple usually sits together with the officer, who reviews the forms and asks general questions about the relationship. Many couples are approved at that first interview. A Stokes interview is longer, the questions are more detailed, and the officer is looking specifically for inconsistencies. Being scheduled for one doesn't mean the officer has concluded the marriage is fraudulent, but it does mean the officer needs more convincing.

## The role of your attorney

An attorney can attend both interviews, object to improper questions, help clarify confusing questions, and make sure the record reflects your answers accurately. Afterward, the attorney can respond to any notice that follows with targeted evidence.
