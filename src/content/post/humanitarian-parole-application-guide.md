Humanitarian parole allows someone outside the U.S. to enter temporarily for urgent humanitarian reasons or a significant public benefit. It's often sought when no visa is available and the situation can't wait. An application for humanitarian parole is discretionary, and it's not a substitute for a visa or a path to status.

## What humanitarian parole is

Parole is permission to enter the U.S. for a temporary period. It isn't a visa, an admission, or immigration status. USCIS grants it on a case-by-case basis for:

- **Urgent humanitarian reasons:** such as medical treatment not available abroad, visiting a dying relative, or attending a funeral
- **Significant public benefit:** such as participating in legal proceedings or law enforcement efforts

## Who can apply

Anyone can file on behalf of a person outside the U.S. The applicant is often a family member in the U.S. The beneficiary must be outside the U.S.

## How to apply

1. **Form I-131,** Application for Travel Documents, Parole Documents, and Arrival/Departure Records
2. **Form I-134,** Declaration of Financial Support, filed online by a financial supporter
3. **Evidence of the urgent reason,** such as medical records, letters from doctors, or death certificates
4. **Evidence of why a visa isn't available or practical**
5. **The filing fee,** or a request for a fee waiver

## The financial supporter

A supporter must show they can support the beneficiary during parole. The supporter files Form I-134 online. USCIS reviews the supporter's income and assets.

## The parole fee

The 2025 budget law, H.R. 1, created a new immigration parole fee that applies when parole is granted, with some exceptions. After an inflation adjustment effective October 16, 2026, the fee is $1,050. It's separate from the I-131 filing fee.

## Evidence of urgency

USCIS looks for:

- **Specific, documented reasons:** medical records with diagnosis and treatment plans
- **Time sensitivity:** why the beneficiary needs to come now
- **Lack of alternatives:** why a visa isn't an option

## How long parole lasts

Parole is usually granted for the time needed to address the urgent situation, often up to one year. Extensions require a new request.

## What parole doesn't give you

- **No immigration status:** Parole isn't lawful status.
- **No path to a green card on its own:** Parolees must qualify through another route.
- **Work authorization isn't automatic:** Parolees can apply for an EAD, and the initial parole-based EAD fee is now $570.

## Processing times

Humanitarian parole processing can take months. Explain urgency clearly and request expedited processing if appropriate.

## Common reasons for denial

- Insufficient evidence of urgency
- No explanation of why a visa isn't available
- Weak financial support
- Prior immigration violations

## Practical example

A U.S. citizen wants to bring her brother from a country without a functioning U.S. consulate for treatment of a rare cancer at a U.S. hospital. She files Form I-131 on his behalf with medical records, a letter from the U.S. hospital accepting him for treatment, the treatment plan and cost estimate, and proof of how the treatment will be paid. She files Form I-134 online as his financial supporter. The application explains why he cannot get a visa in time and why treatment isn't available closer to home. If USCIS approves, he must still pass security vetting and pay the parole fee before travel.

## What happens at the end of the parole period

Parole ends on the date set by USCIS. The parolee must leave before then, request re-parole if the urgent reason continues, or have another lawful basis to stay. Overstaying parole can lead to unlawful presence and removal proceedings.

## Humanitarian parole vs other options

Before filing for humanitarian parole, consider whether another route fits better:

- **A visitor visa,** if the consulate is functioning and the beneficiary can qualify
- **An immigrant visa,** if the beneficiary has an approved family petition and a visa is available
- **A refugee referral,** for people facing persecution abroad
- **Expedited visa appointments,** which some consulates grant for urgent medical needs or funerals

Parole is designed for situations where none of these will work in time. USCIS often asks why the beneficiary couldn't use one of these options, so address that question directly in the application.
