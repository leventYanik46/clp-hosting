Overstaying a visa means staying past the date on your I-94 admission record without filing for an extension or change of status in time. The consequences depend on how long you overstayed and how you entered. For some people, especially spouses of U.S. citizens who entered legally, a path to a green card may still be open. For others, leaving the U.S. can make things worse.

## How does immigration know if you overstay your visa?

CBP records your entry on the electronic I-94 and receives departure information from airline and cruise manifests. These records feed DHS systems that flag people who did not depart by the I-94 date. Overstays also surface when you apply for a new visa, an extension, a green card, or when you cross a border.

Your I-94 date controls, not the visa stamp's expiration date. Many people overstay without realizing it because they look at the wrong document.

## Unlawful presence and the reentry bars

Unlawful presence generally starts the day after your I-94 expires, if you were admitted until a specific date. The consequences are triggered when you leave:

- **More than 180 days but less than one year** of unlawful presence, then departing: a three-year bar on returning
- **One year or more**, then departing: a ten-year bar

People admitted for "duration of status," such as students, follow different rules on when unlawful presence starts.

## What happens if you overstay your visa and get married?

If you marry a U.S. citizen, you may be able to adjust status to a green card from inside the U.S. even after an overstay, as long as:

- You were inspected and admitted when you entered
- The marriage is genuine
- You have no other bars, such as certain criminal issues or a prior removal order

Immediate relatives of citizens are forgiven for overstays and unauthorized work for adjustment purposes. Because you stay in the U.S. through the process, the reentry bars are not triggered.

Marrying a green card holder is different. Spouses of permanent residents usually must be in lawful status to adjust, so an overstay generally rules out adjustment until the spouse naturalizes or a waiver applies.

Expect USCIS to examine closely whether you intended to marry and stay when you entered on a visitor visa. Marriages that develop after entry are generally fine, but timing questions should be answered honestly and with evidence.

## Is there visa overstay forgiveness?

There is no general amnesty. Relief comes through specific rules:

- **Immediate relative adjustment** as described above
- **The I-601A provisional waiver** for certain relatives of citizens and green card holders who must leave for consular processing
- **Section 245(i)** for people with a petition filed on or before April 30, 2001
- **Asylum, U visas, T visas, VAWA**, and other humanitarian relief
- **Cancellation of removal** in immigration court for some long-term residents

## How to fix an overstayed visa

1. Do not leave the U.S. before you understand which bars you might trigger.
2. Get your I-94 history from CBP's website.
3. Gather entry documents, passports, and any filings you made.
4. Get an individualized review before filing anything.

Filing the wrong application can expose you to removal proceedings without a viable defense.


## Frequently asked questions

### Does a pending extension stop unlawful presence?

A timely, non-frivolous extension or change of status application generally stops unlawful presence from accruing while it is pending, if you did not work without authorization.

### Can I get a new visa after overstaying?

An overstay usually voids your current visa. You generally must apply for a new one in your home country, and any bars triggered by departure must be resolved first.

### Does an overstay affect a future tourist visa?

Yes. An overstay is part of your record, and consular officers will look at it closely. If bars apply, you must wait them out or qualify for a waiver before returning.
