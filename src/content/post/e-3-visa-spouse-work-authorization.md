Yes. The spouse of an E-3 worker is authorized to work in the United States, for any employer, without filing a separate petition. Since a USCIS policy change in late 2021, E-3 spouse work authorization is treated as incident to status, which means the spouse's valid E-3D admission is itself the proof of permission to work. That puts E-3 families in a better position than H-1B families, where the spouse usually waits years for an employment authorization document. Our [E-3 visa practice](/practice-area/key-services/e-3-visa) gets this question from nearly every Australian client with a partner, so here is how the rule works and where it still causes friction.

## How E-3D status works for spouses and children

The spouse and unmarried children under 21 of an E-3 worker are admitted in E-3D status. They do not need to be Australian citizens; a Canadian or Indian spouse of an Australian E-3 worker qualifies in the same way. Their status is tied to the principal's: same validity period, same employer-dependent existence, same end date on the I-94.

Dependents can apply for visas at the same interview as the principal or later, once the principal has been issued the E-3 visa. The consulate will want the marriage certificate and, for children, birth certificates, plus the principal's visa or approval notice. A spouse already in the United States in another status can change status to E-3D on Form I-539.

## E-3 spouse work authorization without an EAD

Before November 2021, E-3 spouses had to file Form I-765 and wait for an employment authorization card before taking a job. USCIS then changed its policy to treat E and L spouses as employment authorized incident to status. Customs and Border Protection followed by issuing I-94 records with a distinct class code for E-3 spouses, E-3S, so that the record itself shows the person is a spouse rather than a child.

For Form I-9 purposes, an unexpired I-94 bearing the E-3S notation is accepted as evidence of both identity and employment authorization. The spouse can start work on arrival, change jobs freely, work part time, and take self-employment, none of which requires a filing with USCIS.

Children in E-3D status have no work authorization. They may attend school, including university, without changing to F-1 status, but they cannot take a job, paid internship, or on-campus work.

## Proving it to an employer

The practical hiccups come from employers and HR systems that have not caught up. Three situations recur:

- The spouse's I-94 was issued before the new code existed and shows only E-3D. CBP can correct it at a deferred inspection site, or a new I-94 with the E-3S code will issue on the next entry.
- The employer's onboarding software insists on an EAD card. The spouse can still file Form I-765 to obtain a card as a convenience, and some do, but it is optional and the job can start before it arrives.
- The spouse changed status inside the United States and has an I-539 approval notice rather than an I-94 from CBP. The approval notice includes an I-94 with the class of admission, and the same rule applies.

Retrieve the I-94 from the CBP website right after each entry and check the code. A wrong code is far easier to fix in the first week than when a job offer is on the table.

## Study, travel, and changes in the family

E-3D spouses and children may study full time or part time without any additional permission. They may travel on a valid E-3D visa as long as the principal remains in status. If the marriage ends in divorce, the spouse's E-3D status ends with it, and the former spouse must change to another status or depart. When a child turns 21, E-3D status ends on that birthday, so a change to F-1 or another status should be filed before then. Our [change and extension of status](/practice-area/key-services/change-and-extension-of-status) page covers the timing of those filings.

## When the principal's status changes

E-3D status exists only as long as E-3 status does. If the principal is laid off, the family shares the 60-day grace period described in our article on [changing jobs on an E-3 visa](/changing-jobs-on-e-3-visa), and the spouse's work authorization ends when the grace period or the I-94 ends, whichever comes first. If the principal switches to H-1B, the spouse becomes H-4 and loses the incident-to-status work permission; an H-4 EAD is available only after the H-1B worker has an approved I-140 or qualifies for an AC21 extension. That consequence is one of the main reasons Australian couples stay on the E-3 even when an H-1B is on offer, a tradeoff we walk through in [E-3 versus H-1B for Australian professionals](/e-3-vs-h-1b-australian-professionals).
