The short answer to whether the spouse of an H-1B worker can work is: not automatically, but often yes after the right filing. Holding H-4 status does not itself allow employment. Eligible spouses need a separate work permit, called an H-4 EAD (Employment Authorization Document).

## Who qualifies for an H-4 EAD

A spouse in H-4 status can apply for an EAD only if the H-1B worker meets one of two conditions:

- **The H-1B worker has an approved Form I-140** immigrant petition, which means the green card process is far enough along.
- **The H-1B worker was granted H-1B status beyond the six-year limit** under the rules that allow extensions when a green card case is pending.

H-4 children are not eligible for an EAD. If your spouse's H-1B has not reached either stage, there is no EAD yet, and planning around that gap matters.

## How to apply for an H-4 EAD

The application is made on Form I-765, filed with USCIS. A basic sequence looks like this:

1. **Confirm eligibility.** Gather the H-1B worker's I-140 approval notice or the extension approval.
2. **Prepare Form I-765.** The eligibility category is specific to H-4 spouses, so use the one that matches.
3. **Collect supporting documents** and assemble the package.
4. **File online or by mail**, then wait for the receipt notice, biometrics scheduling if required, and a decision.

If the H-4 status itself is expiring, USCIS often wants the H-4 extension and the EAD filed together. Many families file both on the same day to avoid mismatches.

## Document checklist

- A copy of the H-1B worker's I-140 approval notice, or the extension approval that relies on the pending green card
- Your current H-4 approval notice and I-94 record
- A copy of your passport identity page and previous EAD, if any
- Passport-style photos, if the filing method asks for them
- Proof of the marriage relationship: marriage certificate, and the H-1B worker's approval notice

Check the USCIS form instructions before filing, because the evidence requirements and the filing methods change over time.

## Cost and timing

There is a filing fee, and USCIS adjusts it periodically, so confirm the current amount on the Form I-765 page before you send anything. Processing times vary by service center and filing volume. Plan for months, not weeks, and file as early as the rules allow. If your current EAD is expiring, check whether a timely renewal filing gives you any extra period of work authorization.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting to file until the old EAD has expired, which can create a work gap
- Using the wrong eligibility category on the form
- Filing the I-765 without a valid H-4 status or without the I-140 evidence
- Assuming an H-4 spouse can work while the EAD is pending
