An L-1 visa extension lets a manager, executive, or specialized knowledge employee keep working for a U.S. affiliate after their initial approval period ends. The limits are fixed by law: seven years total for L-1A managers and executives, and five years total for L-1B specialized knowledge employees. Within those limits, extensions are granted in up to two-year increments.

## How long the initial approval lasts

- **Standard L-1:** Up to three years initially.
- **New office L-1:** Only one year initially. A new office is a U.S. operation that has been doing business for less than a year.

The new office rule matters because the first extension is where many new office cases fail.

## The new office extension

To extend a new office L-1, the employer has to show that the U.S. business has grown into what it promised. USCIS will look for:

- Evidence the business is doing business regularly and continuously, not just existing on paper
- Financial statements, tax returns, and payroll records
- An organizational chart showing staff who handle day-to-day work, so the L-1A manager is managing rather than doing operational tasks
- Evidence the qualifying relationship with the foreign company still exists
- That the foreign company continues to operate

If the U.S. office has not hired or generated revenue, the extension is at serious risk.

## Standard L-1 extensions

For established companies, extensions are usually more routine. The employer files Form I-129 with an L Supplement, confirming the employee still holds the same qualifying role and the company relationship is unchanged.

## L-1A extension processing time

Regular processing can take several months. Premium processing is available and brings a decision within the guaranteed window. You can file up to six months before your current status expires. A timely filed extension generally lets you keep working for up to 240 days while it is pending.

## Changing from L-1B to L-1A

If an L-1B employee is promoted into a managerial or executive role, the employer can file to change the classification. If the person has spent at least six months in L-1A status, they can qualify for the longer seven-year limit.

## Recapturing time

Time spent outside the U.S. during L-1 status can generally be recaptured and added back, as long as you document the days abroad with passport stamps and travel records.

## L-1 visa cost: what to budget

An L-1 extension involves several fees. Check the current USCIS fee schedule before filing, because amounts change:

- **Base I-129 filing fee** for L classifications, with a lower fee for small employers
- **Asylum Program Fee**, reduced for small employers
- **Fraud prevention and detection fee** of $500, which applies to initial petitions and change of employer cases, not to standard extensions by the same employer
- **Public Law 114-113 fee** of $4,500 for employers with 50 or more U.S. employees, more than half of whom are on H-1B or L status, for petitions that also owe the fraud fee
- **Premium processing**, if requested
- **Attorney fees**, which vary with complexity and are higher for new office extensions

The L-1B visa cost is generally the same as L-1A for government fees. The difference is usually in the legal work.

## After the maximum

When you reach the limit, you must spend a year outside the U.S. before new L or H status, unless you transition to a green card. The L-1A is a common path to an EB-1C multinational manager green card, which does not require PERM.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can I extend an L-1 with a blanket petition?

Blanket L-1 employees who reach the end of their initial period can extend through a USCIS petition. Rules on combining blanket and individual time apply, so map the total stay carefully.

### Can my L-2 spouse work during an extension?

L-2 spouses are authorized to work incident to status, with an I-94 annotation. Keep the spouse's I-94 current through each extension.
