If you're a green card holder and you move, you must notify USCIS. An address change for green card holders is required by law within 10 days. Many people forget or don't realize that updating one case doesn't update everything.

## The 10-day rule

Most noncitizens in the U.S. for more than 30 days, including green card holders, must report a change of address within 10 days of moving. The form is AR-11, Alien's Change of Address Card.

## How to change your address

**Online:** The easiest way is through your USCIS online account. You can update your address for your AR-11 and pending cases.

**By mail:** You can mail Form AR-11 to the address on the form instructions. Keep a copy and proof of mailing.

## Pending cases need separate updates

Filing an AR-11 doesn't automatically update pending applications in some situations. If you have a pending case, such as an N-400, I-751, or I-485, make sure your address is updated for that specific case. The online change of address tool usually lets you select pending cases.

## The green card itself

You don't need a new green card when you move. Your green card doesn't list your address.

## Immigration court cases

If you're in immigration court proceedings, you must also notify the court with Form EOIR-33/IC within five days. USCIS and the immigration court are separate. Missing a hearing because notices went to an old address can lead to an in absentia removal order.

## Consequences of not updating

- **Missed notices:** interview appointments, RFEs, and biometrics notices may go to your old address
- **Delays or denials:** a missed RFE deadline can lead to denial
- **Legal penalties:** failing to report an address change can be a misdemeanor, though prosecution is rare

## Tips

1. Update within 10 days
2. Update every pending case
3. Update the immigration court separately if you have a case there
4. Keep confirmation records
5. File mail forwarding with the U.S. Postal Service as a backup

## Moving abroad

If you're moving abroad, an address change isn't the issue. Long absences can raise questions about abandoning your permanent residence. Talk to an attorney before moving abroad.

## Practical example

A green card holder with a pending N-400 moves from Virginia to Maryland. She files an AR-11 online and selects her pending N-400 so USCIS updates the case. Because she moved to a different USCIS district, her case may be transferred to a new field office, which can change the interview location and timeline. She also confirms she will meet the three-month residence requirement in her new state or district before her interview.

## Address changes and naturalization eligibility

Moving to a new state can affect naturalization, because you must have lived in the state or district where you file for at least three months. If you plan to move soon, timing your N-400 around the move can avoid a transfer or a residence problem.

## Other places to update your address

Updating USCIS isn't the only step. You may also need to update your address with:

- **The immigration court,** using Form EOIR-33, if you're in proceedings
- **The National Visa Center,** for any family member's pending immigrant visa case you're sponsoring
- **ICE,** if you're on supervision or a bond
- **Your attorney,** so they can track notices
- **Form I-865,** if you're a sponsor who signed an I-864, within 30 days

Missing any of these can lead to missed notices or deadlines.
