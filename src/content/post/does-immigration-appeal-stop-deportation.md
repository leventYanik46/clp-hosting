A timely appeal to the Board of Immigration Appeals stops deportation automatically; almost nothing else does. People lose years of work, and sometimes the country, by assuming that any pending filing protects them. So does an immigration appeal stop deportation? Only one kind does on its own. For the rest, someone has to ask for a stay of removal and get it granted before ICE acts. The lawyers in CLP's [appeals, delays and federal court group](/practice-area/key-services/immigration-appeals-delays-and-federal-court) treat the stay question as the first question in every post-order case, because a winning legal argument is worth little if the client has already been removed.

## When an immigration appeal does stop deportation automatically

Filing a Notice of Appeal with the Board within 30 days of the immigration judge's decision triggers an automatic stay under the regulations. ICE cannot execute the removal order while the appeal is pending. The protection lasts until the Board decides. It attaches only to a timely appeal; an appeal filed on day 31 is dismissed, and the order becomes final and executable. The same automatic protection applies to a timely motion to rescind an in absentia order, the order entered when a person missed a hearing. Filing that motion stays removal until the immigration judge rules on it.

## Filings that do not stop removal by themselves

The list of things that do not stay removal is longer than most people expect.

- A petition for review in the federal court of appeals. Congress removed the automatic stay in 1996. You must file a separate motion for a stay with the court, and the government may remove you while that motion is pending unless the court or an arrangement with the government says otherwise. Practice varies by circuit, so ask what applies where your case sits.
- A motion to reopen or reconsider, other than the in absentia rescission motion. You may ask the Board or the immigration judge for a stay alongside the motion, but it is discretionary and takes time.
- A mandamus or APA lawsuit in district court. These address delay or a denial of a benefit; they do nothing to a removal order, and the district court generally lacks power over the order itself.
- A habeas corpus petition. It challenges detention, not removal, and a judge who orders release has not blocked ICE from carrying out a final order later.
- A pending application with USCIS, such as a new I-130 or I-485, filed after the removal order became final.

## How to ask for a stay of removal

There are three places to ask, and they can be pursued at the same time.

A stay motion in the court of appeals is decided under the four factors the Supreme Court set out in Nken v. Holder: a strong showing of likely success on the merits, irreparable harm without a stay, the balance of harms, and the public interest. Removal alone does not count as irreparable harm; the motion has to show why this person's removal would cause harm the court cannot later undo. Courts rule on these motions quickly, often on a short brief and a declaration.

A stay request to the Board or the immigration judge accompanies a motion to reopen. The adjudicator considers the likelihood that the motion will be granted. An emergency stay request is possible when removal is imminent, and the Board has a dedicated unit for it.

An administrative stay from ICE is requested on Form I-246, filed with the field office that has the case. It is entirely discretionary, carries the current filing fee, and is typically granted for a fixed period while something else is pending, such as a visa petition or a medical situation. It is often the fastest path when a court motion is not yet decided.

## What happens if you are removed while the case is pending

Removal does not automatically end a petition for review or a motion to reopen; many courts have held that the case can continue and that a win may entitle the person to return. But the practical burden of litigating from abroad, and the bars to reentry triggered by the removal itself, make prevention far better than cure. This is why the stay motion is filed with, not after, the appeal or petition.

## Reading your own posture

Start by identifying where the case is: before the immigration judge, at the Board, in the court of appeals, or past all of them with a final order. Then ask whether anything on file carries an automatic stay. If the answer is no, a stay request should be drafted today.
