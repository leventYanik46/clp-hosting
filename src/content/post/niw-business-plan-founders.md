Founders can qualify for the EB-2 national interest waiver, but their petitions are judged on what the business will do, as well as on the founder's résumé. That makes the EB-2 NIW business plan one of the most important documents in a founder's case. It has to show that the venture has national importance and that the founder is well positioned to carry it out, in terms an immigration officer can verify.

## The NIW test applied to founders

USCIS evaluates NIW petitions under Matter of Dhanasar, which has three prongs:

1. **The proposed endeavor has substantial merit and national importance.**
2. **You are well positioned to advance the endeavor.**
3. **On balance, it would benefit the United States to waive the job offer and labor certification.**

For a founder, the "proposed endeavor" is the business and what it will achieve. USCIS updated its policy guidance to clarify how entrepreneurs can meet these prongs, including the kinds of evidence that show a founder is well positioned.

You must still first qualify for EB-2 itself, through an advanced degree or exceptional ability.

## What the business plan must do

### Show national importance, not just profit

A profitable local business is not enough. National importance can come from:

- Broader economic impact, such as significant job creation in an economically depressed area.
- Advancing a field, such as a new technology in health, energy, security, or manufacturing.
- Effects that reach beyond your customers, such as improving supply chains or public services.

The plan should explain the problem, why it matters beyond your company, and how your product or service addresses it.

### Make the numbers checkable

Projections should be specific and tied to assumptions an officer can follow: hiring plans by role and year, revenue drivers, and funding milestones. Inflated projections without support weaken credibility.

### Connect the founder to the plan

The second prong asks whether you are well positioned. The plan should tie the business to your education, experience, prior ventures, patents, or progress. Evidence that helps includes:

- Investment from outside investors or venture funds.
- Government grants or awards.
- Letters of intent, contracts, or customers.
- Incubator or accelerator participation.
- Prior successful companies.

### Address the waiver prong

Explain why requiring a job offer and PERM would not make sense: a founder cannot practically obtain a labor certification for their own company, and the business's value depends on the founder's involvement.

## Where founder NIW petitions go wrong

- **A plan written for investors, not adjudicators.** Pitch decks focus on market size. NIW plans need to explain national importance in plain terms.
- **Generic industry claims.** "AI is a growing field" says nothing about your endeavor.
- **No traction.** A plan with no funding, customers, or progress makes the well-positioned prong difficult.
- **Mismatch between degree and business.** If your EB-2 qualification rests on an unrelated degree, explain the connection.
- **Overreliance on letters.** Expert letters help but cannot replace objective evidence.

## Business plan and supporting documents checklist

- Executive summary of the endeavor and its national importance.
- Market analysis tied to the problem being solved.
- Product or technology description in accessible language.
- Hiring and financial projections with assumptions.
- Evidence of funding, contracts, and progress.
- Founder background linked to each part of the plan.

## Is the NIW right for your startup?

The NIW gives founders independence from employers and PERM, but it demands strong evidence. Some founders are better served by an O-1A first, then an NIW or EB-1A once the company has traction.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my startup need venture funding for an NIW?
No, but outside investment is strong evidence that you are well positioned. Grants, contracts, and customers can also help.

### Can I file an NIW before forming the company?
You can, but a concrete endeavor with progress is much stronger than an idea. Most founders file once there is traction.

### Can an NIW founder employ themselves?
Yes. The NIW is a self-petition and does not require an employer. After the green card, you can work for your own company.

### How long does an NIW take?
It depends on I-140 processing, premium processing availability, and your visa bulletin date, which for some countries means years of waiting after approval.

### Should I file an O-1 first?
If you need to work in the U.S. soon, an O-1 can provide status while the NIW is prepared and pending.

### Do I need letters from experts?
Letters help explain technical work, but they should be backed by objective evidence. Independent experts carry more weight than colleagues.

### What if my business changes direction?
USCIS evaluates the endeavor described in the petition. A major change before the green card is approved may require explanation or a new filing.
