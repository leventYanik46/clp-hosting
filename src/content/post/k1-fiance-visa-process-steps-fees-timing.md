The K-1 fiancé visa lets a U.S. citizen bring a foreign fiancé(e) to the United States to marry within 90 days of arrival. After the wedding, the foreign spouse applies for a green card. It is a two-stage process: first the petition, then the visa, then adjustment. Knowing the order, and what each step costs, makes planning easier.

## Who qualifies

- **The petitioner must be a U.S. citizen.** Green card holders cannot use the K-1.
- **Both of you must be legally free to marry.** Any earlier marriages must have ended by divorce, annulment or death.
- **You must have met in person at least once in the two years before filing,** unless a waiver applies, for instance for extreme hardship or customs that prevent it.
- **You must intend to marry within 90 days of the fiancé(e)'s arrival.**
- **The petitioner must meet the income requirement** through an affidavit of support, and there are limits on how many K-1 petitions one person may file.

## Steps in the process

1. **File Form I-129F** with USCIS, with evidence of the relationship, proof of the citizen's status, and proof of meeting in person.
2. **USCIS approves the petition** and sends it to the National Visa Center and then to the U.S. consulate in the fiancé(e)'s country.
3. **The fiancé(e) completes visa paperwork,** including Form DS-160, civil documents and police certificates.
4. **Medical examination** by an approved physician.
5. **Consular interview.** The officer decides whether to issue the K-1 visa.
6. **Travel to the United States.** The K-1 visa must be used within its validity period.
7. **Marry within 90 days** of entry.
8. **File Form I-485** for adjustment of status, typically with applications for a work permit and a travel document.
9. **Conditional green card** if the marriage is under two years old when approved.

## Fees to expect

There are several, and they change, so check official pages for current amounts:

- USCIS filing fee for Form I-129F
- Consular visa fee
- Medical exam fees
- Costs of obtaining and translating documents
- The USCIS fee for Form I-485 and any related filings after the wedding
- Legal fees, if you hire a lawyer

Together they add up. Build a budget before you begin.

## Processing times

USCIS posts current processing times for the I-129F. The consulate stage adds more time, and each consulate has its own schedule. Time frames shift with workload, so base your plan on the latest official information. In general, people should expect a process of many months, not weeks, and should avoid committing to fixed wedding dates until the visa is in hand.

## Common problems

- Not meeting in person within the required period
- Weak evidence of a genuine relationship
- Missing divorce documents for a prior marriage
- Criminal or immigration history that raises inadmissibility
- Missing the 90-day marriage window

A lawyer can review your eligibility, assemble the petition, prepare you for the consular interview and advise on alternatives, such as a marriage abroad followed by a spousal visa. Consider legal help if you have prior K-1 petitions, a prior marriage, a significant age difference, a criminal record or a short relationship.
