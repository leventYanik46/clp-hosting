An O-1 visa is temporary. It is granted for up to three years at first and then extended in one-year increments for the same event or activity, and nothing in the O-1 itself turns into permanent residence. What the O-1 does is give you lawful status, work authorization and time to build the record that an immigrant petition needs. Moving from an O-1 visa to a green card therefore means choosing an immigrant category, filing Form I-140, and timing the final step so your status never lapses. Our [extraordinary ability visa practice](/practice-area/key-services/talent-and-extraordinary-ability-visas) handles that transition routinely; this post explains the moving parts.

## Filing an immigrant petition does not jeopardize the O-1

Most temporary visas require nonimmigrant intent, and a pending green card case can complicate extensions and consular visits. The O-1 is treated differently. Under 8 CFR 214.2(o)(13), the approval of a permanent labor certification or the filing of an immigrant petition is not, by itself, a basis to deny an O-1 petition, an extension or a change of status. The O-1 is not formally a dual-intent visa like the H-1B, so you should still be prepared to show that you will comply with the terms of the O-1 when you apply for a visa at a consulate, but filing an I-140 while in O-1 status is routine. Our post on [proving extraordinary ability for the O-1](/o-1-visa-extraordinary-ability) covers the standard you have already met.

## Three green card routes from O-1 to green card

### EB-1A: the natural successor

The O-1A criteria and the EB-1A criteria overlap substantially: awards, memberships, press, judging, original contributions, scholarly articles, critical roles and high remuneration appear in both lists. The difference is the standard. The O-1A asks for distinction, while the EB-1A asks for sustained acclaim and a position among the small percentage at the very top of the field. Many O-1 holders cannot meet the EB-1A bar on the day the O-1 is approved and can meet it two or three years later, after their work has been cited, covered and adopted. See our [EB-1A extraordinary ability green card](/practice-area/key-services/eb-1a-green-card) page for the criteria.

### EB-2 National Interest Waiver

If your record is strong but the EB-1A standard is out of reach, the NIW asks a different question: whether your proposed work has national importance and whether you are well positioned to advance it. Founders on an O-1 sponsored by their own company often fit this route well, because job creation and work in a priority field support national importance. Our comparison of [the EB-1A and the NIW](/eb-1a-vs-eb-2-niw-comparison) explains how to choose, and both can be self-petitioned, so your O-1 petitioner does not have to be involved.

### An employer petition

If your O-1 employer wants to sponsor you, it can file an EB-1B for a researcher with a permanent position, or an EB-2 or EB-3 petition after a PERM labor certification. Artists on an O-1B have no employer-filed first preference category of their own, so the EB-1A self-petition is usually the route. An employer petition ties your case to that job until Form I-485 has been pending at least 180 days.

## Timing the I-140 and the I-485

Your I-140 filing date becomes your priority date. The Visa Bulletin tells you whether a visa number is available for your category and country of birth. If it is, and you are in the United States in lawful O-1 status, you may file Form I-485 with the I-140 or after approval, along with Form I-765 for an employment authorization document and Form I-131 for advance parole.

Two cautions. First, once the I-485 is filed, many O-1 holders prefer to keep working on the O-1 rather than the EAD, because the O-1 survives if the I-485 is denied. Second, travel: if you leave the United States on advance parole without a valid O-1 visa stamp, you return as a parolee and not in O-1 status. Plan international trips around a valid O-1 visa in your passport, or around a deliberate decision to switch to adjustment-based parole.

If your country of birth is backlogged in your category, the I-485 waits, and you keep extending the O-1 in the meantime. There is no statutory limit on the number of O-1 extensions, which is one reason the O-1 is a more comfortable waiting room than the H-1B with its six-year cap.

## Common mistakes in the O-1 to green card transition

- Filing the EB-1A too early, on essentially the same record that supported the O-1, and receiving a final merits denial that could have been avoided by waiting a year.
- Letting the O-1 lapse while an I-140 is pending without an I-485 on file, which ends work authorization and can start unlawful presence.
- Assuming the O-1 petitioner must sponsor the green card. EB-1A and NIW are self-petitions, and your agent or employer need not participate.
- Changing fields. The EB-1A requires that you continue working in your area of extraordinary ability, so a move from research into a general management role can weaken the case.
