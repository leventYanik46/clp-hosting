If a lawyer missed your deadline, filed the wrong application, or never told you about a hearing, or if a "notario" or consultant took your money and filed something false, you may still be able to fix the damage. Immigration law allows a case to be reopened for ineffective assistance of counsel, and fraud by unauthorized practitioners can be reported and sometimes undone. The steps are specific, and acting quickly matters.

## Ineffective assistance of counsel

In removal proceedings, you can ask to reopen a case if your lawyer's performance was so poor that it prejudiced you. Common examples include:

- Failing to file an application for relief, such as asylum, before a deadline.
- Failing to tell you about a hearing, which led to an in absentia removal order.
- Missing an appeal deadline.
- Failing to submit key evidence.

To succeed, you generally must show the lawyer's performance was deficient and that it likely changed the outcome.

## The Lozada requirements

The Board of Immigration Appeals' decision in Matter of Lozada sets procedural steps for an ineffective assistance claim. Usually you must:

1. **Submit a detailed sworn declaration** describing your agreement with the former lawyer: what they were hired to do and what they told you.
2. **Notify the former lawyer** of the allegations and give them a chance to respond, then include any response.
3. **State whether you filed a complaint** with the appropriate disciplinary authority, such as the state bar, and if not, why not.

Some federal circuits apply these steps more flexibly than others. Missing a step can cause a motion to be denied even when the underlying mistake was serious.

## Deadlines and equitable tolling

A motion to reopen usually must be filed within 90 days of the final order, and an in absentia order has its own deadlines. Courts can extend these through **equitable tolling** when you show you were diligent once you discovered the problem. That means acting quickly once you learn a lawyer failed you. Months of delay after discovery weaken a tolling argument.

## Notario and consultant fraud

In many countries, a "notario" is a trained legal professional. In the United States, a notary public is not a lawyer and cannot give immigration advice. Some people exploit this confusion to sell services they are not authorized to provide.

Warning signs include:

- Promises of guaranteed results or "new laws" that do not exist.
- Asking you to sign blank forms or forms with false information.
- Keeping your original documents.
- Refusing to give receipts or copies.

Only licensed attorneys and accredited representatives working for recognized organizations can give immigration legal advice.

## Remedies for notario fraud

- **Fix the filing.** Applications filed with false information may need to be withdrawn or corrected, and a lawyer should review your full history before anything new is filed.
- **Report it.** Complaints can go to the Federal Trade Commission, your state attorney general, and EOIR's disciplinary counsel.
- **Recover money.** Some states have consumer protection laws that allow recovery from unauthorized practitioners.

## What to collect now

- Contracts, receipts, and payment records.
- Every document filed in your case. You can request your file from USCIS through FOIA.
- Messages, emails, and texts with the former lawyer or consultant.
- Notices from USCIS or the court.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to file a bar complaint?
Lozada asks you to state whether a complaint was filed and, if not, why not. Some circuits accept a reasonable explanation. Filing is usually the safer course.

### Can I sue my former lawyer for money?
A civil malpractice claim may be possible under state law, separate from immigration remedies. It does not fix your immigration case.

### What if my lawyer was disbarred?
You may still need to follow the Lozada steps, though notice to a disbarred lawyer can be handled differently. Document what happened.

### Can a USCIS denial be fixed if a notario filed it?
Often through a motion to reopen or reconsider, or a new filing that corrects the record. False statements may need to be addressed directly.

### How do I check if someone is a licensed lawyer?
Check the state bar website for the lawyer's license, or EOIR's list of accredited representatives for nonprofit staff.

### Is there a deadline to report notario fraud?
Consumer protection claims have their own deadlines under state law. Report quickly.

### Can I get my documents back from a notario?
Ask in writing for your file. If they refuse, report it to consumer protection authorities and request your records from USCIS through FOIA.

### Will reporting fraud affect my immigration case?
Victims who report fraud are generally not penalized for the report itself, but false information already filed in your name must be addressed carefully.
