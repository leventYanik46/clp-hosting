Since 2025, many people attending immigration court hearings have watched the government attorney move to dismiss their case, only to be arrested by immigration officers in the hallway moments later. The dismissal is not a win. It removes the case from the judge's control so the government can process the person for **expedited removal**, a fast-track process with no hearing before an immigration judge. Understanding this tactic can change what you do at your next hearing.

## Why dismissal can be dangerous

Removal proceedings in immigration court give you a judge, the chance to apply for relief, and the right to appeal. Expedited removal does not. It allows immigration officers to order removal without a hearing for certain people who cannot show they have been in the U.S. long enough or were properly admitted.

When DHS moves to dismiss and the judge grants the motion, the court case ends. If the person falls within the group eligible for expedited removal, ICE can then detain them and process them under that faster system.

## Who is at risk of expedited removal

In January 2025, DHS expanded expedited removal to its maximum statutory scope. It can now apply to noncitizens anywhere in the U.S. who:

- Were not admitted or paroled, and
- Cannot show they have been continuously present in the U.S. for at least two years.

Certain people paroled into the country have also been targeted after their parole was terminated. Lawful permanent residents, people admitted with visas, and people who can prove two years of presence are generally outside its reach.

## What to do if DHS moves to dismiss your case

**Ask for time and oppose the motion.** You, or your lawyer, can ask the judge for time to respond and can oppose dismissal. Judges have discretion to deny a motion to dismiss, especially when you have a pending application for relief such as asylum.

**Show your time in the U.S.** Evidence of at least two years of continuous presence, such as leases, pay stubs, school records, medical records, and tax filings, can take you outside expedited removal. Bring copies to every hearing.

**Raise fear of return.** If you fear persecution or torture, say so clearly. People placed in expedited removal who express fear are entitled to a credible fear interview. A positive result sends the case back to immigration court.

**Prepare for an arrest.** Know your rights. Have someone you trust who knows your A-number, your lawyer's contact information, and where your documents are kept.

## If you are arrested after a dismissal

- Do not sign documents you do not understand, especially a stipulated removal or voluntary departure form.
- State that you want to speak with a lawyer.
- If you fear returning to your country, say so.
- Family members can locate you using the ICE Online Detainee Locator with your A-number or name and country of birth.

Federal court habeas petitions have challenged some of these arrests and detentions. Whether that route fits depends on your facts.

## Should you still go to court?

Yes. Missing a hearing leads to an in absentia removal order, which is far harder to undo. Attend with counsel if at all possible and with evidence of your presence and any pending applications.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the judge refuse DHS's motion to dismiss?
Yes. Dismissal is not automatic. Judges consider the motion and any opposition, and some have denied dismissals where respondents had pending applications for relief.

### Does expedited removal apply to people who entered with a visa?
Generally no. Expedited removal applies to people who were not admitted or paroled, or to certain arriving noncitizens. People admitted on visas are usually placed in regular removal proceedings.

### What documents best prove two years in the U.S.?
Records with your name and dates from independent sources: leases, utility bills, pay stubs, tax returns, school and medical records, and bank statements.

### Can I appeal an expedited removal order?
There is very limited review. Fear-based claims through the credible fear process and certain federal court challenges are the main paths.

### What is a credible fear interview?
A screening interview with an asylum officer for people in expedited removal who express fear. A positive result moves the case to immigration court.

### Should I stop going to court because of the arrests?
No. An in absentia removal order is often worse and can be enforced at any time.

### Can a lawyer be with me at the hearing?
Yes, and having counsel present gives you someone who can respond immediately to a motion to dismiss.

### Does a pending asylum application protect me from dismissal?
Not automatically, but it is an important factor a judge can weigh in deciding whether to grant dismissal.
