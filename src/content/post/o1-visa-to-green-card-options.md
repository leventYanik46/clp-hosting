Moving from an O-1 visa to a green card is common, but it is not automatic. The O-1 is a temporary work visa. A green card requires a separate petition with a different legal standard, and an approved O-1 does not guarantee that the green card case will succeed. What it does give you is a head start on evidence, plus the legal room to plan.

## Why the O-1 works well as a bridge

The O-1 allows dual intent, which means you can pursue permanent residence without jeopardizing your O-1 status. That matters: with some temporary visas, filing for a green card can create trouble at renewal or at the border. With an O-1, you can keep working while a petition is pending.

## The main routes

Three green card categories fit people who already have O-1 status.

### EB-1A: extraordinary ability

The EB-1A is the closest match to the O-1. It does not need a job offer, and you can file for yourself. USCIS uses a two-step review. First, you show you meet at least three of ten listed criteria, or that you have a one-time major achievement. Second, USCIS makes an overall judgment about whether the whole record shows sustained national or international acclaim and a place among the small percentage at the top of your field.

The EB-1A standard is higher than the O-1 standard in practice. A record that satisfies O-1 officers may not satisfy the second step.

### EB-2 national interest waiver

The NIW needs an advanced degree or exceptional ability. Instead of proving acclaim, you show that your proposed work has substantial merit and national importance, that you are well positioned to advance it, and that waiving the usual job-offer requirement benefits the United States. It is a better fit when your achievements are strong but not at the very top, or when you work in an area of clear national interest.

### Employer-sponsored routes

If you have an employer ready to sponsor you, the EB-2 or EB-3 through PERM, or EB-1B for outstanding researchers, may be available. These depend on the employer, not on your own acclaim.

## How your O-1 evidence carries over

A lot of the work transfers. Press coverage, awards, judging experience, expert letters and publications often support both filings. But two points need attention:

- **Different framing.** An O-1 petition shows eligibility to work in a role. An EB-1A shows sustained acclaim and a record. Letters may need to be rewritten, and some may need to come from independent experts who know your work without a personal connection to you.
- **Timing and backlogs.** Priority dates and visa availability differ by category and country of birth, which can make one route faster than another regardless of strength.

## Strategy questions

1. Is your record strong enough for EB-1A now, or would the NIW be safer?
2. Can you file both at once, as a backup?
3. How long does your O-1 last, and when do you need to extend it?
4. How will your travel and your dependents be affected during the process?
