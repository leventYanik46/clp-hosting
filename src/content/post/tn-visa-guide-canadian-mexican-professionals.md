The TN visa lets Canadian and Mexican citizens work in the United States in certain professional occupations under the USMCA trade agreement. There is no annual cap and no lottery, and the application can be fast, which is why it is one of the most useful work visas for people who qualify. The catch is that you must fit one of the listed professions and meet its specific requirements.

## Who qualifies for a TN

You must be a citizen of Canada or Mexico, have a job offer from a U.S. employer in a profession on the USMCA list, and hold the credentials that profession requires. The list includes occupations such as engineers, accountants, scientists, management consultants, computer systems analysts, and others. Each has its own minimum requirement, usually a bachelor's degree or a license.

The job duties must match the listed profession. A common problem is a job title that sounds right but whose actual duties fall outside the list. TN status is for temporary work and generally does not allow self-employment in the U.S.

## How Canadians and Mexicans apply

- **Canadian citizens** do not need a visa. They can apply for TN status at a U.S. port of entry, at certain preclearance airports, or by filing a petition with USCIS.
- **Mexican citizens** must apply for a TN visa at a U.S. consulate in Mexico, then enter on that visa.

Both groups need to present evidence of their citizenship, their credentials, and a letter from the employer.

## The TN support letter

The employer's letter is a key document. It should typically explain:

- The profession and the position being offered
- The job duties in detail, showing they match the listed profession
- The length of the assignment
- The salary or other compensation
- The qualifications of the applicant
- The employer's relationship to the applicant (as employee or contractor)

Vague letters can lead to a denial at the border. Have the employer's attorney or a qualified professional review it before you travel. The letter should be accurate and specific.

## Duration and renewal

TN status is granted for up to three years at a time. It can be renewed, there is no fixed overall limit, but each admission and renewal must reflect a temporary stay. Spouses and unmarried children under twenty-one may be eligible for TD status.

## From TN to a green card

The TN requires that you intend a temporary stay, and it does not allow dual intent like the H-1B or L-1. That does not forbid you from seeking permanent residence. Many TN holders obtain green cards through employer sponsorship under EB-2 or EB-3 through PERM, or through other categories if they qualify. The difficulty is timing: while a green card case is pending, an officer at the border may question whether you intend to remain temporary. Ask an attorney how to sequence the process and how to handle renewals.

## Common problems

- Job duties that do not match the listed profession
- Missing proof of degree or license
- Support letters that are too general
- Self-employed or contractor arrangements that do not qualify
- Renewals after a green card process has started
