Asylum applications used to be free. Under the 2025 budget law known as H.R. 1, they are not. Applicants now pay a $100 fee to file Form I-589, plus an Annual Asylum Fee for each year the application stays pending. USCIS set that annual fee at $102 for fiscal year 2026 after an inflation adjustment. A Department of Homeland Security rule effective May 29, 2026 added serious consequences for not paying it, though a federal court stayed some of those consequences in August 2026.

## The asylum application fee

New asylum applicants pay $100 when filing Form I-589. No fee waiver is available. If USCIS rejects the application for any reason, including later nonpayment of the annual fee, the filing fee is not refunded, and refiling requires a new fee.

## The Annual Asylum Fee

Applicants with an asylum application that has been pending for at least a year must pay the Annual Asylum Fee for each year it remains pending. Key features:

- The fee is per application, not per person. A family on one application pays one fee.
- No fee waiver is available.
- USCIS or the immigration court sends a notice. Applicants have **30 days from the notice** to pay.
- Payment generally goes through the USCIS online account for affirmative cases. Cases in immigration court follow EOIR's procedures, which have their own instructions.

## What happens if you do not pay

Under the interim final rule effective May 29, 2026, failing to pay the annual fee within 30 days of notice can lead USCIS to **reject the pending asylum application**. Nonpayment can also lead to **denial of related employment authorization** applications for asylum seekers. For a person without other status, rejection of the asylum application can lead to referral to immigration court and removal proceedings.

In immigration court, failure to pay can lead a judge to treat the application as abandoned.

## Where the court challenge stands

The fees themselves come from Congress, and a federal judge in Maryland allowed collection to proceed in early 2026. The DHS rule adding penalties was challenged separately. On August 5, 2026, a federal court in Massachusetts stayed the rule's consequences for USCIS cases while litigation continues.

That means, as of this writing:

- The fee is still owed.
- The penalties for nonpayment in USCIS cases are paused by court order.
- Court proceedings and policy can change quickly.

Do not treat the stay as permission to skip payment. If the stay is lifted, applicants who did not pay could face rejection on short notice. Paying on time is the safest course.

## Other fees that affect asylum seekers

The same law and rule affect related filings:

- Employment authorization for asylum applicants carries its own fees and renewal requirements.
- A new fee applies to some Form I-94 requests.
- Certain TPS employment authorization changes were made at the same time.

## Practical steps

- Make sure USCIS and the immigration court have your current mailing address. Notices sent to an old address still count.
- Create or check your USCIS online account.
- Keep proof of every payment.
- If you cannot pay, speak with counsel or a nonprofit legal provider before the 30 days run out, since no waiver exists.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every family member pay the annual fee?
No. The fee is per application. Family members included on one I-589 share one fee.

### Can I pay the annual asylum fee before receiving a notice?
Follow USCIS and EOIR instructions. Payment generally follows notice, and paying through the wrong channel can cause problems.

### What if I never received the notice?
Notices sent to your last address on file generally count. Keep your address updated and check your online account.

### Does the fee apply to withholding of removal?
The fee is tied to pending asylum applications on Form I-589. Withholding claims are made on the same form, so check with counsel how it applies in your case.

### Can a lawyer or nonprofit pay for me?
Third parties can usually make payments on your behalf if the payment is correctly linked to your case.

### Does paying the fee speed up my case?
No. The fee does not change processing order.

### Is there a fee for asylum work permits too?
Yes. Initial and renewal employment authorization applications for asylum seekers carry their own fees under current rules.

### What if my asylum case is in immigration court rather than with USCIS?
EOIR has its own instructions for paying the annual fee in court cases. Follow the court's notice and keep proof of payment for your next hearing.
