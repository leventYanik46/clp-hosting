An E-2 visa does not lead to a green card on its own. It is a temporary status for treaty investors, and nothing about holding it, however long, converts into permanent residence. If you are an E-2 investor who wants a green card, you need a separate basis, and you need to plan around one specific legal issue: the E-2 requires that you intend to leave the United States when your status ends.

## Why the E-2 has no direct green card path

Unlike the H-1B and L-1, the E-2 does not allow dual intent in the same open way. The visa is meant for someone developing and directing a business who is expected to depart when the investment ends. That does not mean filing for a green card automatically ends your E-2 status. But an application for a new E-2 visa at a consulate, or a trip back through the border, can raise questions if an officer sees evidence that you intend to stay permanently. Many investors plan the order of filings with that in mind.

## Realistic routes from an E-2

### EB-5 immigrant investor

The EB-5 is the category built around investment. It generally requires a larger investment than the E-2 and the creation of qualifying jobs. Some E-2 investors move to EB-5 when they have more capital to commit and want permanence. It involves a separate process with its own source-of-funds documentation.

### EB-1C multinational manager

If your U.S. business is affiliated with a foreign company you have managed, and the other requirements are met, EB-1C may be available. It depends on the corporate relationship and your role.

### EB-2 national interest waiver

If your work has substantial merit and national importance, you hold an advanced degree or exceptional ability, and the other prongs fit, an NIW self-petition is possible. This is less common for operators of small businesses but fits some founders with strong technical or scientific records.

### Employer-sponsored routes through PERM

An E-2 employee can be sponsored by the company through PERM, EB-2 or EB-3. For an owner sponsoring themselves, it is more complicated, because the labor certification process must show a bona fide job opening, and an owner's control over the process raises concerns.

### Family-based routes

If you marry a U.S. citizen or have a qualifying relative, a family petition can be an option. This is a different legal track with its own timeline.

### Other

Some investors qualify through the diversity lottery or other categories, depending on country of birth.

## Planning around the intent issue

- **Time your filings.** Discuss the order of any green card filing and E-2 renewal with an attorney before submitting either.
- **Keep the E-2 business genuine.** Whatever you plan for permanent residence, the E-2 case must remain valid and the business real.
- **Document your ties.** Evidence of the business and of your intent to depart when the status ends supports consular renewals.
- **Watch dependents.** Spouses and children may have separate planning needs.

## Choosing a strategy

The right route depends on how much capital you can invest, your role in the business, your education and track record, your family situation and your country of birth. Backlogs differ by category and country, so a route that looks fast on paper may not be.
