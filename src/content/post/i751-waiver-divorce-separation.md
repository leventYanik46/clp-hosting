If your conditional green card came through marriage and the marriage has ended, you can still remove the conditions. Instead of filing Form I-751 jointly with your spouse, you file it alone and ask USCIS to waive the joint filing requirement. The most common basis is that you entered the marriage in good faith even though it ended in divorce. The waiver turns on the evidence you can show about the marriage itself.

## Who needs an I-751 waiver

A conditional green card is issued when a marriage-based green card is approved within two years of the wedding. The card is valid for two years. Normally the couple files Form I-751 together in the 90 days before it expires. You need a waiver if you cannot file jointly because:

- The marriage ended in divorce or annulment.
- You or your child were battered or subjected to extreme cruelty by your spouse.
- Removal from the U.S. would cause you extreme hardship.

A widow or widower whose spouse died can also file without the spouse, under a separate provision.

## The three I-751 waiver grounds

### Good faith marriage ended by divorce or annulment

You must show that you married in good faith, meaning you intended to build a life together, not to obtain a green card. The reason the marriage ended does not matter. The focus is on the start and life of the marriage.

The divorce must be final before USCIS decides the case. You can file the waiver while divorce proceedings are pending; USCIS will usually issue a request for evidence asking for the final decree, giving you time to submit it.

### Battery or extreme cruelty

If your U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse abused you or your child, you can file without the spouse and without waiting for a divorce. USCIS considers credible evidence of abuse, such as police reports, protective orders, medical records, counseling records, and statements from people who know what happened.

### Extreme hardship

This ground asks whether removal would cause extreme hardship to you, based on circumstances that arose during the period of conditional residence. It is harder to prove and less commonly used alone.

You can select more than one ground on the same form.

## Timing

Waiver-based I-751s are not limited to the 90-day window before the card expires. You can file as soon as you qualify, for example once a divorce is final. If your card has already expired, file as soon as possible with an explanation.

A filed I-751 extends your status. The receipt notice, together with your card, serves as proof of status while the case is pending.

## Evidence of a good faith marriage

USCIS looks for proof that you and your spouse shared a real life:

- Joint leases or mortgages and utility bills.
- Joint bank accounts and tax returns.
- Insurance policies naming each other.
- Birth certificates of children born to the marriage.
- Photos over time and travel records.
- Affidavits from friends and family who knew you as a couple.
- Evidence of counseling or attempts to save the marriage.

A short marriage is not disqualifying, but it means each piece of evidence matters more.

## The interview

USCIS may interview waiver applicants. Expect questions about how you met, your life together, and why the marriage ended. Answer honestly. Contradictions with documents cause more problems than a difficult personal history.

## If the waiver is denied

A denial is not reviewable by appeal to USCIS, but the case is typically sent to immigration court, where you can ask the immigration judge to review the waiver again.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file the I-751 waiver while still married but separated?
Yes, under the abuse or extreme hardship grounds. For the good faith ground, the divorce must be final before USCIS decides, though you can file while it is pending.

### Will my ex-spouse be contacted?
USCIS may contact the former spouse or ask for information. Do not ask your ex-spouse to sign anything they are unwilling to sign.

### Does a short marriage hurt my case?
It means USCIS will look closely at your evidence. A short marriage can still be a good faith marriage.

### Can I travel while my waiver is pending?
Yes, with your expired conditional card and the I-751 receipt notice that extends your status. Check that the receipt's extension period covers your trip.

### Do my children need their own I-751?
Children who received conditional residence at the same time can be included on your form. Children who got status later may need their own filing.

### Can I apply for citizenship before the I-751 is approved?
You can file the N-400 when eligible, but the I-751 must be approved before naturalization. USCIS often decides them together.

### What if my spouse abused me but I have no police report?
USCIS considers any credible evidence, including statements, medical records, photos, messages, and declarations from people who knew about the abuse.
