A lawful permanent resident can sponsor exactly two kinds of relatives: a spouse and unmarried children. An unmarried child under 21 falls into the F2A category of the family second preference; an unmarried son or daughter who is 21 or older falls into F2B. Unlike a U.S. citizen's child, an F2A child needs a visa number, so the case runs on a priority date and the monthly Visa Bulletin. Our [F2A and child green card](/practice-area/key-services/child-green-card) practice handles these petitions from filing through the interview.

## What the F2A green card category covers

The F2A category uses the same definition of "child" as every other family petition, found in section 101(b)(1) of the Immigration and Nationality Act: a biological child, a stepchild where the marriage took place before the child turned 18, a child born out of wedlock with the right proof of the parental link, or an adopted child who meets the age, custody and residence rules. The child must be unmarried and under 21.

There is no category for a permanent resident's married child. If an F2A or F2B beneficiary marries before the green card is issued, the regulations treat the petition as automatically revoked. If you later naturalize, you can file a new petition in F3 for a married son or daughter, but the old priority date is gone.

One advantage of F2A is easy to miss. Because it is a preference category, an F2A petition for your spouse carries your spouse's unmarried children under 21 as derivative beneficiaries. That holds even if you married after a child turned 18 and no step relationship exists under immigration law. A U.S. citizen in the same position cannot sponsor a child who was already 18 at the wedding.

## Form I-130 and the priority date

You start with Form I-130 for each child you petition for directly, with a copy of your green card, the child's birth certificate, and whatever documents prove the relationship: a marriage certificate for a stepchild, an adoption decree and custody records for an adopted child, legitimation or relationship evidence for a child born out of wedlock. Foreign-language documents need certified translations. Our guide to what Form I-130 is and how it works walks through the form itself.

The date USCIS receives the petition becomes the priority date, your child's place in line. USCIS approves the petition on the relationship alone; approval does not mean a visa is available. Nothing you do later moves the priority date forward, which is why filing early matters more than filing perfectly. Our post on the difference between a [priority date and a filing date](/priority-date-vs-filing-date) explains how the two interact.

## Reading the Visa Bulletin for F2A

The Department of State publishes the Visa Bulletin every month with two charts. The Final Action Dates chart shows when a visa may actually be issued or an adjustment approved. The Dates for Filing chart shows when the National Visa Center may begin collecting documents and, in months when USCIS says so, when adjustment applicants may file Form I-485. Find the F2A row, then the column for your child's country of birth (most countries share the "All Chargeability Areas" column; a few have their own). If your priority date is earlier than the listed date, that step is open. A "C" means the category is current for everyone.

F2A has historically moved faster than the other family preference categories, but it also retrogresses. Check the current bulletin on the State Department site, and USCIS's monthly announcement of which chart adjustment applicants may use.

## Adjustment of status versus consular processing

If your child is in the United States, the F2A rules are stricter than the immediate relative rules. A preference beneficiary must generally have been lawfully admitted, must be in lawful status when filing, and must not have worked without authorization, unless section 245(i) protects them through an old petition or labor certification. An overstay that would be forgiven for a citizen's child is not forgiven here. When the date is current and the child qualifies, you file Form I-485 with the medical exam, the Form I-864 affidavit of support, and biometrics. Our walkthrough of [Form I-485, step by step](/what-is-form-i-485-how-to-complete-step-by-step) covers that packet.

If your child is abroad, the approved petition sits at the National Visa Center until the Dates for Filing chart reaches your priority date. The NVC then collects fees, the DS-260, civil documents and your I-864, and schedules the consular interview once the Final Action Date is also reached. Our [family reunification](/practice-area/key-services/family-reunification) page describes that consular stage in detail.

## Turning 21, getting married, and the parent naturalizing

Your child turns 21. The Child Status Protection Act subtracts the time the I-130 was pending from your child's age on the date a visa becomes available. If the result is under 21 and your child seeks the visa within one year, the child stays in F2A. If not, the petition converts automatically to F2B with the same priority date. We lay out the arithmetic in our post on [how the CSPA age calculation works](/cspa-age-out-calculation-explained).

Your child marries. The petition is revoked. There is no fix short of your naturalization and a new F3 petition. Tell your child plainly to wait.

You naturalize. An F2A child becomes an immediate relative with no visa wait, and the child's CSPA age is frozen on the date of your naturalization. An F2B son or daughter moves to F1 with the same priority date, unless F2B is moving faster for that country, in which case the son or daughter may elect in writing to stay in F2B. Send a copy of your naturalization certificate to USCIS or the NVC, whichever holds the case. Our [citizenship and naturalization](/practice-area/key-services/citizenship-and-naturalization) page covers the timing of an N-400 for parents who are weighing exactly this.

## Mistakes that cost years

- Waiting to file until you naturalize. File the F2A petition now; the upgrade happens automatically later, and the earlier priority date is preserved.
- Moving without updating the NVC. The State Department can terminate a case if nobody responds within a year of being notified that a visa is available.
- Letting the one-year CSPA window pass without a DS-260 or I-485 on file.
- Assuming a child who entered on a visitor visa and overstayed can adjust. In F2A, that child usually cannot.
