If you were born in India or China and hold an H-1B, your employer-sponsored green card may be many years away. Moving from H-1B to EB-5 is one of the few ways to step out of that line without changing jobs. You invest in a qualifying U.S. business, the investment creates jobs, and you receive a green card in a separate category with its own annual visa numbers.

## Why H-1B workers look at EB-5

EB-2 and EB-3 waits for Indian nationals are measured in years, and in some categories in decades. EB-5 has its own allotment, and the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022 reserved a share of those numbers for investments in rural areas, high-unemployment areas, and infrastructure projects. Those reserved categories have often been current or close to current for applicants from backlogged countries. That is the whole appeal.

The tradeoff is capital. The minimum investment is $800,000 for a project in a targeted employment area or an infrastructure project, and $1,050,000 for everything else. The money must be genuinely at risk. Nobody can lawfully guarantee you a return, or even the return of your principal.

## How the H-1B to EB-5 path works

The sequence is the same for most H-1B investors:

1. **Choose a project.** Most H-1B holders invest through a regional center project rather than building their own business, for reasons explained below.
2. **Document the source of your funds.** USCIS wants a lawful path for every dollar: salary savings, the sale of property, gifts from family members (with proof of the giver's own lawful funds), or loans secured by your own assets.
3. **File Form I-526E.** If the Visa Bulletin shows a visa number available for your category and country, you can file Form I-485 at the same time. Concurrent filing lets you request a work permit and travel document while the petition is pending.
4. **Receive conditional residence.** The green card is valid for two years. Before it expires you file Form I-829 to show the investment was sustained and the jobs were created.

## Keeping your H-1B while you wait

H-1B is a dual intent status. Filing an immigrant petition does not, by itself, put your H-1B at risk. Many investors keep working on H-1B even after filing the I-485, so they are not relying on a pending work permit. Keep your H-1B extensions current until the green card is approved. If the EB-5 case slows down, you want a working status underneath it.

One development to watch: USCIS guidance issued in May 2026 tells officers to approve adjustment of status for people in temporary status only in limited circumstances, while carving out dual intent categories such as H-1B. How that guidance is applied affects whether you finish the process inside the U.S. or at a consulate abroad, so confirm the current position before you file.

## Can you run the business yourself?

Investing passively is allowed on H-1B. Working for the business you invested in is not, unless that business files its own H-1B petition for you. This is why regional center projects dominate among H-1B investors. You hold an interest as a limited partner or member, and the project creates the jobs. You keep your current job, and nothing about the investment requires you to do unauthorized work.

A direct investment in a company you manage can work, but the timing is harder. You generally can't start operating the business until you hold work authorization that permits it, and the job creation clock keeps running.

## Mistakes that slow H-1B to EB-5 cases down

- **Weak fund tracing.** Moving money through several accounts without statements, or relying on cash gifts without showing where the giver got the money, is the most common source of Requests for Evidence.
- **Assuming every project qualifies for a reserved visa.** The rural, high-unemployment, and infrastructure set-asides are counted separately and depend on the project's specific designation. Read the offering documents and confirm which category your investment is filed under.
- **Ignoring the program calendar.** The regional center program is authorized through September 30, 2027. Petitions filed before that date have statutory protections if the program lapses, which makes filing dates worth planning around.
- **Letting H-1B status lapse.** A pending EB-5 case does not replace a valid status. Keep the H-1B alive.

## Is EB-5 right for you?

EB-5 makes the most sense if you face a long backlog, can invest without taking on debt you can't carry, and are comfortable with a passive investment whose outcome you don't control. If your EB-2 or EB-3 priority date is close to current, staying the course may be cheaper and simpler. Many people keep both cases alive and use whichever finishes first. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 can be included as derivatives on the EB-5 case, which matters if a child is close to aging out in the employment-based queue.

Before you commit funds, have the project documents and your source-of-funds story reviewed together. Problems are far easier to fix before the money moves than after.
