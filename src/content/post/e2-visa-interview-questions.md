Most E-2 visas are decided at a U.S. consulate, and the interview is often short. In a few minutes the officer checks whether your investment is real, whether you will run the business, and whether the company will do more than support your family. The questions asked at an E-2 visa interview follow those themes, so you can prepare for them.

## Why the E-2 interview matters

Unlike many visas, the E-2 has no USCIS petition approval in most cases before the consulate. The consular officer reviews your full application, usually a detailed package submitted in advance, and makes the decision. The interview is the officer's chance to confirm that you understand and control the business described on paper.

## Questions about your investment

Expect questions such as:

- How much have you invested, and what did the money pay for?
- Where did the funds come from?
- Is the money already spent or committed?
- What happens to the money if the visa is refused?

**Why they ask:** The investment must be substantial, at risk, and from a lawful source. Money sitting in a bank account that you could withdraw freely is not at risk. Officers look for funds that have been spent or irrevocably committed to the business.

**How to prepare:** Know your numbers without notes. Be able to walk through the path of the funds from their origin, such as a salary, the sale of property, or a business, to the U.S. company's account and then to specific purchases.

## Questions about the business

- What does your company do, and who are your customers?
- How many employees do you have now, and how many will you hire?
- What are your projected revenues in the first few years?
- Who are your competitors?

**Why they ask:** The E-2 business cannot be "marginal," meaning it must have the capacity to generate more than a minimal living for you and your family, or have a meaningful economic impact.

**How to prepare:** Your answers should match your business plan. If the plan says you will hire four employees in the second year, do not say "maybe ten" at the window.

## Questions about your role

- What will you do day to day?
- Do you own at least 50% of the business, or have operational control?
- What experience do you have in this industry?

**Why they ask:** The E-2 investor must develop and direct the enterprise. Passive investors do not qualify.

## Questions about your nationality and intent

- What is your nationality, and how did you obtain it?
- Do you intend to leave the U.S. when your E-2 status ends?

E-2 requires that you are a national of a treaty country, and that the business is at least 50% owned by treaty nationals of that country. You must also intend to depart when your status ends, although you do not need to keep a foreign residence.

## Common reasons interviews go wrong

- **Inconsistent numbers.** Revenue, hiring, or investment figures that differ from the application.
- **Not knowing your own plan.** An investor who relied entirely on a consultant to write the plan may struggle to answer basic questions.
- **Funds not committed.** Money still in an escrow account without a clear release condition, or held in a personal account.
- **Unclear source of funds.** Gifts or loans without documentation.

## Practical tips for the day

- Bring an organized copy of the submitted package with tabs.
- Answer the question asked, briefly and accurately. Officers usually move on once they are satisfied.
- Speak in your own words. Memorized phrasing sounds rehearsed.
- If you do not know an answer, say so rather than guessing.

## If the E-2 is refused

A refusal letter states the legal basis. Some refusals can be addressed by submitting additional documents; others require a new application with a stronger case. Reapplying with the same evidence rarely helps.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does the E-2 interview take?
Often only a few minutes. Most of the evaluation happens before, when the officer reviews your submitted package.

### Should my spouse attend the interview?
Family members applying as dependents usually attend with the investor, depending on the consulate's instructions.

### Can I bring my attorney to the interview?
Attorneys generally cannot attend consular interviews. Your attorney prepares the package and helps you prepare for the questions.

### What should I do if the officer asks for more documents?
You may receive a refusal under section 221(g) with a request for documents. Submit exactly what is requested by the stated method and deadline.

### Do I need to speak English?
No. Interviews can often be conducted in your language, or the consulate can provide interpretation. Clear answers matter more than fluent English.

### Will the officer ask about my home country ties?
E-2 does not require a foreign residence, but officers may ask about your intent to depart when status ends. Answer honestly.

### What happens if I am approved?
The consulate keeps your passport to print the visa and returns it, usually within days.
