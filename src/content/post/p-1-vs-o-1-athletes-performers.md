For an athlete, the choice is usually between the P-1A and the O-1A. For a band or ensemble, it is between one P-1B petition for the group and separate O-1B petitions for members who qualify on their own. Deciding between a P-1 vs O-1 visa comes down to four things: the standard of proof, whether you perform alone or as a unit, how long you need to stay, and whether you can show a residence abroad. Neither classification has an annual cap, both require a U.S. petitioner and a labor consultation, and both can be premium processed. The differences are in the details, and our [P-1 visa for athletes and entertainment groups](/practice-area/key-services/p-1-visa) page is the starting point for the P side.

## The standard of proof

The P-1A asks whether the athlete or team is internationally recognized: a high level of achievement, renowned, leading or well known in more than one country. The petition needs a tendered contract and at least two of seven criteria. The O-1A asks for extraordinary ability shown by sustained national or international acclaim, meaning a place among the small percentage at the very top of the field, proved through a major award or at least three of eight criteria and then a review of the whole record. [O-1 visa: proving extraordinary ability to USCIS](/o-1-visa-extraordinary-ability) describes that review. For most professional athletes the P-1A is the easier standard to meet, which is why clubs use it routinely.

For performers the comparison is between the P-1B group standard (international recognition of the group as outstanding over a sustained and substantial period) and the O-1B arts standard of distinction, applied person by person. A group with a strong collective reputation but no individual stars fits the P-1B. A member whose own career is documented may qualify under the [O-1B visa for artists](/practice-area/key-services/o-1b-visa) even if the group does not.

## Individuals, teams and groups

The P-1 covers individual athletes, athletic teams and entertainment groups. It does not cover a solo entertainer performing apart from a group. The O-1 is always an individual classification; there is no group O-1 petition, so a band using O-1Bs needs a qualifying record for each member who files. The P-1B also brings its own membership rule: at least 75 percent of members must have been with the group for at least a year, subject to limited waivers. [P-1A visa requirements for athletes and teams](/p-1a-visa-requirements-athletes) covers the athlete side of the P test.

## Length of stay and extensions

An individual P-1 athlete can be approved for up to five years and extended for up to five more, with total stay capped at ten years. A P-1 team or entertainment group is approved for the time needed for the event, up to one year, and extended a year at a time. An O-1 is approved for up to three years for the event or activity and extended in one-year increments with no fixed maximum. For a long career with one club, the P-1 athlete term can be more convenient; for an open-ended stay, the O-1 has no ceiling. The P-1 time rules, and the 30-day rule for traded athletes, are covered in [how long a P-1 visa lasts and what happens if you are traded](/p-1-visa-validity-athlete-trades).

## Foreign residence, job loss and green card plans

P-1 status requires a residence abroad that you have no intention of abandoning. O-1 status does not, which removes a common line of questioning at the consulate. The O-1 also benefits from the 60-day grace period that applies when employment ends before the petition expires. As of this writing, that grace period rule lists O-1 but not P-1, so a released P-1 athlete has less room to regroup.

Both sets of regulations state that a pending immigrant petition or approved labor certification is not, by itself, a reason to deny the petition, an extension or admission. Even so, a P-1 holder with a green card case pending should expect the foreign residence question to come up at the consulate. Athletes and performers with top-level records often move to an EB-1A green card, and an O-1 record is built from the same evidence.

## Support staff and family

The support rules run in parallel. O-2 status covers essential support personnel for an O-1 artist or athlete, and P-1S covers essential support personnel for a P-1 athlete, team or group. Both require their own petition and consultation and a residence abroad. Spouses and unmarried children under 21 hold O-3 or P-4 status, which allows study but not work.

## How to choose

Ask whether the reputation belongs to a group or to individuals; a group reputation points to the P-1B. For athletes, ask whether the record clears the O-1A's top-of-field bar with independent evidence. If not, the P-1A is the realistic filing. Then weigh the time horizon and the foreign residence requirement: a player planning to settle in the United States may prefer an O-1A record that later feeds an EB-1A, while a team on a fixed contract is well served by the P-1A.
