A K-1 visa gets your fiancé into the United States. It does not give them a green card. K-1 adjustment of status is the second half of the case: after the wedding, the new spouse files Form I-485 with USCIS and becomes a permanent resident without leaving the country. The filing resembles an ordinary spousal adjustment, with two differences that matter. There is no Form I-130, because the approved I-129F and the marriage to the petitioner are the basis, and the K-1 spouse can adjust only through that one person.

## K-1 adjustment of status: what goes in the package

File as soon as the civil marriage certificate is in hand. The package includes:

- Form I-485, with a copy of the K-1 visa, the I-94 admission record, the I-129F approval notice, the marriage certificate, the spouse's birth certificate and two photographs.
- Form I-864, the affidavit of support, signed by the citizen spouse with the most recent tax return and proof of current income. The I-134 used at the consulate does not carry over; the I-864 is an enforceable contract with an income test set at 125 percent of the Federal Poverty Guidelines, and a joint sponsor is allowed if the citizen's income falls short.
- Form I-765 for a work permit and Form I-131 for advance parole.
- Form I-693, the medical exam. A K-1 applicant who had the full consular medical exam may be able to rely on it for a limited period and submit only the vaccination record; the current I-693 instructions and the timing of the overseas exam decide this, and we check it rather than assume.
- Evidence of the marriage: the certificate is required, but a short set of shared-life documents helps, and the file for the later I-751 starts now.

Our [K-1 fiancé visa](/practice-area/key-services/k-1-fiance-visa) practice treats the petition and the adjustment as one matter, so the adjustment package is prepared while the fiancé is still waiting for the interview. The I-485 form questions themselves are covered in [what Form I-485 is and how to complete it](/what-is-form-i-485-how-to-complete-step-by-step).

## Work and travel while the I-485 is pending

Once the I-485 is filed, the spouse is in a period of authorized stay even though the 90-day K-1 admission has ended. The work permit and advance parole, usually issued together as one card, arrive while the case is pending; check current USCIS processing times for Form I-765.

Until advance parole is approved, the spouse must not leave the United States. The K-1 visa was a single-entry visa and is now used up, and departure without advance parole abandons the I-485. A family emergency abroad in this window is the most painful problem in K-1 cases, and the only prevention is filing the I-131 with the I-485 on day one.

## Biometrics, the interview and the decision

USCIS schedules biometrics at an application support center and, in most K-1 cases, an interview at the field office. Both spouses attend. The officer confirms the marriage took place with the petitioner, reviews the I-485, and asks about the relationship and the shared household. Because USCIS and the consulate already examined the relationship once, the interview is often shorter than in a fresh I-130 case, but the officer still expects to see a life together: a shared address, joint accounts where the couple has had time to open them, and the ordinary paper of a new household.

If the marriage is less than two years old on the day the I-485 is approved, which is nearly always true for a K-1 couple, the spouse becomes a conditional permanent resident with a two-year card. Form I-751 is due in the 90 days before that card expires; the deadline and the evidence are explained in [when to file Form I-751](/form-i-751-removal-of-conditions-timing). The route from there to naturalization runs through the three-year rule for spouses of citizens.

## Rules that are specific to K-1 spouses

The K-1 is a single-purpose visa, and the law carries that restriction into adjustment. A K-1 entrant cannot adjust status on any basis other than marriage to the citizen who filed the I-129F. If the engagement ends and the fiancé later marries someone else, or receives a job offer that would support an employment-based green card, adjustment inside the United States is closed; the person must depart and process at a consulate. The K-1 also cannot be changed to any other nonimmigrant status.

The 90-day marriage deadline affects status, not eligibility. A couple who marries each other after the 90 days have passed can still adjust, because the application rests on the marriage to the petitioner, but the spouse has been out of status in the meantime and the delay should be explained in the filing. What happens when the wedding does not take place at all is covered in [what happens if you do not marry within 90 days](/k-1-visa-90-day-marriage-deadline).

K-2 children adjust alongside the K-1 parent. Each child files a separate I-485 and must remain unmarried. Under Board of Immigration Appeals precedent, a K-2 child who was under 21 when admitted does not lose eligibility by turning 21 while the case is pending, but a child near that age should be filed first and tracked so the question never has to be argued.

## Comparing the K-1 adjustment stage to a spousal visa

The two-step structure is the main cost of the K-1. A spouse who enters on a CR-1 immigrant visa arrives as a resident, can work at once and files nothing further until the I-751. A K-1 spouse enters sooner but then waits again for the work permit and the green card, pays a second set of filing fees and attends a second interview.
