Once the National Visa Center reviews your documents and marks the case "documentarily qualified," the next step is an interview at a U.S. embassy or consulate. For many families, that step takes months, and in some places much longer. The delay usually reflects limited appointment capacity at the post, not a problem with your case. A few things can help, but most applicants cannot jump the queue.

## How the NVC timeline works

After USCIS approves an immigrant petition, it sends the case to the NVC. You pay the fees, file Form DS-260, and upload civil documents and the Affidavit of Support. NVC reviews them. When everything is complete, you are documentarily qualified, or "DQ."

DQ means your case is ready for an interview. It does not mean an interview has been scheduled.

## Why interview scheduling is delayed

- **Limited embassy capacity.** Each post schedules based on staff and space. Some posts have large backlogs.
- **Priorities among visa types.** The State Department sets priorities, generally placing immediate relatives of U.S. citizens, fiancé(e)s, and adoptions ahead of other categories.
- **Visa availability.** For preference categories, a current priority date is needed before scheduling.
- **Policy changes.** Pauses or restrictions affecting certain nationalities, such as the 2026 immigrant visa pause for nationals of 75 countries, can stop issuance even when interviews proceed.

## What you can do

**Confirm your status and post.** Use the NVC's public inquiry form or the CEAC case status to confirm you are DQ and which embassy will interview you.

**Request an expedite when there is a real emergency.** Embassies consider expedites for urgent medical, humanitarian, or other compelling circumstances, with documentation. Routine inconvenience does not qualify.

**Consider a transfer, carefully.** Transfers to another post are possible only if you can show residence or a strong reason to be interviewed there, and many posts restrict transfers.

**Keep documents current.** Police certificates and medical exams have validity periods. Expired documents can cause delays when the interview comes.

**Watch for child age-out issues.** For children nearing 21, the Child Status Protection Act calculation depends on dates, and some families need to act within one year of visa availability to protect a child's eligibility.

**Consider congressional or federal court help.** A congressional office can make an inquiry. In long delays, some applicants file mandamus lawsuits against the State Department. Courts look at the length of the delay and the reasons for it, so outcomes vary.

## What does not help

- Repeated inquiries without new information.
- Paying third parties promising faster appointments.
- Traveling to the U.S. on a visitor visa while an immigrant case is pending, which can be refused because of immigrant intent.

## Adjustment of status as an alternative

A beneficiary lawfully in the U.S. may be able to adjust status rather than wait for an embassy interview. Since a May 2026 USCIS memo treats adjustment as discretionary relief, that choice needs careful review.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long after DQ is the interview usually scheduled?
It varies widely by post and category, from weeks to many months or longer.

### Can a congressional inquiry speed up my case?
It can prompt a status update or attention to a stuck case, but it does not move you ahead of others in the queue.

### Can I choose the embassy?
Generally the case goes to the post in the beneficiary's country of residence or nationality. Transfers are limited.

### What is a 221(g) refusal?
A temporary refusal while the consulate waits for more documents or completes administrative processing.

### Do I need to redo the medical exam if the interview is delayed?
Medical exams are valid for a limited time. If it expires before the interview, a new exam may be needed.

### Will my case be cancelled if I wait too long?
Cases can be terminated if you fail to act for a year after notice of visa availability. Respond to NVC communications and keep the case active.

### Can a mandamus lawsuit help with NVC delays?
In some long delays, courts have ordered action. Results depend on the length of delay and the government's reasons.

### Does paying fees early speed up scheduling?
Paying promptly keeps the case moving through NVC, but once you are documentarily qualified, scheduling depends on the post's capacity, not when you paid.
