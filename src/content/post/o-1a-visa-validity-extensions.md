An O-1A visa is valid for the time needed to complete the event or activity described in the petition, up to three years, and it can be extended in increments of up to one year for as long as the same work continues. There is no statutory maximum stay. That makes the O-1A one of the most durable temporary work classifications, but the clock is tied to a specific petition, a specific petitioner and a specific activity, so each change in your job needs a filing. This article explains the validity rules, the grace periods and the point at which an extension is no longer the right form.

## How long an O-1A visa is valid: the three-year rule

Under 8 CFR 214.2(o)(6), USCIS approves an initial O-1 petition for the period necessary to complete the event or activity, not to exceed three years. The officer does not hand out three years by default. If the employment contract runs for 18 months, expect 18 months. If the petition describes a research project with a stated end date, the approval tracks it. The regulation reads "event" broadly: a scientific project, a business project, a lecture series, a season, a tour or a group of related activities can all qualify, so a petitioner who wants the full three years should describe work that genuinely spans that period. Our [O-1A visa page](/practice-area/key-services/o-1a-visa) explains what the petition must show for the underlying classification; validity is a separate decision made on the employment terms.

Two documents carry different dates. The I-797 approval notice and the I-94 record set how long you may stay. The visa stamp in your passport sets how long you may use it to enter, and its length follows the reciprocity schedule for your nationality. An expired stamp with a valid I-94 is lawful while you remain inside the United States, but you will need a new stamp to return from travel.

## Extensions: one year at a time, for the same work

Under 8 CFR 214.2(o)(12), the petitioner files Form I-129 with a request to extend your stay to continue or complete the same event or activity, and USCIS may grant up to one year per request. The filing must include a statement explaining why the extension is needed. File before the current period expires; a timely extension by the same petitioner lets you keep working for up to 240 days while it is pending under the general rule for nonimmigrant extensions. Extensions are not capped, so an O-1A can run year after year, but every annual filing costs the petitioner time, the current USCIS filing fee and the risk of a fresh look at the file.

When the work has changed in substance (a new role, a new project, a new contract) the better filing is often a new petition rather than an extension. A new petition for a new event or activity can again be approved for up to three years. Petitioners who want to escape the one-year cycle usually draft the next phase of work as a distinct activity and file it as a new case with updated evidence.

## The ten-day and 60-day grace periods

You may be admitted up to ten days before the validity period begins and may remain up to ten days after it ends. You may not work during those ten days. Separately, if your employment ends before the petition expires, 8 CFR 214.1(l)(2) gives O-1 workers a grace period of up to 60 consecutive days, or until the I-94 expires if that comes first, once per authorized validity period. During the 60 days you remain in status and can have a new employer file an O-1 petition, file a change of status to another classification, or leave. You cannot work for the new employer until its petition is approved. If your employer ends the relationship, the petitioner is expected to notify USCIS and the approval may be revoked; the grace period is what protects you while you regroup.

## Changing employers, adding employers and amendments

The O-1A has no portability. If you accept a job with a different company, that company must file its own I-129 and request an extension of your stay, and you may start only after approval. [O-1A or H-1B: which work visa fits your record?](/o-1a-vs-h-1b-which-visa-fits) compares this with H-1B portability, which lets a worker move on filing. If you will work for several employers at once, each must file a petition, or a U.S. agent can file one petition with an itinerary covering all of them. Material changes in the terms of employment with the same petitioner require an amended petition. A new title with the same duties usually is not material; a move from research to a management role, or a founder who restructures the company that sponsors them, usually is. [Can a startup founder get an O-1 visa?](/can-startup-founder-get-o-1-visa) explains why the petitioner structure matters so much in founder cases.

## Planning past the O-1A

Because the O-1A has no maximum, some people stay on it for a decade. That is rarely the best plan. The same evidence that proved sustained acclaim supports an [EB-1A green card](/practice-area/key-services/eb-1a-green-card), and 8 CFR 214.2(o)(13) states that a pending immigrant petition or approved labor certification is not a basis for denying an O-1 extension or admission. [How do you go from an O-1 visa to a green card?](/o-1-visa-to-green-card) sets out the routes. Calendar the expiration date the day the approval arrives, start the extension or the new petition several months ahead, and decide early whether the next filing is a one-year extension, a new three-year petition, or an I-140.
