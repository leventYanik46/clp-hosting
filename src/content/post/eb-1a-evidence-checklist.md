The difference between an EB-1A petition that is approved and one that draws a Request for Evidence is usually not the beneficiary's record. It is whether each claim in the petition is backed by a document that proves it, including the context USCIS needs to weigh it: not just the award, but the award's selection criteria; not just the article, but the publication's circulation. This EB-1A evidence checklist is organized by the ten criteria at 8 CFR 204.5(h)(3) plus the two requirements that apply to every petition. Our [extraordinary ability self-petition](/practice-area/key-services/eb-1a-green-card) page sets out the legal standard; use this post to assemble the file.

## EB-1A evidence checklist for the first five criteria

### Awards

The award certificate or letter; the awarding organization's published selection criteria and eligibility; the number of nominees or applicants and the number of winners; media coverage of the award; and evidence that past recipients are recognized figures in the field. Student, internal and local awards rarely count, so choose the awards you document.

### Memberships

The membership certificate; the association's bylaws or admission rules showing that outstanding achievement is required and that recognized experts judge applicants; and evidence of the association's standing. A membership available to anyone who pays dues or holds a degree does not satisfy the criterion.

### Published material about you

Full copies of each article, with title, date, author and publication name visible; a certified translation if not in English; and evidence of the publication's standing, such as circulation or readership data, media kits or independent rankings. The material must be about you and your work, not a piece you wrote or a passing mention.

### Judging the work of others

Invitation emails or letters from journals, conferences, grant agencies or competitions; confirmation that you completed the review; editorial board appointments; and evidence of the standing of the journal or event. Documentation of completed reviews, such as reviewer records from a publisher's system, carries more weight than invitations alone.

### Original contributions of major significance

This criterion is where petitions most often fall short. Gather citation reports with field-normalized comparisons; evidence that specific papers, methods, products or patents have been adopted, licensed or built upon by others; letters from independent experts describing a particular contribution and its effect; and any press or awards tied to that contribution. The question is influence, not productivity.

## The remaining five criteria

### Scholarly articles

A full publication list with venue, date and co-authors; copies of first pages; journal impact data or acceptance rates; and for industry professionals, trade publication articles with evidence of the publication's reach. Authorship satisfies the plain terms of the criterion; the citation record then does the work at the final merits stage.

### Artistic exhibitions or showcases

Exhibition catalogs, programs, invitations, venue documentation and reviews, with evidence of the venue's standing. This criterion applies to artistic work; business or scientific displays usually fit better as comparable evidence.

### Leading or critical role

Organizational charts, employment contracts, letters from the organization describing the role and your effect on outcomes, and independent evidence of the organization's distinguished reputation, such as rankings, press or funding history. Both halves are required: the role must be critical, and the organization must be distinguished.

### High salary or remuneration

Pay statements, tax documents or contracts, placed next to reliable comparative wage data for your occupation and location, such as government wage surveys or industry salary reports. For founders, equity and total compensation can be documented, but the comparison still has to be made.

### Commercial success in the performing arts

Box office receipts, sales figures, streaming data and chart positions, with evidence of how those numbers compare in the relevant market.

## Evidence every EB-1A petition needs

Two requirements sit outside the criteria. First, 8 CFR 204.5(h)(5) requires evidence that you will continue working in your area of extraordinary ability in the United States: employment contracts, offer letters, letters from prospective employers or clients, or a detailed statement of your plans with supporting documents. Our post on [filing EB-1A without a job offer](/file-eb-1a-without-job-offer) explains what satisfies this for self-petitioners. Second, every petition needs a complete CV, passport identity pages, evidence of current status if you are in the United States, and Form I-140 itself.

Where a criterion does not readily apply to your occupation, 8 CFR 204.5(h)(4) allows comparable evidence, but you must explain why the standard criterion does not fit and why the substitute shows the same thing.

## From checklist to argument

Collecting the documents is half the work. The other half is the brief that connects them to the standard, and in particular to the final merits determination, where the officer asks whether all of this together shows sustained acclaim and a place at the top of the field. Our post on [the EB-1A final merits determination](/eb-1a-final-merits-determination) covers that stage, and our earlier post on [whether you qualify for the EB-1A](/eb-1a-visa-do-you-qualify) helps you decide which criteria to claim before you start gathering. Much of the same file supports an [O-1A visa](/practice-area/key-services/o-1a-visa) petition if a temporary step makes sense first, or an [EB-2 National Interest Waiver](/practice-area/key-services/eb-2-niw-green-card) if the endeavor is the stronger story.
