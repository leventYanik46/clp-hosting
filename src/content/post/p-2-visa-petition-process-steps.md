A P-2 petition moves through two agencies and at least three organizations before the artist steps on a U.S. stage: the labor union that consults on the exchange, USCIS, which decides the Form I-129 petition, and the consulate that issues the visa. Each handoff has its own lead time, and the P-2 visa petition process goes wrong most often when a sponsor starts at the wrong end, booking dates and selling tickets before the paperwork chain has even begun. Here is the sequence in the order it actually happens.

## Step 1: Confirm the exchange and gather the agreement

Before any form is drafted, the U.S. sponsoring organization should pull the signed reciprocal exchange agreement with its foreign counterpart and confirm it covers the specific artists and dates. If the agreement is an umbrella document, as union programs often are, you will also need the paperwork that places this particular engagement under it. The sponsor then writes a statement describing the exchange: who is going each way, when, under what terms, and why the artists are comparable. Our [P-2 visa](/practice-area/key-services/p-2-visa) practice typically starts here, because a weak agreement cannot be cured later with more exhibits.

## Step 2: Obtain the labor organization consultation

Every P petition needs a written advisory opinion from an appropriate labor organization, and a P-2 petition additionally needs evidence that the union negotiated or concurred with the exchange. Request both at the same time. Unions have their own intake procedures and fees, and some respond within days while others take weeks. If the discipline has no appropriate labor organization, the petition should say so and explain why. USCIS can proceed on the other evidence, but the omission must be addressed rather than left silent.

## Step 3: File Form I-129 with USCIS

The petitioner, which must be the U.S. sponsoring organization, a U.S. employer, or a U.S. agent, files Form I-129 with the P classification supplement, the current USCIS filing fee, and the supporting evidence. The evidence package is covered in detail in our [P-2 documents checklist](/p-2-visa-documents-checklist), but at minimum it includes the exchange agreement, the sponsor's statement, proof of comparable skills, the union materials, and the contract or itinerary. Essential support staff go in a separate petition with their own consultation.

USCIS can accept a P petition up to one year before the artist needs to enter, which is the practical answer to how early to file. Premium processing is available for I-129 P petitions as of this writing for an additional fee. If a decision is needed on a fixed date, budget for it; regular processing times vary by service center, and you should check the USCIS processing times page rather than rely on a number from a prior season.

Three outcomes are possible: approval, a request for evidence (RFE), or a denial. An RFE pauses the clock until you respond, so the time to answer it must fit inside the performance calendar. If the engagement will not wait, tell us early; sometimes a narrower filing or an alternative route such as the [P-1 visa for internationally recognized groups](/practice-area/key-services/p-1-visa) fits the dates better.

## Step 4: Visa application at the consulate

Once USCIS approves the petition, the beneficiary applies for the P-2 visa at a U.S. consulate abroad. That means completing the DS-160 online, paying the visa application fee, and attending an interview with the approval notice and a copy of the petition. Consular appointment availability varies widely by post and season, and it is the step sponsors most often forget to budget for. Dependents apply for P-4 visas at the same time, with proof of the family relationship.

Canadian citizens are visa-exempt for most nonimmigrant categories and present the approval notice at the port of entry instead. For everyone else, the consular step is mandatory and cannot be skipped by arriving as a visitor; performing for pay on a B-1 or B-2 admission is not permitted, as our post on [what B-1 and B-2 visitors can and cannot do](/b1-b2-visas-in-the-united-states-what-you-can-and-cannot-do) explains.

## Step 5: Admission and the period of stay

At the port of entry, Customs and Border Protection admits the artist for the period needed to complete the engagement, up to one year, with a short grace period before and after. The I-94 record, not the visa stamp, governs how long the artist may stay. Check it online after entry and compare it with the petition dates. If the exchange is extended, the petitioner files for an extension before the I-94 expires; the rules on duration, extensions, and family members are set out in [how long a P-2 visa lasts](/p-2-visa-duration-extensions-family).

## Where the timeline breaks

The union consultation and the consular appointment are the two steps most sponsors underestimate. Both depend on third parties with their own calendars. The practical sequence is: lock the agreement, request the consultation, then file with USCIS, and only then confirm public dates. Reversing that order is how a sold-out show ends up with a performer stuck abroad.
