If you received a green card through a marriage that was less than two years old at the time, your card is conditional, valid for two years. Removal of conditions on a green card is the process of filing Form I-751 to show the marriage was real and get a permanent ten-year card. Miss the deadline, and your status can end.

## What a conditional green card is

Congress created the two-year conditional period to discourage sham marriages. If your marriage was less than two years old on the day you became a permanent resident, you receive a conditional resident card, and you must apply to remove the conditions before it expires. The card shows an expiration date two years after approval. Conditional residents have nearly all the rights of permanent residents, including the right to work and travel.

(Investors in the EB-5 program also receive conditional residence, but they file a different form, I-829. This article is about marriage-based cases.)

## Who must file and when

If you are a conditional resident through marriage, you generally file Form I-751 together with your spouse during the 90-day period before your card expires. Filing before that window opens is rejected, and filing after the card expires requires a written explanation of good cause. If you are not careful with the date, you risk losing status. Count the window from the expiration date printed on the card.

If your marriage has ended or you cannot file jointly, you may request a waiver of the joint filing requirement. The common waiver grounds are:

- The marriage ended in divorce or annulment, and was entered in good faith
- You, or your child, were battered or subjected to extreme cruelty
- You would suffer extreme hardship if removed from the United States
- Your spouse died

## Fees

USCIS sets a filing fee for Form I-751 and may adjust it. There has also been a separate biometrics fee in the past. Check the current Form I-751 page on the USCIS website for the exact amounts before you file, and ask your attorney what legal fees will cover.

## Filing steps

1. **Check your dates.** Locate the expiration date on your card and calculate the 90-day window.
2. **Decide between joint filing and a waiver.** The choice affects the evidence.
3. **Complete Form I-751.** Both spouses sign for a joint filing.
4. **Collect evidence** that the marriage was entered in good faith and continues. This is covered in more detail in our checklist article.
5. **Submit the package** by mail, or online where available, and keep a copy.
6. **Receive the receipt notice,** which can extend your status while the case is pending. Carry it with your expired card.
7. **Attend biometrics** when scheduled.
8. **Possible interview or request for evidence.** USCIS may call you for an interview or ask for more documents.
9. **Decision.** Approval brings a ten-year green card. A denial can put you in removal proceedings, where you can renew your request before an immigration judge.

## Common mistakes

- Filing before the window opens
- Waiting until the card has expired
- Leaving out evidence from the latest period
- Failing to report a change of address
- Not addressing a divorce or separation in the right way

## When to see an attorney

A divorce or separation does not necessarily end your path, but it changes it. If your marriage has broken down, if you missed the deadline, or if a request for evidence arrives, speak with an attorney quickly.
