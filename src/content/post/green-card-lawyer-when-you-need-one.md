Not every green card case needs a lawyer. A simple spouse petition with no immigration history problems can be filed by the couple. But a large share of cases have something that makes counsel valuable: a past overstay, a prior visa refusal, a criminal record, an unusual family structure, or a choice between adjustment of status and consular processing that can trigger bars to return. A green card lawyer's main job is to find those issues before USCIS does.

## What a green card lawyer does

- **Chooses the right path.** Family-based, employment-based, humanitarian, or another route, and the right category within it.
- **Screens for problems.** Inadmissibility grounds, prior immigration history, criminal records, and public charge.
- **Decides between adjustment of status and consular processing.** This choice has become more significant since USCIS issued a May 2026 memo describing adjustment as discretionary relief.
- **Prepares and files forms and evidence.**
- **Responds to requests for evidence and notices of intent to deny.**
- **Prepares you for the interview and attends with you.**

## Cases that benefit most from a green card attorney

- Any history of entry without inspection, overstay, or working without authorization.
- A prior visa refusal, deportation, or removal order.
- Any arrest or conviction, even if dismissed.
- Prior marriages that were not legally ended, or very short marriages.
- Children approaching 21.
- A sponsor whose income is close to the Affidavit of Support threshold.
- Employment-based cases with job changes during the process.

## How green card lawyer fees work

Most immigration lawyers charge flat fees for defined stages, such as preparing an I-130 or an adjustment of status package, with separate fees for responding to complex requests for evidence or attending interviews. Government filing fees are separate and paid to USCIS or the State Department.

Ask exactly what the flat fee includes: interview preparation, attendance, RFE responses, and work permit and travel document applications.

## Red flags when hiring

- **Guaranteed approval.** No honest lawyer can promise a result.
- **Pressure to file before reviewing your history.**
- **Advice to hide or misstate facts.** Misrepresentation can make you permanently inadmissible.
- **Non-lawyers giving legal advice.** Notarios and consultants are not authorized to advise on immigration law.
- **No written agreement.**

## Questions to ask a green card lawyer

- What is the biggest risk in my case?
- Should I adjust status or process abroad, and why?
- Who will prepare my file and who will attend the interview?
- What happens if USCIS issues a request for evidence?

## Doing it yourself

If your case is simple, you may file on your own using USCIS instructions. A one-time consultation before filing can confirm there are no hidden issues and is often worth the cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a lawyer for a marriage green card?
Not always. Couples with no immigration violations, criminal history, or prior marriages often file on their own. Anything outside that profile is worth a review.

### Can a green card lawyer speed up my case?
No one can change USCIS processing order. A lawyer can prevent delays caused by mistakes and request expedites when facts support them.

### What does a green card attorney charge?
Fees vary by case type and complexity. Most firms quote flat fees for each stage, separate from government filing fees.

### Will a lawyer go to the interview with me?
Many do. Ask whether attendance is included in the fee.

### Can a lawyer help if my green card was denied?
Yes. Options can include a motion to reopen or reconsider, refiling, or renewing the application in immigration court.

### Is an online form-filling service the same as a lawyer?
No. Form-filling services cannot give legal advice or assess risks in your history.

### Can a green card lawyer handle cases in other states?
Yes. Immigration law is federal, so a licensed attorney can represent clients nationwide.

### What should I bring to a first consultation?
Passports, every immigration document and notice, records of entries and departures, and any court or arrest records.

### Can a lawyer help me choose between family and employment routes?
Yes. Some people qualify for more than one path, and the faster or safer route depends on category waits and history.

### How long does a green card take with a lawyer?
Timing depends on the category, processing times, and visa availability. A lawyer can estimate it but not shorten government queues.
