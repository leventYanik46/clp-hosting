Temporary Protected Status lasts only as long as your country's designation, and you must re-register during each re-registration period to keep it. Since 2026 the rules have tightened: TPS work permits are generally limited to one year, renewal fees cannot be waived, and several designations have been terminated or challenged in court. Missing a deadline or a notice can cost you status and work authorization.

## How TPS re-registration works

When DHS extends or redesignates a country for TPS, it publishes a notice in the Federal Register. The notice sets:

- The new designation period.
- The re-registration window, usually 60 days.
- Whether existing employment authorization documents are automatically extended, and until when.

During the window, current TPS holders file Form I-821 to re-register and, if they want to work, Form I-765 for a new EAD. Filing online through a USCIS account is generally available.

## TPS fees

The Form I-821 re-registration fee and biometrics-related charges apply, and the EAD application has its own fee. Under rules implementing the 2025 budget law H.R. 1, fees for TPS work permits are charged annually and cannot be waived. Check the current USCIS fee schedule before filing, since fees have changed more than once.

## The one-year limit on TPS work permits

A DHS rule effective May 29, 2026 limits employment authorization for TPS beneficiaries to one year or the remaining TPS designation period, whichever is shorter. Many TPS holders will need to renew work permits more often than before, and pay each time. Employers should track these shorter validity periods for Form I-9 reverification.

## Late re-registration

If you miss the re-registration window, you may file late only if you can show good cause. USCIS decides case by case. Do not assume late filing will be accepted. If you cannot file on time, file as soon as possible with an explanation and evidence.

## Tracking your country's TPS designation

Designations change, and recent years have brought terminations and lawsuits affecting several countries, including Venezuela, Haiti, Honduras, Nicaragua, and others. Ukraine TPS and El Salvador TPS have each been the subject of extensions with their own deadlines. To track your country:

- Read the Federal Register notice for your country, rather than relying on news summaries.
- Check the USCIS TPS page for your country.
- Watch for court orders that may restore or extend status while a termination is challenged.

When a court order preserves TPS, it often extends documents through specific notices. Keep copies of every relevant notice to show employers.

## When TPS ends

If your country's designation is terminated and no court order applies, you return to whatever status you had before TPS, or none. Before that happens, look at:

- **Other status options**, such as family-based or employment-based green cards.
- **Asylum**, if you have a fear of return and are within or exempt from the filing deadline.
- **Adjustment of status**, which may be possible for TPS holders who were inspected and admitted or paroled before TPS, or who traveled abroad with USCIS travel authorization and returned, under a 2022 USCIS decision treating that return as an admission.

## Practical checklist

- Keep your address current with USCIS.
- Save every receipt notice and approval.
- Calendar the end date on your EAD and the re-registration window.
- Do not travel without advance parole.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I travel outside the U.S. on TPS?
Only with prior travel authorization from USCIS. Leaving without it can end your TPS.

### Does TPS lead to a green card?
Not directly. Some TPS holders qualify through family or employment, depending on how they entered and whether they traveled with authorization.

### What if my EAD expires before USCIS approves the new one?
Federal Register notices often extend existing TPS EADs automatically for a set period. Show your employer the notice along with your card.

### Can I re-register online?
Yes. USCIS generally accepts online filing of Form I-821 and Form I-765 for TPS re-registration.

### Do I need to re-register if my country's TPS is in litigation?
Follow the Federal Register and USCIS instructions. Court orders often require USCIS to keep accepting re-registrations.

### What if I moved since my last filing?
Update your address with USCIS within ten days. Notices about TPS and EADs go to your address on file.

### Can I apply for asylum while on TPS?
Yes. TPS does not prevent an asylum application, and TPS can count as an extraordinary circumstance for the one-year filing deadline in some cases.
