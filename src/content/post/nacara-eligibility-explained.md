The Nicaraguan Adjustment and Central American Relief Act (NACARA) was passed in 1997 to protect certain people who fled conflict in Central America and Eastern Europe. Although NACARA is decades old, some people still qualify, especially Salvadorans and Guatemalans who registered for protection in the early 1990s and their family members.

## The two parts of NACARA

**Section 202:** Allowed certain Nicaraguans and Cubans to adjust status. The filing deadline passed in 2000, so it's rarely available now.

**Section 203:** Provides a special form of cancellation of removal or suspension of deportation for certain:
- Salvadorans
- Guatemalans
- Nationals of former Soviet bloc countries

Section 203 is the part most people mean when they talk about NACARA today.

## Who qualifies under Section 203

**Salvadorans:**
- Entered the U.S. on or before September 19, 1990
- Registered for ABC (American Baptist Churches) class benefits by October 31, 1991, or applied for TPS by that date
- Weren't apprehended at entry after December 19, 1990
- Or applied for asylum on or before April 1, 1990

**Guatemalans:**
- Entered the U.S. on or before October 1, 1990
- Registered for ABC benefits by December 31, 1991
- Weren't apprehended at entry after December 19, 1990
- Or applied for asylum on or before April 1, 1990

**Former Soviet bloc nationals:**
- Entered the U.S. on or before December 31, 1990
- Applied for asylum on or before December 31, 1991

**Family members:**
- Spouses and children of qualifying individuals
- Unmarried sons and daughters who entered by October 1, 1990

## Requirements for relief

Qualifying people must also show:

1. **Continuous physical presence** of at least seven years (or ten years for some grounds)
2. **Good moral character**
3. **Extreme hardship** to themselves or to a U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse, parent, or child. Some qualifying ABC class members are presumed to meet this.

## How to apply

**Form I-881** is the application for NACARA relief.

- **Affirmative filing with USCIS:** if you're not in removal proceedings and meet the requirements
- **Defensive filing with immigration court:** if you're in removal proceedings

## NACARA today

Many original applicants have already received relief. But NACARA still matters for:

- People whose ABC cases were never completed
- Family members who weren't included in earlier filings
- People in removal proceedings who may qualify

## Ley NACARA for Spanish speakers

Many people search for "ley NACARA." The rules are the same regardless of language.

## Common issues

- Proving registration for ABC or TPS by the deadline
- Gaps in physical presence
- Criminal history
- Documents lost over decades

## Evidence that helps in older cases

NACARA cases often depend on events from more than 30 years ago. Useful evidence includes copies of ABC registration forms, TPS applications from the early 1990s, old asylum applications, receipts from the former INS, and records obtained through a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request. A FOIA request to USCIS can often recover filings that the applicant no longer has. Long physical presence can be shown with tax records, school records for children, leases, and employment records.

## NACARA in immigration court

If you're in removal proceedings and think you may be eligible, raise NACARA with the judge as early as possible. The judge may have jurisdiction over the application, or the case may need to be administratively closed or terminated so USCIS can decide it, depending on your circumstances.

## How NACARA relief differs from standard cancellation of removal

Standard cancellation of removal for non-permanent residents requires 10 years of physical presence and exceptional and extremely unusual hardship to a qualifying U.S. citizen or permanent resident relative. NACARA's special rule cancellation uses a seven-year presence requirement in most cases and the lower extreme hardship standard, and the hardship can be to the applicant. For eligible people, that difference can be decisive.

## Asylum interviews in NACARA cases

USCIS asylum offices often handle affirmative NACARA applications, and the process includes an interview. Applicants should be ready to explain their entry, registration, and history in the U.S., and to present evidence of hardship.
