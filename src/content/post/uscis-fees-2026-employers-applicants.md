USCIS fees in 2026 have changed several times. Premium processing went up in March, inflation adjustments to fees created by the 2025 budget law took effect in January and again in October, and the base fee schedule from 2024 continues to set most filing costs. If you're an employer budgeting for immigration filings, here's what to know.

## The base fee schedule

The current base fee schedule took effect on April 1, 2024. It set different fees for different categories of Form I-129 petitions and introduced the Asylum Program Fee. Under that schedule:

- **H-1B petitions:** $780 for most employers, with a reduced fee for small employers and nonprofits
- **L-1 petitions:** $1,385
- **O-1 petitions:** $1,055
- **Asylum Program Fee:** $600 for most employers filing I-129 and I-140 petitions, $300 for small employers, and $0 for nonprofits
- **H-1B registration:** $215 per registration

Small employers (25 or fewer full-time equivalent employees) and nonprofits pay reduced fees for many I-129 categories.

## Premium processing fees from March 1, 2026

On March 1, 2026, premium processing fees increased:

- **I-129 for most classifications (H-1B, L-1, O-1, E, TN, and others):** $2,965
- **I-129 for H-2B and R-1:** $1,780
- **I-140 employment-based petitions:** $2,965
- **I-539 (certain change of status applications):** $2,075
- **I-765 (certain work permit applications, such as OPT):** $1,780

Premium processing guarantees action within 15 business days for most I-129 and I-140 filings, or 30 or 45 business days for some categories. Requests postmarked on or after March 1, 2026, must include the new fees.

## H.R. 1 fees and inflation adjustments

The 2025 budget law, H.R. 1, created new fees for asylum, parole, and TPS-related filings, with annual inflation adjustments. The FY 2026 adjustments took effect January 1, 2026, and further adjustments took effect October 16, 2026, including:

- Annual asylum application fee: $105
- Immigration parole fee: $1,050
- Initial EAD for asylum applicants, parolees, and TPS applicants: $570
- TPS application (Form I-821): $520

These mostly affect individuals, but employers with workers who hold asylum-based, parole-based, or TPS-based work permits should know that renewals now cost more and work permits for some groups have shorter validity periods.

## The H-1B proclamation payment

A September 2025 presidential proclamation added a large payment requirement for certain new H-1B petitions, mainly for workers outside the U.S. The scope and exceptions have been interpreted through agency guidance and litigation. If you're filing a new H-1B petition for a worker abroad, check the current rules before filing.

## Avoiding fee rejections

USCIS rejects filings with the wrong fee. To avoid problems:

1. **Use the USCIS fee calculator** before every filing.
2. **Check the postmark date** against fee effective dates.
3. **Include every required fee.** The Asylum Program Fee is a separate amount from the base I-129 or I-140 fee, and leaving it out is a common reason for rejection.
4. **Use the right payment method.** USCIS has been moving to electronic payment for many filings. Check the current form instructions.
5. **Confirm small employer eligibility** with documentation, such as payroll records.

## Budgeting tips for employers

- Plan for premium processing in time-sensitive cases
- Include the Asylum Program Fee in I-129 and I-140 budgets
- Track inflation adjustments each fiscal year
- Separate government fees from attorney fees in your budgets

## How H.R. 1 fees affect employers indirectly

Employers who hire workers with asylum-based, parole-based, or TPS-based work permits should know that those workers now face higher renewal fees and, in many cases, shorter permit validity. That increases the risk of gaps in work authorization. Keep a reverification calendar under Form I-9 rules, and encourage affected employees to file renewals as early as their category allows.

## Building a fee calendar

Fee changes now arrive on several schedules: biennial premium processing adjustments, annual H.R. 1 inflation adjustments, and any future rulemaking for the base fee schedule. Employers filing regularly should check fee effective dates before each filing season, especially in late winter for premium processing and around the start of the federal fiscal year for H.R. 1 adjustments.

## What the base fees don't include

Government fees are only part of the cost. Employers also pay for PERM recruitment advertising in green card cases, which can't be passed on to the worker, and for the employer's share of legal fees, which in some categories must be paid by the employer under Department of Labor rules. H-1B employers must pay the worker's return transportation if the employer ends employment before the end of the authorized stay. Build these into your budget alongside USCIS fees so the full cost of a hire is clear from the start.
