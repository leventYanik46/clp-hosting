The H-1B has a six-year maximum, but many workers stay much longer. The law that makes that possible is the American Competitiveness in the Twenty-First Century Act, or AC21. It does two things: it allows H-1B extensions beyond six years for people in the green card process, and it gives portability so workers can change jobs without losing their place in line.

## The six-year limit

H-1B status is granted in up to three-year increments with a total cap of six years. Time spent outside the U.S. can be recaptured and added back. Once you hit six years, you generally need to leave for a year, unless AC21 applies.

## One-year extensions (AC21 section 106)

If a PERM labor certification or an I-140 was filed on your behalf at least 365 days before you reach six years, you can extend H-1B status in one-year increments past the limit. The extensions continue while the PERM, I-140, or I-485 is pending or approved.

Key details:

- The 365 days are measured from the PERM or I-140 filing date to the date your six years end
- The sponsoring employer does not have to be the H-1B employer filing the extension
- If the PERM or I-140 is denied and no appeal is pending, the basis for extension ends

## Three-year extensions (AC21 section 104(c))

If you have an approved I-140 but cannot file for a green card because your priority date is not current due to per-country limits, you can extend in three-year increments. This is common for applicants born in India and China.

## I-140 portability (AC21 section 204(j))

Once your I-485 has been pending for 180 days, you can move to a new job in the same or a similar occupation without starting the green card over. You file Form I-485 Supplement J with the new job offer. The new role does not need a new PERM or I-140.

"Same or similar" compares duties, skills, and occupational codes. A software developer moving to another software developer role is straightforward. A move from engineer to sales manager is harder to defend.

## H-1B portability for job changes

Separate from AC21's green card provisions, H-1B portability lets you start work with a new employer as soon as it files a nonfrivolous H-1B petition for you, as long as you are in valid status. Combined with the extension rules, this is what lets long-term H-1B holders move between employers.

## The $100,000 payment and extensions

The September 2025 proclamation payment targets certain new H-1B petitions for workers outside the U.S. USCIS guidance states it does not apply to extension or change of employer petitions for workers inside the U.S. when granted. That makes staying in status and filing extensions from inside the U.S. more important than ever.

## H-1B extension time frame

USCIS can take months to process an extension without premium processing. You may file up to six months before your current status expires. If you file on time, you can generally keep working for the same employer for up to 240 days while the extension is pending. Premium processing brings a decision within the guaranteed processing period.

## Common pitfalls

- Missing the 365-day window by a few days
- Assuming a withdrawn I-140 cannot support extensions (an I-140 approved for 180 days or more generally stays valid for these purposes even if withdrawn)
- Changing to a role that is not "same or similar" under Supplement J
- Traveling abroad with an expired visa stamp and getting stuck in consular delays


## Frequently asked questions

### Does a layoff end AC21 eligibility?

Losing the job ends your H-1B status after any grace period, but an approved I-140 can still support extensions if a new employer files an H-1B for you. The new employer does not need to restart the green card to use the old I-140 for extension purposes.

### Can my H-4 spouse extend too?

Yes. H-4 extensions follow the principal's H-1B extensions. H-4 spouses with an approved I-140 in the family may also qualify for an H-4 EAD.
