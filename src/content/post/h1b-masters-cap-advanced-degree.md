The H-1B master's cap sets aside 20,000 visas each year for workers who hold a master's or higher degree from a U.S. institution of higher education. People who qualify get two chances in the lottery instead of one. That extra chance only applies if the degree meets specific requirements, and the employer must claim it correctly when registering.

## The two H-1B caps

Congress caps new H-1B visas at 85,000 a year:

- **65,000** for the regular cap, open to anyone with at least a bachelor's degree or equivalent.
- **20,000** for the advanced degree exemption, commonly called the master's cap.

Workers hired by cap-exempt employers, such as universities and nonprofit research organizations, are not counted against either number.

## How the master's cap improves your odds

USCIS runs the selection in a specific order. First, it selects from all registrations, including those claiming the master's cap, until it reaches the 65,000 regular cap. Then it selects from the remaining master's-cap registrations to fill the 20,000.

This order means a master's-cap registrant is in both draws. If you are not chosen in the first round, you get a second chance in a smaller pool. That is why the master's cap measurably raises a worker's selection odds.

## Which degrees qualify

To count, the degree must be a master's or higher, such as a master's, MBA, professional doctorate, or Ph.D., and it must come from a U.S. institution that meets all of these conditions:

- It is a public or other nonprofit institution of higher education.
- It is accredited by a nationally recognized accrediting agency, or has been granted preaccreditation status.
- It is located in the United States.

A few consequences follow:

- **Foreign degrees do not count**, even if they are equivalent to a U.S. master's. A foreign master's still supports the regular cap and EB-2 eligibility, but not the master's cap.
- **Degrees from for-profit schools do not count**, even if they are accredited.
- **The degree must be completed.** Registration requires that the worker has earned the degree, or will have earned it, by the time the petition is filed. Expect to provide the final transcript or a letter confirming completion.

## The degree does not have to match the job exactly

The master's cap asks whether you hold a qualifying U.S. advanced degree. The separate specialty occupation test asks whether the job requires a degree in a specific specialty and whether your education qualifies you for it. A degree can qualify for the master's cap and still be a poor match for the position. The petition must satisfy both tests.

## Common master's cap mistakes

- **Claiming the exemption for a degree that does not qualify.** A registration that wrongly claims the master's cap can lead to denial of the petition, even if you would have qualified for the regular cap.
- **Missing proof of completion.** If the degree was not finished when the petition was filed, the claim can fail.
- **Assuming online programs are excluded.** The location and accreditation of the institution matter, not the delivery format, but each program should be checked.
- **Confusing the master's cap with cap exemption.** A master's degree does not exempt you from the lottery; it only adds a second chance.

## Planning a U.S. graduate degree around the H-1B

Some international students pick a U.S. graduate program partly because of the master's cap. That can be reasonable, as long as the school qualifies and the degree supports the job you want. Combined with STEM OPT, a qualifying master's degree can give a graduate several lottery attempts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an MBA count for the H-1B master's cap?
Yes, if it was earned at a qualifying U.S. public or nonprofit accredited institution. The degree type matters less than the institution.

### What if I finish my master's degree after registration?
You must have earned the degree by the time the petition is filed. If you are still enrolled when the filing window closes, the master's cap claim can fail. Plan graduation dates with this in mind.

### Does the master's cap help if I already have an H-1B?
No. Workers who were already counted against the cap in the last six years generally do not go through the lottery again for a new employer or extension.

### Can a foreign master's degree plus a U.S. bachelor's count?
No. The master's or higher degree itself must come from a qualifying U.S. institution. A U.S. bachelor's with a foreign master's goes into the regular cap.

### Are graduate certificates enough?
No. A certificate program is not a master's degree, even if it is offered by a qualifying university.

### Do online master's programs qualify?
They can, if the degree is awarded by a qualifying U.S. public or nonprofit accredited institution. Confirm the school's status rather than relying on the format of the program.
