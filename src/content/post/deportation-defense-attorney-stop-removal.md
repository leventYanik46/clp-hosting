A deportation defense attorney represents you in removal proceedings, challenges the government's case, and looks for any legal basis that lets you stay. The honest answer to whether a lawyer can stop deportation is: sometimes, depending on your history and the options available. What a lawyer can do is make sure you do not lose a case you could have won, by missing a deadline or a form of relief.

## How removal proceedings work

Proceedings begin when the government issues a Notice to Appear (NTA), which lists the allegations and the reasons it says you can be removed. The case then goes before an immigration judge.

1. **Master calendar hearing.** A short initial hearing where you respond to the allegations, tell the court whether you will seek relief, and receive dates.
2. **Applications for relief.** If you are eligible for a form of relief, your lawyer prepares and files it.
3. **Individual hearing.** The full hearing where evidence is presented and you testify.
4. **Decision.** The judge may grant relief, order removal or allow voluntary departure.
5. **Appeal.** Either side may appeal to the Board of Immigration Appeals, and sometimes beyond.

If you miss a hearing, a judge can order you removed in your absence. Never skip a hearing without speaking to a lawyer.

## What defenses and relief may exist

Which of these applies depends on your facts.

- **Challenging the charges.** The government must prove you are removable. A lawyer may argue that you are a U.S. citizen, that the evidence does not support the charge, or that a conviction does not meet the legal definition.
- **Cancellation of removal.** Relief for some permanent residents and some long-term residents without status who meet strict requirements.
- **Asylum, withholding of removal and Convention Against Torture protection.** For people who fear return.
- **Adjustment of status.** A green card application, for example through a family member, filed in court.
- **Waivers.** Forgiveness of certain grounds of inadmissibility or deportability.
- **Voluntary departure.** A way to leave without a removal order, which can avoid certain long-term consequences.
- **Motions to reopen or terminate.** If something went wrong earlier, such as defective notice or new evidence.
- **Prosecutorial discretion.** A request that the government not pursue or pause the case. The government decides whether to grant it.

## If you are detained

Detention changes the process. A lawyer can seek a bond hearing, argue for release and prepare the case from detention. Family members should find out where the person is held and contact counsel quickly.

## The right to counsel

You have the right to be represented in immigration court, but the government does not pay for the lawyer in most cases. Free or low-cost help may be available from nonprofit organizations, so ask the court or a local legal aid group about options. Be careful about unlicensed helpers.

## When to call a lawyer

- You received an NTA or a hearing notice
- ICE contacted you or someone you know
- A conviction or arrest may create immigration consequences
- You are afraid to return to your country
- You missed a court date

## Why speed matters

Deadlines are strict, and options can disappear. Applications for relief have filing windows, and an order issued in your absence is hard to undo. A consultation can tell you what is possible and what steps to take first. Bring all papers you have received from the government.
