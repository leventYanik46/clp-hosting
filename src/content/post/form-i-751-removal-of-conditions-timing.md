If your marriage was less than two years old on the day you became a permanent resident, your green card expires in two years and you must file Form I-751 in the 90-day window before that date. File too early and USCIS rejects it. Miss the window and your status terminates automatically on the expiration date, which can put you in removal proceedings. The window is the single most important date in a conditional resident's life, and it is the one families most often get wrong.

## Why Form I-751 exists

Congress built conditional residence to deter marriage fraud. The idea is simple: give the new spouse residence, then check two years later that the marriage is still real. The check is Form I-751, Petition to Remove Conditions on Residence. In the normal case both spouses sign it, and the file shows the marriage continuing. Our [marriage-based green card](/practice-area/key-services/marriage-based-green-card) practice calendars the window at the moment the conditional card is approved, because the arithmetic is unforgiving.

Conditional residence attaches whether the green card came from adjustment of status inside the United States or from a CR-1 visa at a consulate, and it attaches to a spouse who arrived on a K-1 and adjusted after the wedding. Children who received conditional residence through the same marriage are usually included on the parent's petition if they got status at the same time or within 90 days after.

## The 90-day window, in practice

Count back 90 days from the expiration date printed on the card. The petition must be received by USCIS within that window. A petition filed before the window opens is returned. A petition received after the card expires is treated as late, and USCIS may accept it only with a written explanation showing good cause and extenuating circumstances, at the agency's discretion.

Once a timely petition is filed, the receipt notice extends your conditional resident status while the case is pending. As of this writing, the extension stated on the notice is 48 months. Carry the expired card together with the receipt notice; together they prove status for work, travel and a driver's license renewal. If the case runs beyond the extension, you can get an I-551 stamp at a field office by appointment.

## What to file with a joint petition

The evidence is the same shared-life material that supported the original case, updated for the two years since. The strongest files include:

- Joint federal and state tax returns for the years since residence was granted.
- A current lease, deed or mortgage with both names, and utility accounts at the same address.
- Joint bank and credit statements showing ongoing activity.
- Insurance policies and beneficiary designations naming the other spouse.
- Birth certificates of any children born since the first filing.
- Affidavits from friends and family who know the couple, and photographs from the two years with dates and places.

Our post on [bona fide marriage evidence](/bona-fide-marriage-evidence-uscis) explains why breadth across categories matters more than volume in any one. USCIS may waive the interview when the file is strong, or call the couple in when it is not.

## Divorce, abuse and hardship: filing without your spouse

A conditional resident whose marriage has ended does not lose the right to remove conditions. Form I-751 includes a request to waive the joint filing requirement, available on three grounds: the marriage was entered in good faith but ended in divorce or annulment; the marriage was entered in good faith but the conditional resident was battered or subjected to extreme cruelty by the citizen or resident spouse; or removal would result in extreme hardship.

A waiver petition can be filed at any time, before or after the 90-day window, as long as the conditional resident has not been removed. The proof burden shifts to the marriage's beginning: the file must show the couple married in good faith, using the same shared-life evidence, together with the divorce decree or evidence of abuse. If the divorce is not yet final when the card is about to expire, file the waiver petition anyway and expect a Request for Evidence for the final decree; a petition that sits unfiled because the divorce is pending is the common mistake here.

A couple that is separated but not divorced faces the hardest version of the problem. A joint petition requires both signatures and a marriage that, in substance, continues. If the petitioning spouse will not sign and the divorce is not final, counsel is needed to time the filings.

## What happens if you miss the deadline

Status ends by operation of law on the card's expiration date. USCIS may issue a Notice to Appear, and the case moves to the immigration court. The conditional resident can still file the I-751 with USCIS and ask the immigration judge to continue the case until USCIS decides it, and the judge can review a USCIS denial of the petition. Our [removal defense](/practice-area/key-services/removal-defense) team handles that stage. A late filing with a documented reason, before the Notice to Appear issues, is far better than waiting.

One more date worth calendaring: the spouse of a U.S. citizen can apply for naturalization after three years as a resident, including the conditional years. Many conditional residents file Form N-400 while the I-751 is still pending, and USCIS can decide both at the naturalization interview. The timeline for a spouse who entered on a K-1 is covered in [K-1 adjustment of status after the wedding](/k-1-adjustment-of-status-after-marriage).
