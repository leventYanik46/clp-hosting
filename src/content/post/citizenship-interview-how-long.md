Many naturalization applicants are nervous about the interview. Knowing how long the citizenship interview takes and what the officer tests can make the day much easier. Most interviews are short and follow a predictable structure.

## How long the citizenship interview takes

Most citizenship interviews take about 20 to 30 minutes. Some take longer if:

- Your history includes arrests, long trips, or other issues
- There are inconsistencies in your application
- You need an interpreter for a disability exception
- The officer has questions about your marriage (for applicants under the three-year rule)

Plan to arrive early. Check-in and security can take time, and you may wait before your interview begins.

## What happens at the interview

1. **Oath to tell the truth:** The officer places you under oath.
2. **Identity check:** The officer reviews your ID and green card.
3. **N-400 review:** The officer goes through your application to confirm or update answers.
4. **English test:** reading, writing, and speaking.
5. **Civics test:** questions about U.S. history and government.
6. **Results:** The officer tells you the outcome.

## The N-400 review

The officer asks about your application, such as:

- Your address and employment history
- Your travel outside the U.S.
- Your marital history
- Any arrests, citations, or criminal history
- Tax filing and child support
- Good moral character questions, such as whether you've claimed U.S. citizenship or voted in a U.S. election

Your speaking ability is assessed during this review.

## The English test

- **Reading:** read one sentence correctly out of up to three
- **Writing:** write one sentence correctly out of up to three
- **Speaking:** assessed through your answers during the interview

## The civics test

USCIS introduced a revised civics test in late 2025. Which version you take depends on your filing date. Under the revised test, you're asked more questions and must answer a higher number correctly than under the older version. Check the USCIS study materials for the test that applies to you.

## English exceptions

- **50/20:** age 50 or older with 20 years as a permanent resident
- **55/15:** age 55 or older with 15 years as a permanent resident
- **65/20:** age 65 or older with 20 years as a permanent resident, who also gets a simplified civics test

Applicants with qualifying disabilities can request an exception with Form N-648.

## Possible results

- **Granted:** You passed and the officer approves your application.
- **Continued:** The officer needs more evidence (often by Form N-14) or you need to retake a test.
- **Denied:** The officer finds you ineligible. You can request a hearing on Form N-336.

If you fail the English or civics test, you get a second chance, usually within 60 to 90 days.

## Tips

- Bring your green card, passport, ID, and any documents requested in the notice
- Review your N-400 before the interview
- Bring certified court records for any arrests
- Answer honestly and ask the officer to repeat questions if needed

## Good moral character questions to prepare for

The officer will review the good moral character section of your N-400, which covers a long list of topics: criminal history, unpaid taxes, failure to pay court-ordered child support, false claims to U.S. citizenship, voting in an election where only citizens can vote, and others. If any answer is yes, bring documents that explain the situation, such as court dispositions, proof of tax payment plans, or child support records. A clear explanation backed by records usually goes much better than a surprised answer during the interview.

## Neighborhood and employment questions

Officers may also ask about your employment history and residence, and USCIS has expanded its use of investigations into an applicant's background and community ties in recent years. Answer consistently with your application and be prepared to describe your work and home life.

## What to bring to the citizenship interview

- Your interview notice
- Your green card
- Your passport and any expired passports covering the statutory period
- State ID or driver's license
- Original documents for any name change
- Certified court records for any arrests or citations, even if dismissed
- Tax transcripts if you had tax issues
- Evidence of child support payments if applicable
- For marriage-based applicants: marriage certificate and evidence of your spouse's citizenship

Bring originals and copies. If the officer asks for a document you don't have, you may receive a Form N-14 asking for it after the interview, which delays a decision.

## If your English is improving

If you're worried about the English test, practice reading and writing simple sentences on civics topics, since the test sentences come from a published vocabulary list. You'll have a second chance if you don't pass the first time.
