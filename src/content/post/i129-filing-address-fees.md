Form I-129 is the petition U.S. employers file to bring in or extend most temporary workers: H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN, E, P, and others. Most I-129 problems are not about eligibility. They are about mechanics. A petition sent to the wrong lockbox, paid with the wrong fee, or filed on an outdated edition gets rejected and returned, and a rejection means it was never filed. For a cap case or a worker whose status is about to expire, that can be costly.

## Where to file Form I-129

There is no single I-129 filing address. USCIS assigns addresses based on:

- The classification requested (H-1B, L-1, O-1, and so on)
- Whether the petition is cap-subject or cap-exempt
- The state where the beneficiary will work
- Whether you are requesting premium processing
- Whether you are filing by courier or regular mail

USCIS publishes these on its "Direct Filing Addresses for Form I-129" page, and it changes them from time to time. Check the page on the day you ship, not the day you start preparing. A petition that goes to a service center that no longer accepts that classification may be forwarded, or it may be rejected.

For H-1B petitions, many employers now file online through a USCIS organizational account, which removes the address question entirely and gives an immediate receipt.

## How I-129 fees are calculated

Since the 2024 USCIS fee rule, the base I-129 fee depends on the classification. H-1B, L-1, and O-1 each have their own fee, and lower fees apply to small employers (25 or fewer full-time equivalent employees) and nonprofits.

On top of the base fee, a petition may owe:

- **Asylum Program Fee.** Charged on most I-129 petitions, reduced for small employers and waived for nonprofits.
- **ACWIA training fee.** For most H-1B petitions, scaled to employer size, with exemptions for certain institutions.
- **Fraud prevention and detection fee.** For initial H-1B and L-1 petitions and change of employer.
- **Public Law 114-113 fee.** For employers with 50 or more U.S. employees, more than half of whom are in H-1B or L status.
- **Premium processing fee**, if you request it on Form I-907.
- **The $100,000 payment under the September 2025 proclamation**, for certain new H-1B petitions where the worker is outside the U.S. Petitions requesting a change of status, extension, or amendment for a worker inside the U.S. are not subject to it if that request is granted.

Use the USCIS Fee Calculator and the form instructions, and pay each fee as the instructions direct. Combining fees into one check when the instructions call for separate payments, or underpaying by a small amount, still triggers rejection.

## The edition problem

USCIS revises Form I-129 periodically. The edition date appears at the bottom of each page. After a revision, USCIS typically gives a short grace period and then rejects anything filed on the older edition. This caught many employers after the H-1B modernization rule took effect in 2025 and a new edition was released.

Practical rule: download a fresh copy from uscis.gov for every filing. Do not reuse a saved template from last year, and make sure every page in the packet carries the same edition date.

## Other common reasons for rejection

- Missing signature, or a signature that is not handwritten where required
- Missing supplements, such as the H Classification Supplement or the L Supplement
- Wrong number of beneficiaries on a single petition for classifications that allow only one
- Missing the certified LCA for H-1B, E-3, or H-1B1 petitions
- Filing an H-1B cap petition before the employer receives a selection notice, or filing it outside the filing window

## Rejection vs. denial

A rejection means USCIS never accepted the filing. You get the package and fee back and can refile, but your filing date is lost. A denial means USCIS reviewed the case on the merits and decided against it. For deadline-sensitive petitions, a rejection can be worse than a slow approval because the clock keeps running.

## A pre-filing checklist

Before shipping, confirm the current address or online option, the current edition, each fee and its amount, every signature, and every supplement. Then keep a copy of exactly what went out.
