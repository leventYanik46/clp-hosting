Your first appearance in immigration court is almost always a **master calendar hearing**. It is short, often only a few minutes, and the immigration judge usually hears many cases in the same session. The judge does not decide whether you can stay in the United States that day. But what you say, and whether you show up, shapes everything that follows. Missing it can result in a removal order without you.

## What an immigration judge is

Immigration judges work for the Executive Office for Immigration Review, part of the Department of Justice. They decide whether a person is removable and whether they qualify for relief, such as asylum, cancellation of removal, or adjustment of status. A Department of Homeland Security attorney represents the government. You have the right to a lawyer at your own expense.

## What happens at a master calendar hearing

### Rights and the lawyer question

The judge confirms your identity, explains your rights, and asks whether you have a lawyer. If you do not, the judge will often give you time to find one and reset the hearing. Ask for that time if you need it. Judges usually allow at least one continuance to obtain counsel.

### Pleadings

The judge reviews the **Notice to Appear**, the charging document that started the case. It lists factual allegations, such as your nationality and how you entered, and the legal charge of removability. You, or your lawyer, will be asked to admit or deny each allegation and to concede or contest the charge. This is called "pleading."

Do not admit allegations you do not understand. If the Notice to Appear contains errors, your lawyer can contest them.

### Country of removal

The judge asks you to designate a country of removal if removal is ordered. Your lawyer may decline to designate one. The judge will then usually identify your country of citizenship.

### Relief from removal

The judge asks what relief you are seeking: asylum, withholding of removal, Convention Against Torture protection, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status, or voluntary departure. The judge sets deadlines for filing applications and evidence.

### Scheduling the next hearing

If you are seeking relief, the judge schedules an **individual hearing**, also called a merits hearing, where the case is decided. That can be months or years away depending on the court's docket.

## Appearing in person or by video

Some master calendar hearings are held by video. Your hearing notice or the court's instructions explain how to appear. Check the case status through EOIR's online automated case information system before every hearing, because dates and formats change.

## What to bring

- Your hearing notice and the Notice to Appear.
- Your passport or identity documents.
- Any applications you have already filed, such as an I-589.
- Proof of address.

## What happens if you miss the hearing

If you do not appear and the government proves you were properly notified, the judge can order you removed "in absentia." Reopening such an order is possible only on narrow grounds, such as lack of proper notice or exceptional circumstances, with strict deadlines.

Keep your address updated with the court on Form EOIR-33 every time you move. Notices sent to your last address on file count, even if you never received them.

## How to prepare

- Find a lawyer before the hearing if you can.
- Read the Notice to Appear carefully and note anything wrong.
- Think about the relief you may qualify for, especially asylum, which generally must be filed within one year of arrival.
- Arrive early. Security lines at immigration courts can be long.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can my family come with me to the hearing?
Immigration court hearings are generally open to the public, with exceptions such as some asylum cases. Family members can usually wait in the courtroom or hallway.

### Will there be an interpreter?
Yes. The court provides an interpreter for the respondent if you need one. Tell the court your language early.

### Can I ask to move my case to another court?
Yes, by filing a motion to change venue, usually when you have moved. The judge decides whether to grant it.

### What if I have a pending application with USCIS?
Tell the judge. A pending petition or application can affect scheduling and the relief you seek.

### How long will my case take?
It depends on the court's backlog and the relief you request. Some cases take years between the master calendar hearing and the individual hearing.

### Can I be detained at the hearing?
It has happened in recent years, especially after DHS moves to dismiss a case. Attend with counsel if possible and bring proof of your time in the U.S.

### Is a lawyer free in immigration court?
No. There is no right to a government-appointed lawyer in most immigration cases, though nonprofit legal providers offer some free services. The court has a list of free legal service providers.

### What should I say if I fear returning to my country?
Say so clearly. Fear of return can support asylum, withholding of removal, or Convention Against Torture protection, and the judge will set deadlines for those applications.

### What does "pleading" mean?
It means admitting or denying the factual allegations in the Notice to Appear and conceding or contesting the charge of removability.
