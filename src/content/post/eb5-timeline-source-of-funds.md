The EB-5 timeline depends less on how fast you can wire the money and more on how long it takes to document where that money came from, and on how long USCIS and the visa queue take afterward. If you are thinking about EB-5, the best preparation happens before you invest.

## The EB-5 stages

A typical path looks like this:

1. **Choose a project and investment structure.** Direct investment in a new commercial enterprise, or investment through a regional center project.
2. **Document source of funds.** You assemble records proving the money was lawfully obtained.
3. **Invest and file the petition.** Form I-526 for direct investments, or Form I-526E for regional center investments. This shows that you invested, or are in the process of investing, the required capital and that the project will create the required jobs.
4. **Wait for petition adjudication.** USCIS reviews the investment, the funds and the project.
5. **Visa availability and conditional green card.** Once your priority date is current, you apply for a conditional green card through adjustment of status or consular processing.
6. **Remove conditions.** About two years later, you file Form I-829 to show the investment was sustained and the jobs were created. USCIS then removes the conditions.

## Why the timeline varies

- **Source of funds preparation** can take months if records come from several countries, banks, or business sales.
- **USCIS processing** changes with workload, and the agency posts current processing times. Some project categories receive priority treatment.
- **Visa backlogs.** Applicants from some countries face long waits for a visa number, while others do not. The Visa Bulletin shows the current picture.
- **Set-aside categories.** Reform legislation in 2022 created reserved visa numbers for rural, high-unemployment and infrastructure projects, which can shorten the wait for those investors.
- **Project readiness.** The progress of the project affects the I-829 stage.

## Source of funds: what USCIS wants to see

USCIS expects a clear, documented path from the origin of your funds to the investment. Common documents include:

- Tax returns and employer records for salary income
- Business sale agreements and closing statements
- Property sale documents
- Gift letters, with evidence of the donor's own source
- Loan documents, with proof of how the loan will be repaid and what secures it
- Bank statements showing the movement of funds from account to account

The key is that each transfer can be traced. Gaps, unexplained deposits and funds that came from third parties without documentation draw questions. Start collecting records early, since old statements can be hard to get.

## Due diligence on the project

An EB-5 investment is a financial investment with risk, and no return is guaranteed. Before committing, investors typically review:

- The offering documents and the project's securities compliance
- The track record of the sponsor and the regional center
- How job creation is calculated and whether the numbers are realistic
- How funds are held and released, including any escrow arrangement
- What happens to your capital if the project underperforms
- Whether the project is in a category with set-aside visas

Independent legal and financial advice on the investment is wise. An immigration attorney can speak to the visa side. A securities or financial advisor can speak to the investment side.

## What to do now

Start building the source-of-funds file, ask projects for their documentation, and consult an immigration attorney early. Preparing well can save months.
