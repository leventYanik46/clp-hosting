H-1B lottery results usually arrive by the end of March, through your employer's USCIS online account. If your registration was not selected, you are not out of options. A second selection round sometimes follows, and several alternatives to the H-1B can keep your career in the U.S. on track while you try again.

## When H-1B results come out

The registration window for cap-subject H-1B petitions typically opens in March. USCIS runs the selection after the window closes and notifies registrants by the end of that month. Petitions for selected beneficiaries can then be filed starting April 1, for an employment start date of October 1.

Exact dates change each year, and the registration rules themselves have changed. Since the fiscal year 2025 season, selection has been beneficiary-centric, meaning each worker is entered once no matter how many employers register them. The government has also moved toward weighting selection by wage level, and a 2025 presidential proclamation added a large payment for certain new petitions. Check the current season's rules with your employer's counsel before you rely on last year's process.

## How to check your H-1B lottery result

You do not get a result directly from USCIS unless you are your own registrant. The employer or its attorney logs into the USCIS organizational account and sees each registration's status:

- **Selected**: the employer may file the H-1B petition within the filing window.
- **Submitted**: still eligible, but not selected so far. This status stays until the fiscal year's selection process ends.
- **Not selected**: shown after USCIS finishes all selections for the year.
- **Denied** or **Invalidated**: usually a duplicate or defective registration.

Ask your employer for a screenshot or confirmation of the status rather than relying on informal word.

## Is there a second round?

Sometimes. If USCIS does not receive enough petitions from the first selections to reach the cap, it selects more registrations, usually in summer. A registration marked "Submitted" remains in the pool for that possibility. There have been years with second rounds and years without, so plan as if there will not be one.

## Alternatives to the H-1B visa

If you were not selected, look at these options with your employer:

**Cap-exempt employers.** Universities, their affiliated nonprofits, and nonprofit or government research organizations can file H-1B petitions any time of year without the lottery. A job with one of them can start your H-1B now.

**STEM OPT extension.** If you are on F-1 OPT with a qualifying STEM degree and an E-Verify employer, the 24-month extension gives you more lottery attempts.

**O-1A visa.** For people with strong records of achievement, awards, publications, high salary, or critical roles, the O-1A has no cap and no lottery.

**L-1 visa.** If you have worked for a related company abroad for a year, your employer can bring you in as an intracompany transferee.

**Treaty-based visas.** Canadians and Mexicans may qualify for TN status; Australians for the E-3; Chileans and Singaporeans for the H-1B1. None of these uses the H-1B lottery.

**A green card process.** An employer can start PERM even when you have no H-1B. It does not give you work authorization by itself, but it starts your priority date.

**Further study.** Some people enroll in a new degree program to remain in F-1 status. Do this only for a program you actually intend to complete; schools and USCIS look closely at enrollment that exists only to preserve status.

## What not to do

- Do not let your current status lapse while you decide. Each alternative has its own filing time.
- Do not accept a "job" from a company that asks you to pay for your own registration or petition. Employers must bear H-1B costs.
- Do not register with multiple related companies to increase your odds. Duplicate registrations can be invalidated and investigated.

## Planning for next season

If you plan to register again, use the year to strengthen your position: confirm your degree is in a specialty related to the job, and if you earned a U.S. master's degree, confirm it qualifies for the advanced-degree allocation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I check my H-1B result myself?
Only if you have access to the registration account. Workers usually rely on the employer or its attorney, who can see each registration's status in the USCIS organizational account.

### If I was selected, am I guaranteed an H-1B?
No. Selection only lets the employer file a petition. USCIS still reviews whether the job is a specialty occupation and whether you qualify.

### Can I keep working on OPT if I was selected?
If the petition is filed on time and requests a change of status, cap-gap rules can extend F-1 status and work authorization until October 1. If you were not selected, your OPT ends on its normal date.

### Can I register again next year?
Yes, as long as you have a valid basis to remain or can be petitioned for from abroad. Many workers are selected on their second or third attempt.
