The O-1A and O-1B are both extraordinary ability visas, but they test different things. The O-1A covers sciences, education, business, and athletics and requires that you be among the small percentage at the top of your field. The O-1B covers the arts and requires "distinction," a lower bar, or for film and television, extraordinary achievement. Choosing the wrong one can mean failing a standard you did not need to meet.

## O-1A: sciences, education, business, athletics

The O-1A applies to founders, engineers, researchers, executives, academics, and athletes. You qualify with either a major internationally recognized award or evidence meeting at least three of eight criteria:

1. Nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards.
2. Membership in associations that require outstanding achievement.
3. Published material about you in major media or professional publications.
4. Judging the work of others.
5. Original contributions of major significance.
6. Authorship of scholarly articles.
7. A critical or essential role for distinguished organizations.
8. A high salary relative to others in the field.

Meeting three criteria is the first step. USCIS then looks at the total evidence to decide whether you have sustained national or international acclaim.

## O-1B: arts, and film and television

The O-1B applies to artists, musicians, designers, chefs in some cases, photographers, writers, and people in creative industries. "Arts" is read broadly.

For most arts cases the standard is **distinction**: a high level of achievement shown by skill and recognition substantially above what is ordinarily encountered, so that you are prominent in your field. The evidence includes leading or starring roles in distinguished productions, critical reviews, commercial success, recognition from organizations or experts, and high pay.

For **motion picture and television** work, the standard is **extraordinary achievement**, which is closer to the O-1A level.

For an artist, O-1B is usually the right fit because distinction is easier to show than top-percentage acclaim. A person whose work is both artistic and commercial, such as a creative director at a tech company, should look carefully at which category describes the actual job.

## The consultation letter

Every O-1 petition needs a written advisory opinion, or consultation, from a peer group, labor organization, or management organization in the field. For O-1B film and television cases, consultations from both a union and a management organization are typically required. If no appropriate group exists, the petition can explain that and submit other expert evidence.

## O-1 cost and processing time

Costs include the I-129 filing fee, the Asylum Program Fee, optional premium processing, and legal fees. O-1B cases in entertainment can also involve fees for the consultation from a union. The largest variable is usually evidence preparation, especially for O-1A cases that need expert letters.

With premium processing, USCIS acts within 15 business days. Without it, timing varies by service center.

## Who files and the itinerary

An O-1 must be filed by a U.S. employer or a U.S. agent. Artists who work for many venues or clients often use an agent and submit an itinerary of events or engagements. An O-1 cannot be self-petitioned directly, but founders can be sponsored by their own company.

## O-3 status for your family

Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 can accompany you in O-3 status. O-3 dependents may study but cannot work. A spouse who needs to work must qualify for a visa of their own.

## Choosing between O-1A and O-1B

Ask what the role actually requires day to day, where your recognition comes from, and which criteria your strongest evidence fits. A game designer with industry awards may fit O-1B; the same person leading engineering at a startup may fit O-1A.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I switch from O-1B to O-1A later?
Yes. If your work changes, a new petition can be filed in the category that matches it.

### How long is an O-1 approved for?
Up to three years at first, based on the length of the event or activity, with extensions in one-year increments.

### Does an O-1 lead to a green card?
Not directly. Many O-1 holders later file for EB-1A or the national interest waiver using similar evidence.

### Can an O-1 holder work for more than one employer?
Through an agent and itinerary, or by having each employer file. The petition must describe the work.

### Do I need awards to qualify for O-1B?
No. Awards help, but reviews, leading roles, commercial success, and expert recognition can also show distinction.

### Can my spouse work on O-3?
No. An O-3 spouse may study but must qualify for a separate status to work.

### What if there is no union or peer group in my field?
The petition can explain that no appropriate group exists and submit other expert evidence instead of a formal consultation.
