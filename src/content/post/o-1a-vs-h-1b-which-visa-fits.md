If you lost the H-1B lottery this year, or never entered it because your job does not require your degree, the O-1A is the first alternative to test. Both visas let a U.S. employer put you to work, but they are built on opposite ideas. The H-1B has modest eligibility rules and a hard numbers problem. The O-1A has no numbers problem and a hard evidence problem: you must show that you belong to the small percentage at the top of your field. Choosing O-1A or H-1B is therefore less about preference and more about which set of facts you can document this year. The criteria that decide an O-1A case are laid out on our [O-1A extraordinary ability visa](/practice-area/key-services/o-1a-visa) page; this article is about the comparison.

## O-1A vs H-1B: the five differences that decide it

### The cap and the lottery

Congress caps new H-1B visas at 65,000 a year, with 20,000 more reserved for people holding a U.S. master's degree or higher. Employers register in the spring, USCIS runs a random selection, and an unselected worker waits a full year to try again. The only way around the lottery is an employer that is exempt from the cap, such as a university or a nonprofit research organization; we explain who qualifies in [which employers are H-1B cap-exempt](/h-1b-cap-exempt-employers). The O-1A has no cap and no registration window. A petitioner can file Form I-129 at any time of year, up to one year before the start date.

### The eligibility standard

An H-1B job must be a specialty occupation, meaning it normally requires at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field, and you must hold that degree or its equivalent. The employer also files a Labor Condition Application with the Department of Labor and commits to pay at least the prevailing wage. The O-1A has no degree requirement, no specialty occupation test and no wage floor. Instead, you must show a major internationally recognized award or satisfy at least three of eight regulatory criteria, and then convince the officer, on the whole record, that you have sustained national or international acclaim. [How to prove extraordinary ability for an O-1 visa](/how-to-prove-extraordinary-ability-o-1) walks through that evidence criterion by criterion.

### Maximum stay

H-1B status is limited to six years in total, usually granted as three plus three. Extensions past six years are possible only when a PERM labor certification or an I-140 petition has been pending or approved for the required period. An O-1A is approved for up to three years for the event or activity, then extended in one-year increments for as long as the same work continues, with no statutory maximum. A new project supports a new three-year petition. See [how long an O-1A visa is valid](/o-1a-visa-validity-extensions) for the mechanics.

### Changing jobs

H-1B workers have portability: once a new employer files a non-frivolous petition, you may start work for that employer without waiting for the approval. O-1A workers do not. The new employer or agent must file its own petition and you must wait for the approval notice before you begin. Both classifications carry a grace period of up to 60 days when employment ends early, which gives you time to find a new petitioner or change status.

### Family

An H-4 spouse may apply for work authorization once the H-1B worker has an approved I-140 or an extension beyond the sixth year. An O-3 spouse may study but may not work, and there is no O-3 employment authorization as of this writing. For a two-career household this difference can decide the case.

## Who should file the O-1A instead of the H-1B

The O-1A fits people whose record already looks like the eight criteria. In practice that means:

- Researchers with a publication record, citations by others, peer review service and conference invitations.
- Engineers and product leaders with patents, press coverage, a critical role at a company with a distinguished reputation, or pay well above the field.
- Executives whose compensation and responsibilities are documented in contracts and organizational charts.
- Founders, for whom the H-1B employer-employee question is awkward, while the O-1A can be filed by an agent or by a company they own that is a separate legal entity. [Can a startup founder get an O-1 visa?](/can-startup-founder-get-o-1-visa) explains the structure.
- Athletes and coaches, who rarely hold a degree that matches a specialty occupation.

The honest tradeoff is effort. An O-1A petition takes weeks to assemble, requires a written advisory opinion from a peer group, and invites a Request for Evidence if the "major significance" of your contributions is asserted rather than shown. If your record is a solid degree and a normal job, the H-1B (or a cap-exempt employer) remains the realistic route, and [the H-1B visa for employers](/h-1b-visa-for-employers) covers that process.

## Can you pursue both at once?

Yes. Many employers register a candidate for the H-1B lottery and prepare an O-1A in parallel, then file whichever the facts support. If you already hold H-1B status, an O-1A petition can request a change of status, and time spent in O-1 status does not count against the six-year H-1B limit. The reverse also works: an O-1A worker selected in a later lottery can move to H-1B. On immigrant intent, the H-1B is dual intent by statute. The O-1A is not labeled that way, but the regulations state that a pending immigrant petition or approved labor certification is not a basis for denying an O-1 petition, extension or admission, so a green card plan does not sink the case.

## A practical way to decide

Ask three questions in order. First, do you have a cap-exempt employer or a lottery selection in hand? If so, the H-1B is usually the faster filing. Second, can you document three O-1A criteria today, with independent evidence rather than letters alone? If yes, the O-1A buys you freedom from the lottery and the six-year clock. Third, where do you want to be in five years? An O-1A record is the same record that supports an EB-1A or EB-2 NIW green card, so the work you do now is reused later.
