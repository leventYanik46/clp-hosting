The F2A category is the family preference category for spouses and unmarried children under 21 of green card holders. It is the main way a permanent resident can bring immediate family members to the U.S. Unlike U.S. citizens, green card holders cannot sponsor their parents, married children, or siblings at all.

## What is the F2A visa category?

F2A covers:

- The spouse of a lawful permanent resident
- Unmarried children under 21 of a lawful permanent resident

Children of an F2A spouse can often come as derivatives on the same petition, as long as they are unmarried and under 21.

## What F2B covers

F2B covers unmarried sons and daughters age 21 or older of green card holders. F2B waits are generally much longer than F2A waits.

## Who a green card holder cannot sponsor

- **Parents.** Can a green card holder sponsor a green card for parents? No. Only U.S. citizens age 21 or older can petition for parents.
- **Married sons or daughters.** Only U.S. citizens can petition for them, in the F3 category.
- **Brothers and sisters.** Only U.S. citizens age 21 or older can petition for siblings, in the F4 category.

If you want to sponsor these relatives, becoming a U.S. citizen is the necessary first step.

## How F2A waiting times work

F2A is a preference category, so the number of visas each year is limited. Your relative's place in line is the priority date, which is the date USCIS receives the I-130. The State Department visa bulletin shows each month which priority dates can move forward.

F2A has at times moved quickly and at other times built up a backlog. Applicants from high-demand countries, such as Mexico, can face longer waits. Check the visa bulletin each month for the current cutoff.

## What happens when you become a citizen

If you naturalize while an F2A petition is pending or approved, your spouse and children under 21 automatically convert to immediate relatives. Immediate relatives have no annual limit, which usually eliminates the wait. Notify USCIS or the NVC of the naturalization with a copy of your certificate.

For F2B beneficiaries, naturalization converts the case to F1, which in some countries is slower than F2B. A special option lets the beneficiary opt to stay in F2B.

## Children aging out

A child who turns 21 before a visa is available may lose F2A eligibility and move to F2B. The Child Status Protection Act can preserve eligibility in some cases by subtracting the time the petition was pending. The calculation is technical and depends on when the visa became available.

## Can F2A spouses adjust status in the U.S.?

Only if they are in lawful status when the visa becomes available and meet other requirements. Unlike immediate relatives of citizens, F2A beneficiaries are generally not forgiven for status violations. Many F2A families use consular processing instead.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can my F2A spouse visit while we wait?

Visitor visas are often hard to get with a pending immigrant petition, because officers may doubt the intent to return. A visit is not impossible but requires strong ties abroad.

### What if we marry after I get my green card?

You can file an F2A petition for your new spouse. Marriage after residence does not affect eligibility, but the marriage must be genuine.

### Can my F2A child come before my spouse?

Children can be included as derivatives on the spouse's petition or have their own petitions. Separate petitions can help if a child is close to turning 21 or the marriage status may change.
