Immigration courts now hold many hearings by WebEx video instead of in person. If you are in removal proceedings, that can make attending easier, but it also brings new ways to miss a hearing. And if you move, your case does not automatically move with you. You have to update your address and, in many cases, ask the judge to change venue.

## How WebEx immigration hearings work

The Executive Office for Immigration Review allows immigration judges to hold hearings remotely. Whether your hearing is remote, in person, or hybrid depends on the judge and the type of hearing. Master calendar hearings are more often remote. Individual hearings, where testimony is taken, may be in person or remote depending on the court.

### Finding the WebEx link

EOIR publishes WebEx links for immigration judges by court on its website. Your hearing notice should tell you whether the hearing is remote. Do not rely on a link someone sends you by text or social media. Use the official EOIR source or your attorney.

### Before the hearing

- Test the link and your device a day or two before
- Use a quiet, private space with a stable connection
- Have your hearing notice, A-number, and documents ready
- Keep a phone line available in case the video drops
- Know how to contact the court if you cannot connect

### During the hearing

Treat it like court. Dress appropriately, keep your camera on unless told otherwise, and do not have others coaching you off screen. Interpreters are provided by the court.

## What happens if you miss a WebEx hearing

Failing to appear can result in an in absentia removal order, even if the problem was technical. If you could not connect, contact the court immediately and talk to an attorney about a motion to reopen. Document the problem with screenshots or phone records.

## Moving? Update your address first

Within five days of moving, you must file Form EOIR-33 with the immigration court to update your address. Many people also need to update their address with DHS. Missing this step means hearing notices go to the old address, and missing a hearing can lead to a removal order.

## Changing venue in immigration court

If you move to a different part of the country, you can file a motion to change venue asking to transfer your case to the court near your new home.

The judge will consider:

- Whether you have good cause, such as a permanent move for work or family
- Whether you have filed the address change on Form EOIR-33
- Where the evidence and witnesses are
- The stage of the case, since judges are less willing to move a case close to a final hearing
- DHS's position

Until the judge grants the motion, you must keep attending hearings at the original court. Filing the motion does not postpone anything.

## Common mistakes

- Moving and assuming the case follows automatically
- Missing a hearing because of a wrong WebEx link
- Filing a venue motion without the EOIR-33
- Treating a remote hearing as less serious than an in-person one


## Frequently asked questions

### Can my attorney appear by WebEx if I am in person?

Courts handle this differently. Ask your attorney and check the judge's standing orders.

### Can I request an in-person hearing?

You can ask, but the judge decides the hearing format. Explain any technical or language barriers in a written request.

### Does a change of venue restart my case?

No. The record transfers to the new court, and the case continues from where it was. Hearing dates will be reset by the new court.

### Can I move while waiting for the venue decision?

Yes, but you must keep appearing at the original court, remotely or in person, until the judge grants the motion. Plan travel accordingly.
