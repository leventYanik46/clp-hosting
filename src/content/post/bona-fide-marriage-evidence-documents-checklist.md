A bona fide marriage is one entered into in good faith to build a life together, not to get an immigration benefit. USCIS decides that question from the documents you file and, often, from an interview. The goal of your evidence is to show a real shared life across time, using records that were created in the ordinary course and not assembled for the case.

## What USCIS looks for

An officer wants to see overlap in your lives, supported by third parties. No single document proves a marriage, and no single missing one disproves it. A stack of similar items, such as ten photos from the same weekend, adds less than a few documents drawn from different parts of life.

## Categories of evidence

**Shared finances**
- Joint bank account statements showing regular use
- Joint tax returns, where you have filed together
- Joint credit cards, loans or car financing
- Records showing both names on bills

**Shared residence**
- A joint lease or mortgage
- Utility bills and mail addressed to both of you at the same address
- Driver's licenses or IDs showing the same address

**Insurance and beneficiaries**
- Health, life or auto insurance listing each other
- Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts or policies

**Shared life over time**
- Photos from different dates and settings, including with family and friends
- Travel records, tickets and hotel reservations
- Messages, cards and emails showing the relationship over time
- Membership or subscription accounts held jointly

**Children and family**
- Birth certificates of children you have together
- School or medical records listing both parents

## Using affidavits

Sworn statements from people who know you as a couple, such as relatives, friends, neighbors or coworkers, can add support. A useful affidavit is specific. It states who the person is, how they know you, how long, what they have seen of your relationship, and why they believe the marriage is real. Generic praise is not helpful. Each affiant should sign, date and include contact details and a copy of an ID. There is no required USCIS form for these, so keep to a plain structure.

Affidavits support documents but rarely replace them.

## The general document list for a marriage-based filing

- Form I-130 and Form I-130A, with fees
- Form I-485 for those adjusting status in the United States
- Form I-864 affidavit of support, with the sponsor's tax and income documents
- Form I-693 medical exam from a USCIS-designated civil surgeon, where required
- Applications for a work permit and travel document, if requested
- Proof of the U.S. citizen's status, such as a birth certificate, passport or naturalization certificate
- Marriage certificate and the birth certificate for each spouse
- Divorce decrees or death certificates ending any prior marriages
- Copies of passports, visas, entry records and prior immigration documents
- Passport-style photos
- Any police or court records, if there is criminal history

Check the current form instructions before filing, because requirements and fees change.

## Gaps and how to handle them

Not every couple lives together or shares accounts, especially if one spouse is abroad or work separates you. If your case has a gap, explain it honestly and offer other evidence. A short written explanation can help, and a lawyer can advise on how best to present it.

## Pitfalls

- Submitting evidence that looks staged
- Photos with no explanation of when and where
- Inconsistent addresses across documents
- Leaving out a prior marriage

An attorney can review your evidence in advance and tell you which items strengthen the case.
