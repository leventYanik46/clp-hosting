Your I-94 is the record of your admission to the U.S. It shows your class of admission and, most importantly, the date your authorized stay ends. That date controls whether you are in status, not the expiration date on your visa stamp. Checking your I-94 after every entry is one of the simplest ways to avoid an accidental overstay.

## How to download your I-94

CBP issues I-94s electronically for most air and sea arrivals. To get yours:

1. Go to CBP's official I-94 website at i94.cbp.dhs.gov.
2. Choose the option to get your most recent I-94.
3. Enter your first and last name, date of birth, and passport number and country exactly as they appear on the passport you used to enter.
4. Print or save the record.

You can also view your travel history, which lists your entries and exits for recent years. This is useful for naturalization, green card applications, and tracking time for H-1B or L-1 recapture.

If you cannot find the record, try variations: a different passport, middle names included or excluded, or swapped first and last names. If you received a new I-94 from USCIS with an approval notice, that record replaces the one CBP issued.

## What the I-94 shows

- **Admission (I-94) record number:** An 11-character number
- **Most recent date of entry**
- **Class of admission:** B-2, F-1, H-1B, and so on
- **Admit until date:** The date your authorized stay ends, or "D/S" for duration of status

## Why the I-94 date matters more than your visa

A visa is a travel document. It can expire while you remain lawfully in the U.S. Your I-94 date is what counts for overstays, unlawful presence, and extension deadlines. If your I-94 date is earlier than you expected, you need to act before it passes.

For employees, Form I-9 verification and state ID agencies often rely on the I-94.

## Common I-94 errors

- Wrong class of admission, such as B-2 instead of H-1B
- An admit-until date shorter than your petition or passport allowed
- Misspelled name or wrong date of birth
- A missing record after a land border entry

## How to fix an I-94 error

**If CBP made the error at entry**, contact a CBP Deferred Inspection office or the port of entry where you arrived. Bring your passport, visa, approval notice, and the incorrect I-94. Many offices will correct clear errors.

**If USCIS issued the I-94**, for example on an extension or change of status approval notice, file Form I-102 with USCIS, or in some cases request a correction through the USCIS Contact Center, depending on the type of error.

Fix errors as soon as you notice them. An incorrect date that passes without correction can create an overstay record that is harder to undo later.

## Passport expiration and the I-94

For some classifications, CBP limits the admit-until date to your passport's expiration. Renew your passport early, and after reentry with a new passport, check that the I-94 reflects the full period you need.


## Frequently asked questions

### Do I get an I-94 at a land border?

Yes, most land arrivals receive an electronic I-94. CBP allows travelers to apply and pay for one online before arriving at a land border.

### Is my I-94 the same as my visa?

No. The visa is the stamp in your passport. The I-94 is the admission record. They can show different dates.

### Can I fix an I-94 error by mail?

CBP generally handles corrections in person or through its deferred inspection offices. USCIS-issued I-94 corrections are filed on Form I-102. Keep copies of everything you submit.
