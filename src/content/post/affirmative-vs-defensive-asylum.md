There are two ways to apply for asylum in the United States. In **affirmative asylum**, you file with USCIS and an asylum officer decides your case in a non-adversarial interview. In **defensive asylum**, you ask an immigration judge for asylum as a defense against removal, with a government attorney opposing you. The legal standard is the same, but the process, risks, and timing differ in ways that shape your whole strategy.

## The asylum requirements are the same in both tracks

In either track, you must show that you have suffered persecution or have a well-founded fear of future persecution on account of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group, and that your government either did it or cannot or will not protect you.

You must generally file within **one year of your last arrival**, unless an exception applies, such as changed or extraordinary circumstances. Certain bars, such as serious crimes, persecution of others, and firm resettlement elsewhere, apply in both tracks.

## Affirmative asylum

**Who it is for:** People who are in the U.S. and not in removal proceedings, whether they hold a valid status, have overstayed, or entered without inspection.

**How it works:** You file Form I-589 with USCIS, pay the filing fee, attend biometrics, and later attend an interview with an asylum officer at an asylum office. The interview is not a trial. There is no government lawyer arguing against you. You may bring an attorney and, depending on current rules, an interpreter.

**Outcomes:**
- **Grant**: you receive asylum status.
- **Referral**: if you lack another status, the case is referred to immigration court, where it becomes a defensive case.
- **Denial**: if you have valid status, such as a student visa, the officer can deny and you keep your other status.

## Defensive asylum

**Who it is for:** People in removal proceedings, including those referred from the affirmative process, stopped at the border, or arrested by immigration authorities.

**How it works:** Asylum is requested before an immigration judge. The case typically begins with master calendar hearings, then an individual merits hearing where you testify, present witnesses and evidence, and are cross-examined by a DHS attorney.

**Outcomes:** The judge grants asylum, grants a lesser form of protection such as withholding of removal or Convention Against Torture protection, or orders removal. Either side can appeal to the Board of Immigration Appeals.

## Why the track matters

- **Tone and pressure.** Affirmative interviews are conversational; defensive hearings are adversarial.
- **Evidence rules.** Courts set filing deadlines for evidence and witness lists. Missing them can exclude evidence.
- **Consequences of losing.** A failed defensive case ends in a removal order. A failed affirmative case usually means a second chance in court.
- **Timing.** Backlogs differ between asylum offices and immigration courts, and both change.
- **Fees.** Both tracks are subject to the asylum filing fee and the annual asylum fee, collected by USCIS or the court system.

## When a case moves from one track to the other

A referral from the asylum office starts removal proceedings with a Notice to Appear. Your I-589 goes to the immigration judge, and you can update it. Treat the referral as a new phase that needs preparation, not a continuation of the interview.

## Preparing in either track

- Write a detailed, consistent declaration.
- Gather country conditions evidence specific to your situation.
- Collect documents that corroborate your account, or explain why you cannot.
- Prepare for questions about the one-year deadline if you filed late.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I choose which track to use?
If you are not in removal proceedings, you file affirmatively. If you are already in proceedings, asylum must be requested before the immigration judge.

### Is one track more likely to succeed?
The legal standard is the same, but outcomes vary by office, court, and case. Preparation matters more than the track.

### Can I work while my asylum case is pending?
You may apply for employment authorization after a waiting period counted from filing, if the case is not delayed by you. Rules and fees have changed recently.

### What happens to my family members?
Spouses and unmarried children under 21 who were in the U.S. when you filed can be included on your application as derivatives.

### Does the one-year deadline apply in court?
Yes. The deadline is the same in both tracks, with the same exceptions.

### Can I bring witnesses to an asylum interview?
You may bring witnesses, though their role is more formal in court, where they testify under oath and can be cross-examined.

### What is withholding of removal?
A related protection with a higher standard of proof. It prevents removal to a specific country but does not lead to a green card or allow derivative family members.
