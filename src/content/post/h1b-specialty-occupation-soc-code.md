The H-1B visa is a temporary work visa for jobs that require at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field. USCIS calls these specialty occupations. Whether a role qualifies is decided by the job's actual duties and requirements, not by your degree alone, which is why two people with the same degree can get very different results.

## What is the H-1B visa?

The H-1B lets a U.S. employer hire a foreign professional for up to three years at a time, generally up to six years total, with longer extensions for green card applicants. Most new H-1B workers go through an annual lottery. The employer files the petition, pays most of the costs, and must pay at least the required wage.

## What makes a job a specialty occupation?

The role must require theoretical and practical application of a body of highly specialized knowledge and at least a bachelor's degree in a specific specialty or its equivalent. The petition needs to meet at least one of these tests:

- A bachelor's or higher degree in a specific specialty is normally the minimum requirement for the position
- The degree requirement is common in the industry for parallel positions, or the job is so complex it can only be done by someone with the degree
- The employer normally requires the degree for this position
- The duties are so specialized and complex that the knowledge required is usually associated with the degree

## The 2025 rule on degree fields

The H-1B modernization rule that took effect January 17, 2025 clarified that a job can accept more than one degree field as long as each one is directly related to the duties. A data analyst role that accepts statistics, economics, or computer science can qualify if the employer explains how each field connects to the work.

What does not work is a requirement for "any bachelor's degree." A general degree requirement, such as business administration without specialization, is a weak basis for an H-1B.

## How SOC codes fit in

Every H-1B starts with a Labor Condition Application, and the LCA uses a Standard Occupational Classification code from the O*NET system. The SOC code does three things:

1. It tells USCIS which occupation the employer believes the job falls into
2. It sets the prevailing wage the employer must pay
3. It brings along the government's own description of typical education requirements for that occupation

If the chosen SOC code describes an occupation where a specific degree is not usually required, the case gets harder. If the code is too senior for the duties, the wage may be higher than needed. Picking the code is a legal judgment, not a clerical step.

## Jobs that often face scrutiny

- Entry-level computer roles with generic duties
- Market research analyst and business analyst positions at small companies
- Management roles where the degree field is not tied to the work
- Jobs where the duties described sound like a different, non-degree occupation

## What strengthens the case

- A detailed duty breakdown with percentages of time and the knowledge each task requires
- Evidence of the employer's past hiring requirements for the role
- Industry job postings for similar positions with the same degree requirement
- Expert opinion letters in close cases

## Your degree still has to match

Even if the job qualifies, the worker must hold the required degree or an equivalent. Foreign degrees typically need a credential evaluation. Work experience can substitute at a ratio of three years of experience for each year of missing education, with proper documentation.


## Frequently asked questions

### Can a business analyst role qualify?

It can, but these roles draw scrutiny. The petition should show duties that require a specific field of study, such as statistics, information systems, or finance, rather than general business skills.

### Does a master's degree help?

A U.S. master's degree or higher gives an extra chance in the lottery through the advanced degree exemption. The job still has to qualify as a specialty occupation.

### Who picks the SOC code?

The employer chooses it when filing the LCA, usually with counsel. The choice should be based on the actual duties, not on the lowest available wage.
