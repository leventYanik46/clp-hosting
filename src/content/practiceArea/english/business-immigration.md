    <h2>A guide for employers</h2>
    <h3>Hiring a foreign professional for the first time</h3>
    <p>Start with the candidate's status and the job. A graduate on F-1 OPT, a transfer from a foreign office and a Canadian engineer each point to a different category with different costs, timing and attestations. We recommend a primary route and a fallback before filing.</p>
    <h3>Cap season planning</h3>
    <p>Cap-subject H-1B registrations are submitted in March for an October start, so candidates must be identified early and bridged if not selected. We prepare alternatives such as O-1A, TN or L-1 so a lottery result does not stop a hire.</p>
    <h3>Transfers and extensions</h3>
    <p>A new employer, a new metropolitan area, materially different duties or an extension each requires a filing. We track the dates and changes so no one works outside an approved petition.</p>
    <h3>Compliance: LCA public access files and Form I-9</h3>
    <p>H-1B and E-3 employers must keep a public access file for every Labor Condition Application, with the certified LCA, the wage rate and its basis, the posting notices and a benefits summary, retained after the LCA ends. Every employer must also complete Form I-9 for each new hire within three business days of the start date and reverify expiring work authorization. We set up and audit both.</p>
    <h3>When an employee leaves</h3>
    <p>A bona fide termination of an H-1B worker requires written notice to the worker, notice to USCIS to withdraw the petition, and an offer to pay reasonable return transportation; until then, the LCA wage obligation can continue. The worker generally has up to 60 days to find a new sponsor, change status or depart.</p>

    <h2>A guide for founders and investors</h2>
    <p>The visa follows the stage of the business. A treaty-country founder funding a new U.S. company usually begins with the E-2, which has no fixed minimum investment and is renewable while the business qualifies. An established foreign company opening a U.S. subsidiary transfers its leadership on the L-1A. A founder with a strong record may qualify for the O-1A or an EB-2 National Interest Waiver. Investors seeking permanent residence directly consider the EB-5; executives who have run the U.S. company for a year move to the EB-1C. Our <a href="/practice-area/business-corporate-law">business and corporate law</a> team handles the entity, operating agreements and contracts behind the visa.</p>

    <h2>Turkish companies and investors</h2>
    <p>Turkey maintains a treaty of commerce and navigation with the United States, so Turkish nationals qualify for the E-2 and E-1 visas, and Turkish companies opening U.S. offices use the L-1A and later the EB-1C. Our <a href="/locations/istanbul">Istanbul office</a> works with founders on business plans, source-of-funds documentation and interview preparation; our U.S. offices handle formation and USCIS filings.</p>

faq:
  - question: Which work visa should a startup use for its first foreign hire?
    answer: >-
      It depends on the candidate. A degreed professional usually points to the
      H-1B, but the lottery makes timing uncertain, so we also test the O-1A for
      candidates with a strong record, the TN for Canadians and Mexicans, the E-3
      for Australians, the H-1B1 for Chileans and Singaporeans, and the L-1 when
      the person already works for a related company abroad. We map the primary
      route and a fallback before the offer letter goes out.
  - question: What happens if our H-1B registration is not selected?
    answer: >-
      The company cannot file a cap-subject petition for that fiscal year, but
      the hire is not necessarily lost. The worker may remain on F-1 OPT or STEM
      OPT, move to a cap-exempt employer, qualify for O-1A, TN, E-3, H-1B1 or L-1
      status, or begin a green card process directly. Planning these alternatives
      before registration keeps the business plan intact whatever the lottery
      result.
  - question: What must an employer keep in an LCA public access file?
    answer: >-
      For every certified Labor Condition Application, the employer keeps the
      LCA itself, documentation of the wage rate and the prevailing wage source,
      the actual wage memorandum, the posting notices, a summary of benefits
      offered to workers, and corporate change documents where relevant. The file
      must be available for public inspection within one working day of filing
      and retained after the LCA period ends. We build and audit these files for
      employers.
  - question: Which visa fits a founder who wants to start a company in the United States?
    answer: >-
      For a national of a treaty country, the E-2 is usually the first choice:
      it has no fixed minimum investment, no quota and no lottery, and it can be
      renewed while the business qualifies. Founders from non-treaty countries
      look at the L-1A if they run a company abroad, the O-1A if they have a
      record of achievement, or an EB-2 National Interest Waiver self-petition
      when the venture has national importance.
  - question: Can a Turkish company transfer its managers to a U.S. office?
    answer: >-
      Yes. A Turkish company that forms a U.S. subsidiary or affiliate can
      transfer executives and managers on the L-1A, including through a
      new-office petition when the U.S. entity has operated for less than a year.
      Turkish nationals also qualify for the E-2 and E-1 treaty visas. After the
      U.S. company has operated for a year, the EB-1C green card becomes
      available for qualifying managers.
  - question: How does an employee move from a work visa to a green card?
    answer: >-
      Most employer-sponsored cases run through PERM labor certification and an
      I-140 petition in the EB-2 or EB-3 category, followed by adjustment of
      status when the priority date is current. Executives transferred on the
      L-1A may qualify for the EB-1C without labor certification, and employees
      with a record of achievement or nationally important work may self-petition
      through the EB-1A or EB-2 NIW. H-1B and L-1 are dual-intent categories, so
      a pending green card does not jeopardize the visa.
  - question: What are an employer's obligations when a sponsored employee leaves?
    answer: >-
      For an H-1B worker, the employer should give written notice of
      termination, notify USCIS so the petition is withdrawn, and offer to pay the
      reasonable cost of return transportation. Until the termination is bona
      fide, the LCA wage obligation can continue. Form I-9 records must be
      retained for the required period after separation, and the public access
      file must be kept for the retention period. The worker generally has up to
      60 days to find a new sponsor or change status.
  - question: Did the 2025 fee and policy changes affect business immigration?
    answer: >-
      Yes. New fees, entry restrictions and selection rules affecting the H-1B
      and other employment categories were announced in 2025, and their scope,
      exemptions and implementation have been addressed in agency guidance and
      challenged in court. Because the details continue to change, we confirm
      the rules and costs that apply to your specific filing at consultation
      rather than rely on general summaries.
