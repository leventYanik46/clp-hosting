    <p><strong>If your record places you near the top of your field, U.S. immigration law offers several routes that skip the labor certification and, in two of them, the employer as well.</strong> This overview covers the routes Capitol Law Partners handles, the three EB-1 green cards, the EB-2 National Interest Waiver and the two O-1 visas, each with its own page within our <a href="/practice-area/immigration-law">immigration law</a> practice.</p>

    <h2>Our services in this area</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-1-green-card">EB-1 green card</a>: the first preference category as a whole, covering what the three subcategories share, from Form I-140 to concurrent I-485 filing, and how to choose among them.</li>
      <li><a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-1a-green-card">EB-1A green card</a>: a self-petition for extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business or athletics, shown by a major international award or three of ten criteria and sustained acclaim.</li>
      <li><a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-1b-green-card">EB-1B green card</a>: for outstanding professors and researchers with three years of experience and international recognition. A university or qualifying research employer files and must offer a permanent position; two of six criteria are required.</li>
      <li><a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-1c-green-card">EB-1C green card</a>: for managers and executives transferring within a multinational company after a year of qualifying employment abroad in the preceding three years; the U.S. employer petitions, and the case turns on corporate structure and duties.</li>
      <li><a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-2-niw-green-card">EB-2 NIW green card</a>: a second preference self-petition for advanced-degree or exceptional-ability professionals whose proposed endeavor serves the national interest; no employer, job offer or labor certification is needed.</li>
      <li><a href="/practice-area/key-services/o-1a-visa">O-1A visa</a>: a temporary work visa for extraordinary ability in the sciences, education, business or athletics. A U.S. employer or agent petitions with a peer group consultation, for up to three years plus one-year extensions.</li>
      <li><a href="/practice-area/key-services/o-1b-visa">O-1B visa</a>: the O-1 for artists and entertainers with distinction in the arts and for motion picture and television professionals with extraordinary achievement, with O-2 status for essential support staff.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Which route fits you</h2>
    <h3>Self-petition options: EB-1A and EB-2 NIW</h3>
    <p>Both can be filed without an employer. The EB-1A demands the very top of the field, but the first preference is usually more favorable under the Visa Bulletin. The NIW asks instead whether your proposed work has national importance and whether you can advance it. Many clients qualify for both and file them together.</p>
    <h3>Employer-sponsored options: EB-1B and EB-1C</h3>
    <p>The EB-1B sets a lower bar than EB-1A, but the institution files and the position must be permanent. The EB-1C does not test acclaim; it turns on the corporate relationship and on whether your duties are genuinely managerial or executive. In both, the petition belongs to the employer.</p>
    <h3>The O-1 as a bridge to a green card</h3>
    <p>The O-1A and O-1B are temporary, but they have no annual cap or lottery, and under 8 CFR 214.2(o)(13) filing an immigrant petition is not a basis for denying an O-1 petition or extension. Many clients start on an O-1 and later file an EB-1A or NIW with the same evidence, strengthened over time.</p>

    <h2>The Dhanasar test and the extraordinary ability criteria</h2>
    <p>The NIW is decided under <em>Matter of Dhanasar</em>, 26 I&amp;N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016). Your proposed endeavor must have substantial merit and national importance, you must be well positioned to advance it, and on balance it must benefit the United States to waive the job offer and labor certification. The focus is the endeavor, not a tally of credentials.</p>
    <p>The EB-1A and O-1A test the person. Unless you hold a major internationally recognized award, you document at least three of the listed criteria, ten for EB-1A and eight for O-1A, ranging from awards and press to original contributions and critical roles. For EB-1A, USCIS then applies the <em>Kazarian</em> final merits determination to the whole record.</p>

    <h2>Priority dates, the Visa Bulletin and premium processing</h2>
    <p>Your I-140 filing date becomes your priority date. The Department of State's monthly Visa Bulletin shows, by category and country of birth, whether immigrant visa numbers are available. When your date is current and you are in the United States in lawful status, you can file Form I-485 with or after the I-140; applicants abroad use consular processing.</p>
    <p>Premium processing is available in every category: USCIS commits to act within 15 business days on EB-1A, EB-1B and O-1 petitions and within 45 business days on EB-2 NIW and EB-1C petitions, by approving, denying or issuing a Request for Evidence. It does not shorten the I-485 stage.</p>

  - question: What is the difference between the EB-1A and the EB-2 NIW?
    answer: >-
      Both are self-petitions that need no employer or labor certification. The
      EB-1A requires proof that you are among the small percentage at the very
      top of your field, a higher standard, but the first preference category is
      usually more favorable under the Visa Bulletin. The NIW asks whether your
      proposed endeavor has national importance and whether you are well
      positioned to advance it. Many clients qualify for both and file them
      together.
  - question: Do I need an employer for any of these categories?
    answer: >-
      Not for the EB-1A or the EB-2 NIW, which you file yourself. The EB-1B must
      be filed by a university or qualifying research employer, and the EB-1C by
      the U.S. arm of a multinational company. The O-1A and O-1B require a U.S.
      employer or agent as petitioner, although a company you own can petition
      for you if it is a separate legal entity able to act as your employer.
  - question: Can I file more than one petition at the same time?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Each petition is judged on its own, and filing an EB-1A together with
      an EB-2 NIW, or an EB-1B through your institution alongside a self-petition,
      is common. Each approved I-140 gives you a priority date in its category,
      and you proceed on whichever becomes current first. We decide the
      combination and the order at the first consultation based on your record
      and your country of birth.
  - question: How fast is premium processing in each category?
    answer: >-
      USCIS commits to act within 15 business days on EB-1A, EB-1B, O-1A and
      O-1B petitions and within 45 business days on EB-2 NIW and EB-1C petitions
      once it receives the premium processing request. Acting means approving,
      denying or issuing a Request for Evidence. Premium processing does not
      speed up a pending I-485 or the consular stage, which depend on the Visa
      Bulletin and appointment availability.
  - question: Is the O-1 visa a path to a green card?
    answer: >-
      It can be. The O-1 is a temporary classification, but under 8 CFR
      214.2(o)(13) filing an immigrant petition is not a basis for denying an O-1
      petition or extension. Many professionals and artists begin on an O-1A or
      O-1B and later file an EB-1A or EB-2 NIW using the same evidence,
      strengthened over time. We plan that transition from the first filing so
      the record serves both cases.
  - question: Can a startup founder qualify for one of these routes?
    answer: >-
      Yes. A founder may be sponsored for an O-1A by a company they own if it is
      a separate legal entity, such as a corporation or LLC, that can act as the
      employer. Founders also self-petition under the EB-2 NIW, where job
      creation, investment and work in a priority industry support national
      importance, and under the EB-1A where their record shows sustained
      acclaim. The evidence is business-focused: funding, contracts, revenue,
      customers and expert letters.
  - question: What is the Visa Bulletin and why does it matter?
    answer: >-
      The Visa Bulletin is published monthly by the Department of State and
      shows, by preference category and country of birth, whether immigrant visa
      numbers are available. Your I-140 filing date is your priority date, and
      you can file Form I-485 or receive an immigrant visa only when that date is
      current. Because the first preference is often more favorable than the
      second, the Bulletin shapes whether we lead with EB-1 or the NIW.
  - question: What should I send for a CV evaluation?
    answer: >-
      Your CV, a list of awards with their selection criteria, your publication
      and citation record, links to articles about you, patents, grants or
      funding, roles you have held and the organizations' standing, pay
      information, and a short note on the work you plan to do in the United
      States and whether an employer or institution would sponsor you. From that
      we identify the realistic categories and the sequence we recommend.
