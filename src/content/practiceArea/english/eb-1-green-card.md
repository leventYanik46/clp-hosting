We tell you which subcategory fits, whether the EB-2 National Interest Waiver serves you better, and how to sequence the filing with your status and the Visa Bulletin.</p>

    <h2>The three EB-1 subcategories</h2>
    <h3>EB-1A: extraordinary ability</h3>
    <p>The <a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-1a-green-card">EB-1A green card</a>, under INA 203(b)(1)(A) and 8 CFR 204.5(h), is for people with extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business or athletics, shown by sustained national or international acclaim. You can self-petition without an employer or job offer. Unless you hold a major internationally recognized award, you must document at least three of ten criteria and then pass a final merits determination showing that you are among the small percentage at the very top of the field.</p>
    <h3>EB-1B: outstanding professors and researchers</h3>
    <p>The <a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-1b-green-card">EB-1B green card</a>, under INA 203(b)(1)(B) and 8 CFR 204.5(i), is for academics recognized internationally as outstanding in a specific field with at least three years of teaching or research experience. The employer files the petition and must offer a tenured or tenure-track teaching position, a comparable permanent research position at a university, or a permanent research position with a private employer that employs at least three full-time researchers and has documented accomplishments in the field. At least two of six criteria are required.</p>
    <h3>EB-1C: multinational managers and executives</h3>
    <p>The <a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-1c-green-card">EB-1C green card</a>, under INA 203(b)(1)(C) and 8 CFR 204.5(j), is for managers and executives transferring within a multinational company. You must have worked abroad for the same company or its parent, subsidiary or affiliate in a managerial or executive capacity for at least one year in the preceding three years, and the U.S. employer must have been doing business for at least one year. Many beneficiaries hold L-1A status, though it is not a prerequisite.</p>

    <h2>What the three subcategories share</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>No labor certification.</strong> There is no recruitment process and no prevailing wage determination in any EB-1 subcategory.</li>
      <li><strong>Form I-140.</strong> Every EB-1 case starts with Form I-140, filed by you or by the employer, and the filing date becomes your priority date.</li>
      <li><strong>Premium processing.</strong> USCIS commits to act on EB-1A and EB-1B petitions within 15 business days of a premium processing request, and on EB-1C petitions within 45 business days.</li>
      <li><strong>Concurrent filing.</strong> If you are in the United States in lawful status and your priority date is current under the Visa Bulletin, you can file Form I-485 with the I-140 and request employment authorization and advance parole while both are pending.</li>
      <li><strong>Derivatives.</strong> Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 receive green cards with you.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>How to choose between EB-1A, EB-1B and EB-1C</h2>
    <p>If no employer will sponsor you, or you want a petition that does not depend on one job, EB-1A is the only EB-1 option, and it carries the highest standard. If an institution has offered you a permanent research or tenure-track position, EB-1B asks for two criteria instead of three and tests international recognition rather than the very top of the field, but the petition belongs to the employer. If you have managed a team or function abroad for a multinational company that will employ you in a similar role here, EB-1C turns on corporate structure and duties rather than acclaim.</p>

    <h2>When the EB-2 NIW is the better fit</h2>
    <p>The <a href="/practice-area/key-services/eb-2-niw-green-card">EB-2 National Interest Waiver</a> also avoids a labor certification and allows self-petition, but under <em>Matter of Dhanasar</em> it asks whether your proposed endeavor has substantial merit and national importance, whether you are well positioned to advance it, and whether waiving the job offer benefits the United States. The NIW is often the stronger route when your record is solid but does not yet show sustained acclaim. Because it is a second preference category, the Visa Bulletin can be less favorable than EB-1, so we often file an EB-1A and an NIW together.</p>

    <h2>Evidence and documents</h2>
    <p>EB-1A and EB-1B cases rest on award records, membership criteria, press, peer review records, publication and citation data, patents and letters from independent experts. EB-1C cases rest on corporate documents proving the qualifying relationship between the entities, organizational charts and detailed descriptions of the duties. Employer petitions also require proof of the ability to pay the offered wage.</p>

    <h2>Common problems in EB-1 cases</h2>
    <p>EB-1A petitions most often fail at the final merits stage, where USCIS accepts three criteria but finds no sustained acclaim. EB-1B petitions are refused when the position is not permanent or when a private employer cannot show three full-time researchers. EB-1C petitions are refused when the duties are operational rather than managerial or when the corporate relationship is poorly documented. We address each issue before filing and take over RFEs and denials on petitions prepared elsewhere.</p>

faq:
  - question: What is the difference between EB-1A, EB-1B and EB-1C?
    answer: >-
      All three are first preference green cards that need no labor
      certification. EB-1A is for people with extraordinary ability and can be
      self-petitioned. EB-1B is for outstanding professors and researchers with
      a permanent job offer, and the employer files it. EB-1C is for managers
      and executives transferring within a multinational company, and the U.S.
      employer files it. Which one fits depends on who can petition and what
      your record shows.
  - question: Does any EB-1 category require a PERM labor certification?
    answer: >-
      No. None of the three EB-1 subcategories requires a labor certification
      from the Department of Labor, so there is no recruitment process and no
      prevailing wage determination. The case begins directly with Form I-140.
      EB-1B and EB-1C employers must still show their ability to pay the
      offered wage, and EB-1A self-petitioners must show that they will
      continue working in their field in the United States.
  - question: Is premium processing available for EB-1 petitions?
    answer: >-
      Yes. USCIS offers premium processing for all three EB-1 subcategories.
      For EB-1A and EB-1B petitions USCIS commits to act on the I-140 within 15
      business days, and for EB-1C petitions within 45 business days. Acting
      means approving, denying or issuing a Request for Evidence. Premium
      processing speeds the I-140 decision but does not change the timeline of
      a pending I-485 adjustment application.
  - question: Can I file Form I-485 together with my EB-1 petition?
    answer: >-
      Yes, if you are in the United States in lawful status and your priority
      date is current for your country of birth under the Visa Bulletin.
      Concurrent filing lets you request employment authorization and advance
      parole while both applications are pending. If EB-1 is backlogged for
      your country of birth, the I-485 is filed later when a visa number
      becomes available.
  - question: Should I file an EB-1 petition or an EB-2 National Interest Waiver?
    answer: >-
      EB-1A requires evidence that you are at the very top of your field, while
      the NIW asks whether your proposed work has national importance and
      whether you are well positioned to advance it. The NIW threshold is lower,
      but EB-1 is often more favorable under the Visa Bulletin. Many clients
      qualify for both, and filing them together secures a priority date in
      each category.
  - question: Can my spouse and children get green cards through my EB-1 petition?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 are derivative
      beneficiaries in every EB-1 subcategory. If they are in the United States
      they file their own I-485 applications with yours or after your I-140 is
      approved; if they are abroad they complete consular processing. They
      receive permanent residence when you do.
  - question: Can I file an EB-1 petition while on an H-1B, O-1 or L-1 visa?
    answer: >-
      Yes. H-1B and L-1 status permit dual intent, and the O-1 regulations
      provide that filing an immigrant petition is not a basis for denying an
      O-1 petition or extension. These are the most common starting points for
      EB-1 cases. We time the I-140 and any concurrent I-485 so that your work
      authorization and travel are not interrupted.
  - question: What happens if my EB-1 petition is denied?
    answer: >-
      A denial does not bar you from filing again. Depending on the reasons
      USCIS gives, we may file a motion to reopen or reconsider, appeal to the
      Administrative Appeals Office, or prepare a new petition with stronger
      evidence, in the same subcategory or under a different one such as the
      EB-2 NIW. We review denials of petitions prepared by other firms or by the
      applicant.
