    <h2>Who qualifies for an O-1A visa</h2>
    <p>The O-1A classification is created by INA 101(a)(15)(O)(i) and governed by 8 CFR 214.2(o). You must have extraordinary ability in the sciences, education, business or athletics, shown by sustained national or international acclaim, and you must be coming to the United States to continue work in your area of extraordinary ability. The regulation defines extraordinary ability in these fields as a level of expertise indicating that you are one of the small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field. Artists and entertainment professionals apply under the separate <a href="/practice-area/key-services/o-1b-visa">O-1B</a> standard.</p>
    <p>Three structural rules shape every O-1A case:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>A U.S. petitioner is required.</strong> You cannot file for yourself. The petition must be filed by a U.S. employer, a U.S. agent, or a foreign employer acting through a U.S. agent. An agent may file for several employers with an itinerary of the work. USCIS guidance also allows a company you own to petition for you if it is a separate legal entity able to act as your employer, which makes the O-1A workable for founders.</li>
      <li><strong>A written consultation is required.</strong> Under 8 CFR 214.2(o)(5), the petition must include an advisory opinion from an appropriate peer group, labor organization or person with expertise in your field. If no appropriate peer group exists, USCIS decides on the evidence in the record.</li>
      <li><strong>Validity is tied to the work.</strong> An initial O-1A petition may be approved for the time needed to complete the event or activity, up to three years, with extensions in increments of up to one year for the same work. A new employer must file its own petition before you change jobs.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>The O-1A evidentiary criteria</h2>
    <p>Under 8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii), you qualify either with a major internationally recognized award, such as a Nobel Prize, or by documenting at least three of the following eight criteria:</p>
    <ol>
      <li>Nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field.</li>
      <li>Membership in associations that require outstanding achievements of their members, as judged by recognized national or international experts.</li>
      <li>Published material in professional or major trade publications or major media about you and your work.</li>
      <li>Participation on a panel, or individually, as a judge of the work of others in the same or an allied field.</li>
      <li>Original scientific, scholarly or business-related contributions of major significance.</li>
      <li>Authorship of scholarly articles in professional journals or other major media.</li>
      <li>Employment in a critical or essential capacity for organizations with a distinguished reputation.</li>
      <li>A high salary or other remuneration for services, shown by contracts or other reliable evidence.</li>
    </ol>
    <p>If these criteria do not readily apply to your occupation, the regulation allows comparable evidence. As with the EB-1A, the officer first checks the criteria and then weighs the whole record for sustained acclaim, so a petition that lists three criteria without context is vulnerable.</p>

    <ol>
      <li><strong>Assessment.</strong> We review your record, identify the petitioner and confirm that the proposed work is in your field of acclaim.</li>

    <h2>Common problems in O-1A cases</h2>
    <p>RFEs and denials usually trace back to a few issues: a petitioner that does not qualify as an employer or agent; a founder petition without a separate legal entity or proof of an employer relationship; a missing or inadequate consultation; awards and memberships that are not selective; press that is promotional or written by the petitioner; contributions described as significant without evidence of impact; and proposed work outside the field of your acclaim. Timing errors also cause trouble: filing more than one year before the start date, letting status lapse before an extension, or changing employers without a new petition.</p>
    <p>We prevent these problems by settling the petitioner structure first, documenting the standing of each award, publication and organization, and tying the exhibits to the regulatory language in a focused brief. If you received an RFE or denial on a petition prepared elsewhere, we can take over.</p>

  - question: Can I petition for an O-1A visa myself?
    answer: >-
      No. The O-1A must be filed by a U.S. employer, a U.S. agent, or a foreign
      employer through a U.S. agent. USCIS guidance does allow a company you own
      to petition for you if it is a separate legal entity, such as a corporation
      or LLC, that can act as your employer. We help founders set up and document
      that structure so the petition is not refused on this point.
  - question: How long is an O-1A visa valid?
    answer: >-
      USCIS approves an initial O-1A petition for the time needed to complete the
      event or activity, up to three years. Extensions are granted in increments
      of up to one year to continue or complete the same work. You may be
      admitted up to ten days before the validity period and remain ten days
      after it, and a grace period of up to 60 days applies if your employment
      ends early.
  - question: What is the O-1 advisory opinion and is it always required?
    answer: >-
      The advisory opinion, or consultation, is a written statement from an
      appropriate peer group, labor organization or expert in your field about
      your qualifications and the proposed work. It must be included with the
      petition. If no appropriate peer group or labor organization exists for
      your field, USCIS decides the petition on the evidence in the record. We
      identify the right organization and prepare the request.
  - question: How fast can an O-1A petition be approved?
    answer: >-
      With premium processing, USCIS commits to act on the I-129 within 15
      business days of receiving the request, either by approving, denying or
      issuing a Request for Evidence. Regular processing times vary and are
      published by USCIS. If you are abroad, add time for the consular interview
      and visa issuance. The petition may be filed up to one year before the
      start date.
  - question: How does the O-1A compare with the H-1B?
    answer: >-
      The O-1A has no annual cap or lottery, no prevailing wage requirement and
      no specialty occupation or degree requirement, and it can be extended in
      one-year increments without a fixed maximum stay. In exchange, the
      evidentiary standard is much higher: you must show sustained national or
      international acclaim. Many professionals who missed the H-1B lottery
      qualify for the O-1A once their record is documented properly.
  - question: Can I apply for a green card while on an O-1A visa?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Under 8 CFR 214.2(o)(13), the filing of an immigrant petition or the
      approval of a labor certification is not a basis for denying an O-1
      petition or extension. Many of our O-1A clients move to an EB-1A or EB-2
      NIW green card, often using the same evidence strengthened over time. We
      plan that transition from the start of the O-1A case.
  - question: Can my spouse and children join me in the United States?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 may receive O-3 status for
      the same period as your O-1A. O-3 dependents may attend school but are not
      authorized to work in the United States. If they are abroad, they apply for
      O-3 visas at a U.S. consulate after your petition is approved.
  - question: Can I work for more than one employer on an O-1A?
    answer: >-
      Yes, but each employer must either file its own petition or be covered by a
      petition filed by a U.S. agent that includes an itinerary of the work. If
      you change employers, the new employer must file a new petition before you
      begin, and material changes to the terms of your employment require an
      amended petition from the existing petitioner.
