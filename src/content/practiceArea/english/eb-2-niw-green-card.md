    <p><strong>The EB-2 National Interest Waiver (NIW) lets you petition for a U.S. green card on your own, without an employer sponsor and without the PERM labor certification.</strong> Capitol Law Partners prepares NIW petitions for researchers, engineers, physicians, entrepreneurs and other advanced-degree professionals whose work matters to the United States. We build the legal argument, organize the evidence, and handle the case from the first consultation through approval.</p>

    <h2>What an EB-2 NIW attorney does for you</h2>
    <p>A National Interest Waiver petition is not a form-filling exercise. It is a written legal argument, supported by exhibits, that must persuade a USCIS officer on three separate points. Our role is to:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Evaluate honestly whether the NIW is your strongest route, or whether EB-1A, employer-sponsored EB-2 or another category fits better.</li>
      <li>Define a specific <em>proposed endeavor</em> and frame it in terms USCIS recognizes as nationally important.</li>
      <li>Map your record to each prong of the <em>Matter of Dhanasar</em> test and identify the gaps before filing.</li>
      <li>Draft the petition letter, the endeavor statement and the outlines for expert and recommendation letters.</li>
      <li>Assemble, index and label the exhibits so the officer can verify every claim.</li>
      <li>File Form I-140, track the case, and respond to any Request for Evidence or Notice of Intent to Deny.</li>
      <li>Plan the green card stage: concurrent I-485 adjustment of status where the priority date allows, or consular processing abroad.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Who qualifies for an EB-2 National Interest Waiver</h2>
    <p>You must first meet the baseline EB-2 requirement in one of two ways:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Advanced degree:</strong> a U.S. master's degree or higher, a foreign equivalent, or a bachelor's degree followed by at least five years of progressive experience in your field.</li>
      <li><strong>Exceptional ability:</strong> at least three of six regulatory criteria, such as an academic record relating to the field, ten years of full-time experience, a license or certification, a salary that reflects exceptional ability, membership in professional associations, or recognition of your achievements by peers or government bodies.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>The degree or ability must relate to the endeavor you propose. USCIS updated its Policy Manual guidance on NIW adjudication in January 2025 and now looks closely at that link, so we address it directly in every petition.</p>
    <p>Typical NIW clients include university and industry researchers, software and AI engineers, physicians and public-health specialists, clean-energy and infrastructure professionals, cybersecurity experts, and founders whose companies create U.S. jobs or solve a problem of national scope.</p>

    <h2>The three Dhanasar prongs</h2>
    <p>Since 2016, USCIS has evaluated every NIW under <em>Matter of Dhanasar</em>, 26 I&amp;N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016). You must satisfy all three prongs.</p>
    <h3>1. Substantial merit and national importance</h3>
    <p>The endeavor itself, not your resume, must have substantial merit and national importance. Merit can be shown in science, technology, business, health, education, culture or policy. National importance turns on the prospective impact: does the work reach beyond one employer or one locality? We describe the endeavor concretely, tie it to documented national priorities, and show how its benefits spread across the country.</p>
    <h3>2. Well positioned to advance the endeavor</h3>
    <p>Here the focus shifts to you. USCIS weighs your education, skills, knowledge and record of success, your plan for future work, your progress so far, and the interest of investors, customers, institutions or agencies. Strong evidence includes publications and citations, patents, grants, awards, prior results, letters from independent experts, and a realistic business or research plan.</p>
    <h3>3. On balance, waiving the job offer benefits the United States</h3>
    <p>Finally, you must show that it would be beneficial to waive the job offer and labor certification requirements. Arguments that work include the impracticality of a labor certification for self-employed founders or researchers, the urgency of the work, the harm caused by delay, and your unique contribution relative to other qualified workers.</p>

    <h2>Evidence that wins NIW petitions</h2>
    <p>Every claim in the petition letter must point to an exhibit. The evidence we rely on most often:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>A detailed endeavor statement with milestones, measurable outcomes and a timeline.</li>
      <li>Independent expert letters from people who do not work with you, explaining the national importance of the work and your role in it.</li>
      <li>Recommendation letters from supervisors, collaborators, clients or institutions.</li>
      <li>Publications, citation counts, conference presentations, peer-review service and editorial roles.</li>
      <li>Patents, licenses, product adoption data, revenue, contracts or letters of intent.</li>
      <li>Grants, funding, investment, accelerator admission or government contracts.</li>
      <li>Awards, press coverage, invited talks and professional memberships.</li>
      <li>Documents showing that your work addresses a documented U.S. priority, such as government reports, policy statements or industry data.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Timeline and process</h2>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Consultation and case assessment.</strong> We review your background and tell you whether the NIW is viable and how strong it is.</li>
      <li><strong>Strategy and evidence plan.</strong> We define the endeavor, list the exhibits we need, and draft letter outlines for your recommenders.</li>
      <li><strong>Drafting.</strong> We prepare the petition letter, the endeavor statement and Form I-140, and assemble the exhibit binder.</li>
      <li><strong>Filing.</strong> The I-140 is filed with USCIS. Premium processing is available for NIW petitions and commits USCIS to act within 45 business days. Regular processing times vary and are published by USCIS.</li>
      <li><strong>RFE or NOID, if any.</strong> We respond with targeted evidence and argument within the deadline.</li>
      <li><strong>Green card stage.</strong> If your priority date is current under the Visa Bulletin, you may file Form I-485 concurrently with the I-140 or after approval. If you are abroad, the case moves to consular processing after approval.</li>
    </ol>
    <p>Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 can obtain green cards as derivative beneficiaries.</p>

    <h2>RFE and NOID strategy</h2>
    <p>Most NIW Requests for Evidence target the same weaknesses: an endeavor described too broadly, national importance asserted rather than documented, letters that praise the person but say little about the work, or a plan without concrete next steps. We write petitions to pre-empt those issues. When an RFE arrives, we answer each point with new evidence and a focused brief rather than a restatement of the original filing. If you received an RFE or NOID on a petition prepared elsewhere, we can review it and take over the response.</p>

    <h2>Common mistakes we help you avoid</h2>
    <ul>
      <li>Treating the NIW as a credentials contest instead of an argument about a specific endeavor.</li>
      <li>Letters that are generic, identical, or written entirely by the applicant.</li>
      <li>Claims with no supporting exhibit, or exhibits with no explanation.</li>
      <li>Ignoring the link between your degree or ability and the proposed work.</li>
      <li>Filing without a plan for the I-485 or consular stage and the Visa Bulletin.</li>
    </ul>

faq:
  - question: Do I need a job offer or an employer to file an EB-2 NIW?
    answer: >-
      No. The National Interest Waiver waives the job offer and the PERM labor
      certification, so you file Form I-140 as your own petitioner. You may still
      have an employer or be self-employed; what matters is the endeavor you
      propose and your ability to advance it.
  - question: What is the difference between the EB-2 NIW and the EB-1A?
    answer: >-
      Both allow self-petition. EB-1A requires evidence of extraordinary ability
      at the very top of your field and usually moves faster under the Visa
      Bulletin. The NIW has a lower threshold, focusing on the national importance
      of your work and your position to advance it, which makes it realistic for
      strong professionals who are not yet household names in their discipline.
  - question: How long does an EB-2 NIW petition take?
    answer: >-
      Preparing a petition typically takes two to four months, driven mostly by
      how quickly recommenders return their letters. USCIS processing for the
      I-140 varies by service center; premium processing guarantees action within
      45 business days. The green card itself then depends on your priority date
      under the monthly Visa Bulletin and on whether you adjust status in the
      United States or process at a consulate.
  - question: Can I file Form I-485 at the same time as my NIW petition?
    answer: >-
      Yes, if you are in the United States in a lawful status and your priority
      date is current for your country of birth under the Visa Bulletin. Concurrent
      filing lets you apply for a work permit and advance parole while the case is
      pending. If the category is backlogged, the I-485 is filed later when a visa
      number becomes available.
  - question: Can entrepreneurs and business owners qualify for an NIW?
    answer: >-
      Yes. USCIS guidance recognizes founders whose companies have national
      importance, for example through job creation, innovation, investment or
      work in a priority industry. The evidence is different from an academic
      case: business plans, funding, contracts, revenue, customer letters,
      incubator or accelerator participation, and letters from industry experts.
  - question: What happens if I receive a Request for Evidence?
    answer: >-
      An RFE is not a denial. USCIS tells you which prong it finds weak and gives
      you a deadline, usually around 87 days. We prepare a focused response with
      new exhibits and a brief that answers each point. We also take over RFE and
      NOID responses for petitions originally prepared by other firms or by the
      applicant.
  - question: Can my spouse and children get green cards through my NIW?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 are derivative
      beneficiaries. They can file their own I-485 applications with yours, or
      process at a consulate, and they receive permanent residence when you do.
  - question: Do I need to be in the United States to apply?
    answer: >-
      No. The I-140 can be filed from anywhere. If you are abroad, the approved
      petition moves to the National Visa Center and then to a U.S. consulate for
      an immigrant visa interview. Our Istanbul office works with clients who are
      applying from outside the United States.
