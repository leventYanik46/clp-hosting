    <p><strong>Asylum and humanitarian protection covers every route by which a person who fears persecution, has survived crime or abuse, or faces removal can obtain safety and lawful status in the United States.</strong> This page describes each service, explains which one fits which situation, and tells you what happens at the first consultation; the linked service pages carry the detail.</p>

    <h2>Our services in this area</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong><a href="/practice-area/key-services/affirmative-asylum-applications">Affirmative asylum applications</a>.</strong> For people in the United States, not in removal proceedings, who fear persecution on account of race, religion, nationality, political opinion or membership in a particular social group. We prepare Form I-589 and the evidence, attend the USCIS interview, and stay on if the case is referred to court.</li>
      <li><strong><a href="/practice-area/key-services/green-card-through-asylum">Green card through asylum</a>.</strong> One year after a grant of asylum, you and your derivative family members can apply for permanent residence on Form I-485. We prepare the application, resolve inadmissibility issues early, and press USCIS when the case stalls.</li>
      <li><strong><a href="/practice-area/key-services/special-immigrant-juvenile-status">Special Immigrant Juvenile Status</a>.</strong> For children and young adults under 21 who cannot safely reunite with a parent because of abuse, neglect or abandonment. We handle the state court findings, the Form I-360 petition, protection while the young person waits for a visa number, and the green card.</li>
      <li><strong><a href="/practice-area/key-services/u-visa-vawa-humanitarian-protection">U visa, T visa and VAWA</a>.</strong> For survivors of crime, trafficking and domestic abuse who need status that does not depend on the person who harmed them. We prepare U visa petitions and the law enforcement certification, T visa applications, VAWA self-petitions for abused spouses and children of U.S. citizens and residents and abused parents of U.S. citizens, and VAWA cancellation of removal in court.</li>
      <li><strong><a href="/practice-area/key-services/immigration-detention-and-bond">Immigration detention and bond</a>.</strong> When ICE arrests someone, we locate the person, request a bond hearing, argue for release before the immigration judge, appeal bond denials to the Board of Immigration Appeals, and keep the underlying case moving.</li>
      <li><strong><a href="/practice-area/key-services/removal-defense">Removal defense</a>.</strong> Representation from the Notice to Appear through the individual hearing and any appeal, including defensive asylum, withholding and Convention Against Torture claims, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status before the judge, and motions to reopen old or in absentia orders.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Two court-side tools support all of the above: a <a href="/practice-area/key-services/habeas-corpus">habeas corpus petition</a>, which asks a federal judge whether detention is lawful, and <a href="/practice-area/key-services/bia-appeals-and-motions">BIA appeals and motions</a>, which challenge an immigration judge's or USCIS decision through the Board and the federal courts of appeals.</p>

    <h2>Which route fits your situation</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>You fear returning to your country.</strong> Asylum is the primary route, with a path to a green card and status for your spouse and children. If asylum is barred, most often by the one-year deadline, withholding of removal and Convention Against Torture protection remain available; both require a higher showing of likely harm and are decided only by an immigration judge.</li>
      <li><strong>You were the victim of a crime, or were abused by a U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse or parent.</strong> The U visa covers victims of qualifying crimes who have been helpful to law enforcement, the T visa covers trafficking survivors, and the VAWA self-petition lets the abused family member apply alone, without the abuser's knowledge.</li>
      <li><strong>A child under 21 has been abused, neglected or abandoned by a parent.</strong> Special Immigrant Juvenile Status is built for this, and because the state court must act while it still has jurisdiction over the young person, the case should begin early.</li>
      <li><strong>You already have asylum.</strong> After one year, the next step is the green card through asylum, including for a spouse and children with derivative asylum.</li>
      <li><strong>You or a family member are detained.</strong> Start with a bond hearing request. If the judge denies bond, lacks authority to hear it, or detention drags on, a habeas corpus petition in federal court follows.</li>
      <li><strong>You have a Notice to Appear or a hearing date.</strong> Removal defense is the framework within which the relief above is presented; U visa and VAWA petitions still go to USCIS while the court case continues.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>These routes often overlap, and the order in which they are pursued matters, so we map every option before filing anything.</p>

    <h2>The one-year asylum deadline</h2>
    <p>Form I-589 must generally be filed within one year of your last arrival in the United States. Exceptions exist for changed circumstances, such as a new threat, and for extraordinary circumstances, such as serious illness or a period of lawful status, but each must be proven and the application filed within a reasonable time afterward. If you are near or past the one-year mark, contact us immediately.</p>

    <h2>How we triage your case at the first consultation</h2>
    <p>The first meeting is a structured review of your entry dates and status, any notice or hearing date, whether anyone is detained, the harm you suffered or fear, your family ties, any arrests or convictions, and any prior application. From that we identify the controlling deadlines, the routes realistically open and the evidence each needs. Bring whatever you have: passports and entry records, letters from USCIS, ICE or the immigration court, police reports, medical and counseling records, protective orders, court papers concerning a child, and proof of family relationships. Missing documents are no reason to stay away; many claims rest on your own account, which we help you prepare.</p>

    <h2>Confidentiality</h2>
    <p>Everything you tell us is protected by attorney-client privilege. Asylum applications are confidential under federal regulation, and U visa, T visa and VAWA filings carry statutory protections that bar the government from disclosing the filing or relying on information supplied by the abuser or trafficker. We contact no one about your case without your instruction.</p>

faq:
  - question: What is the difference between asylum, withholding of removal and protection under the Convention Against Torture?
    answer: >-
      Asylum requires a well-founded fear of persecution on a protected ground
      and leads to a green card, derivative status for your spouse and children,
      and travel documents. Withholding of removal and Convention Against Torture
      protection require you to show that harm is more likely than not, are
      decided only by an immigration judge, and prevent removal to the country
      of harm without offering a path to permanent residence or status for
      family members.
  - question: I have been in the United States for more than a year. Can I still apply for asylum?
    answer: >-
      Possibly. The one-year deadline has exceptions for changed circumstances,
      such as a new threat or a change in your country's government, and for
      extraordinary circumstances, such as serious illness or a period of lawful
      status, provided you file within a reasonable time after the circumstance
      arises. If no exception applies, withholding of removal and Convention
      Against Torture protection remain available in removal proceedings. We
      assess the deadline question at the first consultation.
  - question: I am already in removal proceedings. Can I still apply for asylum, a U visa or VAWA?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Asylum, withholding of removal and Convention Against Torture
      protection are presented to the immigration judge as defenses to removal.
      U visa petitions and VAWA self-petitions are filed with USCIS even while
      the court case continues, and we ask the court for a continuance or other
      relief while USCIS decides. People in court may also qualify for VAWA
      cancellation of removal, which the judge decides directly.
  - question: Do I need a police report or criminal charges to qualify for a U visa or VAWA?
    answer: >-
      A U visa requires a certification from a law enforcement agency confirming
      that you were a victim of qualifying criminal activity and have been
      helpful, but it does not require that charges be filed or that anyone be
      convicted. A VAWA self-petition requires no police involvement at all;
      the abuse is proven through your declaration, medical or counseling
      records, protective orders, photographs and statements from people who
      knew what was happening.
  - question: Can my child qualify for SIJS if one parent is still caring for them?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The law requires a state court finding that reunification with one
      or both parents is not viable because of abuse, neglect, abandonment or a
      similar basis under state law. A child living safely with a protective
      parent in the United States can qualify based on the other parent's
      conduct. Timing is critical, because the state court must issue its
      findings while it still has jurisdiction over the young person.
  - question: How soon after winning asylum can I apply for a green card, and does my family apply too?
    answer: >-
      You may file Form I-485 after one year of physical presence in the United
      States as an asylee. A spouse and unmarried children who received
      derivative asylum file their own applications once they meet the same
      one-year requirement. There is no deadline to apply, but delay carries
      risks, so we recommend filing as soon as you are eligible. If an
      inadmissibility issue exists, a waiver may be available.
  - question: ICE has detained my family member. What should we do first?
    answer: >-
      Locate the person using ICE's online detainee locator and note the
      facility, then call us. We confirm whether the person is eligible for
      bond, request a hearing before the immigration judge, and gather the
      evidence of community ties that supports release. If the judge denies
      bond, declines to hear the request, or detention becomes prolonged, we
      file a habeas corpus petition in federal court. Avoid signing any document
      before speaking with a lawyer.
  - question: Will the government tell my abuser or my home country that I applied?
    answer: >-
      No. Asylum applications are confidential under federal regulation, and
      the government may not disclose your filing to third parties, including
      your home government, except in narrow circumstances. U visa, T visa and
      VAWA cases carry statutory confidentiality protections that prohibit
      disclosure of your application and bar the government from relying on
      information supplied by the abuser or trafficker. Everything you tell us
      is also protected by attorney-client privilege.
